# Case Study Format

Every Bangladeshi startup case study follows this 15-field structure. Source-backed only: separate facts from anecdotes, do not overclaim, cite every factual claim.

| # | Field | Question / prompt |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Company | Startup name and basic category. |
| 2 | Problem | What problem did the startup solve? |
| 3 | Bangladesh-specific problem | Why was the problem Bangladesh-specific? |
| 4 | Founding insight | What did the founders see before others did? |
| 5 | First wedge | What narrow entry point helped them start? |
| 6 | Early customers | How did they get early customers? |
| 7 | Trust-building | How did they build trust? |
| 8 | Local lesson | What did they do that generic startup advice would miss? |
| 9 | Constraints | What regulatory, logistics, payment, or talent constraints did they face? |
| 10 | Business model | How did they make money? |
| 11 | Capital | How did they raise capital, if they did? |
| 12 | What to copy | What can a new founder copy? |
| 13 | What not to copy | What should a new founder not copy? |
| 14 | Unknowns | What remains unknown? |
| 15 | Sources | Founder interviews, official announcements, funding articles, reports. |

## Presentation

Keep the company name as the article title. A company banner can identify its sector and the
public-source basis of the study. Add a short timeline of consequential decisions, with citations
beside the events; do not infer an exact date when a source only establishes the sequence.

A focused decision or experiment may receive a distinct visual treatment within the relevant
section. Preserve the complete background, story, tradeoffs, lessons and sources around it. The
timeline and highlighted passage supplement the 15 fields above; they are not a shorter replacement
for the article. Avoid repeating the same lesson under several new headings.

Gallery covers use the finished article's route and publication status from the content manifest.
Only finished studies appear as published cards. Planned studies with approved artwork may appear
alongside them, labelled "To be written" and linking to their starting sources. Company cover copy
is editorial metadata, not a second article or a separate route registry. Keep card headings to two
lines where space permits,
with the full card width available to the text; never truncate a title to force the count.

Reader exercises are deferred in `roadmap.md`.

## Names and attribution

Introduce founders and staff by their full name. Later mentions use their first name or full name,
in both English and Bangla. Do not use a surname alone or assume the final word in a South Asian
name is a surname. Use the full name when the preferred short form is unclear. Preserve names
inside verbatim quotations, source titles and URLs; use full names in our own captions and
attributions.

## Help readers follow the reasoning

- Link turning-point titles to the body section that explains the decision. Keep the timeline's
  citations and the complete story; avoid adding another contents panel.
- Where a process is difficult to understand, annotate the step that causes the problem. Explain
  the cost or limitation beside the relevant choice, not only at the end of the article.
- Compare studies only when they answer the same question differently. A short link beside the
  lesson should explain what to compare, rather than becoming a generic recommendation gallery.
- Give reversals and disappointing results the same care as growth. Keep reported outcomes,
  founder explanations and our interpretation distinct.
- Separate the first customers, early growth and the later business. Explain which conditions
  made a tactic useful at that stage before suggesting it to a new founder.
- Where an interface, transaction or change in the business model carries the lesson, consider
  one short visual sequence beside that passage. Keep a stable layout and reveal only the detail
  being explained. Let readers advance, go back or read the complete explanation without playing
  through the sequence; keep it usable on touch screens and with reduced motion.
- Use dated product screenshots or source artifacts when they help explain a decision. Put the
  annotation next to the relevant detail, and label reconstructions and proposed alternatives.
  Never present a hypothetical improvement as an observed result. Carry the approved company
  palette into these visuals while preserving the site's readable type and contrast.

### Focused visual examples

`CaseCashCycle` in ShopUp shows a simplified cash-on-delivery sequence. Parcel location and
payment location stay on separate tracks. It opens at delivery, when the customer has the parcel
but the seller is still waiting for payment. Native radio controls let readers compare all three
stages without JavaScript; printing includes every explanation. No amount, settlement period or
particular ShopUp contract is implied. Keep each stage's text complete enough to understand
without the diagram. The fixed positions make the two handovers easier to compare than separate
lists of events. All artwork is original. Both editions share the same sequence and translate
every label and caption. At mobile width the controls wrap while the three actors stay aligned;
the diagram needs no image download. Revisit it if the accompanying COD explanation changes.

`CaseLearningEvidence` in Shikho explains two features shown in the company's public illustrations:
practice-test feedback and a chapter report. These are original conceptual diagrams, with direct
links to the supporting illustrations, checked on 14 September 2026. They do not reproduce
Shikho's screens, establish historical product behavior or verify a working in-app journey.
Attendance, answer categories and topics needing improvement remain separate; there are no
invented scores or outcome claims. Recheck the illustrations if their source changes. Keep the
date and evidence limits beside the figure in both editions. The paired panels make the different
signals easy to compare and stack on mobile. Labels and the caption provide the full explanation
in each language, even without the original source images. No third-party image is embedded or
republished; the figure uses semantic text and original icons, without an image or script download.

`CaseAgentLiquidity` in bKash lets readers compare cash-in and cash-out. The two arrows show
cash and electronic value moving in opposite directions. Each state explains which resource the
agent needs; fees and commissions are explicitly omitted. The source is CGAP's general agent
management model, not a particular bKash agent's records. Native radio controls work without
JavaScript, printing includes both explanations, and browsers without the required CSS show the
text instead of an unresponsive diagram. Translate every text prop and the source caption together.

`CaseGroupedDelivery` in Agroshift replaces the generic process list with a branching diagram:
orders combine at a factory, then workers carry their purchases home. The final step makes the
heavy-bag tradeoff visible. The marks are schematic, not order counts. It uses the established
Agroshift palette and stacks according to available content width, including when the sidebar
narrows the article. Keep the source-backed tradeoff and the planned-purchase qualification.

The iFarmer `CaseExchange` uses its company palette to separate bank finance from farm support.
Its November 2022 maize programme describes announced plans and targets, not verified harvest
or repayment results. `CaseExchange` also supports a short note at a specific step, such as
SOLshare's dependence on grid availability. Prefer this small clarification when the existing
visual already explains the process. Review each completed study individually; a clear paragraph
or an existing diagram does not need another visual simply to match the rest of the series.
