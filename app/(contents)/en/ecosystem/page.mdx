# Bangladesh Startup Ecosystem: Who Does What and Where to Start

> **In short:** Identify the obstacle before choosing whom to approach. Customers test whether your offer matters; service providers help operate it; public bodies administer particular rules; investors supply capital on terms. A helpful introduction cannot substitute for customer evidence or an official approval.

A startup rarely operates alone. You need people to buy, people to work with, services to collect money or deliver goods, and public bodies for particular permissions and records. Founders also meet mentors, programs and investors offering support. Together, these relationships are often called the startup ecosystem.

The useful question is where you need help now. If nobody has tried the offer, customer conversations will usually teach more than polishing an investor introduction. If a payment provider cannot approve your account because documents do not match, you need its onboarding team and the relevant issuing office. A mentor may help you understand the problem, but cannot approve that application.

## Start with customers and the work of serving them

Customers give you evidence through what they do: how they manage the problem, whether they try the offer, what they pay and why they return or leave. Their praise can encourage you, but use and payment answer different questions. At the idea stage, [validation](/en/validation) helps you investigate those questions before making a large commitment.

Once sales begin, practical relationships become more important. Payment providers, couriers, suppliers and accountants each handle a part of the work. Choose them around the customer promise you must keep. A courier's coverage and returns process matter if you sell outside your city; a payment provider's settlement schedule matters if supplier bills fall due before the customer money reaches you. The [payments](/en/payments) and [operations](/en/operations) guides explain those choices.

Co-founders and early employees take responsibility for work you cannot sustainably cover alone. Skills matter, but so do available time, judgment and clear agreements. Use [co-founders](/en/cofounders) for shared ownership and founding responsibility, or [team](/en/team) when you need to define a role to hire.

## Use mentors and communities for informed advice

A good mentor helps you think through a decision rather than promising an answer to every startup problem. Explain what you are trying to do, what you have already tried and where you are stuck. Ask about the context behind the advice: a tactic that worked for consumer products may not fit a long corporate buying process.

Communities can connect you with peers, collaborators and people who have faced a similar task. Start with a student, professional or local network if it reaches the relevant people. An event is useful when it helps you learn something or make a relevant connection; attending more events is not progress by itself.

Incubators and accelerators offer structured programs that may include mentoring, workspace, training, introductions or capital. The name does not tell you which benefits are included. Before applying, check the current eligibility, dates, time commitment, fees, ownership terms and reporting duties. Compare the work required with what you need from the program. The [accelerator directory](/en/directory/accelerators) helps you find candidates after deciding what support would be useful.

## Approach funding with a defined purpose

Investors and lenders provide money on agreed terms. Equity investment gives an ownership interest with rights set by the investment; debt brings repayment obligations. Grants can carry spending and reporting conditions. None of these substitutes for understanding what the money will accomplish.

Write the work you need to fund and the evidence behind it before choosing whom to approach. Some businesses need capital before revenue; others can test a smaller version with their own resources or customer income. The [funding roadmap](/en/funding) explains how to compare those choices. Then use the [investor](/en/directory/investors) or [government funding](/en/directory/government-funding) directories to investigate relevant sources.

## Find the right government office

Keep the authority responsible for the decision separate from the portal through which you apply. The official service pages below are starting points checked on 9 September 2026; fees, eligibility and the applicable process still need checking for your case.

| Function | Starting point | First question |
|---|---|---|
| Company incorporation, name clearance and company returns | RJSC | Which entity and application or filing are involved? |
| Income tax, VAT and customs services | NBR | Which taxpayer, activity and reporting period are involved? |
| Banking, payment-system and foreign-exchange rules | Bangladesh Bank; your bank/provider for the transaction | Which rule and licensed service cover the proposed transaction? |
| Trade license for an operating address | Relevant city corporation, municipality or union parishad | Which local authority and activity category cover this address? |
| Finding investment-related government services | BanglaBiz and the listed responsible agencies | Who actually decides this application, and where is its current checklist? |

RJSC's portal separates name-clearance, registration and post-registration services. NBR similarly separates income-tax, VAT, customs and payment services. Local trade licensing is a different administrative function. These distinctions explain why a startup may need to speak to more than one office.[^public-functions]

For a launch, write the actual activity, premises, entity and planned transaction before contacting an office. A broad description such as “an online startup” may hide the information needed to identify the right process.

## Make the first contact useful

A specific request is easier to answer than “please help my startup.” Tell the person what your business does, why you are contacting them and the decision you need help with. For a payment provider, that might mean asking which account covers your activity and requesting its document checklist, charges and settlement terms. For a mentor, it might mean asking for feedback on why customers decline an offer you have already tested.

Share enough context to answer the question. Do not send confidential records merely to obtain an introduction; use the appropriate secure process if documents are later required. For a paid service, agree the scope, fees, exclusions, who will do the work and how problems will be handled before committing.

After the conversation, record the answer and who will take the next step. If a question is unresolved, identify the person or office that can answer it. A useful result might be a corrected document, an agreed customer test or a clear reason to decline a program. It need not be another meeting.

For specific services, use [government services](/en/directory/government-services), [legal and accounting](/en/directory/legal-accounting), [coworking](/en/directory/coworking) or the wider [directory](/en/directory). Listings are starting points for your own checks, not endorsements.
