# Manufacturing: Suppliers, Samples and Quality

> **In short:** Before committing to a production order, define what an acceptable product looks like, how it will be checked and what happens when it fails the check.

## Example: compare two quotes for the same batch

A **specification** describes what must be supplied. **Acceptance criteria** say how you will decide whether it meets that description. **Lead time** is the agreed time between defined events, such as approval of a sample and delivery—not necessarily the date of the first conversation.

For a fictional batch of 100 fabric pouches, the request specifies material, dimensions and tolerance, seam quality, closure, packaging, delivery address and inspection method. The buyer asks both suppliers to quote against the same approved sample.

Suppose the quotes are:

| Cost in this fictional quote | Supplier A | Supplier B |
|---|---:|---:|
| 100 units | BDT 8,000 | BDT 8,500 |
| Sample/setup | BDT 1,500 | Included |
| Delivery | BDT 500 | BDT 500 |
| Stated total | **BDT 10,000** | **BDT 9,000** |

Supplier A's lower unit price is not the lower stated total. Confirm any excluded tax, inspection, rework or other charge before comparing complete costs. Quality and timing may still make either quote unsuitable.

The sample fails if its closure does not work or its size falls outside the written tolerance. The buyer records the defect and requests a corrected sample before approving production. They also agree how failed units in the batch are handled and paid for; sample approval alone is not a remedy for every later defect.

Copy this request structure: **item/specification / quantity / reference sample / acceptance checks / required delivery / price inclusions / payment stages / rejected-goods procedure**. It is usable when two suppliers can quote the same work without inventing missing requirements.

## Work through the next purchasing decision

1. Use [supplier sourcing](/en/manufacturing/supplier-sourcing) to prepare a brief that suppliers can quote against.
2. Use [partner due diligence](/en/manufacturing/partner-due-diligence) to investigate capability and unresolved risks.
3. Use [quality control](/en/manufacturing/quality-control) to agree on sample and batch acceptance criteria.

## Prepare a comparable request

Record the specification, quantity, acceptable variation, packaging, delivery location, required date and inspection process. Ask suppliers to separate unit cost, setup or sample costs, payment timing and delivery charges. Comparing unit price alone can hide a very different commitment.

Your first output should be a supplier comparison with evidence and unknowns. Your next output is an approved sample or a documented reason to revise it—not simply the cheapest quote.

Before ordering, record who pays for defects, rework, delays and rejected goods. Check [licenses and approvals](/en/licenses) for the actual production activity. Use [cash timing](/en/metrics/cashflow-vs-profit) to examine the gap between supplier payments and customer receipts.
