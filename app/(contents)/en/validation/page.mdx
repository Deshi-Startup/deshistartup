# Idea Validation: Decide What to Test Next

> **In short:** Validation reduces a specific uncertainty. Choose the question, run a suitable test and record what the result does—and does not—support.

Use this section when you have a possible customer problem or offer. If you are still choosing which problem to investigate, start with [ideas and market research](/en/ideas).

## Match the method to the question

| What you need to learn | Start here | Evidence to collect |
|---|---|---|
| How does the problem happen today? | [Customer interviews](/en/validation/customer-interviews) | Specific recent situations and existing workarounds |
| How can I ask without leading people? | [Interview scripts and practice](/en/validation/interview-scripts) | Questions and notes separating observation from interpretation |
| Will people take a meaningful next step? | [Test demand](/en/journeys/test-demand) | A defined action in response to an honest offer |
| What should a first experiment contain? | [MVP experiment planner](/en/validation/mvp-experiment-planner) | An assumption, method, limit and review decision |
| How should I investigate price? | [Pricing tests](/en/validation/pricing-tests) | Responses to a specified offer and terms |

## Make the test interpretable

Write down the customer group, assumption, method, time and spending limit, and what result would change your decision. Choose these before seeing the result.

A **hypothetical** example: a founder interviews shop owners about their most recent courier reconciliation. Detailed records of the workaround support a problem hypothesis. They do not yet establish that the owners will buy the founder's proposed software.

An interview, prototype, booking and paid pilot answer different questions. Record exactly what a person committed to, under which terms. Do not combine them into a single “validated” score.

## Finish with a decision

```text
We expected:
We did:
We observed:
Other explanations or missing evidence:
Decision: continue, change, stop, or gather more evidence
Next action and review date:
```

An inconclusive result is useful if you can explain what remains unknown. There is no universal interview count or conversion rate that proves every startup idea.

Before taking payment or testing a regulated or sensitive activity, resolve the relevant [launch requirements](/en/legal-roadmap). Be clear about what exists, what is experimental and what customers can expect.
