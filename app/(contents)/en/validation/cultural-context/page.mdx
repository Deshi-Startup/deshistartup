# Who Actually Releases the Money: Social Realities in Validation

> **Key Takeaway:** The person you sat down with is not guaranteed to be the one who releases the money. An analysis of BDHS 2022 data found that only 2.4% of mothers surveyed made the decision on a major household purchase alone — the rest is shared inside the family. Having a mobile money account is not the same as being able to use it either; GSMA puts Bangladesh's gender gap on this at 56%. And people often hide the truth about money lost or a failed decision, out of shame. So validation is not just about phrasing questions well — you also have to work out who decides, who actually operates the phone, and who is dodging the question.

## The Decision Is Rarely Made Alone

You had a good conversation, the person even admitted the problem was real, and still no order comes in — plenty of founders have lived this. The cause is often not the conversation, it is the decision. In Bangladesh, medium and large spending decisions are rarely made by one person; they get shared inside the family.

A study using Bangladesh Demographic and Health Survey (BDHS) 2022 data sampled 3,607 mothers who had given birth within the previous two years. Among them, only 2.4% made the decision on a major household purchase alone, 7.6% decided their own healthcare alone, and 5.1% decided visits to family or relatives alone. The sample is young mothers, so it is not a picture of every customer, but the direction is clear: money decisions are rarely solo, and usually shared.

That means the enthusiasm of the person across the table is not enough on its own. Someone excited about a cooking problem may not hold the mobile banking PIN, or may need to sit with her husband or father-in-law before spending. Say a Dhaka founder pitches a weekly vegetable box to a homemaker. She says yes happily, the price feels right too. Then the bill arrives, her husband asks where this money is going, and the subscription stops right there.

So add one question to the end of every conversation: can you make this decision alone right now, or do you need to sit with someone else first? Hesitation in the answer means the real conversation is not over yet. There is already a field for this in the note template on the [customer interviews](/en/validation/customer-interviews) page — make sure you do not leave it blank.

## Trust Runs Through Who You Know

Trust here does not attach to a brand, it hangs off a person. Someone responding to an unfamiliar page or ad shows interest. Someone arriving on a familiar person's referral shows something else too — the first person has already told someone, and that word worked. Lump both signals together in validation and the real picture blurs.

So fill in one field at the start of every conversation: how did this person find you? An ad, a Facebook group, or another customer's word? Check it a few weeks later — how many of the referred people actually paid, versus how many from ads. A higher referral rate is a good sign, because that same thread will bring the next customer too.

## The Truth About Money or Failure Stays Hidden

The [customer interviews](/en/validation/customer-interviews) page covers how to spot a polite yes. There is another layer beside that, and it is not politeness, it is shame. On topics like debt, a business loss, illness, or a decision about children, it is hard to tell the truth to a stranger, especially with another family member or a neighbour standing nearby.

Changing the shape of the question changes the answer here. Ask directly, "Are you in debt?" and the answer will not come, or will come false. Instead, shift the question to the third person: "Do a lot of people around you take on debt for this problem?" That question lets someone talk about themselves without putting their own name on it. The answer is not proof, but it is a real signal.

Location is a factor too. Ask a money question in an open courtyard or in front of a shop and the people nearby hear it, and the answer gets arranged to preserve face. Where you can, keep sensitive questions private, between the two of you.

## Having the Phone Does Not Mean Being Able to Run It

If your product depends on a mobile app or mobile money, check one thing separately: an open account and being able to run it yourself day to day are not the same question. Per GSMA's 2024 State of the Industry Report on Mobile Money, Bangladesh's gender gap in mobile money use is 56%. Women's account ownership is stuck at 21%. Awareness is not lagging — between 2022 and 2023 it rose from 61% to 72% among women and from 74% to 81% among men. So knowing about it and using it yourself are two different things.

So do not stop at a "yes" to "do you have a bKash account?" Instead, run a small show-me test: hand the phone back and ask when they last sent a transaction themselves. Someone who enters their own PIN to send money and someone who calls a son or husband over to do it have different experiences using your product. What works for the first group may need a phone-based alternative for the second.

## Ask Directly When Religious or Ethical Hesitation Might Apply

For a loan, savings, or insurance product, interest can be a real barrier — do not assume either way. Per Bangladesh Bank data, Islamic banks held 22.34% of total banking sector deposits as of January 2026. A share that large says this preference drives many customers' actual decisions.

So when validating a financial product, ask directly which structure — interest-based or interest-free — someone finds acceptable. Testing both framings side by side tells you which segment wants which, instead of guessing. The same caution applies to ad imagery, event settings, or product ingredients where religious or social objection could apply. Asking directly instead of assuming is the cheapest test available.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | What follows |
|---|---|
| Talking only to the interested person, not the decision-maker | You get enthusiasm, not sales |
| Counting referral and cold yeses the same way | You cannot tell which channel actually works |
| Asking directly about money or debt | Silence, or a dodge |
| Asking sensitive questions in front of neighbours or family | Answers get arranged to save face |
| Assuming an account means someone can use it themselves | The product stalls for whoever does not actually operate it |
| Skipping the interest question on a financial product | A large segment of customers walk away at the start |

## Checklist

- [ ] Asked whether this decision can be made alone right now
- [ ] Recorded how this person found me
- [ ] Shifted money or debt questions to the third person where needed
- [ ] Kept sensitive questions private
- [ ] Asked for proof of use rather than an account
- [ ] Asked directly about interest for a financial product

Rule of thumb: **If you do not know who releases the money, the validation is not finished.**
