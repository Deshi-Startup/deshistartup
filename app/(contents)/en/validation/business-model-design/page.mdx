# Design a Business Model You Can Test

> **In short:** Start with one customer group and connect what they need, how you will deliver it, and who pays. Use the Business Model Canvas worksheet below to expose the gaps. A completed canvas is still a set of assumptions. Before building more, test the unsupported assumption that would make the model fail.

Use this when you have an idea but cannot yet explain how it becomes a business, or when customers want the product but serving them costs too much. You will leave with a filled worksheet and one test to run. For choosing between a startup, agency or other business type, start with [the business-type comparison](/en/start-here/startup-vs-sme).

A **business model** explains how you create something customers value, deliver it and earn money from it. A subscription is only the charging method; it says nothing about why someone pays or what serving them costs. Strategyzer's Business Model Canvas connects those questions through nine blocks.[^strategyzer-canvas]

## Copy the worksheet

Use a notebook or copy this text. Budget 45 minutes for a first draft, not for validating the business. Bring any customer notes, supplier quotes and cost records you already have. No paid software is required.

Write one or two lines per block for one model. Separate the person using the product from the person paying. For a marketplace, name both sides and connect each side to its benefit, channel and payment role. Beside each answer, write **assumed**, **reported** or **observed**, then the source and date. An observed payment supports that sale; it does not establish future renewals.

```text
Business-model worksheet - name / date / version:

1. Customer segments
   Who uses this? Who pays? Which group comes first?

2. Value propositions
   What useful result do they get?
   Why replace what they do today?

3. Channels
   How will they find, buy and receive the offer?

4. Customer relationships
   What help earns trust and keeps them using it?
   Who handles setup, problems and cancellation?

5. Revenue streams
   Who pays how much, for what, and how often?
   When does the money actually arrive?

6. Key activities
   What must we do well to keep the promise?

7. Key resources
   Which people, tools, data or equipment are needed?

8. Key partners
   Who outside the team is essential?
   What permission or agreement is still missing?

9. Cost structure
   What changes with each sale or customer?
   What must we pay even with no sales?

For each answer: assumed / reported / observed
Evidence reference and date:

Our additional decision notes
What breaks if customers grow tenfold?
Which unsupported assumption could stop the model?
Next test / spending limit / owner / review date:
```

Keep the attribution when reusing it.[^strategyzer-rights]

The worksheet is usable when another person can follow the connection from a specific customer's need to the work and money required. “Everyone,” “social media” and “good service” hide decisions. Replace them with a first buyer group, a way to reach them and a service promise you can actually fulfil.

## Which one-page tool fits the job?

Pick the tool that reveals the uncertainty in front of you. You do not need to maintain three versions of the same plan.

| Tool | Use it to work through |
|---|---|
| Business Model Canvas | How the customer offer, delivery work, partners and finances fit together, for a new or existing business |
| Lean Canvas | Early uncertainty about the problem, solution, key metrics and an advantage others cannot easily copy |
| [Our one-page startup plan](/en/start-here/one-page-startup-plan) | A quick 12-question draft covering the customer, price, margin and next tasks |

Ash Maurya created Lean Canvas as a startup-focused adaptation. It replaces four Business Model Canvas blocks with problem, solution, key metrics and unfair advantage.[^lean-canvas] It is a distinct framework; our startup plan is not its official template. If you already have a useful plan, expand the unclear delivery or money assumptions rather than starting again for appearance's sake.

## Worked example: matching courier payments

Imagine Nadia in Chattogram wants to build software that helps small clothing brands match courier statements against payments received. **Nadia, the businesses, prices, costs and test thresholds below are fictional. Nothing here is evidence of market demand or a provider's current fees.**

Her proposed promise is a daily list of payments to investigate, without retyping every order. She starts with brands using two couriers whose statements can be exported. A tool that only works after Nadia manually repairs every file may be useful, but the amount of repair work changes the business model.

Here is her first version. Every row is still **assumed**.

| Block | Nadia's starting answer |
|---|---|
| Customer segments | Owner pays; owner or staff checks payments. Start with small clothing brands using two couriers. |
| Value propositions | Find unmatched payments with less copying, while letting staff check the result. |
| Channels | Founder-led demonstrations to qualifying brands; then test referrals beyond friends. |
| Customer relationships | Assisted setup, a short Bangla walkthrough and a defined support window. |
| Revenue streams | BDT 1,000 per brand each month; test payment before the service month. |
| Key activities | Import files, match records, flag exceptions and correct errors. |
| Key resources | Import/matching software, secure storage and someone who understands the records. |
| Key partners | Payment provider; courier cooperation if later integrations require access or permission. No agreement is assumed signed. |
| Cost structure | Hosting, payment processing, support and onboarding, plus development, sales and administration. |

Business Model Canvas framework: [Strategyzer.com](https://www.strategyzer.com). This filled example is an original hypothetical adaptation, shared under [CC BY-SA 4.0](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/).

Two dependencies now stand out. Can brands supply usable files with permission to process them? And can the team handle exceptions without spending hours on each account? Write those questions down before promising automatic integrations. Ask for consent and remove unnecessary personal details when testing sample records.

The [competitor map](/en/ideas/competitor-map) helps compare the tool with a spreadsheet, bookkeeper or existing courier dashboard. Here, Nadia only needs to state what buyers would replace and why the change is worth the effort.

## Check what one customer leaves

For a simplified month, suppose Nadia receives BDT 1,000 per paying brand. Allow BDT 100 for hosting and payment costs and value support time at BDT 300 an hour. These are planning assumptions, not quotations. Count the founder's labour even before paying a salary.

At 30 minutes of support, the labour allowance is 0.5 × 300 = BDT 150. That leaves **1,000 − 100 − 150 = BDT 750 per brand-month**, before shared costs and other exclusions below.

Compare that with two hours of support:

| Per paying brand per month | 30 minutes of support | 2 hours of support |
|---|---:|---:|
| Revenue assumed received | BDT 1,000 | BDT 1,000 |
| Hosting and payment costs | BDT 100 | BDT 100 |
| Support labour allowance | BDT 150 | BDT 600 |
| Contribution before shared costs | BDT 750 | BDT 300 |

With BDT 30,000 of shared monthly costs, the simplified break-even is 30,000 ÷ 750 = **40 paying brands** in the first case, or 30,000 ÷ 300 = **100** in the second. At 100 brands, the second case also requires **200 support hours a month**. Check whether the team can actually supply those hours.

This is not net profit or a cash forecast. It leaves out acquisition, initial onboarding, tax, refunds and missed payments; the BDT 30,000 is an assumed allowance for development and administration, not a complete budget. Add the missing items before deciding what you can afford. If contribution is zero or negative, adding customers cannot cover fixed costs under those assumptions. Use [unit economics](/en/metrics/unit-economics) for the fuller calculation.

Cash timing needs a separate line: if a brand pays after the month ends, support and hosting may need funding before that payment arrives. Record the expected receipt date and the bills due before it.

## Can delivery repeat as the business grows?

A **repeatable** model can serve another similar customer through a process the team can use again. A **scalable** model has a credible way to increase business without delivery effort and costs rising at the same rate indefinitely. “It is software” does not establish either.

For Nadia, compare 10 brands with 100. At 30 support minutes each, monthly support grows from 5 to 50 hours; at two hours each, it grows from 20 to 200. Repeat the comparison for setup, selling and file corrections. Which task becomes the limit, and what change might reduce it without losing accuracy?

Use “sustainable” here to mean that revenue, cash timing and delivery can support continuing operations. A canvas does not certify environmental or social performance. Record harmful dependencies too: misuse of customer data, unpaid work that cannot continue, or a promise to customers that depends on access you do not have.

Accelerating Bangladesh's public business-model curriculum also connects revenue and costs with resources, partnerships and testing.[^ab-models] For this example, the useful next step is to measure the expensive work before projecting growth.

## Choose the assumption to test first

Ask two questions for each uncertain answer: **Would the model fail if this were wrong? What evidence do we actually have?** Prioritize a consequential assumption with weak evidence. Strategyzer separates risks about customer demand, ability to deliver and financial viability; a test should address the uncertainty you selected.[^strategyzer-testing]

Nadia might start with delivery: “We can match files from both couriers accurately without extensive manual repair.” She can test this with consenting brands' sample records before building a full product. Check matches against a manually verified answer, count incorrect matches and record repair time. If essential files are unavailable or error rates make the result unsafe to use, change the scope before taking paid orders.

Once that is feasible, a small paid service test could check whether the offer is worth using:

```text
Assumption: qualified brands will pay for this result.
Offer: one month at BDT 1,000, clear scope and cancellation terms.
Recruit: approach 10 qualifying brands; record how they were found.
Early decision rule: at least 3 pay and use the weekly output.
Delivery check: log setup separately from recurring support.
Cost limit: BDT 3,000 cash plus 15 founder hours for the pilot.
Review: after the first month; also record a later renewal decision.
Owner: Nadia.
```

These limits are choices for the fictional pilot, not industry benchmarks. Three buyers from ten contacts would justify another small test, not a market-size claim or proof of retention. No sales could reflect the buyer group, trust, timing or offer; record the reason before changing the model. A spending or time limit reached early is a reason to stop and review, not silently expand the trial.

For your own test, write the offer, observable result, decision threshold, cost limit and review date before starting. Choose a [small MVP format](/en/validation/mvp-examples), use the [experiment planner](/en/validation/mvp-experiment-planner) for detail, or follow [pricing tests](/en/validation/pricing-tests) when willingness to pay is the main uncertainty.

## Before spending more

- [ ] One first customer group, payer and useful result are clear.
- [ ] Delivery work, essential permissions and partners are named.
- [ ] Estimates are marked as estimates, with evidence and dates where available.
- [ ] Labour, cash timing and what happens at ten times the customers have been checked.
- [ ] One unsupported assumption has a bounded test and a review date.
- [ ] The next version will record what changed after the test, including results that contradict the idea.

Nadia's next action is a file-matching test, followed by a bounded paid pilot if delivery works. Yours should be just as specific. Keep the dated worksheet so you can see what the evidence changed.
