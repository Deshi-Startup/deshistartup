# Customer Interview Scripts, in Bangla and English

> **In short:** Use a script to remember what to explore, then follow the person's actual experience. Record what they said, what you think it means, and what you still do not know separately. Prepare the questions before the conversation and take useful notes afterward.

You have arranged to talk to a potential customer, but it is easy to spend the conversation explaining your idea instead of learning how they work. These scripts help you prepare questions about actual experience: what happened, how the person handled it and what it cost them.

Use them when you are investigating a problem. If you already have an offer and want to discuss a purchase, use the [sales conversation guide](/en/customers/whatsapp-messenger-sales). The opening below promises a research conversation, so keep that promise.

The scripts below include Bangla and English. The practice and worksheet on this page are in English. [Try the follow-up exercises](#practise-following-up), or go straight to the script that fits your respondent.

## How to use these

- **Replace the bracketed topics.** Name a concrete task, such as coordinating a class change, rather than an entire industry.
- **Choose relevant questions.** You do not have to ask all ten or follow a fixed order. Start with recent experience and follow useful details before returning to your list.
- **Agree a time limit.** You might ask for 15–20 minutes, but let the person choose whether that works. End on time or ask whether they want to continue.
- **Make promises you can keep.** Adapt the opening to your actual use of the notes. Ask separately before recording audio, using a quotation, or contacting them again.

A blank answer is an unknown. It does not mean the problem is absent or the person has no budget.

## Opening Lines

Explain who you are and why you want to learn about the task. The opening below is for research, not a disguised sales approach. Use it only when you are genuinely not selling during this conversation.

```text
Hello, I'm [name], from [city]. We're working on [topic], and
we're trying to learn from people who actually do this work
every day.

I'm not here to sell you anything today, and I won't be. This
should take 15 to 20 minutes. Your name and your company's name
won't appear anywhere.

Is it all right if I take notes?
```

## Script 1: An Ordinary Consumer

```text
1. What does your day look like? When do you usually deal with [topic]?
2. When did [the problem] last happen? Can you walk me through it?
3. What did you do then? Whose help did you need?
4. How much time did that take? Did it cost you anything?
5. How often does this happen in a month?
6. Had you tried anything else before? Why did you stop?
7. What's the most annoying part of how you handle it now?
8. Does this cost come out of your own pocket, or does someone
   in the family pay?
9. Who have you talked to about this? What did they say?
10. Who else do you know with the same trouble? Could I speak to them?
```

```text
১. আপনার দিনটা কীভাবে যায়? [বিষয়]-এর কাজটা সাধারণত কখন করতে হয়?
২. শেষ কবে [সমস্যাটা] হয়েছিল? কী হয়েছিল, একটু বলবেন?
৩. তখন কী করলেন? কার সাহায্য নিতে হয়েছিল?
৪. এতে কত সময় গেল? টাকা কিছু গিয়েছিল?
৫. এমনটা মাসে কতবার হয়?
৬. এর আগে অন্য কিছু চেষ্টা করেছিলেন? ছেড়ে দিলেন কেন?
৭. এখন যেভাবে সামলান, তার সবচেয়ে বিরক্তিকর অংশ কোনটা?
৮. এই খরচটা আপনার নিজের পকেট থেকে যায়, নাকি পরিবারের কেউ দেন?
৯. এই নিয়ে কার সঙ্গে কথা হয়েছে? কেউ কিছু বলেছেন?
১০. একই ঝামেলা আর কার হয় বলে জানেন? তাঁর সঙ্গে কথা বলা যাবে?
```

## Script 2: A Shop or Small Business Owner

```text
1. How many sales or orders do you handle a day? How many people
   work here?
2. When did you last have to do [the job]? How did you get it done?
3. Who does it, you or someone else?
4. What did this cost you in total last month, counting staff,
   travel, fees, and losses?
5. The last time something went wrong, how much did it cost you?
6. How long have you run it this way? Who set it up?
7. Has anyone offered you something for this? Did you buy it?
   What led to that decision?
8. Do you pay for any service monthly right now? Which, and how much?
9. Do you decide on something new alone, or is someone else involved?
10. Could I have a look at your ledger or register?
```

```text
১. দিনে কত বিক্রি বা কত অর্ডার সামলান? কজন লোক আছেন?
২. [কাজটা] শেষ কবে করতে হয়েছিল? কীভাবে সেরেছিলেন?
৩. কাজটা কে করেন, আপনি নিজে না অন্য কেউ?
৪. গত মাসে এই খাতে মোট কত টাকা গেছে? লোক, যাতায়াত, ফি,
   ক্ষতি সব ধরে।
৫. ভুল হলে শেষবার কত টাকার ধাক্কা লেগেছিল?
৬. এখন যে ব্যবস্থায় চালান, সেটা কবে থেকে? কে সাজিয়ে দিয়েছে?
৭. এই সমস্যার জন্য কেউ কিছু বেচতে এসেছিল? নিয়েছিলেন কি না? কেন সেই সিদ্ধান্ত নিলেন?
৮. মাসে টাকা দিতে হয় এমন কোনো সেবা এখন নেন? কোনটা, কত?
৯. নতুন কিছু নেওয়ার সিদ্ধান্ত আপনি একা নেন, নাকি আরও কেউ থাকেন?
১০. আপনার খাতা বা রেজিস্টারটা একবার দেখতে পারি?
```

A demonstration can help clarify the workflow, but access to records is optional. Ask for a made-up or redacted example instead of a customer ledger with names, balances, or contact details. Accept a refusal without pressure. Treat what you see as one example to understand, not a complete account of the business.

## Script 3: A Decision-Maker Inside a Company

```text
1. Who on your team does this work? How many people does it take?
2. How does the process run today, step by step?
3. How much time or headcount went into it over the last three months?
4. Is there a separate budget for this? If so, who holds it?
5. Which tool or vendor are you using now? When does the contract end?
6. What steps does a new vendor have to clear? How long does that take?
7. Even if you agree, who else could block it by saying no?
8. Is this on the priority list for the year? Where on it?
9. Have you bought something like this before and dropped it? Why?
10. What would a small pilot need? Who signs it off?
```

```text
১. আপনার টিমে এই কাজটা কে করেন? কতজন লাগে?
২. প্রক্রিয়াটা এখন ধাপে ধাপে কীভাবে চলে?
৩. গত তিন মাসে এতে কত সময় বা লোকবল গেছে?
৪. এই খাতে আলাদা বাজেট আছে কি না? থাকলে কার হাতে?
৫. এখন কোন টুল বা ভেন্ডর ব্যবহার করছেন? চুক্তি কবে শেষ হয়?
৬. নতুন ভেন্ডর নিতে হলে কী কী ধাপ পার হতে হয়? কত দিন লাগে?
৭. আপনি রাজি হলেও আর কারা না বললে কাজটা আটকে যাবে?
৮. এই সমস্যা এ বছরের অগ্রাধিকারের তালিকায় আছে? কোন জায়গায়?
৯. আগে এমন কিছু কিনে পরে বাদ দিয়েছেন? কেন বাদ গেল?
১০. ছোট একটা পাইলট চালাতে হলে কী লাগবে? অনুমোদন কে দেন?
```

Questions about budget and approval help distinguish interest from a buying process. The person who uses a tool may not approve its purchase. Record what they actually tell you rather than assuming their authority from their job title.

## Closing Lines

```text
Thank you, this was genuinely useful.

We're working on this problem. If we have something small to show
in a few weeks, could I show it to you? Your reaction would help.

And do you know anyone else doing the same work I could talk to?
```

```text
অনেক ধন্যবাদ, এতে আমার অনেক কাজ হলো।

আমরা এই সমস্যা নিয়েই কাজ করছি। কয়েক সপ্তাহ পর ছোট একটা জিনিস
দেখানোর মতো হলে আপনাকে দেখাতে পারি? আপনার মতামত পেলে উপকার হবে।

আর একই কাজ করেন এমন কাউকে চেনেন, যাঁর সঙ্গে কথা বলা যেতে পারে?
```

Ask about a future demonstration only after you have explored the current experience. Permission to show something later is a follow-up agreement, not an order or proof that they will pay.

## Questions to leave out of a research interview

| Do not ask | Where it goes wrong |
|---|---|
| "This must be a problem for you, surely?" | The answer was handed over inside the question |
| "What problems do you have with orders and delivery?" | Two questions at once, and you cannot tell which was answered |
| "Would you take it at BDT 500 a month?" | You named the price, so all they can do is say yes or no |
| "Could you work out how much time a year this wastes?" | Your arithmetic has been loaded onto them |
| "What did you think of our app?" | That asks for a product opinion, rather than a specific account of the task you are researching |

## Review the evidence after the conversation

Take a few minutes while the details are fresh. Use the downloadable notes above, or make three columns: **observation**, **interpretation**, and **unknown**. A direct quotation belongs in the first column only when you recorded the actual words and have permission to use them.

**Fictional example:** a manager describes spending an hour calling students after last week's class change. They also say their shared calendar usually works well.

| What you can record | What it does and does not establish |
|---|---|
| One reported class change required an hour of calls. | A specific incident to investigate. It does not establish the typical weekly cost. |
| The manager says the shared calendar usually works well. | Evidence that the current alternative has value. Explore when it works and when it does not. |
| You did not ask who approves purchases. | An unknown, not evidence that no budget or authority exists. |

Your next question could be: “What was different about that class change?” That is more useful than turning the conversation into an unsupported number out of ten.

Across interviews, compare recurring observations, differences between respondents, and evidence against your assumption. Keep refusals and recruitment gaps visible too. Use that record to choose another small test, change the question or group, or pause. It does not automatically rank people or validate an idea.

## Compare several interviews without inventing a score

One interview helps you understand one person. After several, look for where the experiences agree and where they differ. In the same hypothetical class-scheduling example, the notes from three managers might look like this:

| Interview | Reported experience | Interpretation to test | Still unknown |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | Last-minute change led to an hour of calls and two missed notifications | Urgent changes may be the difficult case | Frequency and purchasing authority |
| B | Shared calendar handles ordinary changes well | The existing alternative has real value | What happens with an urgent change |
| C | An assistant handles notifications; manager cannot recall the details | We may be interviewing the wrong person about the workflow | Assistant's experience and permission to speak |

The next action is to explore urgent changes and speak to the person doing the work, with permission. This record does not establish that three managers need a new product. Add recruitment gaps and refusals alongside completed interviews so you remember whose experience is missing.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | What follows |
|---|---|
| Reading the questions straight down the list | The interview turns into an interrogation and people close up |
| Picking the wrong script | Questions that do not match the respondent’s role can miss how purchasing actually works |
| Pitching before understanding the current task | You may steer the discussion toward your proposed solution |
| Forgetting to agree a useful next step | You leave without knowing whether or how to follow up |
| Moving to the next interview without notes | Specific events become hard to distinguish later |
| Treating an unanswered question as a negative answer | You reject an assumption without evidence |

## Checklist

- [ ] Picked the right script for the kind of respondent
- [ ] Filled the bracketed slots with my own subject
- [ ] Said the opening lines and asked permission to take notes
- [ ] Followed recent events with relevant questions
- [ ] Kept the research conversation separate from a sales pitch
- [ ] Asked whether a referral or follow-up would be welcome
- [ ] Separated observations, interpretations, and unknowns in my notes
- [ ] Kept evidence that challenges my assumption

Rule of thumb: **The script is there to help you remember your questions, not to fill the respondent's time. Once they are inside their own story, put the script down.**
