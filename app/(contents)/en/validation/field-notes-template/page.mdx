# Field Notes Template for Shops, Markets, and Neighborhoods

> **In short:** [Customer Interview Scripts](/en/validation/interview-scripts) is for sitting down and talking to one person. This template is for beforehand or alongside that – for walking through a place and writing down what you can tell without anyone saying a word. Copy the grid below and fill it out separately for every shop, market, or neighborhood visit. Even when you don't get a chance to talk to anyone, what your eyes pick up already answers a lot of questions.

Your eyes start working before anyone opens their mouth. Stand outside a shop for ten minutes before walking in, or just walk once through a market, and you'll have gathered plenty before asking a single question. This template is where that looking gets organized.

## Copy the Grid

```text
Field notes – [market/shop/neighborhood name] – [date, day, time]

The place
1. Address and area:
2. What kind of place (street shop / market / mall / neighborhood):
3. Day and time (weekday or holiday, morning or evening):

Foot traffic
4. Roughly how many people in half an hour:
5. Who's mostly there (age guess, gender, alone or with family):
6. When it's busiest, when it's quiet (the shopkeeper will tell you if you ask):

Price and competition
7. Nearby prices for the product or service, at least three samples:
8. How many similar shops or sellers in the same spot:
9. Which one gets more traffic, and why it looks that way:

Money and contact
10. Which payment methods are visible (cash, bKash/Nagad QR, card):
11. Any signboard, Facebook page, or phone number visible:

Your own observations
12. What surprised you, or what you hadn't thought of:
13. What to check on the next visit:
```

None of the thirteen boxes need more than one line. The whole grid should take 15-20 minutes to fill in at a shop or market.

## The Boxes That Matter Most

**Box 3, day and time.** The same market looks different on a Friday morning versus a Tuesday afternoon. Don't decide "that's just how this market is" after one visit – go back at least twice, at different times.

**Boxes 4 and 6, foot traffic.** You don't need an exact count, a rough guess is fine. But writing the number down lets you compare against a different location two weeks later. "It felt busy" doesn't give you that comparison.

**Box 8, density.** Five grocery shops in one lane tells you competition is heavy, at a glance. That number is the raw material for [Competitor Map](/en/ideas/competitor-map).

**Box 10, payment methods.** A bKash or Nagad QR code taped to the counter tells you customers there are used to digital payment. Seeing only a cash box is information too – if you're bringing in digital payment there, you'll need to build the habit first.

## A Worked Example: Firoza's Grocery Delivery

Say Firoza is considering a home-delivery grocery service for a neighborhood in Mirpur. She starts by visiting a local kacha bazar and fills out the grid like this.

```text
Field notes – Mirpur Section 11 kacha bazar – 2 August 2026, Sunday, 8 AM

The place
1. Address and area: Mirpur Section 11, two lanes off the main road.
2. What kind of place: open kacha bazar, around 30 shops.
3. Day and time: Sunday morning, the weekly market day.

Foot traffic
4. Roughly how many in half an hour: around 150-180 people walked
   through.
5. Who's mostly there: mostly middle-aged women, alone. Saw a few
   domestic workers with bags and shopping lists.
6. When it's busiest: shopkeepers said 7-9 AM and 5-7 PM are the
   busiest windows. Nearly empty at midday.

Price and competition
7. Prices: potatoes 45 taka/kg, onions 70 taka/kg, eggs 140 taka
   a dozen.
8. Similar shops: 8 vegetable stalls, 5 grocery/general stores.
9. Most traffic: one corner vegetable stall had the most customers;
   the shop next door said it's because he weighs generously.

Money and contact
10. Payment methods: almost every shop was cash-only. Saw bKash QR
    codes at two shops; the owners said they're rarely used.
11. Contact: no signboard had a phone number, no Facebook page
    visible anywhere.

Your own observations
12. What surprised me: almost everyone arrived on foot. Almost no
    space out front for a rickshaw or car. People were hauling
    heavy bags home themselves.
13. To check next visit: ask 3-4 people directly how much extra
    they'd pay to have a heavy grocery order delivered home.
```

Box 12 gave Firoza her real signal. The actual pain of hauling a heavy bag home on foot showed up without asking a single question. Box 10 told her the shopkeepers here aren't used to digital payment yet, so starting delivery with cash-on-delivery is the safer bet. Both decisions fell out of simply filling in the grid.

## Keep Your Notes Alive

- Don't decide anything after one visit to one place. Fill the grid out separately for at least two different times, and ideally two different areas.
- Take photos – signboards, price lists, a busy moment. Write that day's file name under the photo so you can find it again later.
- If you visit several places in one day, open a fresh grid for each. Don't mix them onto one sheet.
- At the end of the week, lay all your notes side by side and see how much boxes 8 and 10 shift from place to place. A shift there tells you a decision that worked in one area won't automatically carry to another.

Rule of thumb: **write down what your eyes tell you before you ask a single question. Let the answers build on that, not the other way around.**
