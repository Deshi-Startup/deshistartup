# Field Research Plan: Make Each Visit Answer a Decision

> **In short:** Before a field visit, write down the decision you need to make. Choose whose work to watch and what you need to learn. Visit people beyond your friends and the nearest shops. Record what you saw and what you still do not know. A few visits cannot tell you what all of Bangladesh needs.

## Copy the visit plan

Use this to plan visits before building a product or entering a new area. Field research means learning where people live or work. The [field notes template](/en/validation/field-notes-template) gives you space to record each visit.

```text
Decision we need to make:
Current assumption:
Evidence that would change our mind:

Customer group and task:
People to include: user / payer / approver / people using alternatives
Differences to include: location / size / work steps / tools or access needs
People our plan will probably miss:
How we will find and invite people:

Visit date, place and activity to observe:
Researcher and note-taker:
How we will ask permission to take notes or record:
Questions and tasks:
Budget: time, travel, agreed payments and extra costs
Contact/check-in plan for the visit:

Record each finding as: observed / reported / interpretation
Time to review notes together:
Decision owner and date:
What this round cannot establish:
Next evidence to collect:
```

Fill in the first three lines before inviting anyone. If the decision is unclear, more interviews can produce more notes without making you less uncertain.

## Decide what a visit can answer

Watch a task when the setting may affect it. A shared phone, a busy shop or a second person may change how the work gets done. The Government Digital Service calls this contextual research: seeing a task in its normal setting.[^gds-contextual]

For example, watch how a shop records a delivery discrepancy. Do not ask only whether a future inventory app sounds useful. A remembered opinion, a recent event and a task you watched are different kinds of evidence.

Use visits to learn **how and why** something happens. They cannot tell you how many firms in a district have that problem. That needs a study designed to count it fairly. Start with a way to reach the full group you want to study. Even if you visit every shop on one street, you have left out all the other streets.

Accelerating Bangladesh's course covers watching tasks, asking questions and testing ideas.[^ab-research] Choose the method that fits your question. One method will not tell you the right product, price and market size all at once.

## Meet people with different ways of working

Choose differences that could change your product. A shop owner who orders stock personally may need a different workflow from a branch manager who must get approval. Someone who shares a phone may behave differently from someone with a dedicated business device.

Make a list of the different people you need to meet. For example, include owners who do the work themselves and staff who do it for them. Speak to people who stopped using a tool, as well as those who like it. Record who said no or could not come. Do not guess someone's income, gender, age or reading ability from their looks.

PriyoShop co-founder Dipty Mandal described checking what shops needed before entering an area. She also discussed hiring local staff who could speak well with shopkeepers.[^fs-priyoshop] This is one firm's account at that time. Use it as a reason to check each area yourself.

## Worked plan: should a restocking tool work through voice messages?

Imagine a startup testing a restocking tool for small grocery shops in Cumilla. All visit counts, findings and budgets below are illustrative.

The decision is whether to build voice ordering first. The team currently assumes typing product names is the main barrier. Evidence that would change its mind: ordering is easy, while checking availability or getting credit approval causes the actual delay.

The team plans six shop visits over two days. At three shops, the owner orders stock. At the other three, someone else does it. The visits cover a central market and a nearby shopping area. These shops are chosen to show different ways of working. They do not stand for all shops in Cumilla.

Here is what each part of the plan must produce.

| Part | Choice for this round | Evidence to bring back |
|---|---|---|
| Before a visit | Confirm when restocking normally happens | A real activity to observe |
| At the shop | Follow the last order from need to delivery | Steps, people, waiting and workarounds |
| Contrast | Include typed, spoken and paper-based ordering | Why each method is used |
| Debrief | Compare barriers across cases | A revised product assumption |

Visit when the task normally happens. Six polite chats will not help you see how work gets done. If no order is placed during a visit, ask the person to walk through their last order. Mark the account as something they told you, not something you watched.

### A 45-minute visit script

This is a suggested short format. Extend or split the visit when the task takes longer; do not rush someone through a business process to fit the script.

1. **First five minutes:** explain the research and get permission. “We are studying how shops order stock. We are not from a regulator or offering a loan. You can skip anything or stop. May we take written notes?” Ask separately about photos or audio.
2. **Next ten minutes:** ask about the last order. What triggered it? Who chose the products, contacted the supplier and approved payment?
3. **Next twenty minutes:** observe the normal task or ask for a reconstruction. Follow one item. Where did a wait, correction or second phone call occur? Do not suggest your feature yet.
4. **Next five minutes:** check your interpretation. “I saw you call again after sending the list. What was missing?”
5. **Final five minutes:** ask what you missed, thank the participant and explain any agreed follow-up.

Use dummy or covered records when private information is unnecessary. Avoid collecting customer phone numbers, PINs, account details or identity documents. Stop recording if another person joins until their permission is clear. Permission from the shop owner does not automatically cover everyone in the shop.

### Budget the whole round

Say two researchers plan BDT 800 for travel and BDT 400 for food and water. They allow BDT 600 to repay agreed costs for people who take part. They keep BDT 200 for extra costs. Total: **BDT 2,000**, before counting their own time. These are made-up budget figures. Check real prices and agree any payments before your visits.

Allow time to find people, travel, make six visits and review the notes. If you repay someone's costs, the payment must not depend on what they say. Carry a charged phone and tell a teammate where you will be. Move or stop a visit if anyone feels unsafe or wants to stop.

## Review notes while the visit is fresh

After each visit, separate three things:

- **Observed:** the worker made a second call after sending the order.
- **Reported:** the worker said the supplier often confirms availability late.
- **Interpretation:** availability uncertainty may matter more than typing.

Then look for cases that differ. In this made-up round, four shops place orders easily but wait to hear if stock is available. Two struggle with typing. Test a way to confirm stock with the first group. Learn more about typing problems with the second group. Do not turn four out of six into a claim about all shops in Bangladesh.

Write the conclusion in this form:

```text
We observed [event] in [these cases].
We did not cover [missing people or conditions].
This changes [specific decision].
Before spending on [next commitment], we will test [remaining uncertainty].
```

## Before you build from the findings

- [ ] The research question leads to a real decision.
- [ ] The sample includes relevant differences and records exclusions.
- [ ] Each visit has permission, a time and a task.
- [ ] Observations, participant statements and interpretations stay separate.
- [ ] The budget includes travel, time and any reimbursement.
- [ ] Private information has been minimized and stored with limited access.
- [ ] The conclusion states what these visits cannot prove.

Your next action is to book the first relevant visit. After the round, use the [experiment planner](/en/validation/mvp-experiment-planner) to test the strongest remaining assumption with actual behavior.
