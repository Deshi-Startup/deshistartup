# Co-founders, Roles and Ownership

> **In short:** Decide what the business needs from another founder before discussing an equity split. Leave this section with responsibilities, unresolved decisions and a plan to test working together.

## Start with the decision you face

- **Do I need a co-founder?** Compare the work and commitment in [solo versus team](/en/cofounders/solo-vs-team).
- **Who does what?** Use the [role matrix](/en/cofounders/role-matrix) to assign outcomes and decision authority.
- **Could we work together?** Use [finding co-founders](/en/cofounders/finding-cofounders) to plan a bounded working trial.
- **How should we document the relationship?** Prepare with [founder agreements](/en/cofounders/founder-agreement) and [equity and vesting](/en/cofounders/equity-and-vesting).

## Example: test working together before deciding the split

**Equity** is an ownership interest. **Vesting** is an arrangement under which an entitlement to ownership develops over specified conditions or time; its actual effect depends on the agreement and applicable law. A proposed split is a negotiation, not a formula that follows automatically from job titles.

Two fictional founders want to build order software in Dhaka. One can work 12 hours a week and speak to shops; the other can work six hours and build a prototype. They should understand that difference before assuming equal availability.

Their discussion record for a two-week trial is:

| Item | Agreed trial arrangement |
|---|---|
| Outcome | Demonstrate one order-confirmation task and document customer feedback |
| Responsibilities | Founder A arranges conversations; founder B makes a prototype with dummy data |
| Spending | No purchases without both agreeing; personal spending cap is written down |
| Review | Compare delivered work, communication and whether commitments were realistic |
| Unresolved | Ownership of pre-existing code, future commitment and any equity proposal |

These are fictional choices, not suggested hours or equity terms. The trial shows whether they can deliver and disagree constructively. It does not transfer ownership by itself. Before using existing work or formalising shares, they clarify permissions and obtain appropriate advice on the agreement.

Your first output is a shared record of commitments and unresolved issues. If the two people describe different expectations, resolve that difference before treating the relationship as settled.

## Have this conversation before making promises

Each person writes their expected weekly commitment, financial limits, responsibilities, other obligations and reasons for joining. Compare the answers together. Agree on a small real task, its deadline and how you will review the result.

Record who can approve spending, who owns existing work and what happens if someone cannot continue. Friendship or shared enthusiasm does not resolve those questions.

A useful output is a one-page discussion record: **agreed / unresolved / person responsible / review date**. It is preparation for an agreement, not a substitute for reviewing ownership and legal terms properly.

If you need a defined job done rather than a shared founding commitment, start with [team and hiring](/en/team).
