# Co-founders, Roles and Ownership

> **In short:** A co-founder shares responsibility for building the business, including decisions whose answers are not yet clear. First decide whether that is the relationship you need. Then test working together, discuss each person's commitment and settle ownership terms before treating the partnership as agreed.

You may have an idea but lack the skills to build it. Or you and a friend may already be working together without deciding who is responsible for what. In either case, the first question is what the business needs from a second person. The answer will shape how you choose them, what you expect from them and whether sharing ownership makes sense.

A co-founder helps decide what the business should become and takes responsibility for making it happen. That is a wider commitment than completing a defined job. If you need a website built from an agreed brief, hiring someone may be enough. If you need someone to make product decisions, work through uncertain customer demand and share the consequences over time, you are considering a founding relationship. The [solo-versus-team guide](/en/cofounders/solo-vs-team) helps you compare these options.

## Talk about the work each person can actually take on

Start with the coming months, not job titles. Who will talk to customers, build or deliver the product, sell, and keep track of money? Which decisions can each person make alone? Two people can both be founders while having very different responsibilities.

Availability matters as much as enthusiasm. Someone keeping a full-time job may be able to contribute reliably in the evenings, but cannot promise the same daytime availability as someone working only on the business. Discuss other commitments, how long each person can manage without income, and what would need to change before they could give more time. An honest limit is easier to plan around than a promise neither person can keep.

Write the responsibilities in terms of work that can be seen. “Handles sales” could mean finding prospects, attending meetings or collecting overdue invoices. Agree which of those are included and who decides when priorities conflict. The [role matrix](/en/cofounders/role-matrix) offers a way to record that division once the conversation is clear.

## Learn how you work together before making a lasting commitment

A small shared task can reveal things that an interview or friendship does not: whether someone follows through, asks for help in time and handles disagreement respectfully. Choose a task the business actually needs, with a finish point both people understand.

Suppose you are considering a partnership to build order software for shops. One person could arrange customer conversations while the other prepares a simple demonstration using dummy data. Review what you learned together, including whether the demonstration addressed the shopkeepers' actual difficulty. The result matters, but so does how you dealt with a missed conversation, a changed requirement or work that took longer than expected.

Keep the trial proportionate to each person's stated availability. Agree spending and access to information before starting. Also clarify whether existing code or other material can be used and what happens to new work if you do not continue together. A working trial does not itself settle ownership. [Finding co-founders](/en/cofounders/finding-cofounders) goes further into meeting candidates and planning this stage.

## Discuss ownership alongside the commitment it represents

**Equity** means an ownership interest in the company. The split should reflect the relationship you are agreeing to, including future work and risk. Equal shares can be sensible; so can an unequal arrangement. Neither follows automatically from who first suggested the idea or who holds a particular title.

The harder question is what happens if the commitment changes. **Vesting** makes the right to retain agreed shares depend on specified conditions over time. It can help address an early departure, but a schedule written in a note does not automatically move legally issued shares. The mechanism needs to work under the applicable law and the company's documents.

Before finalising the relationship, discuss leaving, outside work, ownership of existing material and how you will resolve a deadlock. Use [founder agreements](/en/cofounders/founder-agreement) and [equity and vesting](/en/cofounders/equity-and-vesting) to prepare those questions, then obtain appropriate legal advice on the actual arrangement. For a defined job without shared founding responsibility, continue with [team and hiring](/en/team).
