# Co-founder Equity Splits and Vesting in Bangladesh

> **Key Takeaway:** Agree ownership around the work and risk ahead, then write what happens if someone leaves. Equal or unequal splits can be appropriate; neither replaces that conversation. <Term name="vesting">Vesting</Term> makes the right to retain shares depend on agreed conditions over time. In Bangladesh, issued shares and contractual leaving terms are separate matters: a vesting schedule alone does not transfer shares back.

## Deciding the Split Isn't a Fairness Question

An equal split can reflect equally important long-term commitments. Unequal time, capital or responsibilities may justify a different agreement, but the original idea or a short head start should not automatically dominate years of future work. Y Combinator argues for equal or near-equal splits with vesting; this is founder advice, not a legal formula.[^yc-equity]

At minimum, discuss these questions separately when setting the split.

- Whose idea was it originally, and how much weight should that carry
- Who's full-time, who's part-time
- Who brought the first capital or assets
- Whose skills or network are hard to replace with someone else
- Who's taking on more risk — quitting a job versus staying part-time

No single answer decides the split by itself, but writing the questions down grounds the conversation in reasoning instead of feelings. What to avoid: defaulting to an equal split "just to avoid a fight." When contributions turn out unequal six months later, that decision becomes the real fight.

A later founder may receive newly allotted shares, which dilute existing holders, or shares transferred by an existing shareholder, which change the sellers’ holdings. Agree the route and price before calculating percentages. The dilution math is on [Share Capital, Directors, and Shareholders](/en/registration/shares-and-directors).

## What Vesting Actually Means

Vesting means the right to retain the agreed shares is earned in stages over time; legal issuance may happen earlier. Say Rafi and Sumaiya start a company together, 50% each. Three months in, Rafi gets a good job offer and leaves. Without vesting, Rafi does not lose his issued shares merely because he leaves, despite never doing another day of work for the company. Sumaiya could spend years building the business alone, while any later allotment could still dilute both holders.

A widely used startup convention is four-year vesting with a one-year cliff. The cliff means nothing is earned at all until the first full year passes; the moment that year is up, 25% vests all at once, and the remaining 75% vests gradually, month by month, over the following three years. In Rafi's case, leaving at three months means he never cleared the cliff — with vesting in place, his earned share would have been zero.

These specific numbers (four years, a one-year cliff) are not a Bangladesh statutory requirement — they're a convention popularized by accelerators like Y Combinator. Your team can set two years or three instead if you want, but dropping the cliff entirely is risky: without one, someone who quits after a single month still walks away with some vested shares.

## Issued Shares and Vesting Are Different

A founder can hold issued shares while a contract sets conditions on retaining the unvested portion. One structure uses a transfer right exercisable if the founder leaves. The permitted buyer, price, procedure and enforceability depend on the jurisdiction and the company’s documents; a foreign template does not settle those questions in Bangladesh.

In Bangladesh, the Companies Act restricts a company’s purchase of its own shares.[^companies-act-buyback] Do not promise an automatic company buyback. Have counsel choose a lawful mechanism, such as an appropriately documented transfer to an eligible remaining shareholder, and reconcile it with the articles. A transfer still needs the applicable <Term name="share-transfer">share transfer</Term> steps, including <Term name="form-117">Form 117</Term>, stamp requirements and the member register; see [Share Capital, Directors, and Shareholders](/en/registration/shares-and-directors).

## How to Actually Set This Up

An ordinary private company requires at least two subscribers to the <Term name="moa-aoa">MoA</Term>; that does not require giving every founder their entire intended long-term allocation at incorporation. For an arrangement involving already-issued shares, write at least these three things into the founder agreement.

1. **Vesting period and cliff.** How many years until the full share is earned, and how many months must pass before anything vests at all.
2. **Repurchase price.** Agree a legally supportable price or formula for the unvested portion; nominal value is not automatically valid in every transaction. Write this as an exact number — don't leave it as something vague like "fair price."
3. **How it gets executed.** Who can lawfully acquire the shares, and what paperwork — a transfer instrument, board approval — is prepared in advance to make the transfer actually happen.

The third point matters because a contract does not itself complete a share transfer. Ask counsel to document notices, approvals, signatures, custody of documents and dispute handling. Do not rely on a blank pre-signed transfer form as a shortcut: the transaction details and authority must be valid when it is used.

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Result |
|---|---|
| Defaulting to an equal split just to avoid discomfort | Unequal contributions turn into a grievance later |
| Assuming vesting exists automatically under RJSC or company law | No legal basis for protection if someone leaves |
| Setting up vesting with no cliff | Someone who quits after one month still keeps some vested shares |
| Leaving the repurchase price vague | The price itself becomes a fresh fight during a dispute |
| Not agreeing a lawful transfer process in advance | You end up depending on a departing founder's cooperation |
| Not planning for a later founder's dilution in advance | The share math becomes a fresh headache every time |

## Checklist

- [ ] Discussed idea origin, time, capital, and risk separately before setting the split
- [ ] Didn't default to an equal split just to avoid discomfort
- [ ] Wrote the vesting period and cliff
- [ ] Wrote the repurchase price as an exact number
- [ ] Arranged the paperwork for executing a transfer in advance
- [ ] Had the whole arrangement drafted by a lawyer

**Remember:** a vesting schedule describes what should be earned. The company documents and a lawful transfer mechanism determine how those terms can actually be enforced.
