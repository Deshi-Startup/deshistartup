# Directory: Find the Right Organisation to Contact

> **In short:** Begin with the job you need help with, then shortlist organisations that serve your location and stage. Compare their current scope, costs and terms before choosing. A listing is a starting point for that conversation, not an endorsement.

You may need a courier for a new route, an accountant for the company's filings or a programme that supports a team at your stage. Finding a name is the easy part. The harder part is knowing whether the organisation can handle your particular work, what its quote includes and whom to contact when something goes wrong.

The directory brings potential contacts together by the kind of help they offer. Use the category that matches your immediate need:

- **Operating services:** [payment gateways](/en/directory/payment-gateways), [couriers](/en/directory/couriers), and [legal and accounting firms](/en/directory/legal-accounting).
- **Space and structured support:** [coworking](/en/directory/coworking) and [accelerators](/en/directory/accelerators).
- **Funding contacts and programmes:** [investors](/en/directory/investors) and [government funding](/en/directory/government-funding).
- **Public offices and application services:** [government services](/en/directory/government-services).

If you are unsure whether you need a service provider, an adviser or a funding organisation, the [ecosystem guide](/en/ecosystem) explains those roles. Getting that distinction right saves you from sending a funding pitch to an organisation whose job is to provide training or process an application.

## Give each candidate the same description of the work

Before contacting anyone, write a short request with your location, business stage, the work involved and when you need it. Include the facts that affect the service: delivery routes for a courier, transaction types for an accountant, or the stage and purpose of funding for an investor. State a budget where it will help establish whether the conversation is practical.

Ask what the organisation can cover, what it excludes and what it needs from you. A monthly accounting quote, for example, is difficult to compare if one firm includes annual work and another prices it separately. Obtain the scopes before deciding which is cheaper. Also establish who will do the work, what dates both sides must meet and how corrections or complaints are handled.

For a programme, check eligibility and whether applications are currently open before preparing a long submission. For a commercial provider, ask for the current written quote and terms. These are different kinds of commitment, so the same “best provider” ranking would not answer both questions.

## Check the listing, then check the current offer

Entries carry source links and check dates. They show where the information came from and when it was reviewed, not a promise that availability, pricing or quality has stayed the same. Follow the organisation's own source and confirm the details relevant to your work directly.

Keep the replies together so you can compare the same scope across candidates. Where an answer is missing, ask again rather than filling the gap with a favourable assumption. You are ready to choose when you can explain why the organisation fits the work, what you are committing to and which responsibilities remain yours.

If a listing is broken or out of date, send the page and a supporting source through [contact](/en/contact). That lets us check the correction without guessing which organisation or claim you mean.
