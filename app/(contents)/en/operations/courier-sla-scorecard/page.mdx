# Compare Courier Terms and Costs

> **In short:** Compare couriers on the parcels and routes you actually use. Check written terms, then run a small fair trial. Count delivery, returns, cash received and the full cost. A lower headline fee may not mean a lower cost for each order that reaches the customer.

## Copy the terms sheet

Use this if your startup is choosing a courier or reviewing the one it uses. **SLA** means service level agreement: the service promises agreed with a provider. A website claim, a private quote and your measured result are three different records.

```text

Pickup location and destination zone:
Parcel type, weight, size and value:

Return timeline and proof of handover:
Loss/damage claim process, exclusions and compensation limit:

Source: public terms / written quote / agreed contract
What remains unclear:
```

<Term name="cod">COD</Term> means cash on delivery: the buyer pays the courier, which later pays you. Check both the delivery promise and the cash promise. They may run on different clocks.

## Read the fee rules as a whole

Do not compare only the first price on a poster. Ask what happens when a parcel is returned, sent again, delivered to a changed address or moved to a remote zone. Confirm whether the quote includes tax and special handling.

Two public examples show why this matters. Pathao's Bangladesh merchant terms include delivery and return fees for failed or cancelled deliveries.[^pathao-sla] Steadfast's terms say there is no separate return-parcel charge, but also say delivery charges apply to parcels handed over, whether delivered or cancelled.[^steadfast-sla]

So “no return charge” does not mean an unsuccessful order costs nothing. These terms were checked on 7 September 2026. Get the current terms for your account and service before deciding. This is not a ranking of the two providers.

Also read packaging and claim limits. A compensation cap is not a promise that every claim will be paid. Check the conditions, proof needed and items the service will not carry. A claim still being discussed is not cash recovered.

## Choose a fair trial

Start with routes you can compare. If one courier gets easy central-city orders and another gets remote addresses, the results will not tell you which does the same job better.

Use similar parcel types, values, locations and dispatch days. Split them fairly where practical. Keep customer promises truthful and stay within the provider's accepted service area. A trial does not justify sending a parcel the service is unsuitable for.

Decide the measures before starting:

| Measure | Write the rule clearly |
|---|---|
| Pickup on time | Collected within the agreed pickup window |
| Delivery on time | Delivered within the promised window, using the agreed start time |
| Completed delivery | Customer received the parcel, with supporting record |
| Returns | Status and date the physical parcel came back |
| Cash | Expected payout matched to bank or wallet receipt |
| Problems | Lost, damaged, misrouted or disputed parcels, with case records |

For every rate, keep the count and the group counted. If the report covers deliveries due this week, do not add new parcels whose promised delivery date is next week. Record any exclusions rather than dropping difficult orders from the result.

## Keep a parcel-level log

```text
Order ID | Courier parcel ID | Route | Service | Weight/value band
Booked time | Actual pickup | Promised delivery window
Delivery time/status | Evidence reference
Return requested | Return received
All courier charges | Extra support or recovery costs
Expected payout | Matched receipt and date | Open difference
Issue, cause if known, owner and next action
```

Keep customer names, phone numbers and addresses out of a shared comparison report. Use parcel IDs and secure links to the needed records. The courier still needs the correct delivery information through its approved channel.

## Worked example: the cheaper fee costs more per delivery

Imagine a Dhaka product startup testing two couriers on similar routes. A and B are fictional providers; none of these figures is a quote or performance result for a real courier.

Each takes 20 parcels. The batch is now closed: every parcel is delivered or returned, and all charges are known. All fees in this example include their applicable tax treatment.

| Result | Courier A | Courier B |
|---|---:|---:|
| Parcels handed over | 20 | 20 |
| Delivered | 18 | 19 |
| Delivered within the promised window | 17 | 18 |
| Delivery fees for the batch | BDT 1,800 | BDT 2,000 |
| Separate return fees | BDT 200 | BDT 100 |
| Total courier charges | BDT 2,000 | BDT 2,100 |

For this comparison, all 20 parcels in each group had a delivery deadline in the test period. On-time rates are **17 / 20 = 85%** and **18 / 20 = 90%**. Returned parcels did not become on-time deliveries.

Now divide all courier charges by completed deliveries:

```text
A: BDT 2,000 / 18 = about BDT 111.11 per delivered order
B: BDT 2,100 / 19 = about BDT 110.53 per delivered order
```

B has the higher delivery fee but a slightly lower cost per completed delivery in this batch. The result is close and the trial is small. It is not proof that B will always perform better.

These figures cover courier charges only. Add extra packaging, support work and unrecovered product losses when comparing your full cost. Do not count a returned item as lost stock if it came back usable. Do not deduct compensation that has not been confirmed and received.

## Make a decision by route and need

Set any must-have conditions first. For example, the courier must accept your product type, cover the route and offer a workable cash schedule. A cheap provider that fails a must-have condition is not a match.

Then compare the remaining options using actual records. You may choose one provider for a route and keep another as a backup. Keep the trial size and open claims visible when sharing the decision.

If the data is still incomplete, name what you need next. “Wait for the two open returns and match the last payout” is more useful than assigning an unsupported star rating.

## Review after changes

Repeat the check when you add a route, change packaging, sell a more fragile product or receive new terms. Review recurring problems with the courier using parcel-level evidence.

Allow a few hours to collect terms and prepare the log. Run the trial long enough for delivery, returns and payouts to finish. The courier's actual cycles, not an arbitrary seven-day deadline, should set that window.

- [ ] Quotes describe the same service, parcel and route.
- [ ] Full fees, return costs and tax treatment are checked.
- [ ] Packaging and claim limits fit the product.
- [ ] Rates keep their counts, dates and exclusions visible.
- [ ] Cash received is checked separately from delivery status.
- [ ] Open returns and claims remain open in the decision.
- [ ] A review date and backup plan are recorded.

Start with your busiest route and the oldest unresolved service problem. Use the [COD reconciliation template](/en/payments/cod-reconciliation) to check the payout part of the scorecard.
