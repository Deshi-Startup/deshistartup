# Guided Journeys: Complete One Practical Goal

> **In short:** A journey puts relevant guides in a useful order for a particular task. Choose the result you need; you do not need to complete every journey.

| Your goal | Journey | Intended output |
|---|---|---|
| Test an idea before building | [Test demand](/en/journeys/test-demand) | An experiment, evidence and next decision |
| Move from an idea toward a product | [Idea but no product](/en/journeys/idea-but-no-product) | A bounded first-version plan |
| Formalise a company | [Register a private limited company](/en/journeys/register-a-private-limited-company) | A preparation and application workflow to verify |
| Take digital payments | [Accept digital payments](/en/journeys/accept-digital-payments) | A payment and recordkeeping setup plan |
| Sell to businesses | [Sell to SMEs](/en/journeys/sell-to-smes) | A customer approach and sales process |
| Prepare the first hire | [Hire the first employee](/en/journeys/hire-first-employee) | A role and employment preparation plan |

## Use a journey without starting over

Read its goal and starting conditions. Mark what you have already completed, collect any missing evidence and choose the next unresolved task. Record an owner and review date.

Follow links for the details you need. A planned article is not a completed step or proof that a requirement has been met. Where a specialist question remains unresolved, record it and confirm the answer before acting.

For general stage planning, use the [roadmap](/en/roadmap). For a single subject, use [Browse by Topic](/en/guides).
