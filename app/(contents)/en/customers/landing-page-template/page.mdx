# Landing Page Template: Explain One Offer Clearly

> **In short:** Give one kind of buyer one clear offer and one main next step. Explain what they get, what it costs and what happens after they act. Show real proof. A short page with clear terms is more useful than a long page full of claims the reader cannot check.

## Copy the page outline

A **landing page** is a page focused on one offer. People may arrive from a message, ad, search result or event link. Use this when you need to explain a product, early trial or service without building a large website.

Replace the brackets below with facts you can stand behind. Remove any section that does not help the buyer decide.

```text
HEADLINE
[Help this buyer do this specific job.]

SHORT EXPLANATION
For [buyer], [product/service] helps you [result] by [how it works].
Current stage: [available now / limited pilot / not yet available].

MAIN ACTION
[Clear button or link label]
After you act: [what happens, who replies and when].

WHAT YOU GET
- [Useful result or included item]
- [Useful result or included item]
- [Important limit or requirement]

HOW IT WORKS
1. [What the buyer does first]
2. [What we do]
3. [The result and how the buyer gets it]

PRICE AND TERMS
[Amount, currency and billing period]
[What is included, extra charges and relevant limits]
[Payment, cancellation or return terms that matter to this offer]

PROOF
[Real demo, accurate product photos, checked results or permitted reviews]

QUESTIONS BEFORE YOU START
[The main concern and a direct answer]
[The next concern and a direct answer]

CONTACT AND DATA USE
[Working business contact]
[What information you ask for and why]
[Relevant full terms and privacy information]

REPEAT THE SAME MAIN ACTION
[Same clear next step]
```

This is a writing template, not a finished website or a legal policy. Check which disclosures and terms your actual offer needs before publishing.

## Choose the page's job

Decide what one useful action should happen: book a demo, request a quote, join a clearly labeled waitlist or buy an available product. These actions are not the same.

If you cannot deliver yet, say so before asking for details. Do not use a “Buy now” button that secretly becomes a waitlist. If you collect money, the offer needs clear delivery and payment terms you can meet.

Government content-design guidance starts with what the user needs to know or do.[^gds-content] For a business page, ask: what must this buyer understand before taking the next step?

Accelerating Bangladesh's go-to-market course covers whom to serve, what to offer, how to reach buyers and how to deliver.[^ab-page-gtm] Your page should make those choices visible. A promise of personal setup also creates work your team must plan and price.

## Write the headline around the job

Use words the buyer uses. “Plan next week's classes in one shared timetable” tells them more than “Transform education with an innovative ecosystem.”

Keep three things clear near the top: who it is for, what it helps them do and whether they can use it now. Explain how it works in the next sentence. You do not need to fit every feature into the headline.

Avoid claims such as “best,” “guaranteed growth” or “saves ten hours” unless you can support the exact claim. If you only have a demo, show the demo and explain its limits.

## Filled example: a class-planning tool

Imagine a small software startup selling a timetable tool to training centres in Bangladesh. The product, price and terms below are made up to show the writing pattern. They are not an offer from Deshi Startup.

```text
Plan next week's classes in one shared timetable

ClassPlan helps small training centres put rooms, teachers and class times
in one place. The current version checks for overlapping room bookings.
It is ready for a small paid pilot.

[Book a 15-minute demo]
Send your name and work contact. We will reply within two working days
to agree a time. No payment is needed to book the demo.

What you get
- A weekly timetable that two staff members can edit.
- A warning when two classes use the same room at the same time.
- A printable view to share with the team.

How it works
1. Add your rooms and class times.
2. Check the clashes the tool flags.
3. Confirm and print the week's timetable.

Pilot price
BDT 1,500 for one month, one centre and two staff accounts.
For this example, the price includes all charges for that month.
We confirm the start date and included setup help before payment.
There is no automatic renewal. Ask us before the next month if you want
to continue. Full pilot terms are shared before you pay.

See it work
The demo uses made-up rooms and class times. We do not yet have customer
results or reviews to publish.

Does this send messages to students?
No. The current version plans the timetable; it does not send messages.

Do we need to upload student records?
No. The demo and timetable task do not need student names or phone numbers.

Questions?
Contact [your real, working business contact].
We use the demo request details to arrange and follow up on that request.
[Link to your actual privacy information and terms.]

[Book a 15-minute demo]
```

The example makes a small promise. It names a limit, shows what happens next and avoids invented praise. Before using this pattern, replace every product, price, reply-time and policy detail with your own checked facts.

## Ask only for what the next step needs

A demo request may need a name, work contact and one question about the task. It rarely needs an identity document, home address or a full customer list.

Explain why you ask for each field. Make optional fields clear. Separate a request for help from permission to receive ongoing marketing. Provide accurate privacy information for what your tools and team actually do.

Do not add extra form fields just because the software makes them easy to add. Every field is another task for the reader and another piece of information you must handle.

## Check the whole page on a phone

Ask a few intended readers to use the page on their own phones. Give them a task, such as finding the full price and booking a demo. Do not guide their finger.

GDS usability guidance uses realistic tasks to reveal where a service is hard to use.[^gds-page-test] Watch for these problems:

- The reader cannot tell what is available now.
- The price or an important limit is hard to find.
- A photo or table spills off the screen.
- The button is vague or opens the wrong contact route.
- A form submits, but nobody knows what happens next.
- The page needs a fast connection to explain the main offer.

Then ask the reader to explain the offer in their own words. Fix wrong expectations, not just spelling mistakes.

## Track the next step, not just visits

Keep a simple <Term name="funnel">funnel</Term>, the count of people who reach each step. For a demo page, track requests, suitable requests, booked times, attendance and paid starts separately.

If 20 people request a demo but only two attend, the headline may not be your main problem. Check whether the offer was clear, replies arrived on time and the booking process worked.

Allow a few hours to gather the facts and write a first page. Building and testing it takes extra time based on your tools. You can learn from a simple page before paying for a large redesign.

## Before sharing the link

- [ ] One buyer and one offer are clear.
- [ ] The offer's current stage is stated honestly.
- [ ] The full price and important terms are easy to find.
- [ ] Proof is real, checked and used with permission where needed.
- [ ] The main action works and its next step is explained.
- [ ] The form asks only for needed information.
- [ ] Someone owns each reply and can meet the stated response time.
- [ ] A phone reader can explain what they will get.

Start by filling the headline, current stage and next action. Then use the [trust-test worksheet](/en/validation/trust-signals) to check the biggest question buyers still have.
