# Customer Relationship Management: Start with a Simple Tracker

> **In short:** Keep one shared record for each customer. Write who will do the next task and when. Change the status only when you have evidence of what happened. A longer contact list does not mean more customers, and a reminder is not permission to keep messaging someone.

Use this when customer conversations are spread across calls, messages and teammates. You will leave with a customer tracker and a short review routine. If you still need people to speak with, use the [first-customer guide](/en/customers/first-100-customers). If you need help with the sales conversation itself, see [selling to small businesses](/en/b2b/sme-sales).

**Customer relationship management**, or **CRM**, means keeping track of your work with people who might buy and people who already buy from you. CRM software stores contact details, past conversations, possible sales and service problems.[^salesforce-crm] Start with a spreadsheet your team can keep up to date. You do not need to buy software to try this way of working.

## Copy the customer record

Put these fields into spreadsheet columns, or copy the card for each customer. A **prospect** is someone who might buy. Keep people considering a purchase separate from customers who have agreed to buy. Record payment separately: agreeing to buy does not mean they have paid.

```text
Customer record ID:
Person or business / contact person and role:
Contact channel / where the contact came from:
Contact preference or request to stop:
Need and evidence / date:
Current status / evidence for that status:
Last contact date / what happened:
Promise or open issue:
Next action / due date / owner:
Payment or order reference, if relevant:
```

For a business buyer, link its staff to one business record. Do not count three people from the same buying team as three separate sales. If one customer has several orders, link the order records rather than forcing every order into one status.

The card and routine below are suggestions you can adapt. Start with a few ongoing conversations. Set aside time to gather scattered notes, then choose a regular time to check the list. You may not need to spend on software, but adding, correcting and checking records still takes team time.

## Make the status describe an event

A status should help the next teammate understand where things stand. Choose a few labels your team can explain. These examples show what evidence each label needs.

| Status | Evidence to record | What to decide next |
|---|---|---|
| New enquiry | A real question or request, with its source | Who should reply, and by when? |
| Need discussed | Dated notes about the customer's task | Is there a useful next step both sides want? |
| Offer sent | The actual offer and date | What decision or information are you waiting for? |
| Customer | An accepted purchase, with payment recorded separately | Who owns delivery and the next check-in? |
| Paused or closed | Reason, date and any agreed return date | Is a future contact welcome, or should it stop? |

“Offer sent” is not “offer accepted.” “Customer” is not proof that money has arrived. Keep payment evidence in your order or accounting records and link it here. The [invoice-collection guide](/en/b2b/invoice-collection) covers balances and collections.

Do not invent a percentage chance of winning each sale and treat the result as available cash. If a buyer has not agreed a date, record that uncertainty.

## After a conversation, write the next action

Record the date, a short factual note and the promised next step. “Interested” is vague. “Asked for a demo using last week's stock list; agreed to Tuesday at 3 pm” tells someone what to do. A CRM can also hold notes about work done outside the software: HubSpot, for example, documents manual activity records for calls, meetings and messages.[^hubspot-activities]

Separate the customer's words from your guess. Write “said the owner is away until Thursday,” rather than “will buy Thursday.” Keep only the detail needed to serve the customer.

Give each open action one owner. Write “send the revised setup steps by Thursday,” not “follow up soon.” If the customer must act first, your next action can be to check on an agreed date. Do not keep moving an overdue date forward without recording what happened.

## Worked example: handing over a software enquiry

Imagine a two-person startup in Bogura that sells stock-ordering software. The people and events below are fictional. One founder takes a call, and the other handles demonstrations. The shop owner approves spending, but a staff member will use the tool.

The first founder records:

```text
Record: Customer A
Source: introduction the shop owner agreed to
Contact: shop owner; call after 4 pm
Need: staff re-enter order notes; reported in today's call
Status: need discussed; no offer accepted or payment received
Open issue: staff member needs to try entering an order
Next action: confirm a demo time with owner and staff member
Due: 29 September 2026 / Owner: demo founder
```

Before calling, the demo founder reads the note. The owner asks to wait until after a stock count. The founder records the reason, marks the discussion paused and adds the date the owner agreed to hear back. The missed demo is no longer mistaken for a lost customer or a likely payment.

Later, if the shop buys, the team records the agreed offer and links the payment record. A setup problem gets a named owner and a due date. It stays visible until someone checks that the customer can complete the task. A sale does not end the relationship.

## Review the list without chasing everyone

At each review, filter for overdue actions, actions due next and records with no owner. Read the context before sending a reminder. Where no date was agreed, ask whether another conversation would be useful instead of assuming repeated contact is welcome.

Check open delivery or support promises before sending another sales offer. Use the [support scripts](/en/customers/support-scripts) for the reply itself. Close or pause work honestly when the customer says no. Keep a clear stop-contact flag so another teammate does not restart unwanted sales messages.

The useful result is a list of actions someone can complete. Count unresolved promises and missed follow-ups if that helps the team improve. A list full of names, messages sent or colourful status labels does not show customer value or sales.

## Keep customer information safe

Let only people who need the tracker for work open it. Avoid public sharing links. Do not put passwords, one-time codes, national identity card (NID) scans or unrelated personal notes in it. Keep order and payment documents in a separate place that only the right people can open. Link to them instead of copying them into every customer note.

Agree who removes outdated contact details and who checks access when someone leaves the team. If you download a backup, protect that copy too. A request to stop sales messages does not necessarily mean you should delete every business record. Get advice if you need to know what the law says to keep or how to handle sensitive personal data.

## When a spreadsheet stops being enough

Consider software when handovers, duplicate records, permissions or missed reminders repeatedly cause trouble. Test it with fictional records first: can a teammate find the last conversation, take ownership, record a stop-contact request and export the useful data? Check those functions, current plan limits and total costs before moving real records. The [affordable-tools guide](/en/tools/affordable-tools) covers software choices.

- [ ] Each active relationship has one clear record and owner.
- [ ] Status changes have dated evidence.
- [ ] Promises, next actions and contact preferences are visible.
- [ ] Paid amounts are checked against payment records.
- [ ] Access and exported copies are controlled.

Try the tracker with a few current conversations. Ask a teammate to take over one using only the record. Repair whatever they have to ask you again.
