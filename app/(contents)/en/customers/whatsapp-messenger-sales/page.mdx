# Prepare Your First Sales Conversation

> **In short:** Say what you are offering, find out whether it fits the person's work, and explain the price and delivery terms. Then agree what happens next. A request for details is a useful opening; it becomes an order only when the buyer agrees to buy.

Your first sale may begin with a Messenger reply, a WhatsApp message or a short call. You need enough detail for the person to decide whether to keep talking, without sending your entire business plan. The scripts below help you move from that first message to a clear offer and follow-up.

## Start with a short message

```text
Hi [name], I'm [your name] from [business].
We help [customer group] with [specific task].
Would you like a short explanation of what we do and what it costs?
```

Replace the brackets with something you can deliver today. If you have spoken before, add one sentence about that conversation. On a call, ask whether this is a suitable time before continuing. If someone asks the price immediately, answer the price question; they do not need to follow your script in order.

Throughout this guide, imagine you are testing a service that checks a shop's courier statements against its order records. You do the first checks manually before building software. This is a fictional offer, and its prices and dates are for illustration.

Your opening could be: “We check courier statements against shop order records and list any unexplained differences. Would you like to hear what a one-week check includes and what it costs?” That tells the shop owner what the service does without promising to recover money or fix all their accounts.

Use a channel where the approach is appropriate and follow its rules. Make the sales purpose clear. If you are still trying to understand a problem and have nothing to offer yet, use [customer interview scripts](/en/validation/interview-scripts). A research invitation should not turn into a surprise pitch.

## Find out whether the offer fits

```text
How are you checking [the task] at the moment?
Which part, if any, would you like help with?
```

Ask one question, then listen. For the courier service, an owner might already match each statement in a spreadsheet. Find out whether there is any unfinished work before explaining why they should change. They may have a method that works well enough.

If they want help, the answer also tells you what you would need to deliver. A missing statement, an unexplained deduction and a request to prepare all the business's accounts are different jobs. When the request goes beyond your offer, say so before discussing payment.

## Put the offer in writing

```text
For [price], we will provide [specific work or item] by [date].
This includes [what the customer will receive].
It does not include [important exclusions].
We need [information or other dependency] by [date] to meet that deadline.
[Explain the payment and cancellation or refund terms you actually offer.]
Does that cover the work you need?
```

The courier example could read:

> For BDT 500, I will compare one week's courier statement with your order records and send a list of matched items and unexplained differences. This does not include contacting the courier, recovering deductions or preparing your full accounts. If you send the agreed records by Tuesday, I can return the check by Thursday. I will first confirm that the records are usable. If I cannot deliver the agreed check, I will refund your payment.

The price now refers to a specific job. The owner can see what they will receive and what remains their responsibility. Use only deadlines and terms you can honour; the example's refund promise does not set the policy for your own business. If you need to check a detail, tell the customer when you will answer instead of guessing.

For this service, agree which fields are needed and remove unnecessary customer names, phone numbers and addresses before sharing records. Do not put confidential statements into a practice worksheet. For any sale, give payment instructions through your agreed channel and never ask for the customer's PIN or one-time password.

## Handle the reply and agree what happens next

The shop owner replies, “Send the details. I need to ask my partner.” You can answer:

```text
I'll send the written offer today so you can discuss it.
Would a follow-up on Monday suit you?
If your partner has a question, I can answer it before you decide.
```

That is progress, but the order is still pending. Record **offer sent, partner's decision pending, no payment, Monday follow-up agreed**. If the owner instead agrees to buy, confirm the work, price, payment arrangements, delivery date and terms together so both sides are agreeing to the same order.

For the agreed follow-up:

```text
Hi [name], checking back as agreed about [offer].
Were you able to discuss it with [person, if relevant]?
[Answer any question you promised to check.]
Would you like to proceed, or leave it for now?
```

A direct refusal ends the sales follow-up. Do not send another teammate to get around it. No reply leaves the outcome unknown; it does not mean the person accepted. Keep your notes short: what they asked, what you promised and whether you agreed to speak again.

Ask your practice partner to explain back what they would receive, what it costs and what happens next. If they cannot, revise the unclear sentence. A polished delivery cannot repair an unclear offer.

Before using the script, check:

- [ ] The opening says what I am selling.
- [ ] I can deliver the work, price and timing I describe.
- [ ] I explain important exclusions and terms before the buyer agrees.
- [ ] I can answer a price question directly and record questions I cannot yet answer.
- [ ] The person can decline, and any follow-up has been agreed.

When real customers respond, record their actual questions and revise the offer or wording accordingly. If the conversation exposes an untested assumption, [plan a small demand test](/en/journeys/test-demand). Before promising payment timing, check [how dates affect available cash](/en/metrics/cashflow-vs-profit). Once a buyer needs help with an order, use the [customer support scripts](/en/customers/support-scripts).
