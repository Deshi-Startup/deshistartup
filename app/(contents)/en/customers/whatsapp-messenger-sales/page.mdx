# Prepare Your First Sales Conversation

> **In short:** Be clear that you are offering something. Understand the person's current situation, explain the scope and price you can stand behind, and agree one useful next step. A friendly reply or booked demo is not a sale. Prepare your script here, then adapt it to the person and channel.

Use this when you have something real to offer, including a clearly described manual service or limited trial. If you are still learning whether a problem exists, use [customer interview scripts](/en/validation/interview-scripts) instead. Do not introduce a research conversation and then surprise the person with a pitch.

These are original conversation templates, not WhatsApp or Messenger policy guidance. They work as preparation for an inbox exchange or call. Use an appropriate contact channel, respect group or platform rules, and honour a request to stop. This page does not send messages for you.

## Copy a short opening

Start with context and a clear description of the offer. Use the line about prior context only if it actually happened.

```text
Hi [name], I'm [name] from [business].
We offer [specific service or product] for [relevant customer group].
You mentioned [actual context, if any].
Would you like a short explanation of how it works and what it costs?
```

Replace every bracket. Avoid a long introduction or a claim about results you cannot substantiate. If they decline, stop the sales approach. If they ask a direct question, answer it rather than forcing them through the script.

## Understand enough to check fit

Pick the question that helps you understand their situation:

- “How are you handling this task today?”
- “What would need to change for a different approach to be worthwhile?”
- “Which part of the offer would you need to understand before deciding?”
- For an organisational buyer: “Who would need to evaluate or approve this?”

Listen for scope, timing, existing alternatives, and constraints. Ask one question at a time. Someone who is happy with their current approach may not need your offer.

## Explain what you can actually provide

```text
For [price], we can provide [scope] by [date or timeline].
This includes [important inclusions] and excludes [specific tasks, services or items that are not part of the offer].
[State any material conditions or dependencies.]
Does that address the part you described?
```

A price without scope is hard to compare. A scope without a delivery commitment leaves the next step unclear. If a detail is still uncertain, say what you need to check and when you will answer.

**Fictional example:** a startup offers a manual scheduling setup for a training centre before investing in a software product. The example price below is chosen for practice, not a market recommendation.

| Draft | What to improve |
|---|---|
| “Our solution will transform your operations and save hours.” | The outcome is vague and unsupported. It does not tell the buyer what is included. |
| “For BDT 2,000, we will set up one shared class calendar and teach one staff member to update it in a 30-minute session. We can finish by Friday if you provide the schedule by Wednesday. This trial does not include an app or ongoing updates.” | The buyer can inspect scope, price, timing, dependency, and limits. The founder must still confirm that they can deliver each promise. |

The second version is easier to evaluate, but it does not guarantee interest or a sale. The buyer may decide their current calendar is enough. That is a valid outcome.

## Agree a next step, not a vague “keep in touch”

A next step could be a short demonstration, a written answer to a question, a discussion with the decision-maker, or a decision not to proceed. Agree who will do what and when.

```text
Would a [specific next step] help you decide?
If so, who should be involved and what time works?
I will send [the agreed material] by [date].
It's fine if this is not a fit.
```

If the person wants to buy, confirm the actual item or service, amount, delivery arrangements, payment instructions, and applicable terms before treating the exchange as an order. Use your real terms; a generic script cannot decide them for your business. Never ask for a customer's PIN or one-time password.

For a follow-up, refer to what was agreed:

```text
Hi [name], following up on the [material or demonstration] we agreed.
Here is [the agreed item]. Your open question was [question].
[Give the checked answer, or explain what is still being checked.]
Would you like to take the next step, or should I close this for now?
```

Keep promises, unanswered questions, and permission to follow up in the worksheet. No response leaves the outcome unknown. A clear refusal ends the sales follow-up; do not approach through another teammate to get around it.

## See a conversation reach a clear outcome

**Fictional example:** A founder offers a manual courier-statement check to a shop owner. The amounts and deadlines are illustrative.

**Owner:** “Can you fix all my accounts?”

**Founder:** “This offer covers one week's courier statement against your order records. I will list matched items and unexplained differences; it is not a full accounting service. We can use records with customer contact details removed.”

**Owner:** “What does it cost, and when do I get it?”

**Founder:** “BDT 500 for that scope. If you send the agreed records by Tuesday, I can return the check by Thursday. Before you pay, I will confirm that the records are usable. If I cannot deliver the agreed check, I will refund the payment. Shall I send the scope and payment details in writing?”

**Owner:** “Send them. I need to ask my partner.”

**Founder:** “Of course. Would a follow-up on Wednesday suit you?”

**Owner:** “Yes.”

The founder records **scope sent / partner approval pending / no payment / Wednesday follow-up agreed**. This is not a sale yet. The next message checks the agreed decision; it does not pretend the owner already ordered. Use only promises, prices and terms you can actually honour.

## Rehearse once, then use it

Ask a partner to play a buyer who already has a workable alternative. Read your opening, explain the offer, and respond to one concern. Your partner should point to the exact sentence that was unclear or unconvincing. Use the examples above for feedback, not a personality score.

Working alone, read your draft aloud and check:

- [ ] I clearly say what I am offering.
- [ ] Every result, price, and deadline is something I can substantiate or deliver.
- [ ] The scope and exclusions are understandable without a second document.
- [ ] I can answer a direct price question without a detour.
- [ ] I have a way to record an unanswered question without guessing.
- [ ] The next step has an owner and timing, and the person can decline.

You are ready to try the conversation when another person can explain your offer back to you and you have corrected any promises you cannot keep. Actual customer responses will still be uncertain. Record what happened and revise from that evidence.
