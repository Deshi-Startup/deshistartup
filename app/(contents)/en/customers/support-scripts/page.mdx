# Customer Support Scripts

> **In short:** Say what you know, what you will do and when you will update the customer. Give each case an owner. Promise only what that person can arrange. A quick reply is useful, but the job is not done until the customer gets an answer or an agreed next step.

## Copy the reply that fits

Use these when your startup handles support by inbox, email, phone or chat. These are English scripts to adapt to your real offer and the customer's preferred language. They are not return terms, refund rights or platform rules.

Replace every bracket. Check the facts and the person's authority before sending a promise.

### 1. You need time to check

```text
Thanks for telling us. I understand that [specific problem].
I am checking [the record or team needed].
Your case number is [reference], and I will handle the follow-up.
I will update you by [day and time], even if the check is still open.
```

Use this when you do not yet know the cause. Do not say the issue is fixed or blame another party before checking.

### 2. A delivery is late

```text
Your order [reference] has not arrived within the time we told you.
I am sorry about the delay.
The latest confirmed status is [status and time checked].
We are checking [specific next step] with [relevant team/provider].
We will update you by [day and time].
[State any confirmed option available under the applicable terms.]
```

Promise an update you control. Do not promise “tomorrow” just because a tracking page says the parcel is moving.

### 3. The item is wrong or damaged

```text
I am sorry the item you received does not match what was expected.
Please send [only the details needed to identify the order and issue].
If safe and practical, [a photo or description] will help us check it.
We will confirm the next step by [day and time].
Please do not send passwords, payment codes or unrelated personal records.
```

Do not make an unnecessary photo the only way to get help. If there may be a safety issue, tell the customer not to keep using or testing the item and route the case to the right responsible person promptly.

### 4. A payment is not yet matched

```text
We have not yet matched the payment to order [reference].
Please share the transaction reference, amount and date through
[our verified support channel]. We do not need your secret PIN or
one-time password (OTP).
We will check our receiving account and update you by [day and time].
Please do not pay again while we check unless we first agree the next step.
```

Check the real receiving account. A screenshot helps locate a transaction but does not prove that money reached you.

### 5. A refund has been approved but not yet sent

```text
We have approved a refund of [currency and amount] for [case/order].
The next step is [what our team must do].
We expect to submit it by [date, if confirmed].
We will send the payment reference when it has been submitted.
```

Approval is not payment. If the date is not confirmed, say when you will confirm it instead of inventing one.

### 6. A refund was sent and the customer is waiting

```text
We submitted the refund of [amount] on [date].
The reference is [reference].
[Give a checked provider timeframe only if it applies to this payment.]
If it has not arrived by [appropriate follow-up point], contact us using
case [reference]. We will trace it with the payment provider.
```

Do not label the refund “received” until there is suitable confirmation. Keep sent, pending and received as different states.

### 7. A software feature is not working

```text
I understand you cannot [task].
Please tell us [the few details needed, such as time and error message].
Do not send login details or private customer data.
We are checking whether [known issue, only if confirmed].
[Give a tested safe workaround, if one exists.]
Our next update is due by [day and time].
```

Do not claim that a fix is on its way unless the team has agreed one. A workaround must be safe for the person's records and task.

### 8. The requested option is unavailable

```text
We cannot offer [requested option] because [short, accurate reason].
The options we can confirm are [option A] and [option B, if available].
Here is what each involves: [cost, timing and important limits].
Please tell us which works for you, or let us know what we have missed.
```

Do not invent an alternative to make the message sound helpful. Check applicable customer rights and terms before refusing a remedy.

## Give every case one owner

GoZayaan's Marc Thiry describes support as part of the whole service, with human help where the product cannot resolve a problem.[^gozayaan-support] A script only works when the team can carry out the next step.

Keep a small case log:

```text
Case and order/account reference:
Customer's problem in their own words:
What we have checked and when:
Urgency and reason:
Owner and backup:
Next action and responsible team:
Customer update promised by:
Approved options and limits:
Current state: new / checking / waiting / action sent / resolved
Final result and confirmation:
```

Keep contact details in the proper customer record rather than copying them into every message. Share a case only with people who need it to do the work.

## Separate the reply clock from the fix clock

You may control when you reply but not when a bank or courier finishes a task. Give a realistic update time during your actual support hours. At that time, update the customer even if you are still waiting.

“We have not received the courier's answer yet; our next step is to call the branch” is more useful than silence. Do not keep sending the same apology without changing the action behind it.

For serious safety concerns, suspected data exposure or a customer left without an essential service, use a clear urgent route to a responsible person. Do not leave such cases in the ordinary inbox queue.

## Worked example: a delayed replacement

Imagine a small product startup in Rajshahi. A customer received a storage pouch with the wrong zip. The case and times below are made up.

On Monday morning, the support owner checks the order and confirms the error. The approved remedy is a replacement at no extra cost. The owner promises an update by 4 pm, not a delivery date they cannot yet know.

At 4 pm, packing has not finished. The owner says so, explains that the stock team will check the replacement next morning and gives a new update time. On Tuesday, the checked parcel is handed over and the customer gets the real tracking reference.

The case remains open until delivery is confirmed and any remaining concern is handled. Internally, the team also asks why the wrong zip passed its check. A friendly reply would not fix that cause.

## Review repeated problems each week

Count the number of cases, what caused them, how long people waited and which cases reopened. Keep “first reply sent” separate from “problem resolved.” Do not close cases just to make a report look faster.

Start with one recurring cause you can change: unclear product size, a missing setup step, an inaccurate stock count or a handover that loses details. Use the real cases to improve the product or process.

Allow a short session to adapt the scripts and choose owners. Then plan support hours based on actual case volume. A promise of round-the-clock help needs staff who can provide it.

## Before using the scripts

- [ ] The wording matches the problem and checked facts.
- [ ] One person owns the next step and the promised update.
- [ ] Prices, remedies and dates are approved before being promised.
- [ ] Only needed personal information is requested.
- [ ] Urgent cases have a clear route to a responsible person.
- [ ] A case is closed based on its result, not a sent reply alone.

Start by adapting the two replies your team needs most. Use the [founder role matrix](/en/cofounders/role-matrix) to settle who can approve unusual remedies.
