# Test Your Offer on Facebook and WhatsApp Before Building a Website

> **In short:** If your intended customers already use Facebook or WhatsApp, you can test one small offer there. Show what is available, agree the work in private, and record what people actually use. Build a website when it solves a problem you have seen in the trial.

You do not need to build every part of a future business before learning from a customer. Early work can include finding people and delivering a small result by hand.[^graham-manual] This guide helps you run that trial through messages. It does not replace the product you eventually need to build.

Use it when you can safely deliver a small sample or service, and can reach the intended customers through these channels. If you do not know where to find them, start with [the first-100-customers guide](/en/customers/first-100-customers). If the idea needs sensitive records, specialist advice or strict access controls, get suitable help before trying it through an inbox.

## Write one offer people can understand

Start with the card below. Fill every bracket before posting. A trial offer should say what is ready today, including work a person will do by hand.

```text
For: [one kind of customer with a specific problem]
We are testing: [the small result we can deliver now]
You receive: [exact output and what it does not include]
Price: [total trial price, or clearly say this sample is free]
Time: [when we can reply and when we can deliver]
Limit: [how many requests we can safely handle]
To ask for a place: [one contact method and what to send]
A message is a request. We will confirm the work and time in a reply.
```

For an imaginary example, a Sylhet startup wants to build timetable software for training centres. It first offers to turn a centre's made-up weekly class plan into one clear sample timetable. No student names or contact details are needed. A person makes the sample; the offer does not pretend that working software exists.

The useful question is: “Can the person who plans classes spot a timetable clash with this sample?” A friendly comment or a request for a free sample does not answer that question. Ask the person to try it and tell you what happened.

## Use one public invitation and one reply route

Choose a place where the intended customers already take part. That might be your Facebook Page or a relevant group that allows this kind of post. Read the group's rules and ask an admin when permission is unclear. Do not copy the same sales message into unrelated groups.

Post the completed offer card with an honest sample. Use your own image or one you have permission to share. Remove personal details from any example. Give people one clear way to contact you; do not make them repeat the same request across several apps.

You can reply through Messenger or the WhatsApp Business app, depending on what your customers use. The Business app supports a business profile, saved quick replies, away messages and labels for organising chats.[^wa-features] Check the features in your own account. This guide does not depend on in-app payments, ads or a paid service.

Set a reply time you can meet. WhatsApp's own setup guide suggests putting this time in an away message.[^wa-onboarding] An automatic greeting should explain the next step; it should not say that a place is booked.

## Agree the work before starting

Use a short reply like this. Replace the brackets and adjust it to the person's actual question.

```text
Thanks for asking about [trial].
We can provide [output] by [time].
Price: [write the total amount, or say the sample is free].
It does not include [exclusions].

We need only [minimum details]. Please do not send private records.
Would you like us to confirm this work?
If you need to change or cancel it, contact us at [route].
```

Confirm only after you have checked the team's time and the customer's agreement. Write down the agreed output, price, timing and cancellation terms. If you accept money, use a suitable payment method and check its own transaction record. A chat message or a screenshot alone does not prove payment arrived.

Keep messages about one customer's work private. For WhatsApp, get the required permission before contacting people and honour requests to stop. Its Business Messaging Policy also requires an accurate profile and a published privacy policy, and restricts certain goods and activities. Check that your offer is allowed.[^wa-policy]

Make your privacy notice available before asking for customer details, even if you use a hosted page instead of a full website. Explain what you collect, why, who can see it and how people can contact you. Do not add someone to a promotional list just because they asked one question.

## Write down the next action

A private sheet or notebook is enough for a tiny trial. Give each person one record. Link repeated messages to that record so five messages from one person do not become five customers.

Copy these fields:

```text
Enquiry ID and date:
Where the person found the offer:
Customer type and problem:
Work agreed / price / delivery time:
State: New / Agreed / Delivered / Tried / Cancelled
Next action, date and person responsible:
What the customer actually did with the result:
Money received or refund owed, if any:
Team time and direct cash cost:
```

These states are your team's record, not proof supplied by an app. “Delivered” means you sent the result. “Tried” means the customer reported or showed that they used it. Note how you know; do not turn your guess into a fact.

Check open promises at the end of each working day. If you miss a delivery time, tell the customer and agree what happens next. Close the invitation when you reach capacity, while keeping existing promises visible in the log. The [customer follow-up guide](/en/customers/customer-crm) gives a fuller tracker when the small log stops being enough.

## Read the result before buying a website

Choose a review date and a time-and-cash limit before the trial. Set a decision rule that fits the question you are testing. There is no useful universal number of likes, chats or free samples that proves demand.

At the review, separate people who asked, agreed, received the result, tried it and paid. Record cancellations too. A free trial can teach you about use; it cannot by itself show that people will pay your proposed price.

| What you observe | A useful next step |
|---|---|
| Relevant people saw the offer but cannot explain it | Rewrite the offer and test understanding before changing tools |
| People ask, but do not agree to try | Ask about the obstacle: trust, timing, scope or price may differ by person |
| People try the result and describe a useful outcome | Test the next uncertainty, such as a paid trial or repeat use |
| Many replies repeat the same basic information | Put those answers on one simple page |
| Requests get lost or two people promise the same slot | Fix the work log and booking process; a website that only describes the service will not fix this |

Treat these as questions to investigate, not proven causes. If people are not interested, a more polished website may leave the same problem unresolved.

Build a simple page when customers need a stable place to compare the offer, check details or share it with a colleague. Use the [landing-page template](/en/customers/landing-page-template). Choose a [form and private work log](/en/product/no-code-mvp) when collecting requests needs more structure. Customer accounts, secure records and dependable booking limits need a design suited to those jobs.

## Before the first real enquiry

- [ ] A new reader can say who the offer is for, what they get and what it costs.
- [ ] The sample and capacity claims are honest; any manual work is clear.
- [ ] One person owns replies, confirmations, changes and cancellations.
- [ ] Contact permission, privacy information and access to records are checked.
- [ ] A teammate has tried the full message-to-delivery path on a phone with made-up details.
- [ ] The trial has a review date, a budget and a question it can answer.

At that review, record whether you will continue, change the offer or stop. The goal is a useful customer result and a clearer next decision, not a busy inbox.
