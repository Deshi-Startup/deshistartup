# Intellectual Property: Know What You Own and Use

> **In short:** Start with an inventory of the names, content, designs, code and other work your business uses. Identify ownership and permission questions before commissioning work or committing to a brand.

## Understand the distinction before making the inventory

Intellectual property covers different kinds of rights. A **trademark** distinguishes the source of goods or services; **copyright** concerns original creative expression, such as text, artwork or code; a **patent** concerns an invention; and **trade-secret** protection concerns qualifying confidential information. These are different questions, not a single registration that protects everything. Eligibility and enforcement depend on the applicable law.[^ip-basics]

**Fictional example:** A software team has a product name, a logo made by a contractor and a code library found online. Its initial inventory reads:

| Asset | Evidence held | Decision before use |
|---|---|---|
| Product name | Domain purchase receipt | Keep brand clearance/protection as a separate question |
| Contractor logo | Invoice but unclear usage terms | Obtain and review the permission/ownership terms |
| External code library | License file in the source package | Check whether the intended distribution complies with that license |

The invoice, domain and downloaded file each prove something, but none answers all three questions. The team records where the agreements are stored, who created each asset and who owns the follow-up. It does not label all assets “ours” simply because they are in the project folder.

Your inventory is ready when every item has either a documented basis for the intended use or a named unresolved question and owner. This prepares a useful review; it does not grant an IP right.

## Make an asset record

| Record | Questions to answer |
|---|---|
| Brand and product names | What checks have we made? Which names are still provisional? |
| Code, designs and written content | Who created each item? What agreement or license covers our use? |
| Contractor and founder work | Where are the ownership terms recorded? Are any terms unresolved? |
| Third-party material | What use is permitted, and where is the permission recorded? |
| Confidential business information | Who needs access, and how is access removed? |

Keep a link to the relevant agreement or license beside each item. Possessing a file, paying an invoice or obtaining a domain name does not by itself answer every ownership or protection question.

## Resolve the next uncertainty

Before sharing sensitive work or signing an agreement, list the exact question for an appropriately qualified adviser. Use the [legal and accounting directory](/en/directory/legal-accounting) to begin a shortlist. For founder responsibilities, see [co-founder agreements](/en/cofounders/founder-agreement); for entity-name checks, see [name clearance](/en/registration/name-clearance).

Name clearance and protection of a brand are different questions. Do not use a company-name result as a complete IP assessment.
