# Your First Hire: Choose the Work Before the Job Title

> **In short:** Hire for a repeated task that is holding the business back. Decide what the person must do, what the full cost will be and who will train them. Test the skills the job needs. Then give the new hire the tools, support and power to do it. Being busy does not always mean you need, or can afford, another employee.

## First decide what is actually missing

Use this to decide whether to make one of your first hires and how to choose the person. Before making an offer, get help with the contract and the Bangladesh work rules that apply to the role.

List the work that took too much time or caused delays in the last two weeks. For each task, note how often it happens, the hours it takes and what goes wrong. Does a founder need to do it? Check whether this is a lasting gap or just a busy week.

Then choose among these responses.

| What you find | Sensible response to investigate |
|---|---|
| Repeated work with a clear result | A role with explicit ownership |
| A one-time specialist deliverable | A bounded external project |
| Repeated copying or rework | Fix the process before adding people |
| An unclear product or sales strategy | Founder investigation and decisions |

You can ask someone to do a clear task. It is harder to ask them to solve a business plan you do not yet understand. Also check whether the work calls for an employee or a contractor. The real working terms matter, not just the title you give it.

## Define three results, not a list of personality traits

**Job analysis** means listing the work and the skills needed to do it. The U.S. Office of Personnel Management uses this as a starting point for hiring.[^opm-job-analysis] We use the method here, not U.S. work rules.

“Smart and hardworking” does not explain the job. Name the results instead. For example: yesterday's payments are checked by noon. Every order with a problem has someone following it up. Replies to customers contain the right facts.

For each result, decide what the person must know now and what you will teach. Do not reject a junior hire for a tool you plan to teach. Do not hire a junior person for work that needs a skilled manager from day one.

Copy this brief before posting the role:

```text
Role and why it is needed now:
Manager:
Three results expected in the first 60–90 days:
Current baseline and how each result will be checked:

Daily/weekly tasks:
Must already be able to do:
We will train:
Decisions the person can make:
Decisions that need approval:
Tools, information and support we will provide:

Work location, hours and travel requirements:
Pay range and what it includes:
Selection steps, practical exercise and expected time:
Start-date range:
First-month training owner and schedule:
```

Make the goals possible with the time, tools and power you give the person. A new hire cannot force a courier to pay on time. They can spot a late payment, check the facts and ask the right person to help.

## Budget more than the monthly salary

Add up pay, benefits, tools, travel, hiring and training costs. Ask for help to check costs required by law or contract. Include the founder's time spent managing the person. That time has a value even if it creates no extra bill.

Use the dates money leaves the account. An equipment purchase at the start and a slower-paying customer can create a cash gap before the hire has had time to contribute.

### Worked example: an operations hire

Imagine a small Dhaka software startup whose founders spend evenings correcting payment records. All figures below are fictional budget assumptions, not Bangladesh salary or benefit benchmarks.

The proposed pay is BDT 45,000 a month. The founders allow BDT 5,000 for benefits and other employer costs while they check the real amount. Tools and internet add BDT 3,000, and travel adds BDT 2,000. The total new monthly cost is **BDT 55,000**. Equipment and hiring add **BDT 25,000 once**.

The firm has BDT 600,000. It keeps BDT 120,000 as a cash reserve, leaving BDT 480,000 to spend. Each month, it spends BDT 40,000 more than it collects. This gap is called <Term name="burn-rate">cash burn</Term>. Before hiring, the spending pot lasts 480,000 / 40,000 = **12 months**, if the gap stays the same.

Paying the one-time BDT 25,000 cost leaves BDT 455,000 above the reserve. The monthly cash gap rises to 40,000 + 55,000 = BDT 95,000. Now **455,000 / 95,000 ≈ 4.8 months** remain before the reserve is reached. New sales or costs would change this result.

Compare the cases before deciding.

| Case | Cash above buffer after setup (BDT) | Monthly net burn (BDT) | Approximate months |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Current operation | 480,000 | 40,000 | 12.0 |
| Hire, no extra collections yet | 455,000 | 95,000 | 4.8 |
| Hire, collections BDT 20,000 lower | 455,000 | 115,000 | 4.0 |

The hire could still be right if the role removes a real constraint. But “only BDT 45,000 a month” would conceal the financial decision. Do not fill the gap with assumed sales the employee has not had time to generate.

For your own case, calculate:

```text
New monthly cost = salary + relevant employer costs
                   + tools + travel + other regular costs
Cash above buffer after hiring = available business cash
                                - protected buffer - one-time hiring costs
New net burn = existing monthly net burn + new monthly cost
Approximate months = cash above buffer after hiring / positive new net burn
```

Use a weekly cash forecast if income or outgoings are uneven. If the plan depends on a new fundraise, resolve the funding uncertainty before making promises to a candidate.

## Recruit beyond the easiest referral

Post the tasks, work setting, pay range and hiring steps where suitable people will see them. This could be a job site, university group or work community. Ask trusted contacts to suggest people. Use the same checks for everyone, including friends of the team.

Homefectionery's Umayer Islam described telling people clearly about the problems they would solve and the freedom they would have.[^fs-homefectionery-hiring] Use that idea. Explain the messy parts, the help on offer and what the new hire can decide. Do not promise an orderly job if the work is still being set up.

Do not add degree, English-fluency or previous-employer filters unless they matter to the work. Check the language and skills the person will actually need with customers or teammates.

## Select using work evidence

Review each person's application, hold a short interview and use a small job task. A **structured interview** means asking everyone the same core questions. You also use the same rules to judge answers.[^opm-interviews]

Ask for a past example: “Tell us about a time the numbers did not match. How did you find the cause, and what did you do when you could not resolve it?” Then use fictional records to observe the relevant skill. Explain allowed tools and give comparable instructions to every candidate.

Keep the exercise brief and avoid using candidate work commercially. Agree payment for substantial trial work. A selection task should help you assess ability, not produce free operational work.

Write your scores before discussing people with the team. Note what you saw, what you still do not know and what you would need to teach. The [hiring scorecard](/en/team/hiring-scorecard) has a full example. Before choosing someone, check that they can do each part the job needs from day one.

## Prepare the job before the start date

Before making an offer, get qualified help to check the terms for this job in Bangladesh. Put pay, hours, work conditions and duties in writing. The sample figures in this guide are not a list of costs required by law.

Name the person's manager and who to ask when a problem arises. Give them access to the tools they need. They do not need every password or the power to move money without checks.

Use the first month to build reliable ownership.

1. **First days:** show the whole workflow and one completed example. Explain customer promises and how to raise a problem.
2. **First week:** work through a small batch together. Check understanding rather than assuming an explanation was enough.
3. **Next two weeks:** let the person own the task with scheduled review. Correct the process as well as the individual when errors appear.
4. **End of month:** compare results with the brief. Agree training, workload or authority changes and the next review date.

This is a suggested onboarding sequence, not a prescribed probation period. A weekly ten-minute review is better than saving every concern for an end-of-month surprise.

## Before opening the role

- [ ] The repeated work and its business consequence are documented.
- [ ] The role has achievable outcomes and a manager.
- [ ] Required skills and training needs are separated.
- [ ] Full costs and a downside cash case are affordable.
- [ ] Candidates receive an accurate account of pay and working conditions.
- [ ] Selection tests the job using comparable evidence.
- [ ] Employment terms and obligations are checked before an offer.
- [ ] Tools, access and the first-month plan are ready.

Budget a focused session for the brief and affordability check, then allow for sourcing, interviews and candidate availability; there is no reliable fixed hiring duration. Your next action is to write down the work you want off the founder's desk and the result that would show it is being done well.
