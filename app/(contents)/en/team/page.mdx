# Team and Hiring: Define the Work Before the Role

> **In short:** Hire for a clear outcome you cannot sustainably cover with the current team. Leave with a role brief, a fair assessment method and the responsibilities you need to prepare for.

## Start with the work

List the repeated tasks that are falling behind, the results required and the time they take. Decide what can be simplified or stopped before adding a role.

Use [first hires](/en/team/first-hires) to compare priorities. Then use the [hiring scorecard](/en/team/hiring-scorecard) to define the outcomes, evidence and consistent questions you will use to assess candidates.

## Example: turn an overloaded task into an assessable role

A **role outcome** describes the result required; an **assessment criterion** describes the evidence you will use to judge ability. A job title alone supplies neither.

A fictional shop-software team spends too much founder time answering routine onboarding questions. Its first draft says “hire a smart operations person.” A more useful brief is:

```text
Purpose: help new customers complete the agreed onboarding task.
Outcomes: accurate setup records; unresolved issues reach the right owner.
Work: explain the setup steps, record issues and check completion.
Manager: one named founder; first review after an agreed initial period.
Budget and arrangement: write the actual pay range, hours and terms before advertising.
Access: only the systems and records needed for the role.
```

For an assessment, give each candidate the same fictional customer question and setup instructions. Ask for a reply and a note describing anything they cannot resolve. Assess **accuracy**, **clear explanation** and **appropriate escalation**. A confident reply that invents an answer should not beat a clear reply that identifies a real limitation.

Tell candidates the time expected and how the sample will be used; do not use an assessment to obtain undisclosed production work. Record the evidence for the decision against the same criteria. Then prepare the actual working terms and applicable employer obligations before making an offer.

You have a usable brief when a candidate can understand the work, the manager can judge its outcome and both know which expectations still need agreement.

## Draft a role brief

Include **purpose / expected outcomes / responsibilities / working arrangements / pay budget / manager / review date**. Name the access and information the person needs, and who will train them.

A useful assessment checks the work the role actually involves. Explain the process, time commitment and how work samples will be used. Keep candidate records limited to the hiring purpose.

## Prepare for the relationship

Before making an offer, check the terms and obligations that apply to the actual working arrangement. Calling someone an intern or freelancer does not settle that question. Include supervision, payment, leave where applicable, confidentiality and handling of access when the relationship ends in your preparation.

Use the [first-employee journey](/en/journeys/hire-first-employee) for the sequence. If you are sharing ownership and founding responsibility, use [co-founders](/en/cofounders) instead.
