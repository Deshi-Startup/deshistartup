# Selling to Businesses and Organisations

> **In short:** A useful conversation is only the beginning of a business sale. Find the user, the buyer and the approval process, then agree on a next step that both sides can verify.

## Find the point where the sale is stuck

| Problem | Use | Leave with |
|---|---|---|
| You cannot reach the right business customer | [Selling to SMEs](/en/b2b/sme-sales) | A specific customer group and outreach approach |
| A pilot is not becoming a contract | [Pilot to contract](/en/b2b/pilot-to-contract) | Success criteria, decision owner and review date |
| Work is delivered but payment is late | [Invoice collection](/en/b2b/invoice-collection) | A record of what is due and the next follow-up |

## Example: interest is not purchase approval

**B2B** means selling to a business or organisation. The **user** does the work, the **buyer** evaluates a purchase and an **approver** authorises it. A **pilot** is a bounded trial with agreed terms. A **purchase order** records a buyer's order; an invoice requests payment for the agreed sale. Neither document is cash received.

In a fictional Chattogram training business, an administrator likes a scheduling service. The branch manager approves the budget and head-office finance pays invoices. The seller initially writes “customer ready to buy,” but the administrator has only agreed to a demonstration.

The seller makes the buying process visible:

| Person | Question | Next step |
|---|---|---|
| Administrator | Does the demonstration solve the actual scheduling task? | Try one made-up timetable |
| Branch manager | What would justify a paid pilot? | Agree scope, price and acceptance criteria |
| Finance | What documents and payment timing apply? | Confirm requirements before signing |

The next message is specific: “We can demonstrate one timetable on Thursday. If it fits, could we review a one-branch pilot with the manager? We will agree delivery, price and payment terms before starting.”

After the meeting, the record says **demonstration agreed; purchase not approved; manager discussion next**. That distinction prevents unpaid work being mistaken for revenue. Make your own record with the people, decision, evidence and date. It is ready when you can identify who must act next and what they have actually agreed.

## Prepare for one buyer conversation

Write down who experiences the problem, who approves the purchase, who signs and who pays. They may be different people. Ask what the customer uses today and what would need to happen for them to change.

After the conversation, send a concise record of the proposed scope, exclusions, evidence of success, price, payment timing and next decision. Do not treat a friendly introduction or an unpaid pilot as a confirmed sale.

For a sequence from first outreach to delivery, use [sell to SMEs](/en/journeys/sell-to-smes). For consumer sales and inbox conversations, use [customers and sales](/en/customers).
