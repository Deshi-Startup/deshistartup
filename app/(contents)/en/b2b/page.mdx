# Selling to Businesses and Organisations

> **In short:** A useful conversation is only the beginning of a business sale. Find the user, the buyer and the approval process, then agree on a next step that both sides can verify.

## Find the point where the sale is stuck

| Problem | Use | Leave with |
|---|---|---|
| You cannot reach the right business customer | [Selling to SMEs](/en/b2b/sme-sales) | A specific customer group and outreach approach |
| A pilot is not becoming a contract | [Pilot to contract](/en/b2b/pilot-to-contract) | Success criteria, decision owner and review date |
| Work is delivered but payment is late | [Invoice collection](/en/b2b/invoice-collection) | A record of what is due and the next follow-up |

## Prepare for one buyer conversation

Write down who experiences the problem, who approves the purchase, who signs and who pays. They may be different people. Ask what the customer uses today and what would need to happen for them to change.

After the conversation, send a concise record of the proposed scope, exclusions, evidence of success, price, payment timing and next decision. Do not treat a friendly introduction or an unpaid pilot as a confirmed sale.

For a sequence from first outreach to delivery, use [sell to SMEs](/en/journeys/sell-to-smes). For consumer sales and inbox conversations, use [customers and sales](/en/customers).
