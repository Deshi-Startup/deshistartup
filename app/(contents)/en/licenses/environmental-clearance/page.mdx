# Environmental Clearance: Four Classes, Two Certificates

> **In short:** The Environment Conservation Rules 2023 sort industrial establishments and projects into four classes — green, yellow, orange, and red.[^ecr-class] Green needs one certificate, the environmental clearance, wherever it is set up.[^ecr-obligation] The other three need a **location clearance** first, then the environmental clearance.[^ecr-obligation] That order is where it bites: without the location clearance you may not develop the land or build any infrastructure, and gas, electricity, and water connections may not be supplied.[^ecr-obligation] Only once it is in hand may land development, construction, and opening a letter of credit begin.[^ecr-location-effect] And without the environmental clearance, no class may run even trial production.[^ecr-obligation]

If yours is a software or services business, this page is probably not for you. But if there is any physical manufacturing in the plan — food, apparel, printing, cosmetics, agro-processing — this clearance is as old a question as the conversation about buying the land.

---

## Four classes, and what decides them

The Department sorts industrial establishments and projects into four classes for clearance purposes, weighing the extent of the activity, the range and magnitude of likely pollution, and the likely harmful effect on the environment and human health.[^ecr-class]

| Class | How the Rules explain it |
|---|---|
| Green | Comparatively very little effect on the environment and human health, with mitigation available |
| Yellow | A medium level of effect, needing mitigation to avoid it |
| Orange | A considerable effect that needs to be avoided, with suitable mitigation necessary to reduce it |
| Red | A severe effect needing avoidance to a suitable degree, with significant mitigation measures necessary |

Which business sits in which class is listed in **Schedule-1** of the Rules.[^ecr-class] Anything not on that list is classified under **Schedule-14** instead.[^ecr-class] So "my business isn't on the list" is not an exit — a class gets assigned either way.

Older blogs and advice still talk about "Orange-A" and "Orange-B." Those belonged to the previous Rules; under the 2023 Rules there are four classes.[^ecr-class]

---

## Two certificates, and who needs which

**Green class.** Wherever the new establishment or project is set up, no location clearance is needed. Only the environmental clearance.[^ecr-obligation]

**Yellow, orange, and red.** Before setting up or taking on the project, the location clearance comes first and the environmental clearance after.[^ecr-obligation]

**One large exception.** For establishments of any class set up in a government or private export processing zone, an economic zone, or a BSCIC industrial estate, no location clearance is required — only the environmental clearance.[^ecr-obligation] That line feeds straight into the arithmetic of choosing a site: inside a zone, a whole step disappears.

---

## Where the order bites

The location clearance is not just a piece of paper; it is a lock. The Rules make that explicit in three places:[^ecr-obligation]

- Without it, land for a yellow, orange, or red establishment or project **may not be developed and no infrastructure may be built**
- Without it, **gas, electricity, water, and other utilities may not be supplied** to those classes
- Without the environmental clearance, **no class may run trial production or start the project**

The Rules state the other side of it too. Once the location clearance is in hand, the entrepreneur may begin land development, construction of infrastructure or installations, and **opening letters of credit**, along with related work.[^ecr-location-effect]

So an L/C for machinery and the location clearance are in a fixed order, and reversing them strands money. Put this step in the calendar while you are still planning the land or the plant.

## Where to apply, and how long it takes

Applications go in online. Where that is not possible for some reason, they may be filed directly.[^ecr-apply]

Which office depends on where the establishment sits:[^ecr-apply]

| Location of the establishment | Where the application goes |
|---|---|
| A district with a departmental district office | That district office |
| A district without one | The district office with jurisdiction, or the divisional office |
| A metropolitan area | The metropolitan office, or the district office if there is no separate one |
| Several districts within one division | The regional or divisional office |
| Across more than one division | The Department's head office |

Two numbers are worth remembering. For the green class, within **seven working days** of receiving the application the office either grants the clearance after a site inspection, or refuses it and gives written reasons.[^ecr-green] For the environmental clearance generally, it must be granted within **30 working days** of the application, and a refusal must be communicated in writing with reasons within seven working days of the decision.[^ecr-ec-grant]

The red class runs a longer road. There, an environmental impact assessment has to be carried out by a listed environmental consultant, and a **public consultation** has to be arranged in the project area.[^ecr-eia] For large projects, or those spanning more than one division, the consultation moves to the regional or national level.[^ecr-eia]

## How the fees work

Two kinds of fee, resting on two different bases.

The **application processing fee** follows the class:[^ecr-fee6]

| Class | Processing fee |
|---|---|
| Yellow | Tk 2,000 |
| Orange | Tk 5,000 |
| Red | Tk 10,000 |

VAT at the government rate is added on top.[^ecr-fee6]

The **clearance fee** follows the amount invested, and the renewal fee is half of it:[^ecr-fee7]

| Investment | Clearance fee |
|---|---|
| Tk 1 lakh to 5 lakh | Tk 3,000 |
| Tk 5 lakh 1 to 10 lakh | Tk 6,000 |
| Tk 10 lakh 1 to 50 lakh | Tk 10,000 |
| Tk 50 lakh 1 to 1 crore | Tk 20,000 |
| Tk 1 crore 1 to 5 crore | Tk 40,000 |
| Tk 5 crore 1 to 20 crore | Tk 80,000 |
| Tk 20 crore 1 to 50 crore | Tk 1,60,000 |
| Tk 50 crore 1 to 100 crore | Tk 2,40,000 |
| Tk 100 crore 1 to 200 crore | Tk 4,00,000 |

The schedule carries further steps for larger investments.[^ecr-fee7] Note that in the red class the consultant's assessment and the public consultation are costs on top of these fees.

## Term and renewal

The term differs by class, and the clock runs faster the further you move from green:[^ecr-validity]

| Class | Term | Renewable every |
|---|---|---|
| Green | 5 years from the date of issue | 5 years |
| Yellow | 2 years | 2 years |
| Orange and red | 1 year | 1 year |

An application to renew the location clearance goes in at least **30 days before expiry**, on Form-4 with the Schedule-7 fee.[^ecr-renew] The office inspects the site and renews if the conditions have been followed.[^ecr-renew] The environmental clearance is renewed on Form-5, with the fee from the same schedule.[^ecr-renew]

In the orange or red class this is effectively an annual task. Add it to the compliance list — see [Annual compliance](/en/tax/annual-compliance).

## What you have to verify yourself

- **Which class your business is in.** The Schedule-1 list is the only answer, and Schedule-14 covers anything absent from it.[^ecr-class] Do not guess.
- **The site-selection guideline.** It sits in Schedule-9, and whether it was followed is one of the things weighed for the location clearance.[^ecr-siting]
- **Which documents and particulars are needed.** Whatever Form-3 asks for, and it varies by class.
- **Which consultants are listed.** In the red class, the assessment must be done by a consultant on the Department's list.[^ecr-eia]

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Buying land and starting development | Land development and construction are barred without the location clearance | Clearance first, earthmoving after |
| Opening the machinery L/C first | The Rules place the L/C after the clearance | Keep to the order |
| Budgeting against an "Orange-A" class | That belonged to the previous Rules | Use the four 2023 classes |
| Treating the clearance as one-and-done | Orange and red run one year | Renewal date in the calendar |
| Applying to renew at the last minute | Location clearance needs 30 days' notice | At least a month before expiry |
| Not knowing the industrial-estate benefit | Inside a zone no location clearance is required | Factor it in when choosing the site |

## Checklist

- [ ] Found the class in Schedule-1
- [ ] Settled whether that class needs one certificate or two
- [ ] Checked whether the site is in an EPZ, economic zone, or BSCIC industrial estate
- [ ] Matched the site against the Schedule-9 selection guideline
- [ ] Got the location clearance before land development and the L/C
- [ ] Worked out the fee from Schedule-7 against the investment figure
- [ ] For the red class, budgeted the time and cost of the assessment and consultation
- [ ] Put the class-appropriate renewal date in the calendar

Rule of thumb: **This is not the paperwork for starting production — it is the paperwork for touching the land. Keep the order: class, location clearance, land and L/C, environmental clearance, production.**
