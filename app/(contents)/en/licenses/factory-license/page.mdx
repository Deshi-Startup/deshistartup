# The DIFE Licence: Not Only for Factories, but Shops and Offices Too

> **In short:** It is called the factory licence, but the Labour Rules cast a much wider net. Factories, industrial establishments, commercial establishments, commercial banks and insurance companies, shops, and contractor agencies — the owner or occupier of each must apply to the Chief Inspector of the Department of Inspection for Factories and Establishments on Form-77 for registration and a licence.[^lr-354] For a new establishment the application goes in **at least 30 days before work starts**.[^lr-354] The fee runs on headcount, and for a small establishment it is a few hundred taka.[^lr-schedule7] A licence stays in force only until 30 June of the financial year in which it is granted.[^lr-355] Late renewal climbs through 25, 50, and 100 per cent, and a licence not renewed within one year of expiry is automatically cancelled — at which point the authority may file a complaint and a case to close the establishment.[^lr-355]

The general shape of labour law is in [Labour law basics](/en/team/labor-law). This page covers only registration and the licence.

---

## This is not just a factory matter

In the Labour Act, "establishment" means a shop, a commercial establishment, an industrial establishment, or premises where workers are employed to run an industry.[^labour-act] Three of those definitions are worth separating out, because most startups land not in the first but in the other two.

**Factory.** Premises where, on any day of the year, five or more workers generally work, and in any part of which a manufacturing process is carried on. A mine is not included.[^labour-act]

**Shop.** Premises used wholly or partly for selling goods for cash or credit, retail or wholesale, or where any service is rendered to a customer. It also takes in the office, storeroom, room, godown, or workplace mainly used in connection with that trade — whether on the same premises or elsewhere.[^labour-act]

**Commercial establishment.** One carrying on the business of advertising, commission, or forwarding, or which is a commercial agency. It also includes the clerical department of a factory or of an industrial or commercial establishment, the office-establishment of a person who employs workers to implement a contract with an industrial or commercial establishment, any unit of a joint stock company, any insurance company, banking company or bank, a broker's office, and a stock exchange.[^labour-act]

Two things stand out. First, an office that renders a service to customers can fall inside the definition of a shop without a trace of a factory about it. Second, the definition of a manufacturing process expressly covers printing by letterpress, lithography, computer, photo compose, or offset, and book-binding.[^labour-act] So a press-based business with five workers raises the factory question straight away.

Do not settle which box you are in by assumption — take the definitions to the Department and ask.

## Thirty days before work starts

The Rules set the application timetable in two parts:[^lr-354]

- For establishments already running before the Rules came into force — within six months of the date they took effect
- For establishments set up after the Rules took effect, or already in production — **at least 30 days before work starts**

The application goes to the Chief Inspector on Form-77, in duplicate.[^lr-354] The Labour Act separately requires notice to the Chief Inspector before work begins, and covers plan approval and the fees for licensing and registration in another section.[^labour-act]

Most founders start work before they learn about those 30 days. They open the office, hire staff, and find out from an inspection letter. The only way to avoid that is to pick up Form-77 as soon as the opening date is fixed.

The Chief Inspector may carry out plan approval, licence grant and renewal, class change, and expansion permission online, though every party still has to keep the paper and the evidence.[^lr-359]

---

## For a factory, the layout plan comes first

A factory has one more step before the licence — approval of the layout plan, and of an expansion layout plan if you expand.[^lr-353]

Where the factory building is pucca or has more than one storey, the application has to carry the structural design prepared by the authority the government designates, a soil test, and a construction certificate from a recognised engineering body.[^lr-353]

And an approved layout plan cannot be altered in any way without the Chief Inspector's written approval.[^lr-353] Keep that in mind before planning to move machines or add a line.

## The fee runs on headcount

Schedule-7 carries six separate tables, and every one rests on the same basis — the maximum number of workers or employees employable on any day of the year.[^lr-schedule7]

**For a factory:**

| Class | Workers | Licence fee | Renewal fee |
|---|---|---|---|
| A | 5–30 | Tk 500 | Tk 300 |
| B | 31–50 | Tk 1,000 | Tk 700 |
| C | 51–100 | Tk 1,500 | Tk 1,000 |
| D | 101–200 | Tk 2,500 | Tk 1,800 |
| E | 201–300 | Tk 3,000 | Tk 2,200 |
| F | 301–500 | Tk 5,000 | Tk 3,500 |
| G | 501–750 | Tk 6,000 | Tk 4,800 |
| H | 751–1000 | Tk 8,000 | Tk 5,000 |
| I | 1001–2000 | Tk 10,000 | Tk 7,000 |
| J | 2001–3000 | Tk 12,000 | Tk 8,400 |
| K | 3001–5000 | Tk 15,000 | Tk 10,000 |
| L | 5001 and above | Tk 18,000 | Tk 12,000 |

**For a commercial establishment** (excluding clubs, hotels, restaurants, banks, and insurance) the table starts lower — Tk 500 licence and Tk 300 renewal for 1 to 10 people, Tk 1,000 and Tk 700 for 11 to 30, and Tk 1,500 and Tk 1,000 for 31 to 50.[^lr-schedule7]

**For shops, super stores, clubs, restaurants, residential hotels, and manufacturing units that are not factories**, the steps are smaller still — Tk 100 and Tk 50 for 0 to 1 worker, Tk 200 and Tk 70 for 2 to 3, Tk 400 and Tk 100 for 4 to 6, and Tk 500 and Tk 200 for 7 to 10.[^lr-schedule7]

Industrial establishments, commercial banks and insurance companies, and contractor agencies each have their own table in the same schedule.[^lr-schedule7]

The numbers make one thing clear. The cost of this licence is never the obstacle to a decision — annual renewal for a ten-person office is Tk 300. What costs money is delay and mismatch.

## 30 June, and the three steps of lateness

The term works as it does for the fire licence: every licence stays in force until 30 June of the financial year in which it is granted.[^lr-355] Renewal means applying on Form-77 before expiry, with the prescribed fee paid.[^lr-355]

Late, and the additional fee climbs in steps:[^lr-355]

| How late | Additional fee |
|---|---|
| Past the deadline but within the next three months | 25% of the renewal fee |
| More than three months | 50% of the renewal fee |
| No application within six months | An amount equal to the renewal fee — 100% |

Where part of the renewal fee was paid by the due date, the additional fee applies only to the unpaid part.[^lr-355]

The heaviest provision comes last. If the licence is not renewed with the late fee within one year of expiry, it is automatically treated as cancelled, and the authority may file a complaint and a case to close the establishment.[^lr-355]

The licence, or a copy of it, also has to be displayed in a public place at the establishment.[^lr-355] Keeping it in a drawer does not satisfy the condition.

---

## When headcount changes, the licence changes too

Almost everyone skips this step, and for a growing team it is the most relevant one. If a rise or fall in worker numbers changes the class, or if the name, address, or ownership changes, the licence has to be amended — an application on Form-77 to the Chief Inspector, describing the nature of the amendment and surrendering the original licence.[^lr-356]

The amendment fee is Tk 500 in every case except shops, where it is Tk 200. Where the class goes up, the Schedule-7 fee for the new headcount is payable on top, less the fee paid when the licence was first taken.[^lr-356]

A change of ownership carries one extra condition: information on payment of the dues and benefits of workers employed under the previous owner has to be attached.[^lr-356] Ask for that document early in any conversation about buying a business.

---

## Lost, closed, or stopped by an inspector

**If the licence is lost or destroyed.** Apply on Form-77, with a fee of Tk 1,000 in all cases except shops, where it is Tk 500. A newspaper advertisement, or an attested copy of a general diary filed at the relevant thana, has to go with it.[^lr-357]

**If the establishment closes permanently.** Surrender the original licence with an application stating the reasons, and the inspector may cancel it after verification.[^lr-358]

**If the inspector sees a risk.** Where an establishment, or any part of it, appears dangerous to human life and safety, the inspector may suspend the licence and order production stopped until the workplace is safe.[^lr-358]

**To restore a cancelled or suspended licence.** Double the renewal fee is payable for every financial year, starting from the year in which the cancellation or suspension happened.[^lr-358] Leave it alone and the clock does not stop — it runs at twice the speed.

---

## What you have to get from the office

- **Which class your establishment falls in.** Factory, commercial establishment, shop — the fee tables differ and the definitions can overlap.
- **How "workers employable" is counted.** The fee rests on the maximum on any day of the year, so check how seasonal and part-time hiring is counted.[^lr-schedule7]
- **How much can be done online.** The Rules permit it, but the practical arrangement varies by office.[^lr-359]
- **Which office covers your area.** The Chief Inspector has divisional and district offices.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Skipping it because "we're not a factory" | Shops and commercial establishments are inside the net too | Match yourself against the definitions |
| Applying after work has started | The rule says at least 30 days before | Form-77 as soon as the date is fixed |
| Keeping the old class as the team grows | A change of class needs an amendment | Apply for the amendment when the number changes |
| Keeping the licence in a drawer | Breaks the display requirement | Put it on the wall |
| Letting the term lapse | 25, 50, 100 per cent, then cancellation | Renew before 30 June |
| Running on the seller's licence after an acquisition | A change of ownership needs an amendment and workers' dues information | Ask for the papers during the negotiation |

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Settled by definition whether this is a factory, a commercial establishment, or a shop
- [ ] Filed Form-77 at least 30 days before work started
- [ ] For a factory, got the layout plan approved first
- [ ] Found the class and fee in Schedule-7 from the headcount
- [ ] Displayed the licence in a public place once issued
- [ ] Put the 30 June renewal in the calendar
- [ ] Noted that a change in team size means amending the class
- [ ] Filed the original licence and the fee receipt

Rule of thumb: **Ask yourself whether anyone draws a salary at your establishment. If the answer is yes, getting on DIFE's register is your question too — chimney or no chimney.**
