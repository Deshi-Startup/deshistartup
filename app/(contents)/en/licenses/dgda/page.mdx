# The DGDA Pathway: Supplements Are Medicines in Law

> **In short:** A circular the Directorate General of Drug Administration issued on 24 December 2025 removes the ambiguity. Under section 2(5)(cha) of the Drugs and Cosmetics Act 2023, dietary supplements, herbal supplements, nutritional supplements, medical and therapeutic nutrition, and food-therapy or supplementary-therapy items are all **defined as "medicines."**[^dgda-supp] And under section 22(1), **manufacturing, importing, exporting, selling, distributing, storing, or displaying any of them without registration is a punishable offence**.[^dgda-supp] The circular adds that **no no-objection certificate will be issued** for these items, and that where they are imported commercially without registration, the Directorate cannot give any opinion or NOC for release from the customs house or port.[^dgda-supp]

Selling vitamins, protein powder, or herbal capsules on Facebook looks simple. The paperwork behind it is not. This page maps that side.

---

## Why a supplement is a medicine

Many people assume a supplement is a food product and therefore the Food Safety Authority's territory. In practice the Drug Administration has pulled it inside its own Act, and the December 2025 circular says so with the sections cited.[^dgda-supp]

Three things follow directly:

- **Registration first, selling after.** Without registration, none of the six — manufacture, import, export, sale, distribution, storage, display — is permitted.[^dgda-supp]
- **The NOC loophole is closed.** No NOC will be issued for these items. Only small quantities for personal use, and special needs in specialised treatment, will be considered.[^dgda-supp]
- **You will not find out at the port.** For a commercial consignment arriving without registration, the Directorate will give no opinion and no NOC for release.[^dgda-supp] So a plan of shipping first and papering later leaves the consignment sitting at the port.

## Importing: reference countries and the document list

An application for recipe approval, filed with the fee paid, goes to the **Drug Control Committee (DCC)** for disposal, and is settled according to the Committee's decision.[^dgda-supp]

For registering an imported product, subject to DCC approval, the **reference countries** are the European Union member states, the United Kingdom, the United States, Switzerland, Canada, Australia, Japan, South Korea, Singapore, and Russia.[^dgda-supp] There is one condition, and it is a hard one — **the item must be registered in those countries under the same brand name**.[^dgda-supp]

That line changes sourcing decisions. If the plan is to bring in a cheap white-label supplement and sell it under your own brand, check first whether that brand name is registered in a reference country.

What goes with the application:[^dgda-supp]

- An application on the importer's official letterhead, and an authorisation letter from the manufacturer
- A certificate from the chamber of commerce of the manufacturing country
- A **Free Sale Certificate (FSC)** from the manufacturing country's regulatory authority, attested by the Bangladesh embassy
- Where the manufacturing country is outside the reference list, the Country of Origin plus an FSC or **Certificate of Pharmaceutical Product (CPP)** from any one listed country, embassy-attested
- **MSDS, Certificate of Analysis (COA), Product Test Method**, and composition detail
- **Manufacturing process and stability data** — six months of accelerated data and real-time data for the shelf life
- The manufacturer's **GMP certificate**, draft packaging material, the **wholesale licence**, and the challan for the fee

Read that list once and it is clear this is not a few weeks' work. Embassy attestation and stability data take time, and both depend on the foreign supplier's cooperation. Ask for these documents while you are still choosing the supplier.

## Manufacturing locally

For registering a locally manufactured dietary supplement or similar item, **the established registration procedure and rules for medicines** must be followed.[^dgda-supp]

That is to say the route is the medicine route, not the food route — manufacturing licence, recipe approval, packaging approval, GMP. The fees and timelines for those steps are in the table below.

## DGDA services: how long, and how much

From the Directorate's citizen charter, the entries that matter to a startup:[^dgda-charter]

| Service | Time | Fee |
|---|---|---|
| Evaluation and approval of a project proposal for a new medicine factory | 120 working days | Free |
| Licence to manufacture biological and non-biological medicines | 120 working days | Biological Tk 1,00,000; non-biological Tk 50,000 |
| Unani, Ayurvedic, homeopathic, and herbal manufacturing licence | 120 working days | Tk 10,000 |
| Recipe approval for a new medicine | 120 working days | Tk 5,000; herbal recipe Tk 500 |
| Approval of label, carton, and insert | 20 working days | Free |
| Marketing authorisation | 15 working days | Free |
| GMP certificate | 90 working days | Free |
| Retail sale licence, model pharmacy, or model medicine shop | 60 days | Allopathic: Tk 2,500 in municipal and metropolitan areas, Tk 1,500 under a union council |
| Retail licence renewal | 7 working days | Tk 1,800 inside a municipal area, Tk 700 outside |
| Wholesale medicine sale licence | 30 working days | Tk 10,000 |
| Wholesale licence renewal | 15 working days | Tk 5,000 |
| Preliminary registration for importing a medicine, medical device, or IVD reagent | 90 working days | Tk 5,000 |
| Grant of the import registration | 90 working days | Registration and analysis fee Tk 50,000 |
| Renewal of the import registration | 60 working days | Tk 10,000 |
| Medicine export licence, Form-10A | 15 working days | Free |
| CPP or FSC for export | 7 working days | Free |

**15% VAT** is added to every fee, and payment goes by treasury challan under the specified economic codes.[^dgda-charter] Unani, Ayurvedic, and homeopathic streams carry their own retail and renewal fees, both given in the charter.[^dgda-charter]

Look at the timelines. A manufacturing licence at 120 working days is close to six months in practice, and the factory project approval before it is another 120 working days. Put those two numbers side by side when you draw the timeline for a health-products business.

## Limits on advertising

On 17 June 2026 the Directorate issued a notice on stopping the advertisement and promotion of medicines on social media without the licensing authority's prior approval.[^dgda-ad]

Since a supplement is a medicine in law, that notice is worth reading before planning promotion on Facebook or TikTok. Using influencers does not move the liability — it stays with the owner of the product.

Similar controls have arrived on the food side too — see [Food business paperwork](/en/licenses/food-safety).

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Treating a supplement as a food product | In law it is a medicine; selling it unregistered is an offence | DGDA registration first |
| Planning to import on an NOC | No NOC is issued for these items | Take the registration route |
| Shipping first and papering later | No release opinion will come from the Directorate | Register before the consignment ships |
| Not checking registration in a reference country | The same brand name must be registered there | Verify while choosing the supplier |
| Not budgeting for embassy attestation | An unattested FSC or CPP stalls the application | Allow the time and the cost upfront |
| Promoting on social media without approval | Prior approval is required | Read the notice and get the approval |

## Checklist

- [ ] Checked whether the product falls within the Act's definition of a medicine
- [ ] Settled on import or local manufacture
- [ ] For imports, verified the product is registered in a reference country under the same brand name
- [ ] Had the FSC or CPP attested by the Bangladesh embassy
- [ ] Collected the MSDS, COA, test method, and composition detail
- [ ] Obtained six months of accelerated and real-time stability data
- [ ] Obtained the manufacturer's GMP certificate
- [ ] Prepared the wholesale licence and the fee challan
- [ ] Drew the timeline against the per-step working-day limits
- [ ] Checked the rules on prior approval for advertising

Rule of thumb: **The question is not "is this a food or a medicine." The question is what the Act says — and the Act says a supplement is a medicine. So put DGDA registration on the first line of the plan, not the last.**
