# Licenses and Approvals: Check What Your Business Will Actually Do

> **In short:** Describe the work, products and premises before asking which licence you need. Company incorporation and local trade licensing are separate, and neither should be treated as a complete list of activity-specific approvals. Get the current requirements from the responsible authorities before starting the affected work.

You may have registered a company or found a place to operate, and now someone asks whether the business has “all its papers.” That question is difficult to answer without describing the work. A website that takes orders for packaged food, a kitchen that prepares it and a software company serving food sellers can look similar online while carrying out different activities.

The purpose of this section is to help you make that description precise enough to check the requirements. It is useful before opening premises, adding a product or changing how you operate, as well as when starting a new business. You do not need to apply for every approval mentioned here. You do need to find out which ones apply to the work you intend to do.

## Explain the activity before asking for a checklist

Write down what you sell and what happens between receiving an order and delivering it. Include work done before the order, such as manufacturing, preparing food or storing stock. Note the operating and storage addresses, who uses the premises and which parts of the work another business will perform.

This is more useful than calling the business “e-commerce” or “technology.” Those labels do not tell an authority whether you handle food, import goods, operate machinery or only supply software. For a food seller, for example, explaining where preparation, packaging and storage take place makes it possible to ask about each of those activities. It still does not establish the full food-business licensing list by itself.

Local trade licences are handled by the relevant city corporation, municipality or union parishad, while incorporation is a separate company-registration process.[^license-authority] Start with the authority responsible for the business address to check the local licence category. The [trade-licence category guide](/en/licenses/trade-license-category) explains how to prepare that question.

## Follow the questions raised by the work and premises

Once the activity is clear, use the relevant guide to investigate the next requirement:

- For premises and fire-safety questions, see [fire licences](/en/licenses/fire-license).
- For the environmental requirements of the proposed activity, see [environmental clearance](/en/licenses/environmental-clearance).
- For preparing or handling food, see [food safety](/en/licenses/food-safety).
- For setting up production premises, see [factory licences](/en/licenses/factory-license).
- For a trade licence already in use, see [renewal](/en/licenses/trade-license-renewal).

The links are starting points for specific questions, not a universal list or proof that an approval applies. Check the responsible authority's current instructions against your activity. The body that decides an approval may also differ from the portal through which you submit it; make sure you know whose requirement you are following.

If an instruction seems unclear, send the activity description and ask which category, documents and process apply. Keep the response with the official checklist. A competitor's practice or a service provider's general package is not enough to settle your own requirements. For a substantial commitment or an unclear regulated activity, take the question to a qualified specialist before signing or starting work.

## Fit the approvals into the launch plan

For each applicable approval, record who will prepare the application, which documents are missing, the current fee and the stated processing time. Check what the time estimate covers and what happens if the application is returned for clarification. This lets you schedule the work around actual dependencies rather than assuming that paying a fee means the approval is complete.

Keep unresolved questions visible too. If you have not confirmed a food or premises requirement, do not mark it “not needed” merely to finish the list. Resolve it before the affected activity begins, and adjust the launch date if necessary.

After an approval is issued, check the business name, address, activity and any conditions. Save the document and any renewal date with the person responsible for maintaining it. The [legal roadmap](/en/legal-roadmap) connects these checks with company structure, tax, contracts and later duties.

To begin, write the activity description and obtain the current local checklist. Then follow the questions your actual products, premises and process raise. That is a more reliable way to find the necessary papers than buying a package before you know what it covers.
