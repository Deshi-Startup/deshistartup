# Fire Licence: Who Needs One, What It Takes, and the 30 June Clock

> **In short:** The fire licence obligation is narrower than most people assume, and the penalty far heavier. The Act requires a licence to use a building or place as a warehouse or a workshop, and both terms are defined around flammable material.[^fire-act] Operating without one carries imprisonment of not less than three years plus a fine, and the goods in that place are liable to confiscation.[^fire-act] Where the offender is a company, every director, manager, and responsible officer is deemed to have committed the violation themselves unless they can prove it happened without their knowledge.[^fire-act] And note the date: whatever day the licence is issued, it runs only to 30 June, and the renewal fee must be paid in advance by that day.[^fire-rules]

Other sector approvals are in [Licences and approvals](/en/licenses). This page covers only the licence from the Fire Service and Civil Defence Directorate.

---

## Who needs one, and who does not

In the Act's own words, the duty begins here: any person wishing to use a building or place as a warehouse or a workshop must obtain a licence from the Director General.[^fire-act]

Both terms are defined in the Act, and both turn on flammable material:[^fire-act]

- **Warehouse** — any building or place used for preserving, storing, pressing, sorting, and trading flammable material
- **Workshop** — a building or place used for processing flammable material

What counts as flammable material is not fixed by the Act itself; the government declares it.[^fire-act] The 2014 Rules carry the list in column 2 of the First Schedule.[^fire-rules] So the question is not "do I have an office?" but "is what I store or process on that list?"

**A software or services business in a rented office?** This licence generally does not bind it. But the building carries its own duty: no plan for a multi-storey or commercial building may be approved without the Director General's clearance on fire prevention and extinguishment, and that requirement overrides any other law in force.[^fire-act] Ask the landlord for a copy of that clearance before you take the space. In the Act, a multi-storey building means one of not less than seven storeys, and a commercial building means one used for a bank, insurance or other financial institution, a shopping complex, or business, commerce, or government work.[^fire-act]

**A warehouse, workshop, or processing operation?** Then the licence has to be in your own name, whatever clearance the building holds.

## The steps

The Directorate's published flowchart runs the work in four steps.[^fire-flow]

**1. Apply online.** The establishment applies online through the Fire Service and Civil Defence Directorate's website.[^fire-flow] The Rules put the form in the Second Schedule and set the application form fee at Tk 100, deposited by treasury challan into the government head, with the challan number and date entered on the form and the original challan copy, the plans, and attested copies of the other listed papers attached.[^fire-rules]

**2. Submit the hard copies.** These go to the office of the relevant Assistant Director:[^fire-flow]

- A copy of the fire safety plan, where applicable, in the first year — and an effectiveness certificate in the second
- The rent agreement or the annual valuation document
- An undertaking on a Tk 300 stamp
- A certificate from the owner that no case is pending
- A photocopy of the trade licence
- A photocopy of the national identity card
- The layout plan

**3. Inspection and assessment of the fee.** The relevant inspector visits the premises and fixes the licence fee, which is then deposited under the Ministry of Home Affairs economic code.[^fire-flow] The Rules bound this step too: the empowered officer completes verification and the site inspection within seven working days of receiving the application form.[^fire-rules]

**4. The licence is issued.** It comes from the office of the Sub-Assistant Director, Assistant Director, or Deputy Director.[^fire-flow]

The Act adds two outer limits. Within 90 days of receiving the application the Director General grants the licence if satisfied; if not satisfied, the decision comes within 120 days, after giving the applicant a hearing.[^fire-act]

Do not invert one part of the order. The Act is explicit that an application is inadmissible unless the prescribed fee is deposited by treasury challan and a copy attached, and that no licence is granted unless the licence fee is deposited the same way.[^fire-act]

## How the fee is set

You cannot budget from a printed table here, and that is this licence's most uncomfortable feature. The fee for a warehouse or workshop handling flammable material is fixed at the rates in the First Schedule, and what it comes to depends on which material, how much of it, and what kind of building — the inspector works it out after the visit.[^fire-rules][^fire-flow]

There is one provision that saves money, though. To encourage fire prevention and extinguishment arrangements, a rebate is allowed against the total assessed fee, capped at 20 per cent.[^fire-rules] Money spent on equipment and systems comes partly back through the licence fee.

One more thing to note. Under no circumstances may the licence fee be paid in cash, by pay order, bank draft, or cheque — treasury challan only.[^fire-rules] And if the money fails to reach the correct head because of an erasure or error on the challan, the liability is the licence-holder's.[^fire-rules]

## The 30 June clock

The renewal date for this licence is not a matter of guesswork; the Rules state it.[^fire-rules]

- Whatever date in the financial year the licence is issued, it stays in force until **30 June** of that financial year, and must be renewed before the next financial year begins
- The next year's licence fee must be paid in advance **by 30 June** each year
- Past 30 June, an additional late fee of **5 per cent** of the licence fee applies for each month or part of a month, for up to **six months**
- Not renewed within that window, the licence is **treated as cancelled**

The renewal application goes to the Director General on the company's letterhead or plain paper, with the original challan copy and the original licence.[^fire-rules] An empowered officer then completes an inquiry and reports within seven working days, and where renewal is recommended the Director General renews within three working days.[^fire-rules]

If the report says the conditions have not been met, the Director General writes to the applicant within three working days and sets a deadline for implementing them. Miss that deadline and the renewal application is rejected, and the licence is treated as cancelled.[^fire-rules]

---

## The licence is not transferable

The Act says it directly: no licence granted under it is transferable.[^fire-act] And where ownership of a building or place changes hands, the new owner cannot use it as a warehouse or workshop, or allow it to be so used, without obtaining a licence of their own.[^fire-act]

This matters in two situations. First, when buying a business that comes with a warehouse or workshop, the seller's licence is worth nothing to you — put it on the diligence list. Second, moving the warehouse means starting again for the new address.

## Without one, or in breach of the conditions

| What happens | The penalty in law |
|---|---|
| Using a place as a warehouse or workshop without a licence | Not less than 3 years' imprisonment plus a fine, and the goods liable to confiscation[^fire-act] |
| Failing to comply with a licence condition | Not less than 6 months' imprisonment plus a fine[^fire-act] |
| Storing, processing, pressing, or sorting flammable material in breach of the Act or Rules | Not less than 2 years' imprisonment plus a fine, and the material forfeit to the government[^fire-act] |

For a company there is one more provision, and it is the one that reaches founders. Where the violator is a company, every director, manager, secretary, or other responsible officer or agent engaged in the relevant work is deemed to have committed the violation — unless they can prove it happened without their knowledge, or that they made every effort to prevent it.[^fire-act] "Director" here includes a partner or a member of the board of a commercial establishment.[^fire-act]

## Objection and appeal

If a decision goes against you, there is no need to sit with it; the steps are in the Act.[^fire-act]

| Step | To whom | Time |
|---|---|---|
| Review of an empowered officer's decision | Director General | Within 30 days of the memo |
| The Director General's decision | — | Within 30 days of the application |
| Appeal against the Director General's decision | The government | Within 30 days of the memo, subject to the prescribed fee |
| The government's decision | — | Within 60 days of the appeal, and it is final |

A licence also cannot be cancelled without a reasonable opportunity of hearing, and the same review and appeal routes apply to a cancellation.[^fire-act]

## What you have to get from the office

- **Whether your material is on the list.** The list in column 2 of the First Schedule is attached to the Rules — check your own goods against it.[^fire-rules]
- **What the fee will actually be.** It is assessed after the inspection; there is no single published figure.[^fire-flow]
- **Which arrangements earn how much rebate.** The method sits in the First Schedule's table, capped at 20 per cent.[^fire-rules]
- **Who needs a fire safety plan.** The flowchart says "where applicable," so confirm it for your building and operation.[^fire-flow]

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Treating the building's clearance as your licence | A warehouse or workshop needs its own | Keep the two separate |
| Using the seller's licence after buying a business | The licence is not transferable | A fresh licence in the new owner's name |
| Paying the fee by cheque or pay order | Prohibited by the Rules; the money never reaches the head | Treasury challan only |
| Letting 30 June pass | 5% a month, cancelled after six | Pay before June ends |
| Sitting on a conditions letter | Renewal is refused once the deadline passes | Act within the time given |
| Opening a warehouse without a fire safety plan | It fails inspection and breaches the conditions | Plan and equipment before opening |

## Checklist

- [ ] Checked whether the goods or raw material fall on the flammable-material list
- [ ] Settled whether the place is a warehouse or a workshop
- [ ] For rented premises, got a copy of the building's clearance from the landlord
- [ ] Applied online and paid the Tk 100 form fee by challan
- [ ] Submitted all seven hard-copy documents to the Assistant Director's office
- [ ] Paid the assessed fee by treasury challan after the inspection
- [ ] Installed fire-fighting arrangements and claimed the rebate
- [ ] Put the 30 June renewal in the calendar
- [ ] Filed the original licence and the challan copy

Rule of thumb: **The fire licence question is not whether you have an office but whether you have flammable material. If you do, remember one date — 30 June — and pay by challan, always.**
