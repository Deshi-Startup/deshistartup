# Can You Charge Customers Automatically Every Month?

> **In short:** Say you want to sell something for BDT 500 a month. Your whole plan rests on one thing: next month's 500 taka arriving without you asking for it. In Bangladesh that usually does not happen. bKash can save a customer's wallet so paying is faster, but the customer still has to type their PIN every single time. bKash lists a separate "Subscriptions" product that would do the automatic pulling, but publishes no instructions for it. SSLCOMMERZ's public v4 API documentation exposes EMI but no recurring-payment or subscription API. So before you announce a monthly price, email your provider and ask. Until they answer in writing, plan on a person collecting the money by hand every month, and count those hours as a cost.

## What this page is about

A subscription business sounds simple. The customer pays you every month, and you build once and earn many times.

That only works if the money moves on its own. Somebody has to be able to take BDT 500 out of the customer's account on the first of the month while the customer is asleep. That is called **auto-debit**, or a recurring payment.

In most countries you switch it on and forget it. In Bangladesh it is hard to get, and most founders find that out after they have already promised customers a monthly price.

This page tells you what you can and cannot do here, what it costs to collect the money by hand instead, and the exact questions to send your payment provider.

## Three different things share one name

Ask whether you can take subscriptions on bKash and you will get three different answers. That is because three separate things travel under the same word.

| Service | What it does | Does the customer have to do anything each month? |
|---|---|---|
| Tokenized checkout | Saves the customer's wallet so paying is faster | **Yes.** They must type their PIN |
| Subscription, or auto-debit | You get permission once, then pull the money later | **No.** This is the one you want |
| Card EMI | Splits one purchase into instalments | No, but this is the bank's instalment plan, not your monthly bill |

You want the middle row. It is also the one with the least information available.

## What "saved wallet" actually saves you

bKash's tokenized checkout works like this. The first time, your customer clicks "Add bKash Account", goes to a bKash page, types their wallet number, agrees to the terms, and enters the six-digit code sent by SMS. After that an agreement is created and saved against their account on your side.

Here is the part that matters. For every payment after that, the customer still has to enter their bKash PIN.[^bkash-tokenized-checkout] So they must be on your site, choose their saved account, and type the PIN.

That is genuinely useful. It saves them retyping a wallet number, makes checkout faster, and fewer people abandon halfway. But it does not take money on the first of the month while nobody is watching. It is a faster front door, not an automatic one.

## The product that exists but has no manual

bKash does list a product called **Subscriptions**, marked "Available". Its description says that after a customer authorises it once, subscription-based merchants can collect recurring payments, giving EMI and insurance premiums as examples.[^bkash-subscriptions]

That is exactly what you want. The problem is what comes next.

Every other product has its own instructions page. Checkout has one. Tokenized checkout has one. So do auth and capture, refunds, payouts and webhooks. Subscriptions does not.[^bkash-subscriptions]

That is not just a headache for your developer. Without instructions you do not know which businesses qualify, what it costs, how long approval takes, or what the conditions are. None of that is something you can guess.

So email your account manager before you announce a monthly price. Ask for the answer by email, not on a phone call.

## Card EMI is not a monthly subscription

SSLCOMMERZ's public v4 API documentation exposes no recurring-payment or subscription API. It does expose EMI: switch on the `emi_option` setting and the payment page can show 3, 6 or 9 instalments.[^sslcommerz-emi]

These are not the same thing, and mixing them up is expensive.

EMI still begins with **one initiated checkout for one purchase**. The public documentation does not say when the merchant is settled, whether an acquiring bank pays the merchant up front, or who carries the financing. Confirm those terms in writing with SSLCOMMERZ and the acquiring bank. Whatever the settlement arrangement, EMI does not authorise a new charge for your service next month.

The public page also does not define which customers or payment instruments qualify across every merchant setup. Ask that in writing too; do not treat EMI as a substitute for recurring billing.

## Collecting by hand is a real cost

If you cannot charge automatically, somebody has to do it manually. Remind the customer, send a payment link, check whether the money arrived, then chase the ones who did not pay.

Each one is a small job. The problem is that you do it again for every customer, every month.

Say you have 200 customers on a BDT 500 monthly plan, and each one takes about 3 minutes to remind and collect from:

```
200 customers x 3 minutes  =  600 minutes  =  10 hours a month
```

Ten hours a month is fine. One person fits that around their other work.

Now grow to 1,000 customers:

```
1,000 customers x 3 minutes  =  3,000 minutes  =  50 hours a month
```

Fifty hours a month is not a side task any more. It is somebody's job. At that point you are earning BDT 5,00,000 a month and paying a salary just to collect it.

So put those hours in your costs before you set a price. They belong in your [unit economics](/en/metrics/unit-economics).

## If you cannot charge automatically

You still have a business. You just run it differently.

- **Sell longer plans up front.** Offer 3, 6 and 12-month packages next to the monthly one, at a discount. Take the money once and the collecting work for those months disappears.
- **Bill everyone on the same day.** If every customer is due on the first, the work is done in a day. Spread across the month, it eats a little time every single day.
- **Automate the reminders even if you cannot automate the payment.** One message three days before, one on the day, one three days after. That alone cuts the chasing calls sharply.
- **Make paying a one-tap job.** Put a payment link straight in the reminder. This is where a saved wallet earns its place, because the PIN is all that stands between the message and the money.
- **If you get paid before you deliver, look at auth and capture.** bKash lists this one as available too. You hold the money now and take it later. Useful when delivery comes after the order, though it is still not a monthly bill.

## Six questions to email your provider

Send these before you announce a price. Ask for written answers.

1. Can my business pull money every month without the customer doing anything?
2. If yes, what are the conditions? Business type, transaction volume, separate approval?
3. Is the rate different for it, and is there a setup cost?
4. How will we find out when a customer cancels their permission?
5. If a month's payment fails, is it retried? How many times?
6. How many days does it take to switch on?

**Question four is the one people forget.** The customer cancels their permission, your system never hears about it, so your service stays switched on and the money quietly stops. Ask how you will be told.

## Common mistakes

Use this table before setting the price or promising automatic collection to customers.

| Mistake | What happens |
|---|---|
| Setting a monthly price assuming auto-debit works | You end up collecting by hand, every month |
| Thinking a saved wallet means automatic payment | The customer still types a PIN every time |
| Treating card EMI as a subscription | It is one sale on the bank's instalment terms, not a monthly plan |
| Leaving collection hours out of your costs | Your margin quietly shrinks as you grow |
| No way to hear about a cancelled permission | The service runs on, the money stops |
| Offering only a monthly plan | You lose both the cash up front and the lighter workload |

Every row points to the same control: get the payment flow in writing, then price the human work that remains.

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Emailed your provider to ask whether auto-debit is available to you
- [ ] If it is not, decided who does the collecting each month
- [ ] Counted those hours as a cost in your pricing
- [ ] Offered a 3, 6 or 12-month package next to the monthly plan
- [ ] Set the billing dates and written the reminder schedule
- [ ] Know how you will learn that a customer cancelled their permission
- [ ] Written down when service stops if the money does not arrive

**Rule of thumb:** until the written answer arrives, assume a person has to collect the money by hand every month. Price for that, and staff for that.
