# Payment Gateways in Bangladesh: Compare Fees and Settlement

> **In short:** Compare the cost on the money you actually collect through each payment method. Card rates alone miss the <Term name="mfs">MFS</Term> rate, flat fees and setup cost. Shortlist two gateways, calculate the same sales scenario for both and get settlement, refund and document requirements in writing.

This comparison is for a Bangladesh-based startup choosing a gateway for its website or app. If you only need to receive wallet payments, first check [whether a gateway is necessary](/en/payments/mfs-and-gateways). Use the [payment gateways directory](/en/directory/payment-gateways) for provider links and application requirements.

## Published Gateway Fees

Start with the setup cost and the rates for your main payment methods. These are provider-published figures, not a negotiated offer.[^gateway-published-pricing]

| Provider / plan | One-time setup | Standard cards | MFS payments |
|---|---|---|---|
| SSLCOMMERZ Basic | BDT 25,500, non-refundable | 2.50% | 2.50% |
| aamarPay aamarGrowth | BDT 20,000 on the registration form; confirm the quote | Quote required | Quote required |
| PortPos | No signup fee | 2.50% | 2.20% |
| ShurjoPay | Quote required | Quote required | Quote required |

PortPos also publishes BDT 1 per successful transaction and no monthly fee. SSLCOMMERZ and PortPos list Amex at 3.50%. Ask separately about international cards, taxes on fees, refunds and any minimum charge.

**Price-check dates:** SSLCOMMERZ and PortPos were rechecked on 13 September 2026. On the same date, aamarPay's live pricing page requested a quote without displaying card or MFS rates. Its registration form listed BDT 20,000 as the non-refundable aamar Growth setup fee. Confirm that amount and the transaction rates together in writing.[^aamarpay-current-pricing] ShurjoPay's July review did not find a public rate. A missing published fee means unknown, not zero.

## Calculate Cost from Sales Value, Not Order Count

A percentage fee applies to the amount collected. Ten small bKash orders and one large card order cannot be weighted simply as ten to one. Add up the **sales value** on each channel for the same period.

Use percentages as decimals in this formula, so 2.5% is `0.025`:

```text
Monthly transaction fees =
  (card sales × card rate)
  + (MFS sales × MFS rate)
  + (successful transactions × flat fee)

Effective rate (%) = monthly transaction fees ÷ total sales × 100
First-year comparison = monthly transaction fees × 12 + setup fee
```

Add any monthly charge, tax on fees, foreign-card surcharge and refund or chargeback costs from the written quote. The first-year formula assumes the same sales each month and includes only the charges entered.

### Worked Example: BDT 240,000 of Monthly Sales

Suppose 200 successful orders average BDT 1,200 each, with BDT 192,000 collected through bKash and BDT 48,000 through standard domestic cards. MFS therefore accounts for 80% of **sales value**. With the listed rates, the partial cost comparison is:

| Gateway | Monthly transaction fees | Twelve months plus listed setup |
|---|---|---|
| SSLCOMMERZ | BDT 6,000 | BDT 97,500 |
| PortPos | BDT 5,624 | BDT 67,488 |

For PortPos: `(192,000 × 0.022) + (48,000 × 0.025) + (200 × 1) = 4,224 + 1,200 + 200 = BDT 5,624`.

SSLCOMMERZ's single 2.5% rate gives `240,000 × 0.025 = BDT 6,000`; twelve months plus BDT 25,500 setup gives BDT 97,500.

These totals include only the listed transaction and setup charges. Get rates for aamarPay and ShurjoPay before adding their totals; the old aamarPay rates are not a current offer.

### aamarPay vs SSLCOMMERZ: Compare the Written Quote

SSLCOMMERZ publishes a standard-channel rate; aamarPay's current card and MFS rates need a quote. For the same BDT 240,000 monthly sales example, ask aamarPay to price BDT 192,000 of MFS payments and BDT 48,000 of domestic-card payments separately.

If the quote confirms the listed BDT 20,000 setup fee, aamarPay's monthly transaction fees must be below roughly **BDT 6,458** to cost less than SSLCOMMERZ over the first year on the included charges:

```text
Monthly fee ceiling = (97,500 − 20,000) ÷ 12
                    = BDT 6,458.33
```

This is a break-even calculation, not an aamarPay transaction rate. Recalculate with the actual quote and sales mix. Ask both providers whether VAT is included in setup and transaction fees, what amount it applies to, and what they retain on refunds. Add those charges before choosing; neither a lower setup fee nor a headline percentage establishes the cheaper total.

Use the last three months of records if available. For a new startup, mark the figures as a forecast and check what happens if sales are lower. A large setup fee matters more when there are fewer transactions to spread it over.

## Check Settlement and Refunds Before Signing

Settlement means the gateway sends collected funds to your bank. aamarPay's FAQ, checked on 13 September 2026, states twice-weekly disbursement subject to working days and holidays. That is a payout schedule, not a promise that every payment reaches the bank within two days. SSLCOMMERZ's FAQ explains how to download settlement reports but does not give a fixed payout cycle.[^gateway-onboarding-and-faq] Get the timing, minimum amount and any reserve for your account in writing.

Send the same questions to each provider:

1. Which payment methods and currencies are approved for our product?
2. What are all setup, transaction, monthly and tax charges? Which fees are retained on refunds?
3. How many working days after a successful payment will the money reach our bank? Is there a minimum settlement amount or reserve?
4. How do weekends, holidays, disputes and chargebacks affect settlement?
5. Can we export order IDs, fees, refunds and settlement references for reconciliation?
6. Which documents and website policies are required for our business structure?

Before routing all sales through a provider, test a payment, refund and settlement with its permitted test or onboarding process. Match the order, fee and bank deposit using [payment reconciliation](/en/payments/reconciliation).

## Prepare the Right Documents

Document requirements differ by provider and business structure. SSLCOMMERZ's published onboarding list includes <Term name="e-tin">TIN</Term>, trade licence, VAT and DBID for a proprietorship; a private limited company has additional corporate documents. Its page uses the abbreviations CBR and RCBFI without expanding them, so ask the account manager what to supply.[^gateway-onboarding-and-faq]

aamarPay's FAQ lists trade licence, TIN, VAT “if any,” NID and a photograph, with a partnership deed or <Term name="incorp-certificate">incorporation certificate</Term> and MoA/AoA as appropriate. It also describes its application and bank sub-merchant forms.[^gateway-onboarding-and-faq]

Get the checklist for **your** structure and product. Do not interpret one provider's onboarding list as a universal legal duty to obtain every certificate. Use the [merchant-account guide](/en/payments/merchant-account) and [DBID guide](/en/registration/dbid) to prepare, then confirm any missing items with the provider.

## Avoid Mixing PortWallet and PortPos Prices

PortPos identifies itself as the successor to PortWallet. The older PortWallet pricing page and the PortPos page show different rates and signup charges.[^portwallet-portpos] Use the current brand's written quote. If accepting foreign cards, have the cross-border surcharge confirmed explicitly rather than copying an old comparison.

## Ready to Choose?

- [ ] I used sales value by payment method, not just the number of orders.
- [ ] Both quotes cover the same sales scenario, methods and billing period.
- [ ] Unknown fees are still marked unknown in the comparison.
- [ ] Settlement timing, refund deductions and any reserve are in writing.
- [ ] My business structure and product are accepted, and I have the requested documents.
- [ ] I can reconcile a payment, fee, refund and bank settlement.

Keep the written quote with your agreement. Recheck the comparison when your payment mix or contract changes.
