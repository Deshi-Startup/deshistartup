# A Roadmap for Building Your Startup

> **In short:** Start with the problem your business needs to solve next. The four stages below help organise the work: learn whether customers need the offer, make delivery reliable, grow what works, then give a larger team the systems and authority it needs.

Starting a business brings several kinds of work at once. You might be talking to customers, arranging a supplier, deciding whether to hire and trying to understand registration requirements. A long checklist can make all of them seem equally urgent.

This roadmap helps you choose what deserves attention now. Each stage describes a different challenge and links to the guides that help with it. The stages are not a timetable or a requirement to raise investment. You can return to an earlier one when you introduce a new product or discover that an assumption was wrong.

## Find the stage that matches your work

Use your actual situation to choose a starting point. The age of the business, size of its website or amount of funding does not settle which work it needs.

| Stage | The question you are working on |
|---|---|
| [1. Validate](/en/roadmap/validate) | Do these customers have the problem, and is this offer worth trying? |
| [2. Build](/en/roadmap/build) | Can we deliver the first useful version reliably and keep the work organised? |
| [3. Grow](/en/roadmap/grow) | Can we reach and serve more customers while controlling cost and cash? |
| [4. Scale](/en/roadmap/scale) | Can a larger team make good decisions and manage more complex work? |

For example, a founder may have registered a company and built an app but still not know why anyone would use it. The customer work in Validate is still relevant. Another founder may be serving paying customers manually, without an app at all. Build may be the right place to organise that delivery before accepting more orders.

## Turn the stage into this week's work

Open the stage you chose and look for the part that is currently blocking progress. Then choose one task you can finish or learn from this week. If delivery mistakes are losing customers, correcting the order process is more useful than preparing a funding deck simply because it appears later in the roadmap.

Write down what you will do, who will do it and when you will look at the result. Keep enough detail to recognise progress: “check why last week's deliveries failed” is a clearer task than “improve operations.” When you review it, decide whether the problem is resolved or whether the next task needs to change.

Some work cannot wait for a later stage. Before taking payments, hiring or carrying out a regulated activity, check the requirements that apply to it. The [legal roadmap](/en/legal-roadmap) helps you identify those questions alongside the commercial work.

If you already have a specific goal, such as setting up payments, a [guided journey](/en/journeys) gives you a more detailed sequence. To look up one subject without following a stage, use [Browse by Topic](/en/guides).
