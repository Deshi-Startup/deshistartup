# A Roadmap for Building Your Startup

> **In short:** To start a startup in Bangladesh, choose a customer problem, speak with the people facing it and test a small solution. Work out who would pay and what delivery would cost. Check the rules before the activity they cover. Then use the four stages below to choose your next job.

Starting a business brings several kinds of work at once. You might be talking to customers, arranging a supplier, deciding whether to hire and trying to understand registration requirements. A long checklist can make all of them seem equally urgent.

This roadmap helps you choose what deserves attention now. Each stage describes a different challenge and links to the guides that help with it. The stages are not a timetable or a requirement to raise investment. You can return to an earlier one when you introduce a new product or discover that an assumption was wrong.

## Your first steps in Bangladesh

Start with these questions if you are still choosing an idea. Keep your answers in a short note, and separate what you have seen from what you are guessing.

1. **Who has the problem?** Pick a group you can reach and one task they struggle with. The [startup ideas for Bangladesh](/en/startup-ideas) collection can give you starting points to investigate. An idea in a list is not proof of demand.
2. **What do they do now?** Ask about the last time the problem happened, how they handled it and what it cost in time or money. Use the [customer interview guide](/en/validation/customer-interviews) to prepare. Listen for evidence that could change your mind.
3. **What is the smallest useful test?** Try a simple demo or a small manual version before building the full product. Write down what result would lead you to continue, change the idea or stop. The [demand-test planner](/en/journeys/test-demand) helps you set this up.[^startup-playbook]
4. **How would the business earn money?** Name the customer, the person who pays, the price you want to test and the cost of delivering the offer. Use the [business model worksheet](/en/validation/business-model-design) to connect those choices.
5. **What needs checking before you act?** Before taking payments, handling personal data, hiring or testing a regulated service, use the [legal roadmap](/en/legal-roadmap) to find the relevant questions. Check the rules for your actual activity, even during a small test.
6. **What time and money can you commit?** Set a limit that leaves essential living costs covered. If the next step needs more money, use the [startup funding guide](/en/funding) to compare possible sources and their conditions.

Choose one unanswered question to work on this week. If you want a month-long test plan, use the [30-day startup roadmap](/en/start-here/30-day-roadmap). If you already have customers, start with the stage below that matches your current problem.

## Turn the stage into this week's work

Open the stage you chose and look for the part that is currently blocking progress. Then choose one task you can finish or learn from this week. If delivery mistakes are losing customers, correcting the order process is more useful than preparing a funding deck simply because it appears later in the roadmap.

Write down what you will do, who will do it and when you will look at the result. Keep enough detail to recognise progress: “check why last week's deliveries failed” is a clearer task than “improve operations.” When you review it, decide whether the problem is resolved or whether the next task needs to change.

Some work cannot wait for a later stage. Before taking payments, hiring or carrying out a regulated activity, check the requirements that apply to it. The [legal roadmap](/en/legal-roadmap) helps you identify those questions alongside the commercial work.

If you already have a specific goal, such as setting up payments, a [guided journey](/en/journeys) gives you a more detailed sequence. To look up one subject without following a stage, use [Browse by Topic](/en/guides).
