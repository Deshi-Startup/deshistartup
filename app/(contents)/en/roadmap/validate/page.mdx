# Phase 1 Roadmap – Idea, Validation and the First Formal Foundations

> **In short:** You do not need to build a large company in Phase 1. Drop weak assumptions quickly, talk to real customers, see whether a small test earns money or a meaningful commitment, and work out which documents and payment setup you need now.

Moving too fast at this stage increases the risk of building the wrong thing. First check whether the problem actually appears in people's daily lives. In Bangladesh, a small test might use a Facebook page, Messenger, Google Sheets, a simple landing page, a manually delivered service or a pilot with a few customers.

Finishing Phase 1 does not mean everything is settled. It means you should know whose problem you are solving, how people handle it today, whether anyone will spend money or time on your offer, and which documents and operating arrangements may come next.

## Start with a bounded test

Use [test demand](/en/journeys/test-demand) to practise interpreting evidence and download an experiment plan. Before running the activity, check [applicable permissions and risks](/en/legal-roadmap). Record whether a commitment is paid, unpaid, conditional or only an expression of interest; those are different results.

## What you are trying to prove

| Question | A useful signal |
|---|---|
| Is the problem real? | The same problem appears in independent accounts from different people |
| What do people do today? | They already spend time, money or effort on a workaround |
| Will anyone pay? | Someone pays a deposit, places an order or booking, or agrees to a paid pilot |
| Can you deliver on a small scale? | You can deliver the product or service manually if necessary |
| Do you understand the risks? | Payment, delivery, refund and legal risks are written down |

## Recommended order of work

1. Use [Idea Validation](/en/validation) to understand the problem, customer and existing alternatives.
2. Define one test with the [demand-test planner](/en/journeys/test-demand).
3. Use the [Legal Roadmap](/en/legal-roadmap) and official sources to check applicable permissions before running the activity, including <Term name="trade-license">trade license</Term>, <Term name="e-tin">TIN</Term> and VAT/<Term name="vat-bin">BIN</Term> questions.
4. Read [Finding Customers](/en/customers) to decide how to reach the intended participants.
5. If the test involves payments, prepare [Payments](/en/payments), delivery and refund records before accepting money.
6. Run the test, record actual behaviour and decide whether to continue, change, stop or collect more evidence.

## Common Phase 1 mistakes

| Mistake | Better response |
|---|---|
| Building the product before looking for customers | Look for evidence of the problem and willingness to pay first |
| Treating praise from family or friends as validation | Watch the behaviour of independent, realistic customers |
| Trying to complete every document at once | Do the legal work that applies at the current stage |
| Starting without payment and refund rules | Keep records from the first order |
| Stopping at “the market is large” | Write down who the earliest customers could be |

## What you should have at the end of Phase 1

- [ ] One sentence naming the customer and problem
- [ ] Enough customer-interview notes to see whether the problem repeats
- [ ] A map of current alternatives and competitors
- [ ] Results from a small MVP test
- [ ] Recorded the actual response, including no response, without treating an unpaid commitment as payment
- [ ] A simple plan for payment, delivery, refunds and support
- [ ] A list of upcoming legal and tax tasks

## Example: decide what the evidence earns

A fictional scheduling-service founder interviews four training-centre managers. Three describe missed class-change notifications; one says their calendar works well. Two agree to try a manual reminder workflow, but nobody has paid or completed the trial yet.

Her phase record reads: **Problem evidence:** specific reported incidents. **Alternative:** an existing calendar works for one manager. **Next commitment:** two trial agreements. **Unknown:** actual use, payment and repeat value. **Decision:** deliver the small agreed trial and review those unknowns; do not describe the idea as commercially validated.

Use the same distinctions in your own record. A prototype, interview count or certificate alone does not mean it is time to scale. The next phase begins with something concrete to deliver and learn from, with the relevant requirements checked.

## Choose one next action

Write the decision you need to make, the evidence you have, the missing information and one action with an owner and review date. Treat fundraising, expansion and hiring as choices to justify, not mandatory milestones.
