# Startup Opportunities in Bangladesh: 6 Sectors

> **In short:** LightCastle Partners' reports and investment guidance published by BIDA, the predecessor to Invest Bangladesh, keep listing the same six sectors as high-growth: fintech, agritech, edtech, healthtech, logistics, and e-commerce. Each carries different regulation, capital needs, and competitive intensity. Fintech regulation depends on the activity; over 230 million registered <Term name="mfs">MFS</Term> accounts do not mean that many unique customers or prove saturation;[^financial-express-mfs] edtech has two companies absorbing most of the funding;[^traxcn-edtech] healthtech has different rules for drugs, devices, clinical practice and facilities. Before jumping in because "the sector is growing," check which sub-sector your idea actually sits in, how crowded it already is, and how heavy the regulation runs.

By LightCastle Partners' count, Bangladesh now has more than 2,500 active startups, supporting roughly 1.5 million jobs directly and indirectly, and more than 450 investment deals since 2013 have brought in close to USD 1 billion.[^lightcastle-startup-ecosystem] Fintech, e-commerce, and mobility have shaped this market's visible face the most, with software, logistics, retail tech, agritech, and healthtech also gaining weight.[^lightcastle-startup-ecosystem]

But hearing "the sector is big" and knowing "my idea has room" are two different things. Below are the six sectors examined separately, each with real examples, the regulator involved, and a rough sense of capital needs.

## 1. Fintech: scale is not proof of saturation

Fintech covers mobile financial services (MFS, such as bKash, Nagad, Rocket), digital lending, insurtech, and payment gateways. Identify the regulated activity: banking/MFS/payment services involve Bangladesh Bank; insurance activities involve IDRA; microcredit may involve MRA. Technology does not merge these permissions.[^sector-regulators]

The scale is genuinely large. Total MFS accounts stood at 239.3 million in January 2025, up from 219.1 million in the same month of 2024. Total transactions that same month reached nearly Tk 1.72 trillion, a 32.56 percent rise year over year.[^financial-express-mfs] Nagad alone processed roughly Tk 44,639 crore in a single month, May 2026.[^business-standard-nagad] Registered accounts are not unique active people, and transaction value is not provider revenue. Neither figure alone measures unmet need or proves that a sub-sector is closed to entrants. From October 2025, work began to connect banks, MFS providers, and payment service providers onto one interoperable platform (NPSB), which may open new opportunities in payment-adjacent services rather than in core MFS itself.[^bb-npsb]

Possible hypotheses include bookkeeping, credit-assessment tools or distribution services. Test the specific buyer and regulatory boundary. Access to MFS transaction data requires an appropriate lawful arrangement; adjacent services are not automatically unregulated.

## 2. Agritech: a small market, but more open ground

Agritech means connecting farmers to markets or capital. Bangladesh currently has roughly 75 agritech startups, notably iFarmer, Fashol, and Dr.Chashi, but only 7 of them had raised funding as of May 2026.[^traxcn-agritech] Across the whole sector, the last decade has seen about 53 investment deals bringing in roughly USD 20 million total,[^exitshub-agritech] with a record USD 6.6 million raised in a single year, 2021.[^lightcastle-agritech] iFarmer now supports more than 100,000 farmers with collateral-free financing.[^exitshub-agritech]

LightCastle Partners' analysis shows most agritech funding has gone to two places: output-market linkage (50 percent) and farmer financing access (41 percent).[^lightcastle-agritech] That funding split does not count competitors or prove unmet demand. Investigate input supply, post-harvest processing and advisory directly before treating them as gaps. Regulation here is lighter than in fintech, though it can brush up against fintech rules once financing gets involved.

## 3. Edtech: funding concentration is not market share

Bangladesh now has 313 active edtech companies, and the sector has collectively raised around USD 17.6 million to date.[^traxcn-edtech] But most of that funding sits with two companies. 10 Minute School has raised USD 9.26 million to date, and Shikho roughly USD 8 million – together the two hold most of edtech's funding.[^traxcn-edtech] For a new entrant, this means competing directly with the two large players (general exam prep or curriculum video) is difficult, but there's still room in a narrow niche – skills training, job-prep content, or a specific age group.

## 4. Healthtech: regulated, and still growing

Healthtech covers telemedicine, e-pharmacy, and diagnostic booking. DGDA covers drugs and medical devices; clinical practice and telemedicine also involve BMDC rules, while DGHS handles private-facility licensing.[^health-regulators] A consultancy estimates 4–6 months for device registration; this is not a universal official service deadline.[^pure-global-dgda]

An older projection put the medical-device market at USD 820 million in 2025, up from USD 442 million in 2020; this is a forecast, not a verified 2025 outcome, and the private sector holds about 64 percent of the country's total health spending (trade.gov country report).[^trade-gov-healthcare] Bangladesh currently has 468 healthtech companies by one count, only 15 have raised funding, and just one has cleared a Series A.[^traxcn-healthtech] That means the market is still young – there's room here if you have the patience for the regulatory steps.

## 5. Logistics: crowded, and also volatile

A September 2022 report described 10 to 12 major e-commerce logistics players, including Pathao, REDX, eCourier, Paperfly, and Delivery Tiger at that time.[^future-startup-logistics] Money alone doesn't guarantee a sustainable business here – Paperfly is the sharp example. Founded in 2016 as Bangladesh's first homegrown tech-enabled logistics company, it raised significant investment and still shut down, for lack of a profitable model and financial discipline.[^linkedin-paperfly] REDX and eCourier remain active, working on national coverage and tech-driven tracking.

For a new entrant, building a direct national courier network is therefore a high-risk path – Paperfly's collapse shows exactly that. The more realistic opportunity may be hyperlocal last-mile delivery in a specific area, or software that helps existing couriers reconcile accounts and simplify tracking.

## 6. E-commerce: big numbers, but the definition shifts the picture

E-commerce market-size figures vary sharply by source. One estimate puts the 2026 market at roughly Tk 1.5 lakh crore (ResearchAndMarkets.com, cited by The Business Standard); another puts it at roughly USD 10.5 billion (Prothom Alo English).[^business-standard-ecommerce][^prothom-alo-ecommerce] e-CAB reports around 700 active e-commerce websites in the country,[^e-cab] and the US Commerce Department's country guide says roughly 50,000 Facebook pages also sell products, outside that website count; this does not mean no official dataset covers them.[^trade-gov-ecommerce] That gap is exactly why "how big is the market" doesn't have one answer – platform-based and Facebook-based e-commerce are two different realities.

Regulation is comparatively light here (mainly consumer protection and VAT), but competition is intense at both levels – large platforms and countless Facebook pages. Room for a new entrant tends to sit in a specific niche (one city, one product category) or in supporting software – bookkeeping and inventory tools for Facebook-page sellers – rather than in competing directly with Daraz or Chaldal.

## The six sectors at a glance

| Sector | Regulatory intensity | Capital needs | Room for a new entrant |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fintech | Activity-specific (BB, IDRA or MRA) | High | Low in core MFS, some in adjacent services |
| Agritech | Light to moderate | Moderate | Investigate input supply and advisory; funding data alone is insufficient |
| Edtech | Light | Moderate | Low in general subjects, more in narrow niches |
| Healthtech | Activity-specific (DGDA, BMDC or DGHS) | Moderate to high | Moderate, market still young |
| Logistics | Light | High (network) | Low at national scale, more in local/supporting software |
| E-commerce | Light | Low to moderate | Moderate, in niches or supporting tools |

The numbers in this table are rough comparisons, not precise measurements. Substitute your idea's specific sub-sector and verify for yourself.

## Checklist

- [ ] I've written down which of these six sectors my idea falls into
- [ ] I've identified that sector's regulator
- [ ] Rather than the sector's overall size, I've checked how many competitors exist in my specific sub-sector
- [ ] I've kept in mind how much of the market the top two or three players already control
- [ ] I've noted the source and year for every figure, rather than stopping at "the sector is big"

Rule of thumb: **however large the sector's numbers, your actual fight happens in a specific sub-sector. The sector's size is the first question; the sub-sector's competition is the real one.**
