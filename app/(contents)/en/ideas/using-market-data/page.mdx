# Understanding the Market with BBS, BTRC, World Bank, and DataReportal

> **In short:** Don't see "64 million users" on Facebook and assume that's the country's internet-user count. Facebook ad reach and social-media user identities are different measures; neither is a count of unique internet users. Read the label and methodology of the specific series. [The previous page](/en/ideas/market-data-sources) covers the gaps in BBS, BTRC, and Bangladesh Bank's official numbers. This one covers the other two sources — World Bank and DataReportal — and how to combine all four into a real estimate.

What actually happens when someone picks a number for a pitch deck's market-size slide? Often it's whatever comes up first in a Google search, without checking what the source is actually measuring. This page exists to help you avoid that.

---

## Four Sources, Four Different Jobs

| Source | Measures | Useful for |
|---|---|---|
| BBS, BTRC, Bangladesh Bank | Official government counts and registrations | The base number, for anchoring market size |
| World Bank | Globally comparable development indicators | Seeing where Bangladesh sits against peer or neighboring countries |
| DataReportal | A compilation of population, internet, connection and platform-audience estimates | Digital context; preserve each series’ own definition |

The first row is covered in detail on [where to find market data](/en/ideas/market-data-sources). The other two are below.

---

## What World Bank Tells You, and What It Doesn't

Two World Bank databases matter most. World Development Indicators (WDI) carries large economic figures — GDP, poverty, internet-related development indicators. The Global Findex database carries financial inclusion, bank account, and mobile money usage numbers; the 2025 edition shows 79% of adults globally now have some kind of account.[^wb-findex]

The real strength of these two databases is comparison, not one Bangladesh number in isolation. Global Findex data shows Bangladesh, Brazil, and Paraguay — outside Sub-Saharan Africa — all saw mobile money account growth between 2014 and 2021, an example of placing Bangladesh alongside peer economies.[^wb-findex-2021] But this data only refreshes every few years, monthly BTRC and Bangladesh Bank statistics can add recent activity context. Their connection or account counts do not replace a survey of unique adults.

## What DataReportal Is Actually Counting

DataReportal doesn't run its own survey. It combines Meta's, Google's, and TikTok's advertising planning tools with GSMA Intelligence and Ookla data, and publishes an annual "Digital: Bangladesh" report, analyzed by a firm called Kepios.[^datareportal-bd] Social-media user identities may not represent unique people. Where deduplication is limited, the methodology may rely on the largest platform; it does not always do so.[^datareportal-methodology] The same report also publishes population estimates, individual internet use and connection speeds, which are not advertising audiences.

The October 2025 DataReportal report puts Facebook's "users" in Bangladesh at 64.0 million, YouTube's at 49.8 million, and TikTok's (among users aged 18+) at 56.2 million.[^datareportal-bd] These three numbers look close but are actually measuring three different things — different age floors, different platform definitions, different counting methods. The report warns that advertising audience estimates should not be treated as monthly or daily active user counts or reconciled directly with census figures.[^datareportal-bd]

So if you use a DataReportal platform ad-reach number in a pitch deck or market research, write "X million potential ad reach (DataReportal, 2026)," not "X million users." The gap between those two phrasings looks small, but to a careful reader they're completely different claims.

## Two Population Numbers, Two Different Reasons

[The previous page](/en/ideas/market-data-sources) explains why BTRC's connection count exceeds the population. Here's a different gap: Bangladesh's 2022 census counted an adjusted population of 169,828,911 — an estimate adjusted after enumeration. Based on UN data, DataReportal put Bangladesh's population at 176 million as of October 2025 — a projection built from births, deaths, and migration between census years, not a direct count.[^datareportal-bd] Both are built with sound methods, but one is a census-based adjusted estimate and the other a later population estimate, so it's normal for them not to match. State clearly in your own report which one you're using.

## The Rule for Combining All Four

Use sources in this order when building a market estimate:

1. **Anchor the denominator with BBS or BTRC** – how many people, businesses, or connections, from a government count or registration
2. **Check current activity with BTRC or Bangladesh Bank** – what happened last month, since both refresh monthly
3. **Bring in comparative context from World Bank** – where Bangladesh sits against similar countries, useful context but not sufficient alone
4. **Take directional signals from DataReportal, with the caveat attached** – a sense of direction on online behavior, not a final number, and never use the figure without citing the methodology note

Keep these four steps in mind before copying a number straight out of any one source. How to write the actual inline citation is covered at [how to cite market data](/en/ideas/citing-market-data).

## Checklist

- [ ] The base measures the right unit: people, businesses, subscriptions or spending
- [ ] Platform advertising figures are labelled potential ad reach; other series retain their actual definitions
- [ ] World Bank figures are used for comparison, not as the sole source
- [ ] Every number carries its source, date, and what it actually measures
- [ ] When two sources' numbers don't match, I understand why (count vs. projection, ad reach vs. users)

Rule of thumb: **Before who made the number, read what the number is actually counting. Ad reach, connections, and people are three different things.**
