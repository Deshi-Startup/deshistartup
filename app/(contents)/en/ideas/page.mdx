# Ideas and Market Research

> **In short:** Use this section to choose a customer problem worth investigating. Leave with a specific problem, a reachable customer group and the most important thing you still need to learn.

## Work through a problem choice

A **customer problem** is an unwanted difficulty in a real task; your proposed product is one possible response. **Evidence** is what you observed or what someone actually reported. Your explanation of why it happened is an interpretation to test.

Consider a fictional founder investigating clothing shops in Sylhet. She has two ideas: an order dashboard and a promotional-post generator. She has heard one owner describe an unmatched courier settlement; she has only her own impression that shops need more posts.

Her completed starting note is:

```text
Customer: owners of clothing shops using two couriers.
Task: match delivered orders with settled money.
Current method: an order notebook plus two courier reports.
Evidence: one owner described an unmatched order from last week.
Unknown: frequency, consequence and whether the owner wants a change.
Next research: ask relevant owners to reconstruct their latest settlement.
Decision for now: investigate reconciliation first; park the post-generator idea.
```

She chooses reconciliation because she can investigate a concrete incident, not because one account proves a better business. If subsequent owners say the existing method is reliable, she records that and reconsiders. A scorecard should expose these reasons and unknowns rather than turn guesses into a precise ranking.

For your own shortlist, record the customer, recent event, alternative, consequence, source of evidence and next unanswered question for each idea. Choose the one where a feasible investigation can most improve your understanding. You are done when you can explain both the choice and what evidence might change it.

## Do one useful piece of research now

Write: “For **[specific people]**, **[task]** becomes difficult when **[situation]**. They currently use **[alternative]**. I have observed **[evidence]**; I still need to check **[unknown]**.”

For example, a **hypothetical** starting point is shops struggling to match courier settlements to orders. That is a research question, not proof of a market. Talk to people who handle those records before deciding to build software.

Separate what you saw, what someone told you and what you inferred. National population or internet-use figures cannot tell you how many customers you can actually reach or serve.

Once you have a problem to test, move to [idea validation](/en/validation). You do not need to finish every research guide first.
