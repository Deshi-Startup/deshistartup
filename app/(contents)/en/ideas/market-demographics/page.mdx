# Understanding Demographics, Internet Penetration, Mobile-First Behavior, Urban vs. Rural Dynamics

> **Key Takeaway:** 98.9% of households in Bangladesh have a mobile phone, and 72.4% have at least one smartphone. Yet only 48.9% of people actually use the internet themselves, fewer than one in two. That gap isn't an error; owning a device and being a customer are two different things. A phone sitting in the house doesn't mean someone will find your app, buy data, or make the spending decision alone. So before you size a market, pull these three numbers apart: who has a phone, who actually goes online, and who decides how the money gets spent.

## A Young Population, and an Address That Depends on Who's Counting

Bangladesh's median age was about 26 in late 2025: half the population was younger and half older.[^median-age] This is a median, not an arithmetic average. Age alone does not establish technology adoption, savings, purchasing power or brand loyalty; test those characteristics in the actual customer group.

Where people actually live depends on which source you ask. The 2022 Population and Housing Census reports 68.34% rural and 31.66% urban.[^census-2022] DataReportal, by contrast, estimated 42.2% urban and 57.8% rural as of October 2025.[^datareportal-bangladesh-2026] The sources refer to different years and may use different definitions and estimation methods. Do not attribute the entire gap to one cause without reconciling those details. Which one you should use depends on what "urban" means for your own launch plan — pull the number and write down its definition together.

Division-level population is useful for picking a launch city too: Dhaka division has the most people, Barishal the fewest.[^census-2022] The full breakdown and sources are on [Where to Find Bangladesh Market Data](/en/ideas/market-data-sources).

## Owning a Device Doesn't Mean You Have a Customer

This is where the real gap shows up. BBS's ICT usage survey (Q1 of FY2025-26, July-September 2025) found that 98.9% of households have at least one mobile phone, and 72.4% own a smartphone. The urban rate is 80.8%, the rural rate 68.8% — a gap of 12 percentage points in household smartphone ownership.[^ict-survey-2025-26]

But a device sitting in the house is not the same as one person actually using it. The same survey put individual internet use at just 48.9%, with men at 51.2% and women at 46.3%. Personal handset ownership shows the same split: 63.2% of men own their own phone, against 52.8% of women.[^ict-survey-2025-26] In other words, even where a household owns a smartphone, who actually holds it, opens the app, and places the order varies household by household. If you're building for women specifically, this gap needs to be part of your plan from the start.

DataReportal estimated individual internet use at 47.0% in October 2025,[^datareportal-bangladesh-2026] close to the survey's 48.9%. Similarity is not independent validation: data sources and methods may overlap. BTRC's June 2026 figure counts mobile and fixed internet subscriptions, not people. One person may have several mobile connections, and a fixed connection may serve a household or business. A connection inactive for the full 90-day reporting window is excluded. The figures also cover different periods, so dividing subscriptions by an older population count does not produce current individual penetration. See [Where to Find Bangladesh Market Data](/en/ideas/market-data-sources).

## Test the Product on Customers’ Phones

Computers fall outside this picture almost entirely. The same survey found computers in only 9.1% of households.[^ict-survey-2025-26] This argues for testing on customers’ phones, not against websites: a responsive website can work in a mobile browser. Discovery is a separate problem from the choice of app or website. Instead of building for desktop first and adapting for mobile later, you have to work in the other direction — whatever works on mobile first is the actual product.

Data cost enters the picture here too. Say your app pulls a few MB every time it opens, and your customer buys a 500 MB package to last the week. A heavy app or a large image burns through that package fast, which may make repeated use harder. This is one consideration when choosing whether to run on a Facebook page, Messenger, or WhatsApp rather than a standalone app. DataReportal reports potential advertising audiences of 64.0 million for Facebook and 35.0 million for Messenger. Those are not verified unique customers or comparable app usage counts.[^datareportal-bangladesh-2026] The playbook for validating demand through Facebook is on [Testing Demand in Facebook Groups](/en/validation/facebook-group-tests).

For customers with neither a smartphone nor internet access, a USSD menu on a basic phone still works. bKash built its agent network exactly this way, without requiring data or a smartphone. That model is covered on [Copied Ideas vs. Localized Ideas](/en/ideas/copy-vs-localize).

## These National Averages Aren't Your Specific Customer

Everything above is a national average. Where your actual customer sits within that average is something you still need to work out separately. For bookkeeping software, investigate the actual buyer's device, workflow and budget. For rural agri-input retailers, remember that 68.8% refers to households owning a smartphone, not individual retailers or farmers controlling one. Observe who uses the device and makes purchasing decisions rather than assigning those traits from a national average.

To bridge that gap, size your market by stepping down from the national number to your actual buyer's layer instead of jumping straight from one to the other — the method is on [How to Size a Market in Bangladesh](/en/ideas/market-sizing) and [Calculating TAM/SAM/SOM](/en/ideas/tam-sam-som). For how to separate multiple family members ordering from the same phone in your tracking, see [Cohort Retention](/en/metrics/cohort-retention).

## Common Mistakes

| Mistake | Result |
|---|---|
| Treating a smartphone-owning household as a customer directly | Whoever actually operates the phone gets left out of the count |
| Mixing urban/rural figures from different sources without noting the definition | The comparison stops being apples-to-apples |
| Treating BTRC's connection count as internet users | The real customer count comes out much smaller than the number used |
| Building desktop-first and adapting for mobile later | Most customers get a broken first experience |
| Assuming men and women have equal digital access | Reach for a women-focused product gets miscalculated |
| Applying a national average as your specific sector's number | Software or premium-product market estimates come out inflated |

## Checklist

- [ ] Separated device access from individual users
- [ ] Noted which source's definition the urban/rural number uses
- [ ] Didn't conflate SIM connections with people
- [ ] Thought through men's and women's access separately
- [ ] Drew my specific customer's picture apart from the national average
- [ ] Recorded the source and date for every number

Rule of thumb: **A phone in the house is not a customer. Who operates it, and who decides how the money gets spent, is the real question.**
