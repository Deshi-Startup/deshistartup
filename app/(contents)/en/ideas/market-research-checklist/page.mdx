# Market Research Checklist for Bangladesh

> **In short:** Market research isn't about reading a stack of reports – it's about having answers to five specific questions on hand. How big the market is, where the numbers came from, what your customer actually looks like, who the competitors are, and what you saw with your own eyes in the field. Leave any one of the five blank and it shows up in the first question from an investor or a pitch-deck reviewer. This page gives each question a short checklist and a link to go deeper – it doesn't manufacture the answers for you.

Once a problem is validated, or alongside that work, run this checklist before writing a business plan or sitting down with an investor. You won't have every answer on the first pass, and that's fine – note where a gap remains and add it to your to-do list. Reopen this same page before entering a new idea or a new city.

---

## 1. You've sized the market

- [ ] Ran a top-down calculation at least once, narrowing a large number step by step
- [ ] Ran a bottom-up calculation at least once, multiplying up from one real buyer
- [ ] Checked whether the two land close together, and if not, found out why
- [ ] Wrote TAM, SAM, and SOM down separately instead of collapsing them into one number

The method is on [How to Size a Market in Bangladesh](/en/ideas/market-sizing) and [Calculating TAM/SAM/SOM](/en/ideas/tam-sam-som).

## 2. You've noted the source, date, and definition behind every number

- [ ] Wrote the source name and year next to every number
- [ ] Checked exactly what the number counts – connections or people, registrations or active accounts
- [ ] Kept a company's own claims separate from independently reported figures
- [ ] Downloaded the source file and kept it, not just the link

Where to find the underlying data and how to cite it is on [Where to Find Bangladesh Market Data](/en/ideas/market-data-sources) and [How to Cite Bangladesh Market Data Cleanly](/en/ideas/citing-market-data).

## 3. You've drawn your specific customer apart from the national average

- [ ] Wrote down whether your customer skews urban or rural, and which source's definition that's based on
- [ ] Separated device access from the person actually using it themselves
- [ ] Noted who actually makes the spending decision – the customer themself, or someone else in the household

The full demographic picture and its sources are on [Understanding Demographics, Internet Penetration, Mobile-First Behavior, Urban vs. Rural Dynamics](/en/ideas/market-demographics).

## 4. You've mapped competitors – not just the app store

- [ ] Found and listed online competitors: apps, websites, Facebook pages
- [ ] Found and listed offline competitors: shops, agents, in-person service
- [ ] Included informal alternatives too – however people currently cope counts as a competitor
- [ ] Wrote down why people aren't fully using existing competitors yet, despite them existing

Say you're building a food delivery app. Your competitor isn't only other delivery apps – it's also the neighborhood tiffin service and a restaurant's own delivery boy. Neither shows up on a competitor spreadsheet, but the customer's money is already going there. The mapping template is on [Competitor Map](/en/ideas/competitor-map).

## 5. You've gone to the field and seen it yourself

- [ ] Visited at least one market, shop, or neighborhood in person, rather than relying only on what you read online
- [ ] Wrote down the reason for picking a launch city or area, rather than defaulting to "Dhaka first, obviously"
- [ ] Placed what you saw in the field next to the report numbers and checked whether they agree

The field research templates are on [Field Research Plan](/en/validation/field-research-plan) and [Field Notes Template](/en/validation/field-notes-template).

## What the Whole Picture Is Telling You

An empty group doesn't mean your market research has failed – it means it isn't finished yet. An empty group 1 means your market size is still a guess and won't hold up in front of an investor. An empty group 3 means a claim like "everyone is my customer" will break under the first question. An empty group 5 means the whole exercise was done at a desk, and field reality hasn't been checked yet.

Rule of thumb: **having numbers doesn't mean market research is done – ask for a source, a definition, and a piece of field evidence behind every number.**
