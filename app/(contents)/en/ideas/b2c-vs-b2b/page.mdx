# B2C vs B2B vs B2B2C in Bangladesh

> **In short:** B2C sells to consumers; B2B sells to businesses; B2B2C reaches consumers through a business relationship. For each, write down who uses the service, decides, pays and receives the money. Sales speed, margin, repeat orders and settlement timing depend on the actual offer and terms, not the label. In B2B2C, either layer may pay, and the consumer may know both brands.

## What actually differs across the three models

**Sales cycle and who decides.** In B2C, an ordinary buyer decides with their own money, and can go from seeing a Facebook post to placing an order within minutes. In B2B, the decision sits with a shop owner or a person responsible for purchasing, who checks the cost, negotiates, and may take weeks to decide. In B2B2C, you have to sell directly to the business itself – the end consumer may or may not know your brand, depending on the arrangement.

**Cash flow.** An instant customer payment is not always instant merchant cash. With <Term name="cod">cash on delivery</Term>, payment first depends on successful delivery and then on the courier’s settlement; gateways also have settlement terms. B2B may use advance payment, payment on delivery or credit. Write down the actual collection date, settlement delay, returns and working-capital need for either model.

**How trust gets built.** In B2C, trust comes from reviews, word of mouth, and brand recognition. In B2B, trust comes from relationships, references, and proven results – once broken, it's hard to win back.

## Real examples of the three models in Bangladesh

**B2C: Chaldal.** Chaldal sells groceries directly to consumers, delivering from its own warehouses, with cash on delivery available. A 2021 report noted the company handling 2.5 million orders a year and generating more than USD 40 million in revenue.[^techcrunch-chaldal] Here, each order is small, but there are many of them, and the customer decides for themselves inside the app.

**B2B: ShopUp (Mokam).** Bangladesh has roughly 4.5 million small neighborhood grocery shops, making up 98 percent of the retail sector, and these shops regularly struggle to procure goods from multiple wholesalers.[^future-startup-shopup] ShopUp's Mokam platform brings the supply chain – producer to wholesaler, wholesaler to shop – into one place, adding logistics (REDX) and a supply-on-credit financing product (Baki) on top.[^future-startup-shopup] Here, the customer is the shop owner – once trust is earned, large recurring orders follow, but building that first trust takes time and field staff.

**B2B2C: SSLCOMMERZ.** SSLCOMMERZ is a payment gateway that doesn't sell directly to the end consumer – instead, it's integrated into more than 14,000 businesses' websites and apps under contract.[^sslcommerz] When an end consumer pays by card on some online shop, SSLCOMMERZ's technology works behind the scenes, but the consumer never pays SSLCOMMERZ directly and may not even know its name. Here, the customer (who pays) is the businesses; the user (who the service actually works for) is those businesses' own consumers.

## Which model to choose, and when

| Question | Points toward B2C | Points toward B2B |
|---|---|---|
| What must you fund before collection | Stock, marketing, delivery and settlement delay | Sales effort, delivery and any agreed credit period |
| Where's your sales skill | Strong in marketing and content | Strong in relationship-building and negotiation |
| What kind of revenue suits you | Many small transactions | Fewer but larger, recurring orders |
| Can you absorb cash-flow risk | Model failed deliveries, returns and settlement | Model late invoices, defaults and settlement |

B2B2C is usually the hardest model to start with as a first business, because it requires understanding two layers of need at once – the paying business's, and their end consumer's. If you're heading toward a franchise or white-label model, this is a decision worth making upfront.

## Checklist

- [ ] I've decided who makes the decision in my business – the consumer directly, or a business owner
- [ ] I've recorded the actual payment and settlement terms, including delays and returns
- [ ] I've thought through how trust will be built – ads and reviews, or direct relationships
- [ ] I've checked whether my capital and patience can handle B2B's longer sales cycle
- [ ] If considering B2B2C, I've written down both layers of customer need separately

Rule of thumb: **Identify the buyer and user, then map who decides, who pays and when usable cash arrives. Check those assumptions with the actual customer and provider.**
