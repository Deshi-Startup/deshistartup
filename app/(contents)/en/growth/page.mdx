# Growth: Test Expansion Before Committing

> **In short:** Growth means serving more customers while keeping delivery, cash and customer outcomes workable. First identify what is limiting the business; expansion is only one possible response.

## Choose the next decision

- **Existing customers do not return:** investigate the experience using [cohort retention](/en/metrics/cohort-retention) before increasing acquisition spend.
- **Sales grow but cash becomes tighter:** examine [cash flow versus profit](/en/metrics/cashflow-vs-profit) and payment timing.
- **A new city looks promising:** use the [city launch playbook](/en/growth/city-launch-playbook) to design a bounded launch.
- **You are considering another country:** begin with [cross-border rules](/en/growth/cross-border-rules) and check the requirements for the specific activity and destination.

## Example: more sales can leave less room to operate

**Contribution per sale** is revenue minus the variable costs included in your calculation. It helps show what remains to cover fixed costs; it is not final profit. **Retention** asks whether customers return over a defined period. Compare the same event and time window, not orders placed in one area with orders delivered in another.

A fictional seller considers entering a second city. For simplicity, both areas have BDT 1,000 revenue per completed order. In the existing area, specified variable costs are BDT 700; in the test city they are BDT 850 because delivery and acquisition cost more.

- Existing contribution: BDT 1,000 − 700 = **BDT 300 per completed order**.
- New-city contribution: BDT 1,000 − 850 = **BDT 150 per completed order**.
- Twenty completed test orders contribute **BDT 3,000**, before fixed launch work and other omitted costs.

Suppose setup work for this bounded test costs BDT 5,000. The test has not covered that amount: 3,000 − 5,000 = **−BDT 2,000**, before other business costs. More orders alone do not justify expansion. Delayed collections could create an additional cash problem.

The founder records a decision to investigate the higher costs and delivery outcomes before extending the launch. The example does not establish a universal target; it shows which inputs change the decision. For your own test, list revenue, all relevant variable costs, setup spending, cash dates and a limit you can afford. Mark uncertain values and rerun the case if they worsen.

## Write an expansion test

Choose one customer group, area or channel. Record the expected demand, delivery capacity, spending limit, person responsible and review date. Decide in advance what would justify continuing and what would make you stop or revise the test.

Compare contribution per completed sale, complaints, repeat behaviour and cash collection with your existing operation. Extra orders alone cannot show whether the expansion works.

Your output is a decision memo: **what we tried / what happened / what it cost / what remains uncertain / next decision**. For the wider operating transition, use the [growth stage roadmap](/en/roadmap/grow).
