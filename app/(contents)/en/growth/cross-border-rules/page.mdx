# Cross-border Expansion: Export, Partner, Office or Subsidiary

> **In short:** Before you spend anything on a foreign company, decide *which* border you are actually crossing — selling abroad, appointing a partner, opening an office, or owning a company abroad. Bangladesh law treats those four as completely different things, and each needs its own eligibility and banking checks. The single most expensive mistake is leaving foreign earnings parked in a foreign bank, gateway or wallet: retention of export proceeds abroad is a contravention of the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947, not a grey area.[^bb-export-consolidated] Your next action is to fill the readiness scorecard below and count your months of repeat foreign revenue.

Say Sumaiya runs a twelve-person data-annotation studio in Sylhet. Two clients in London and one in Utrecht pay her about USD 24,000 a month between them, and the London client has just asked a reasonable question: *"Can we contract with a UK entity instead? Our procurement team dislikes overseas suppliers."*

Her first instinct is the one almost every founder has. Open a company in London — or Singapore, or Dubai, everyone says Dubai — move the client there, and keep the money in that account so it is easier to pay for tools and contractors.

The lawful route depends on the transaction and the permissions available. Incorporating abroad and retaining Bangladesh export earnings there are separate questions; check both before moving money.

---

## Who needs this, and when

Read the branch that matches your situation:

- **You have no foreign customer yet, only interest.** You do not need this guide, and you certainly do not need a foreign company. Win one paying foreign customer from Bangladesh first. Come back when the second one arrives.
- **You have one or two foreign customers paying into your Bangladeshi bank.** This guide is for you. The answer is usually "keep exporting, and fix your paperwork" — sections 3 and 4 are the ones that matter.
- **A foreign customer, marketplace or platform is refusing to contract with a Bangladeshi entity.** This guide is for you. Work through the entry-mode table and the readiness gates before you accept that the only fix is a company abroad.
- **You want to sell physical goods into a specific nearby market.** Start here for the decision, then go to [regional export](/en/growth/regional-export) for country-by-country execution, and to [import/export registration](/en/trade/import-export-registration) for the licence you will need.
- **A foreign investor has told you the round must close into a Singapore or Delaware entity.** That is a different decision with different mechanics, and it belongs to [FDI rules and the Singapore flip](/en/funding/fdi-rules). Come back here only if you also want an operating presence abroad.
- **You are an individual freelancer, not a company.** The export-proceeds rules below still apply to you; the company-formation sections do not.

What you will have at the end: a chosen entry mode for the next twelve months, the arithmetic showing how much foreign currency you may legally hold, a filled readiness scorecard, and the evidence pack your bank will ask for.

## Four ways to cross a border

"Going international" is four different transactions in Bangladeshi law. Founders lose money by picking the fourth when the first would have done.

| Entry mode | What it actually is | What Bangladesh asks of you | What it costs you |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Export from Bangladesh** | You invoice the foreign customer from your Bangladeshi company and they pay into your bank here | Declare the export, repatriate the proceeds through an authorised bank within the prescribed period[^bb-export-consolidated] | Bank charges, gateway fees, and the customer's discomfort with a foreign supplier |
| **2. Appoint a partner abroad** | A distributor, reseller or agent in the target country sells on your behalf for a margin or commission | The full invoice value is still declared and repatriated; the agent's commission is disclosed, not netted off quietly[^bb-export-consolidated] | Negotiated margin or commission; define customer ownership, support duties and termination terms |
| **3. Open an office abroad** | A representative, liaison or branch office that markets, supports and collects requirements — but is part of your Bangladeshi company | Fund it from your Exporters' Retention Quota account; no prior Bangladesh Bank approval is needed for establishing and maintaining offices abroad[^bb-export-consolidated] | Real foreign currency out of a pocket that refills slowly, plus the host country's own registration rules |
| **4. Own a company abroad** | A subsidiary registered in the foreign country, owned by your Bangladeshi company | Check the limited 2025 startup permission first; otherwise assess the applicable approval route, including the 2022 overseas-equity-investment rules[^bb-oei-rules] [^bb-oei-circular] | Application, due diligence and ongoing reporting; eligibility differs by route |

Choose the smallest arrangement that meets the actual need. Office funding and equity investment have different rules. A representative office remains part of the Bangladesh company and may not satisfy a customer that requires a separately incorporated local counterparty.

There is a fifth thing people confuse with all of these: putting a foreign holding company *above* your Bangladeshi company so a foreign fund can invest. That is not market entry, it is a financing structure, and it lives on the [FDI rules page](/en/funding/fdi-rules).

## Two rules decide most of this

### Rule 1: money earned abroad cannot stay abroad

This is the rule founders break first, usually by accident.

> **Rule:** Except through the specific notional and merchant accounts your bank operates for you, keeping export proceeds abroad in any form — a foreign bank account, a payment-platform balance you never withdraw, equity or physical assets bought with the earnings, or cryptocurrency — is a contravention of section 5(1)(e)(i) of the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947, and is subject to action under section 23(1) of that Act.[^bb-export-consolidated]

Two practical consequences:

- **A Stripe, Payoneer or Wise balance is not a savings account.** Proceeds have to come home through an authorised dealer bank — a commercial bank licensed by Bangladesh Bank to handle foreign exchange. Small-value service export proceeds may be repatriated through recognised online payment gateway service providers, subject to a ceiling of USD 10,000 per transaction, and the earnings may not be parked in the notional account abroad.[^bb-freelancer-circular]
- **There is a clock.** The prescribed period for repatriating export proceeds is four months, under a Bangladesh Bank notification that has been in force since 1977. Miss it without authorisation and the exporter is exposed to action under the Act.[^bb-export-consolidated]

The reason this matters for an expansion decision is simple: whatever route you choose has to end with the money legally arriving in Bangladesh. A structure that only works if the cash stays offshore is not a structure, it is an offence with a slide deck.

### Rule 2: outward investment needs an applicable permission

Outward capital movement needs an applicable permission. FEID Circular 02 of 27 March 2025 allows eligible startups to establish one legal entity abroad with investment up to USD 10,000 through an AD bank, subject to its conditions and reporting. Resident enterprises must have operated for fewer than ten years and use internal funds, not domestic bank borrowing or refinance. The circular also provides a route to submit share-swap proposals for Bangladesh Bank consideration. See the [FDI guide](/en/funding/fdi-rules) before using this route.[^bb-startup-outward]

What you *do* have, if you are already exporting, is an **Exporters' Retention Quota (ERQ) account** — a foreign currency account at your own bank, holding a fixed percentage of your repatriated export earnings, in US Dollars, Pounds, Euro, Yen or Yuan.[^bb-export-consolidated]

That account is the legal pocket for cross-border spending, and its rules draw the line between entry mode 3 and entry mode 4:

- **Allowed with no prior Bangladesh Bank approval:** business visits abroad, participation in export fairs and seminars, **establishment and maintenance of offices abroad**, import of raw materials, machinery and spares, and repayment of authorised foreign loans. For IT and software exporters, banks may also remit software registration, domain, hosting, server maintenance and account verification fees from this account.[^bb-export-consolidated]
- **ERQ equity investment:** prior approval is required under the *Capital Account Transaction (Overseas Equity Investment) Rules, 2022*.[^bb-export-consolidated]

Office expenses and equity investment are separate banking questions. Confirm the appropriate general permission or specific approval for the proposed transaction.

## How much foreign currency can you actually keep?

Your retention rate depends on what you export, and the rates moved twice in July 2026. These are the current limits:

| Exporter type | Retention limit | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Software, data entry/processing and other ICT-related services | **50%** of net repatriated export earnings | Raised from 35% in July 2026[^bb-freelancer-circular] |
| Merchandise with high domestic value added | **30%** of repatriated FOB value | Consolidated July 2026[^bb-export-consolidated] |
| Other service exporters (services in non-physical form) | **30%** of repatriated receipts | Consolidated July 2026[^bb-export-consolidated] |
| Merchandise with high import content (apparel from imported woven fabric, POL products, electronic goods) | **15%** of repatriated FOB value | Doubled from 7.5% in July 2026[^bb-erq-circular] |
| Resident agents and trade facilitators | **10%** of commission earnings | Conditional on valid permissions and active trade-association membership[^bb-export-consolidated] |

These percentages limit the share of repatriated receipts credited to ERQ; they are not a universal ceiling on every permitted overseas payment. Other authorised payment routes may apply. A software exporter may retain half of eligible net earnings; the high-import-content merchandise rate is 15%, not exactly one seventh. Confirm permitted use and available balance with the AD bank.

> **Warning:** These percentages are the most volatile numbers on this page. The 30 July 2026 consolidated circular states one year’s validity; later circulars may amend or replace it. Do not assume the next issue date in advance. Check the current circular before you budget — and do not rely on the 2018 *Guidelines for Foreign Exchange Transactions* chapter on retention quotas, which still circulates online but was repealed for export transactions by the consolidated circular.[^bb-export-consolidated]

### The arithmetic, on Sumaiya's numbers

Data annotation is a data-processing service, so her retention rate is 50%.

```
Monthly repatriated export earnings:              USD 24,000
ERQ retention at 50%:                             USD 12,000  (stays in foreign currency)
Converted to taka for payroll, rent, tax:         USD 12,000
Maximum ERQ build-up over 12 months:              USD 144,000  (if she spends none of it)
```

Now put an imagined London representative office against that pocket. Say it costs USD 3,500 a month all-in — a part-time local salesperson, a registered address, accounting. That is USD 42,000 a year, or **29% of everything she is allowed to hold in foreign currency**, spent on a market that currently produces two customers.

Use the ratio as one planning check. The one-third threshold below is an illustrative internal budget rule, not a regulatory limit or evidence that expansion will succeed. Also budget cloud bills, equipment and other demands on the same foreign-currency balance.

## The readiness gates

Score yourself before you choose a mode. Each gate is pass or fail; there are no half marks.

| # | Gate | Pass condition | Sumaiya |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Repeat revenue** | Two or more foreign customers have paid you more than once, unprompted | Pass — 3 customers, 14 months |
| 2 | **Pull, not push** | A named customer, platform or regulator is asking for local presence; you are not guessing | Pass — the London client asked |
| 3 | **Delivery holds** | You are meeting current foreign commitments without heroics | Pass |
| 4 | **Money comes home cleanly** | Every proceeds cycle repatriated within the prescribed period, no unresolved outstanding entries | Pass |
| 5 | **Foreign-currency headroom** | The annual foreign-currency cost of the move is under one third of your annual retention ceiling | Pass — 29% is below one third; still test other foreign-currency needs |
| 6 | **Someone owns it** | A named person will spend at least half their week on this market | Fail — nobody is free |

Five gates pass; gate 6 fails. Gates 1–3, 5 and 6 are commercial planning checks. Gate 4 concerns remittance compliance. Passing this scorecard is not legal permission, and lacking an accountable owner is enough to defer the office even with five passes.

**Rule of thumb:** treat a failed critical condition as a reason to pause or redesign the proposal. Do not use a total score to override legal compliance or the absence of someone responsible for delivery.

## If you really do need a foreign subsidiary

For a proposal outside the limited 2025 startup permission, one relevant route is the *Capital Account Transaction (Overseas Equity Investment) Rules, 2022*, made by the Financial Institutions Division under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act. The following limits concern that route, not every possible foreign startup entity.[^bb-oei-rules] [^bb-oei-circular]

**How much you may invest.** Subject to an adequate balance in your retention quota account, the proposed investment may not exceed the *lower* of 20% of the applicant company's average annual export earnings over the previous five years, or 25% of the net assets shown in its latest audited annual financial report. The selection committee may raise or lower that limit.[^bb-oei-rules]

Run that on Sumaiya's numbers — say USD 260,000 of average annual export earnings and USD 95,000 of net assets:

```
20% of average annual export earnings:   0.20 x 260,000  =  USD 52,000
25% of net assets:                       0.25 x  95,000  =  USD 23,750
Ceiling (lower of the two):                                 USD 23,750
```

Except she never gets that far. The eligibility conditions ask for five years of audited accounts, an active export record with a satisfactory retention-quota balance, no loan default, and a credit rating at or above grade 2 on Bangladesh Bank's risk-based capital adequacy mapping.[^bb-oei-rules] [^vdb-oei-digest] Her studio is three years old. This 2022 route is not available on those facts, but she should ask the AD whether the separate 2025 permission fits a smaller proposal; and saying so plainly is more useful than a checklist she cannot start.

**Who decides.** A selection committee chaired by the Governor of Bangladesh Bank, with members drawn from the Financial Institutions Division, predecessor BIDA, BSEC, the Export Promotion Bureau, the insurance regulator, RJSC, NBR, the Prime Minister's Office and several ministries. The 2022 rules still use the name BIDA; that agency became part of Invest Bangladesh on 23 August 2026, so confirm the current administrative routing with your authorised dealer bank. The committee may ask the relevant Bangladesh mission abroad for a due-diligence report on the proposed investment. Applications go through your bank to the General Manager, Foreign Exchange Investment Department.[^bb-oei-rules] [^bb-oei-circular]

**Conditions that outlive the approval.** The rules follow the money after it leaves:

- Investment funds must be sent directly to the subsidiary's own bank account — or, when buying shares in an existing company, directly to the seller.[^bb-oei-rules]
- Approved initial capital must be remitted within the time limit set in the approval.[^bb-oei-rules]
- If the investment does not go ahead for any reason, the money must be brought back to Bangladesh immediately, and the repatriation progress reported to Bangladesh Bank.[^bb-oei-rules]
- Profits, dividends, interest, share-sale proceeds and liquidation proceeds must be repatriated, and cannot be reinvested abroad without the committee's recommendation.[^bb-oei-rules]

Those are obligations on profits, dividends, interest and disposal proceeds under the approved investment route. They do not mean every dollar of the subsidiary’s gross customer revenue must bypass its legitimate operating costs and come straight to Bangladesh. Have advisers map each intercompany payment and the approval conditions.

## Choosing the country

Country choice is usually made emotionally — a cousin lives there, a conference happened there — and then defended commercially. Reverse the order. Answer these six questions in writing before naming a country:

1. **Where are your existing foreign customers?** Not your target market: your paying customers. Expansion follows demand you already have.
2. **Who buys, and can you reach them without a local entity?** If the buyer is an enterprise procurement team, a local entity may be genuinely required. If the buyer is a startup or a developer, it usually is not.
3. **What does the host country require of a foreign supplier?** Registration thresholds, local tax registration, VAT/GST on services, data-residency rules. This is host-country law and it does not appear in any Bangladeshi circular.
4. **Is there a double taxation agreement with Bangladesh?** NBR publishes the list; as of 14 August 2026 it names 42 partner jurisdictions, including Singapore, the UAE, Malaysia, India, Nepal, Bhutan, Sri Lanka, Saudi Arabia, the United Kingdom and the United States.[^nbr-dtaa] A treaty does not remove tax, but it decides which country taxes what and gives you relief from being taxed twice on the same income.
5. **Is the destination permitted?** The 2022 rules prioritise countries with bilateral investment agreements and double-taxation treaties with Bangladesh, and exclude destinations under international sanctions or without diplomatic relations.[^bb-oei-rules] [^vdb-oei-digest]
6. **How will you get paid, and how fast will it come home?** Add the contractual payment delay and bank processing time to the applicable repatriation clock. Three months of bank processing leaves little headroom within a four-month limit; it does not by itself exceed four months.

Singapore is the common answer for South and Southeast Asian venture-backed startups, and the Accelerating Bangladesh and Doerscircle workshop on Singapore entry is a useful practitioner walkthrough of what a corporate service provider actually does there.[^ab-singapore] But note what the choice is being made *for*. If the driver is an investor's term sheet, you are choosing a holding jurisdiction, and the mechanics, resident-director requirement and annual running costs are on the [FDI rules page](/en/funding/fdi-rules). If the driver is customers, choose the country your customers are in, even if it is Kathmandu.

## Worked example: what Sumaiya actually does

Her London client wants a UK counterparty. Working through this guide:

1. **Which border?** Ask what “UK entity” means to the client. A representative office is not a separate UK company. A UK reseller may meet the requirement; a Bangladesh contract only works if the client agrees.
2. **Gates.** Five of six pass. The 29% ratio passes her illustrative one-third rule, but no one owns the market, so she defers the office.
3. **Legal check.** The five-year accounts requirement blocks this 2022 application on the stated facts. Ask the AD whether a limited 2025 startup application is eligible and sufficient; do not assume all ownership routes are closed.
4. **The decision.** Propose continuing to invoice from Bangladesh, with agreed contract terms, an account manager on UK hours and customer references. Confirm acceptance; those measures cannot satisfy a firm local-incorporation requirement by themselves.
5. **The trigger she writes down.** “Review local presence when foreign revenue passes USD 40,000 a month and a named owner is available; choose an office only if it meets the client’s requirement and the bank confirms funding.” These are her hypothetical planning triggers, not eligibility rules.
6. **What she does this month instead.** Confirms her retention account is actually being credited at 50%, checks that all three clients' proceeds cleared within four months, and asks her bank in writing what the London office would cost to fund from the retention account when the time comes.

Step 6 is the one to copy. The cheapest expansion research available to you is a written question to your own bank's foreign-exchange desk, and it is free.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Consequence | Safe action |
|---|---|---|
| **Leaving earnings in a foreign gateway or bank account** | Contravention of section 5(1)(e)(i) of the FER Act, 1947, actionable under section 23(1)[^bb-export-consolidated] | Repatriate through your authorised dealer bank; treat foreign balances as in-transit, never as savings |
| **Remitting equity without establishing the applicable permission** | An unauthorised capital transaction can breach exchange-control rules | Check the 2025 startup permission or another applicable approval route with the AD before remitting |
| **Netting an agent's commission off the invoice** | The export declaration must carry the full invoice value without adjusting for foreign agent commission or trade charges[^bb-export-consolidated] | Declare the gross value; disclose commission separately, with tax deducted as applicable |
| **Budgeting from the old 2018 retention-quota chapter** | Rates are wrong in both directions; the chapter was repealed for export transactions[^bb-export-consolidated] | Budget from the current consolidated circular, and re-check every July |
| **Opening the entity before finding the second customer** | Fixed foreign-currency cost against unproven demand, in the pocket that is hardest to refill | Set a numeric trigger, then wait for it |
| **Assuming a company abroad reduces your Bangladesh tax** | Related-party pricing is scrutinised, and treaty relief is not exemption | Take professional tax advice in both countries before, not after, the structure exists |

Four of those six are decisions made in a hurry to please one customer. Slow that conversation down: the customer wants certainty of delivery, and there is usually a cheaper way to give it to them.

## Your evidence pack

Whichever mode you choose, your bank, and later any regulator, will ask for the same things. Assemble them before you apply for anything.

- [ ] Company documents: <Term name="incorp-certificate">certificate of incorporation</Term>, memorandum and articles, and current [trade licence](/en/trade-license)
- [ ] Export registration where goods are involved — the [IRC/ERC](/en/trade/irc-erc-steps) certificate
- [ ] Twelve months of foreign invoices with the matching inward remittance advices
- [ ] Proceeds realisation certificates, showing each cycle repatriated within the prescribed period
- [ ] Retention quota account statement, showing the account exists and is being credited at the correct rate
- [ ] Audited financial statements for every year you have them
- [ ] The customer or platform's written statement of what it requires from a local entity
- [ ] A one-page memo: which mode, why now, the numeric trigger, and the named owner
- [ ] For an equity application: the documents for the applicable route, including rule 5 when using the 2022 rules, submitted through your bank[^bb-oei-rules]

**Done looks like this:** you can name your entry mode in one sentence, state the two numbers that would change it, and show a banker a retention account statement plus a clean repatriation record without opening a drawer.

## What to do next

- If the answer was "keep exporting": tighten the paperwork with [import/export registration](/en/trade/import-export-registration) and [trade documents](/en/trade/trade-documents), and make sure proceeds come home on time via [export-proceeds repatriation](/en/trade/export-proceeds).
- If the answer was a partner in a neighbouring market: [regional export](/en/growth/regional-export).
- If the money side is the hard part: [foreign exchange basics](/en/trade/foreign-exchange).
- If a foreign investor is driving this: [FDI rules and the Singapore flip](/en/funding/fdi-rules).
- Whatever the answer: write the trigger and the owner into your next board or team update, with the date you will re-check it.
