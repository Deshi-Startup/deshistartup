# Cashflow vs Profit: A Simple Explanation for Founders

> **In short:** Profit tells you what remains after costs. Cash flow tells you when money actually arrives and leaves. A profitable project can still leave a bill unpaid. Try the simulation below: keep profit fixed at BDT 10,000, move the payment dates, and find the first cash shortfall.

Use this when sales look healthy but you are unsure whether upcoming bills can be paid. [Jump to the cash timing simulation](#try-it-change-the-dates-keep-the-profit) to practise, then use the calendar below for your own dates.

## Why profit and cash flow aren't the same thing

In the examples on this page, **profit** means earned revenue minus the costs of earning it. **Cash flow** tracks receipts and payments when the money moves. Opening cash is not new revenue, and receiving money owed from an earlier sale does not mean earning that revenue a second time.

Do not automatically count an order or a dispatched parcel as earned revenue. What has actually been delivered under the agreement matters. For the simplified examples below, assume the goods or service have been delivered and the stated revenue earned. Payment may still arrive later.

That leaves two separate questions: did this work earn more than it cost, and will cash be available when each bill is due?

## The first gap: COD orders and courier delay

**Fictional example:** Nasrin runs a cosmetics page in Sylhet. Thirty <Term name="cod">cash-on-delivery (COD)</Term> orders worth BDT 45,000 are delivered on Monday. Assume that is earned revenue and the courier remits the full amount the following Monday. To isolate timing, this example leaves out courier fees, returns, and taxes. Actual settlement dates and deductions depend on the service and transactions.

| Day | On the ledger | In the bank |
|---|---|---|
| Monday, orders delivered | 45,000 taka | 0 taka |
| The following Monday, courier pays out | 45,000 taka | 45,000 taka |

Now suppose a BDT 20,000 supplier bill falls due that Wednesday and Nasrin has no other available cash. The receivable cannot pay that bill until the courier transfers it. Revenue alone does not tell us her profit, because we have not listed all her costs. It does show why a completed sale and available cash are different things.

## The second gap: B2B receivables

A **receivable** is money a customer owes you. For a fictional business-to-business sale with 30-day payment terms, the supplier may have to cover costs before the customer pays. The agreed term and actual payment date need separate attention. Credit is also part of Bangladesh's retail supply chain: Future Startup's September 2021 account described Baki as ShopUp's embedded finance product within Mokam. That is historical context, not a statement of today's eligibility or terms.[^shopup-baki]

An invoice is not cash in the bank. If a customer pays late, a calendar based only on the promised date will be too optimistic. Keep the expected date visible and check what happens if it slips.

## Put Nasrin's gap on a calendar

Using the first fictional example, start with no available cash. This is the **unfunded projection**, so the negative balance flags a payment that cannot actually be made without a change.

| Date | Cash in | Cash out due | Projected balance |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Monday: orders delivered, courier owes money | 0 | 0 | BDT 0 |
| Wednesday: supplier bill due | 0 | BDT 20,000 | **−BDT 20,000** |
| Following Monday: expected courier remittance | BDT 45,000 | 0 | BDT 25,000 |

Ending at BDT 25,000 does not make Wednesday affordable. Before Wednesday, Nasrin must confirm an earlier receipt, agree a later supplier payment, find an affordable source of cash or change the commitment. Each option needs an agreed date and terms; editing a spreadsheet does not move money.

She marks the remittance **expected, not received**, and records who will confirm it. If the supplier agrees to payment after the transfer arrives, she updates the calendar and keeps the agreement. The example excludes other costs as stated above; her real calendar must include them.

## Make a cash calendar for your own business

Copy this small worksheet into a notebook or spreadsheet. Start with money you can actually use, then add expected receipts and bills in date order. You do not need to enter personal financial information on this site.

```text
Opening available cash: BDT ______

Date | Receipt and expected clearing date | Payment due | Projected balance
____ | _________________________________ | ___________ | _________________
____ | _________________________________ | ___________ | _________________
____ | _________________________________ | ___________ | _________________

Each balance = previous balance + receipts - payments
First date with uncovered payments:
Largest shortfall:
Receipt that could be delayed:
What happens if it is delayed:
Action to discuss, with whom, and by when:
Next review date:
```

Treat an uncertain receipt as uncertain. Make a second version with it delayed, and check the first shortfall in both versions. Include every relevant bill, not just the costs in the teaching example. A daily total can also hide a payment due in the morning before a receipt clears that afternoon.

You are done when you can name the first date you may be short, the amount, and one action to discuss before that date. A positive closing balance alone is not enough.

## Build one lasting habit

Review the calendar on the same day each week and whenever a material receipt or bill changes. Replace expected receipts with actual receipts, and check upcoming due dates against available cash. For a broader weekly review, use the [Monthly Founder Dashboard](/en/metrics/founder-dashboard).

Keep in mind, how much profit one order or one customer actually leaves you is a separate question, covered in [Unit Economics](/en/metrics/unit-economics). This page asks whether receipts arrive in time to cover payments.

## Checklist

- Write down the cash in the bank on the same day every week
- Separate COD orders, completed deliveries, and actual courier settlements
- For B2B credit sales, know the credit term and when the cash actually lands
- Match supplier, rent, and salary payment dates against when cash actually arrives
- Check the first shortfall as well as the ending balance
- Recalculate with an important receipt delayed

Rule of thumb: **Whatever the ledger says about profit, what matters before a bill is due is how much cash is actually in the bank.**
