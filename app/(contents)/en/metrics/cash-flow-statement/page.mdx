# Cash Flow Statement: Where Did Last Month's Cash Go?

> **In short:** Pick a completed month. Start with the cash your business had on day one, list money that actually came in and went out, then check whether the result matches your month-end balances. Keep customer payments, equipment purchases and loans in separate groups. A higher closing balance does not always mean sales paid for the increase.[^ias-seven]

This guide is for a founder who needs to explain **what happened to cash last month**. You will make a small statement and find any missing amount. If you need to plan whether next week's bills can be paid, use a [cash calendar](/en/metrics/cashflow-vs-profit) after this. If you need to model future sales and runway, use [financial projections](/en/metrics/financial-projections).

This is a **management worksheet**, not complete accounts ready to file or give an investor. IAS 7 is the accounting standard for statements of cash flows. It also has rules for **cash equivalents**: short-term, low-risk holdings that can quickly turn into a known amount of cash. It covers foreign currency and disclosures too. Ask an accountant which reporting rules apply to your business and have any formal statement reviewed.[^ias-seven]

## What goes on the statement?

A cash-flow statement shows cash movements during a **past period**. Use the same start and end dates for every line. For this simple worksheet, list actual money in and out of the business cash box and bank accounts you have chosen to include. Write down those accounts before you start. Do not mix a business balance with a founder's personal money.

IAS 7 groups cash flows by what caused them:[^ias-seven]

| Group | Plain meaning | Examples for a startup |
|---|---|---|
| Operating | Cash from the everyday work | Customer payments received; suppliers, salaries and rent paid |
| Investing | Cash used to buy or sell long-term assets | Buying a work laptop or selling old equipment |
| Financing | Cash raised from or returned to lenders and owners | A loan received or loan principal repaid |

We use the **direct method** for the operating group: show the main cash receipts and payments, rather than starting with accounting profit. The three groups still need to add up to the change in the cash balances you included.[^ias-seven]

An unpaid invoice is money owed, not cash received. Depreciation spreads an asset's cost over time on the books; it is not a payment today. Keep both out of this direct cash-movement list. If you move money between two business accounts that are **both** in your opening and closing balances, do not record the transfer as a new receipt and payment. That would inflate both totals even though your cash did not change.[^ias-seven-text]

## Work through one month

**Fictional example:** A Dhaka startup closes August. It starts with **BDT 60,000** across the business balances it chose for this worksheet. Every amount below was actually received or paid during August. It also issued a BDT 30,000 invoice that remains unpaid; that invoice is **not** in the cash table. To keep the example small, it has no other payments, interest, tax or foreign-currency balance. Loan **principal** below means the amount borrowed, apart from interest.

| Group | What happened in August | Cash in | Cash out |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| Operating | Customers paid | BDT 1,80,000 | — |
| Operating | Suppliers paid | — | BDT 70,000 |
| Operating | Salaries paid | — | BDT 60,000 |
| Operating | Rent and internet paid | — | BDT 20,000 |
| Investing | Work laptop bought | — | BDT 25,000 |
| Financing | New loan received | BDT 40,000 | — |
| Financing | Older loan principal repaid | — | BDT 10,000 |

Now add each group. **Operating:** 1,80,000 − 70,000 − 60,000 − 20,000 = **BDT 30,000 in**. **Investing:** BDT 25,000 out. **Financing:** 40,000 − 10,000 = **BDT 30,000 in**. So the month's net change is **30,000 − 25,000 + 30,000 = BDT 35,000 in**.

Opening cash **BDT 60,000 + net change BDT 35,000 = calculated closing cash BDT 95,000**. The startup checks the same bank and cash balances on the last day of August. They total BDT 95,000, so the **difference is zero**. Total receipts of BDT 2,20,000 minus total payments of BDT 1,85,000 also gives BDT 35,000.

The new loan brought in BDT 40,000. Without that receipt, and with every other amount unchanged, the net change would be **BDT 5,000 out**, even though operating cash was positive. The closing balance alone hides that story. It also does not prove the business made a profit; [profit and cash answer different questions](/en/metrics/cashflow-vs-profit).

## Make your own copy

Gather the month's bank statements, cash-book entries and payment-provider reports. Match each actual receipt or payment to its record. A provider may deduct charges before paying your bank, so check its report against the bank credit; do not guess a gross amount or count the same fee twice. Ask your accountant how to present complex settlements in formal accounts.

Copy this into a notebook or spreadsheet. Put **positive amounts** in the “in” and “out” columns, then subtract the out column. Leave a group at zero if nothing happened there.

```text
Completed period: ______________ to ______________
Business cash/bank accounts included: ______________
Opening balance across those same accounts: BDT __________

OPERATING                         Cash in       Cash out
Customer payments received        _______       —
Suppliers paid                    —             _______
Salaries paid                     —             _______
Rent, utilities and other         —             _______
Other actual operating cash       _______       _______
Operating net = in − out: BDT __________

INVESTING                         Cash in       Cash out
Equipment and other long-term assets
                                  _______       _______
Investing net = in − out: BDT __________

FINANCING                         Cash in       Cash out
Loans received / principal repaid _______       _______
Owner funding / money returned    _______       _______
Financing net = in − out: BDT __________

Net change = operating + investing + financing: BDT __________
Calculated closing = opening + net change: BDT __________
Actual closing across the SAME accounts: BDT __________
Difference = actual closing − calculated closing: BDT __________
Unpaid invoices and bills to follow up separately: __________
```

Do not use an unexplained “other” line to make the difference disappear. Check for a missing bank fee, cash payment, wrong date, duplicated transfer or an account you left out. If the opening and closing account lists differ, fix that first. For foreign-currency balances, cash equivalents, taxes, interest, mixed-use accounts or financing with no cash payment, get an accountant to check the treatment; this simple sheet does not settle those questions.[^ias-seven]

## Read the result, then take one action

If operating cash is negative, find which receipts were late or which payments rose. If a loan or founder payment kept the closing balance up, show that separately from customer cash. If the difference is not zero, reconcile the records before drawing a conclusion.

Keep the dated worksheet with its bank and provider records. Then put known upcoming receipts and bills on the [cash calendar](/en/metrics/cashflow-vs-profit). The statement tells you **what did happen**; the calendar asks **whether you can pay on time next**. A monthly [founder dashboard](/en/metrics/founder-dashboard) can carry the closing cash and operating cash result once you trust them.

## Checklist

- [ ] I used one completed period and the same cash/bank accounts at both ends.
- [ ] I recorded actual receipts and payments, not unpaid invoices or non-cash charges.
- [ ] I separated operating, investing and financing cash.
- [ ] Opening cash plus the three net groups equals calculated closing cash.
- [ ] Calculated closing cash matches the actual closing balances, or I am tracing the difference.
- [ ] I know whether daily work, borrowing or owner funding drove the change.
- [ ] An accountant will check any formal statement or complex classification.
