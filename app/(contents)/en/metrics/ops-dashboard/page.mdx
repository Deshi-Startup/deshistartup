# Manual Operations Dashboard for Early Teams

> **In short:** Once a team grows to 2 to 5 people, no single head can hold every number anymore. Keep one Google Sheet that logs orders, returns (RTO), pending deliveries, support tickets, stock, and staff attendance, daily or at least weekly. This isn't the monthly financial review, it's a picture of whether day-to-day operations are running smoothly. The monthly money picture lives in the [Monthly Founder Dashboard](/en/metrics/founder-dashboard); this sheet is what feeds it.

## The sheet's structure

Open a fresh Google Sheet and set up these columns. One new row per day, one summary row at the end of the week.

| Column | What goes in |
|---|---|
| Day/date | Which day this row covers |
| Orders in | New orders received that day |
| Delivered | Orders that actually reached the customer that day |
| RTO/returns | Parcels the courier brought back (the full mechanics of RTO are in [E-commerce Metrics](/en/metrics/e-commerce)) |
| Pending deliveries | Orders still in hand, not yet sent out, at day's end |
| Support tickets: new | New complaints or questions from customers, by phone or Messenger |
| Support tickets: closed | Tickets resolved that day |
| Open backlog | Total tickets still hanging, previous day's leftover carried forward |
| Staff present | How many showed up, out of how many total |
| Low-stock items | Which products are running out, name them |

If the business doesn't touch physical goods or delivery, drop the RTO and stock columns; the rest works for almost any small team.

## One week: Tanvir's sheet

Imagine Tanvir, running a small grocery and household-goods delivery business out of Bogura, taking orders over a Facebook page and WhatsApp and dropping them off door to door. For the first six months he ran everything alone. As orders grew he brought on three people: one handles delivery, one handles packaging and stock, one talks to customers by phone and Messenger. Once the team grew, Tanvir realised that checking the bank balance at month's end wasn't enough anymore, because it couldn't show him where trouble was quietly building up during the month. So he set up a sheet with the structure above. One week looked like this:

| Day | Orders | Delivered | RTO | Pending | Tickets (new) | Tickets (closed) | Backlog | Staff | Low stock |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sun | 45 | 40 | 2 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 0 | 3/3 | 2 items |
| Mon | 50 | 44 | 3 | 3 | 6 | 5 | 1 | 3/3 | 2 items |
| Tue | 48 | 38 | 8 | 2 | 9 | 6 | 4 | 2/3 | 3 items |
| Wed | 46 | 39 | 7 | 4 | 8 | 5 | 7 | 3/3 | 3 items |
| Thu | 47 | 41 | 5 | 3 | 7 | 4 | 10 | 3/3 | 4 items |
| Fri | 25 | 20 | 2 | 6 | 3 | 3 | 10 | 2/3 | 4 items |
| Sat | 44 | 45 | 3 | 5 | 5 | 9 | 6 | 3/3 | 5 items |

Looking at the week's rows together on Saturday night, three things stood out to Tanvir.

First, RTO jumped to 8 on Tuesday, against 2 to 3 on every other day. Only two of three staff were in that day; one was out sick. Digging in, Tanvir found that with fewer hands, the confirmation call to customers before dispatch had been skipped. The orders sent out without a call were the ones that mostly came back. The fix wasn't complicated: on a short-staffed day, take fewer orders, or don't send anything out that hasn't been confirmed by phone, and write that rule down so it sticks.

Second, the open backlog climbed from 1 on Monday to 10 by Thursday, because new tickets kept outnumbering closed ones every single day. Looked at one day at a time, none of it looked alarming. It was the backlog column that showed one support person could no longer keep up. Saturday's push to close 9 tickets brought it down to 6, but it took the whole week to even notice the problem.

Third, Friday's shorter working day pushed pending deliveries up to 6, which had to be absorbed the next day. And low-stock items climbed steadily from 2 to 5 across the week, meaning a restock order should have gone out well before Saturday.

## Why this sheet, and not the founder dashboard

The [Monthly Founder Dashboard](/en/metrics/founder-dashboard) is for sitting down once a month to see the whole business's financial picture, and it works fine even when a solo founder is doing everything. This sheet is different: it watches whether day-to-day operations are on track, daily or weekly. When it's time to close the month's books, these weekly numbers are exactly what feeds in. RTO adds to cost, and pending orders and backlog show whether customers are being kept happy. One team, two sheets, two jobs.

## Who updates it, and when

Solo, a founder took every order, made every call, and held the whole picture in their own head. That habit stops working once a team exists, because different people now own different parts of the work. So keep the rule simple: whoever does a job fills in their own column at day's end. The delivery person logs their own orders and RTO, the support person logs their own tickets. The founder doesn't need to enter numbers daily; once a week, Saturday or Sunday, they look at the whole sheet and hunt for patterns: spikes, and backlog that keeps growing. Five minutes a day to log, fifteen minutes once a week to review; this sheet shouldn't cost more time than that.

## Checklist

- Set up a Google Sheet with daily rows for orders, deliveries, RTO, pending, support tickets, staff, and stock
- Whoever does the work fills in their own column at day's end
- Reviewed the whole week's sheet together at week's end, looking for spikes and growing backlog
- Investigated and acted within a day whenever RTO or backlog jumped
- Rolled the week's summary into the [Monthly Founder Dashboard](/en/metrics/founder-dashboard)

Rule of thumb: **whatever isn't measured daily can go wrong every day without anyone noticing, until the bank account feels it at month's end.**
