# Metrics: Numbers That Help You Decide

> **In short:** Start with a business question, then choose the measure that can change your decision. A dashboard is useful when someone knows what to do with the result.

## Work through one weekly record

Define the event before calculating a rate. An **order placed** may be cancelled or returned. A **completed sale** is not necessarily a cash receipt. **Contribution** is revenue less the variable costs explicitly included; **profit** also depends on other costs. **Retention** measures whether a defined group returns over a defined period.

Suppose a fictional shop reviews one week's 50 orders after their delivery outcomes are known. Forty were delivered and kept; ten were cancelled or returned. Delivered sales earned BDT 40,000, and total specified variable costs for the whole batch—including failed-order costs—were BDT 28,000.

- Delivery completion rate: 40 ÷ 50 × 100 = **80%**.
- Contribution for the batch: 40,000 − 28,000 = **BDT 12,000** before fixed costs.
- Contribution per completed sale: 12,000 ÷ 40 = **BDT 300**.

If only BDT 25,000 of those sales has been collected, the remaining BDT 15,000 is not available cash. It is a separate collection question, not a reason to change the completion-rate denominator.

The founder records one next action: inspect the ten failed orders by reason before increasing acquisition spend. A different business may need another measure. The useful part is the chain from definition to calculation to action—not the example's 80% as a target.

Your first metric record can be: **question / definition / period / numerator / denominator / data source / result / owner / decision**. Include unresolved or still-pending outcomes instead of silently counting them as successes or failures.

## Make one number trustworthy

Write its definition, time period, source and owner. For a rate, record both the numerator and denominator. Separate orders placed from orders delivered, and invoices issued from cash collected.

Then add: “If this changes, we will investigate **[cause]** or decide **[action]**.” If no decision follows, the number may not belong in your regular review.

Compare like with like. A small test, a forecast and an established operating result are different kinds of evidence. None is a universal benchmark for a Bangladesh startup.
