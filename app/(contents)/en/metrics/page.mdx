# Metrics: Numbers That Help You Decide

> **In short:** Start with a business question, then choose the measure that can change your decision. A dashboard is useful when someone knows what to do with the result.

| Your question | Start here | Useful output |
|---|---|---|
| Can we pay bills on time? | [Cash flow versus profit](/en/metrics/cashflow-vs-profit) | A cash-timing scenario and the gap to investigate |
| Does each sale help cover costs? | [Unit economics](/en/metrics/unit-economics) | Contribution per completed sale with costs stated |
| Do customers return? | [Cohort retention](/en/metrics/cohort-retention) | Repeat behaviour for comparable customer groups |
| What should we review weekly? | [Founder dashboard](/en/metrics/founder-dashboard) | A short set of measures with owners |
| What might happen under different assumptions? | [Financial projections](/en/metrics/financial-projections) | A forecast with explicit assumptions |
| These terms are new to me | [Metrics basics](/en/metrics/metrics-101) | A working vocabulary for the numbers |

## Make one number trustworthy

Write its definition, time period, source and owner. For a rate, record both the numerator and denominator. Separate orders placed from orders delivered, and invoices issued from cash collected.

Then add: “If this changes, we will investigate **[cause]** or decide **[action]**.” If no decision follows, the number may not belong in your regular review.

Compare like with like. A small test, a forecast and an established operating result are different kinds of evidence. None is a universal benchmark for a Bangladesh startup.
