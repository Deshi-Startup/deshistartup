# Startup Metrics 101

> **In short:** More followers, likes, or reach do not keep a business alive. What does: cash actually building up in the bank, real profit per order or customer (contribution margin), and how long customers stick around, that's retention. Sort metrics into three buckets, money, growth, and operations, instead of tracking everything at once. This page does not go deep into any single formula; it is a map that points you to the right guide in the Metrics section based on your stage and business type.

## The vanity metrics trap

Imagine Rahim, running a Facebook clothing page in Chittagong; the full unit-economics maths behind his numbers is [here](/en/metrics/unit-economics). Over a year his page grew from 5,000 to 50,000 followers. Some posts reached hundreds of thousands of people, and comments kept rolling in. Friends kept telling him the business looked like it was booming. Yet his bank balance quietly shrank every month, because boost spend grew faster than sales.

This trap has a name: **vanity metrics**, numbers that feel good and look impressive climbing on a screen, but say nothing about whether the business survives. Followers, likes, page reach, even raw website visits, all belong to this group when they stand alone.

The trap runs wider in Bangladesh than in most places. By late 2025 the country had roughly 64 million Facebook users, about 36 percent of the population.[^datareportal-bangladesh-2026] With almost everyone on the platform, a growing page feels like a growing business. The real question is different: what is actually left in Rahim's hand?

The answer lives in two places. First, how much cash is actually building up in the bank. Second, what one order truly leaves after every cost, the contribution margin. For Rahim that number is only 160 taka, less once returned orders are counted, and none of it shows up in a follower count or a reach graph.

So the job this week is simple: close the page insights tab, and open the bank statement and the real maths on your last few orders instead. The number you find may not feel as good, but it's the one that's real.

## The three-bucket model

Instead of drowning in every possible number, sort them into three buckets:

- **Money metrics:** cash in hand, real profit per order or customer (contribution margin), whether the month closes in profit.
- **Growth metrics:** cost to win a new customer (CAC), what one customer is worth across the whole relationship (LTV), how long customers stick around (retention), and how many leave (churn).
- **Operational metrics:** how many orders got processed, whether delivery landed on time, how fast support complaints got resolved.

All three matter, but not in the same order. Get the money metrics wrong and growth metrics only help you sink faster. Solid operations make the other two easier to trust.

## Checklist

- This week, ignored followers, likes, and reach, and checked the bank balance and profit per order instead
- Identified which of the three buckets, money, growth, or operations, is the urgent one right now
- Opened the right page for the current stage: idea, first sales, or scaling
- Picked the right deep-dive guide for the business type: sell-once, subscription, or marketplace

Rule of thumb: **question the number that feels good. Trust the number that builds up in the bank.**
