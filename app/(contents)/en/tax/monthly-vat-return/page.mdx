# The Monthly VAT Return After Getting a BIN

> **In short:** Once the BIN arrives, VAT stops being annual work and becomes monthly work. The return for each month is due within 15 days of that month ending.[^vat-return-deadline] The arithmetic is straightforward: output tax for the period, less the input tax credits you are entitled to claim, plus increasing adjustments, minus decreasing ones.[^vat-net-tax] Two things trip up most startups. First, the net payable tax must be paid **before** the return is filed.[^vat-net-tax] Second, where a taxable supply exceeds one lakh taka and the consideration is paid in cash instead of through a banking channel, no input tax credit is available on it.[^vat-input-credit] And the window to claim a credit is only three tax periods, after which it is time-barred.[^vat-input-credit] So file the invoice the day it arrives, not at the end of the month.

Getting the BIN is covered in [BIN application and VAT registration](/tax/vat-bin), and invoice rules in [Mushak 6.3 invoices](/tax/mushak-63). This page is only about the monthly cycle.

---

## How the month's figure is built

The Act sets out the calculation of net payable tax in four steps:[^vat-net-tax]

1. Add together all output taxes and supplementary duty payable in the tax period
2. Subtract all input tax credits you are entitled to claim in that period
3. Add all increasing adjustments for the period
4. Subtract all decreasing adjustments for the period

Output tax is the tax on what you sold. Input tax is the tax you paid on what you bought. The two adjustments come from specific events — VAT withheld from a vendor's bill, for instance, goes in as an increasing adjustment; see [TDS and VDS on vendor payments](/tax/tds-vds).

---

## Money first, return second

Plenty of people have this the wrong way round. The Act states that the net payable tax computed above must be paid by the taxpayer, in the prescribed manner, *before* filing the return for that period.[^vat-net-tax]

So treating the 15th as the deadline means doing two things that day — pay, then file. Banking hours, challan generation, or portal load: any one of them stalls and both slip. Close the numbers by the 10th instead.

---

## Where input tax credit gets blocked

Credit is what brings the month's bill down, so read these conditions closely.

**Pay in cash and the credit is gone.** Where the value of a taxable supply exceeds one lakh taka and the consideration is paid, in part or in full, in cash instead of through a banking channel, no input tax credit may be claimed on that supply.[^vat-input-credit] Remember the threshold — and if paying large bills in cash is a habit, change it.

**Some costs carry no credit at all.** The Act blocks credit outright on:[^vat-input-credit]

- passenger vehicles, their spare parts, and their repair and maintenance services — unless dealing in vehicles, renting them out, or supplying transport is part of your economic activity and the vehicle was acquired for that purpose
- entertainment, or acquisitions used to provide entertainment — unless providing entertainment is part of your economic activity in the normal course
- membership or right of entry to a club, association, or society
- transportation services, with narrow exceptions

**No paperwork, no credit.** At the time of filing you must hold the supporting documents: for an import, a bill of entry bearing the importer's name and BIN plus the Customs clearance instrument; for a supply, the tax invoice issued by the supplier; and for a withholding entity, the combined tax invoice and withholding certificate issued by the supplier.[^vat-input-credit]

Imported services carry an extra condition: no credit unless the output tax payable on that imported service is included in the return.[^vat-input-credit]

---

## The claim window is only three months

This one quietly costs the most and gets discussed the least.

The Act requires every input tax credit claim to be made either in the tax period in which the VAT was paid, or within the two succeeding tax periods. After that the claim is time-barred.[^vat-input-credit]

Three months, in other words. Find a July purchase invoice in October and the credit is simply gone. Chasing vendors for invoices is monthly work, not something to leave hanging.

---

## The order to work the month in

You have 15 days after the month ends. Doing it in this order avoids a scramble on the last day.

1. **Reconcile sales invoices.** Gather the month's invoices and total the output tax.
2. **Collect purchase invoices.** Chase vendors whose invoices haven't arrived, keeping the three-period window in mind.
3. **Test the credit conditions.** Check whether any bill over one lakh went out in cash, and whether anything falls into a blocked category.
4. **Enter the adjustments.** Increasing adjustments for VAT you withheld; decreasing adjustments where someone withheld from your bill.
5. **Work out the net and pay it.** Before the return — that is the statutory order.
6. **File the return.** On the VAT Online portal, by the 15th.
7. **File the evidence.** Invoices, payment receipt, and the filing acknowledgement in one folder.

A month with no transactions still needs a return. The obligation in the Act sits on the registered or enlisted person, not on whether anything was sold.[^vat-return-deadline]

---

## What lateness and errors cost

**Late.** Not filing within the prescribed time carries a monetary penalty of 10,000 taka.[^vat-return-penalty] The Commissioner may permit late filing on application, but that cannot move the actual payment date out by more than a month, and the interest liability remains.[^vat-return-deadline]

**Wrong.** Errors can cost more than lateness. Failing to include output tax in the return carries a penalty of twice the amount omitted, and taking more input tax credit than you are entitled to carries twice the amount irregularly taken.[^vat-return-penalty]

So when in doubt, leaving a credit out is safer than pulling it in — and there is a route back if an error surfaces: with the Commissioner's permission, clerical mistakes and omissions can be corrected through an amended return.[^vat-return-deadline]

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Set the 15th as a repeating monthly entry on the calendar
- [ ] Made it a rule to close the numbers by the 10th
- [ ] Filing sales and purchase invoices within the month itself
- [ ] Paying bills over one lakh taka through a banking channel
- [ ] Not claiming credit on passenger vehicles, entertainment, or club costs
- [ ] Chasing vendor invoices against the three-period claim window
- [ ] Entering withheld-VAT adjustments in the return
- [ ] Paying before filing
- [ ] Filing even in months with no transactions

Rule of thumb: **Credit is what brings the monthly bill down, and credit lives on paper. File the invoice the day it arrives — the window shuts after three months.**
