# Accounts, Audit, and Bookkeeping: What to Set Up at the Start

> **In short:** The board has to appoint the company's first auditor within one month of registration — waiting for the first AGM is not an option.[^ca-auditor] How you keep the accounts is not entirely your choice either: the Companies Act defines what "proper books of account" means, the books belong at the registered office, and books and vouchers have to be preserved for twelve years.[^ca-books] If you are VAT-registered, a separate five-year clock runs on the VAT records.[^vat-records] And the auditor has to state in the report whether proper books were kept as the law requires.[^ca-audit-report] So empty books do not stay between you and your auditor — they travel, in writing, to RJSC and to NBR.

The order in which the year's work happens is in [Annual compliance](/en/tax/annual-compliance). This page is only about the setup: once the registration papers are in hand, how you stand up the accounting and audit arrangement.

---

## The one-month clock: your first auditor

The Companies Act requires the board to appoint the first auditor within one month of the date of registration, and that auditor holds office until the conclusion of the first AGM.[^ca-auditor] If the board fails to do it, the shareholders may appoint at a general meeting.[^ca-auditor]

New companies routinely let this month pass. Nothing has been sold yet, so what is there to audit — that is the reasoning. But appointing an auditor and having an audit done are two different things. The appointment is a board decision that belongs in the minute book. The rules for meetings and minutes are in [Board meetings, AGMs, and the minute book](/en/registration/board-meetings-agm).

**Who can be the auditor.** Only a chartered accountant within the meaning of the Bangladesh Chartered Accountants Order 1973.[^ca-auditor-qual] A firm may be appointed in its own name if all of its partners practising in Bangladesh are qualified.[^ca-auditor-qual]

**Who cannot.** An officer or employee of the company; anyone who is a partner of, or employed by, such an officer or employee; and anyone indebted to the company for more than one thousand taka.[^ca-auditor-qual] The Act's definition of "officer" includes a director.[^ca-officer] So your bookkeeper, or a co-founder who sits on the board, cannot audit the company — chartered accountant or not.

## What the law counts as proper books

Section 181 asks for records of four things:[^ca-books]

- all money received and spent by the company, and what for;
- all sales and purchases of goods;
- assets and liabilities; and
- for a company in production, distribution, marketing, transportation, processing, manufacturing, milling, extraction, or mining, further particulars on how material, labour, and other overhead were used.

The Act adds a condition. Having books is not the same as keeping proper ones: they must be such that they give a true and fair view of the state of the company's affairs and explain its transactions.[^ca-books]

**Where the books live.** At the registered office, open to the directors during business hours.[^ca-books] The board may decide to keep them somewhere else in Bangladesh for up to six months, but within seven days of that decision the company has to file a notice with the Registrar giving the full address of the other place.[^ca-books] Where there is a branch, its accounts may be kept at the branch, provided summarised returns are sent to the registered office at intervals of not more than three months.[^ca-books]

The Registrar, or a government officer authorised for the purpose, may inspect these books and papers during business hours and take copies.[^ca-inspection]

**How long to keep them.** At least twelve years of books preceding the current year, together with the vouchers behind the entries.[^ca-books] For a company younger than twelve years, the whole period since incorporation.[^ca-books]

**What happens if you don't.** The liability sits on a person, not on the company. The managing director or manager where there is one, otherwise every director — up to six months' imprisonment for each offence, a fine of up to five thousand taka, or both.[^ca-books] Bankers, auditors, and legal advisers are excluded from that list.[^ca-books]

## When your first financial year actually ends

Most founders assume the first year runs twelve months from the date of incorporation. The Income Tax Act says otherwise. For a newly set-up business, the first income year begins on the date the business is set up and ends on the following 30 June.[^ita-income-year]

Say the company is registered in February 2026. Whatever day the business actually starts, that first income year ends on 30 June 2026 — a short year of five months or less. It still needs accounts, an audit, and a return. Banks, insurers, and finance companies run January to December instead, and a subsidiary of a foreign parent may, with the Deputy Commissioner of Taxes' approval, use a different twelve-month period in order to consolidate with the parent.[^ita-income-year]

Check the Companies Act side too. There the financial year may be shorter or longer than a calendar year but must not exceed fifteen months — eighteen with the Registrar's special permission.[^ca-financial-year]

In plain terms: unless you have a specific reason not to, end the financial year on 30 June. One set of audited accounts then serves the AGM, RJSC, and the income tax return.

## Which standards the accounts follow

For companies, the Income Tax Act states the requirement directly: maintain accounts and prepare financial reports in accordance with International Accounting Standards (IAS), International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRS), and the laws in force in Bangladesh.[^ita-standards]

What goes with the return is specified as well. Every person other than an individual and a Hindu Undivided Family must file, with the return, a copy of the income statement and the financial statement, certified by a chartered accountant.[^ita-audited-report] The certificate covers three things: that the accounts are maintained under IAS and IFRS, that the Board's prescribed criteria have been followed, and that the audit was done under International Standards on Auditing (ISA).[^ita-audited-report]

There is one exemption, and it can change your choice of structure. Under the proviso added by the Finance Act 2024, the requirement does not apply to a firm, trust, association of persons, foundation, society, or cooperative society whose gross receipts do not exceed Tk 5 crore.[^ita-audited-report] Read that list again — a company is not on it.

Which means a partnership firm under Tk 5 crore owes no CA-certified statements, while a private limited company with zero sales does. Price that in when you choose the structure — see [Private limited, OPC, or sole proprietorship](/en/registration/structure-decision-tree).

## VAT records are a separate pile, on a separate clock

The VAT Act requires every taxpayer to keep, for five years, all accounts, documents, and other records of their economic activities.[^vat-records] The list covers:[^vat-records]

- all statements of purchase of goods, services, or immovable property and the related tax invoices, taxable or exempt;
- all statements of sale;
- all tax invoices, credit notes, debit notes, and withholding certificates issued and received;
- customs documentation for imports and exports;
- records of the prices at which you sell what you manufacture, the input-output coefficient, and any discounts;
- records relating to services or goods subject to supplementary duty;
- treasury challans, or other evidence of tax paid by another route; and
- the return for every tax period.

Two retention periods, then — twelve years under the Companies Act, five under the VAT Act. Use the longer one. And remember that VAT paperwork is not only about storage: an input tax credit claim survives or dies on whether the invoice is on file. The detail is in [The monthly VAT return](/en/tax/monthly-vat-return) and [Mushak 6.3 invoices](/en/tax/mushak-63).

## Your books appear in the auditor's report

The auditor's rights are wide. They have a right of access at all times to the company's books, accounts, and vouchers, whether kept at the head office or elsewhere, and may require information and explanation from the officers.[^ca-audit-report]

The report must state:[^ca-audit-report]

- whether the auditor obtained all the information and explanations needed for the audit;
- whether proper books of account as required by law have been kept; and
- whether the balance sheet and profit and loss account agree with the books.

If any of these is answered in the negative or with a qualification, the report has to give the reasons.[^ca-audit-report] That report is then read at the AGM, goes to RJSC with the balance sheet, and goes with the income tax return. That is where the risk sits: a year of unwritten books becomes a written remark that lands in three places.

## What an audit costs

Under ICAB's minimum fee schedule, where a private limited company's gross assets or gross turnover — whichever is higher — does not exceed Tk 5 crore, the minimum audit fee is Tk 50,000.[^icab-fees] Above Tk 5 crore and up to Tk 25 crore, add 10% of Tk 50,000 for every Tk 2 crore (or part of it) of increase over Tk 5 crore.[^icab-fees]

Work it through. At Tk 9 crore turnover, the increase over Tk 5 crore is Tk 4 crore — two blocks of Tk 2 crore. That is Tk 5,000 per block, Tk 10,000 in total, so the minimum comes to Tk 60,000.

Two caveats belong with that number. It is not a government fee but a minimum approved by the ICAB Council, and the schedule has been in effect since 1 January 2016.[^icab-fees] Budgeting against a ten-year-old floor invites a surprise, so get a written quote from the firm before the year-end rather than after it.

## The setup decisions you only make once

The six below are not the legal part of this page — they are the choices that, made early, save work later.

**Decide who writes the books.** You, a part-time bookkeeper, or a CA firm. If the firm writing your accounts is also the one auditing them, ask the firm itself whether that raises an independence problem before you appoint them.

**Keep the company's money separate.** Running company expenses through a personal account means the assets and liabilities do not give a true picture, and every transaction has to be explained during the audit. Read [The business bank account](/en/payments/bank-account) alongside [Personal e-TIN vs company TIN](/en/tax/personal-vs-company-tin).

**Set up the account heads first.** Structure them the way the income tax return and VAT return will ask for the information. That is far easier than re-sorting a year of bank statements in July.

**No money leaves without a receipt.** Vouchers have to be preserved with the books, so an expense with no paper behind it will not stand up in the accounts later.[^ca-books]

**Close the month, every month.** Reconcile the bank, file the invoices, update what is owed and owing, and close. Twelve small jobs left undone become one large job after June.

**Habit first, software second.** Spreadsheet or accounting package, the requirement is the same: you must be able to produce the books at the registered office on request, and produce statements under IAS and IFRS.[^ita-standards]

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | What happens | Better |
|---|---|---|
| Not appointing the first auditor | The one-month window closes and the appointment falls to a general meeting | Appoint at the first board meeting after registration, recorded in the minutes |
| Treating a bookkeeper or director as the auditor | They are disqualified in law, so the appointment does not hold | An outside chartered accountant or firm |
| Mixing personal and company money | Assets and liabilities stop reconciling, and each item needs explaining | A separate account from day one |
| Spending without receipts | No voucher, so the books are hard to call proper | Every expense on file |
| Moving the books quietly | Breaks the seven-day notice requirement | Keep them at the registered office; notify the Registrar if they move |
| Discarding everything after five years | Breaks the Companies Act's twelve-year requirement | Keep to the longer period |
| Assuming a twelve-month first year | The first income year actually ends on the following 30 June | Write down the first year-end date right after registration |

## Checklist

- [ ] Appointed the first auditor at a board meeting within one month of registration
- [ ] Confirmed the auditor is a chartered accountant and not disqualified
- [ ] Opened a separate bank account in the company's name
- [ ] Written down the end date of the first income year
- [ ] Keeping books and vouchers at the registered office
- [ ] Structured the account heads to match the tax and VAT returns
- [ ] Reconciling the bank and closing each month
- [ ] Filing VAT invoices and returns separately
- [ ] Arranged storage for a twelve-year retention period
- [ ] Got a written audit fee quote before the year-end

Rule of thumb: **The audit is year-end work, but the arrangement for it is first-month work. The audit gets easier from the day you start writing the books, not from the day it begins.**
