# The Tax Calendar: What Is Due on Which Date

> **In short:** Keep separate deadlines for VAT, withholding tax, advance income tax and the annual income-tax return. The 2026 Finance Act changed the VAT schedule and the annual return rules, so an old “15th every month, 15 January annually” calendar is no longer reliable.[^finance-act-2026] Record your entity type, income-year end and VAT filing basis before setting reminders.

This page covers an ordinary company and flags the different individual-return rule. It is general guidance, not a calculation of your tax. Use [filing returns](/en/tax/filing-returns) for the submission process and [RJSC annual returns](/en/registration/annual-returns) for company filings.

## VAT: three tax periods, or voluntary monthly filing

From 1 July 2026, the ordinary <Term name="vat-bin">VAT</Term> return covers three monthly tax periods and is due within 15 days after the third period. Government, semi-government and autonomous bodies, banks, insurers and nil-return filers have 20 days. A taxpayer may instead file for each month during the following month.[^finance-act-2026]

For July–September 2026, that means 15 October ordinarily or 20 October for the special classes. For voluntary monthly filing, July's return can be submitted during August, by 31 August. The ordinary 15-day rule expressly allows the next working day when the deadline is a government holiday; check the applicable rule or order for other dates.

Full VAT registration and turnover-tax enlistment now share monthly tax periods, although their tax calculations and forms differ. Keep monthly records whichever filing basis you use. Pay the tax before filing and retain both receipts. See [VAT return workflow](/en/tax/monthly-vat-return).

## Withholding return: the 25th of each month

Section 177(3) of the published Income Tax Act requires the preceding month's <Term name="withholding-tax">withholding tax</Term> return by the 25th of each month, moving to the following working day if it is a weekly or public holiday.[^income-act] The Finance Act 2026 adds categories of filers to section 177; it does not replace that monthly deadline.

A return deadline is not automatically the deadline for depositing tax deducted from a payment. Track the prescribed deposit date separately. Use [TDS and VDS](/en/tax/tds-vds) to identify the payment, rate, certificate and deposit work.

## Advance income tax: four instalments

For an existing taxpayer, section 154 generally tests **total income in the latest income year**, not sales or estimated tax payable. The Finance Act 2026 increased its threshold from 6 lakh to **10 lakh taka**. Statutory exceptions apply, and a newly assessed business needs the separate new-taxpayer rule checked.[^finance-act-2026]

Section 155 sets four equal instalments: 15 September, 15 December, 15 March and 15 June. A public-holiday date moves to the next working day. The amount accounts for applicable tax and source-tax credits; review an estimate with the tax adviser when income changes.[^income-act]

For an illustrative advance-tax requirement of 2 lakh taka after the relevant credits, each instalment is 50,000 taka. Do not apply that example before determining whether advance tax applies and what the actual amount is.

## Annual income-tax return: use the current section 170

The Finance Act 2026 replaced the filing schedule. For persons other than individuals and Hindu undivided families, the ordinary deadline is **the 15th day of the ninth month after the income-year end**, or **15 September following that year-end** if that ninth-month date would fall earlier.[^finance-act-2026]

A company with a 30 June 2026 year-end therefore has a 15 March 2027 deadline. The old 15 January date is not the current rule. The Act also has early-filing incentives and additional tax for later filing, so the final deadline is not the only financially relevant date.

For individuals and Hindu undivided families, filing by **31 December** falls in the ordinary no-additional-tax window; the Act sets separate early and later windows. First-time individual filers, persons returning from abroad and specified study/deputation cases have separate provisions. Check the rule for the actual taxpayer rather than copying the company's date onto a founder's personal return.

## Example calendar for a 30 June year-end

| When | Work |
|---|---|
| Each month | Close the books, reconcile invoices and track tax deposits |
| 25th each month | Previous month's withholding return |
| 15 October, January, April and July | Ordinary three-period VAT return; use the special or monthly rule if applicable |
| 15 September, December, March and June | Advance income-tax instalments where required |
| 15 March after the June year-end | Ordinary company income-tax return deadline |
| AGM-linked dates | Schedule X and accounts filings with RJSC |

For RJSC, Schedule X is generally due within 21 days of the AGM, and accounts within 30 days. **Failing to hold an AGM does not remove the accounts-filing obligation:** section 190 addresses filing when the AGM was not held.[^companies-act-filings] Put the legally required AGM date in the calendar too. Add trade-licence expiry and any sector-specific renewals separately.

## If a deadline is missed

Identify the taxpayer, return, period and applicable law before calculating the consequence. The old income-tax formula of 2% per month is not a safe universal estimate after the 2026 changes. The Act now distinguishes filing windows and replaces the late-return provision. VAT penalties also depend on the offence.[^finance-act-2026]

Preserve the records, tell the adviser what was missed and arrange the correct payment and filing. An extension must come from a valid applicable order; do not plan on one arriving or assume it waives tax and interest.
