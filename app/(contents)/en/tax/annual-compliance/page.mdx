# Annual Compliance: The Order from Audit to Return

> **In short:** The year's obligations are not separate items but a chain. Without the audit there is no AGM, because audited accounts are what the meeting exists to receive.[^companies-act-year] Missing the AGM does not remove RJSC filing obligations: section 190 also provides for filing when no AGM is held.[^companies-act-year] And a company's income tax return has to carry audited financial statements with it, so a stalled audit risks missing the return deadline; it does not extend it.[^ita-year] The first link in the chain is the audit — and that is where delay begins too. With a 30 June year-end, engage the auditor in July or August, not in December.

Which date carries what is in [the tax calendar](/en/tax/tax-calendar). This page is about the order that keeps any of it from jamming.

---

## Why the audit is the bottleneck

The Companies Act requires the board to lay a balance sheet and profit and loss account before the members at every AGM, and those accounts must be audited.[^companies-act-year] Only a chartered accountant may audit them.[^companies-act-year] Meanwhile the Income Tax Act makes audited financial statements a mandatory attachment to a company's return.[^ita-year]

So one audit report is needed in three places: at the AGM, in the RJSC filings, and with the income tax return. A late report puts all three obligations at risk; their deadlines remain independently binding.

There is also a timing limit. The accounts laid before the AGM must cover a period ending within the nine months before the meeting — twelve for a company with business or interests outside Bangladesh.[^companies-act-year] On top of that sits the requirement to hold an AGM in each calendar year.[^companies-act-year] With a 30 June year-end, holding the meeting before 31 December can satisfy both. Also check the maximum 15-month gap since the previous AGM; the first AGM has an 18-month incorporation limit. This example assumes those limits do not require an earlier meeting.

## July to March, in order

For an ordinary private limited company whose financial year ends 30 June. An OPC has special meeting and filing rules; use the [OPC guide](/en/registration/opc) for those:

**July–August.** Close the books, reconcile the bank, and schedule the work with the duly appointed auditor. This is audit planning, not permission to delay the statutory appointment: the first auditor is appointed within one month of registration and later appointments ordinarily take place at the AGM. Gather outstanding invoices, vouchers, and bank statements now — they are the first things the audit will ask for.

**September.** The audit runs. 15 September is the first advance tax instalment date, so pay that separately if it applies.[^ita-year]

**October–November.** Finalise the audit report. Call a board meeting to approve the accounts, then send the AGM notice. The notice period and method come from both the Act and the company's <Term name="moa-aoa">AoA</Term> — see [Board meetings, AGMs, and the minute book](/en/registration/board-meetings-agm).

**December.** Hold the AGM. The audited accounts are laid before it, and next year's auditor is appointed at the same meeting.[^companies-act-year] 15 December is the second advance tax instalment.[^ita-year]

**The 30 days after the AGM.** Schedule X to RJSC within 21 days of the meeting, and the audited balance sheet within 30.[^companies-act-year] The full process is in [The RJSC filing calendar](/en/registration/annual-returns).

**15 March.** The company's income tax return — Tax Day.[^ita-year] It goes in with the audited statements, the evidence of compliance with verification criteria, and the computation sheet reconciling profit with income.[^ita-year] Details in [How to file the income tax and VAT returns](/en/tax/filing-returns).

**15 March and 15 June.** The remaining two advance tax instalments.[^ita-year]

## What goes to which office each year

| Office | Annual work |
|---|---|
| RJSC | AGM, Schedule X, audited balance sheet, the outcome of the auditor's appointment |
| NBR, income tax | The company return on Tax Day, and where applicable four advance tax instalments |
| NBR, VAT | Returns for the applicable filing periods; confirm any additional accounts-submission obligation |
| City corporation or municipality | Trade licence renewal |

Sector licences lengthen the list. A fire licence, environmental clearance, or food and pharmaceutical approval each has its own renewal cycle. The [Licences and approvals](/en/licenses) section has sector-by-sector guides.

---

## What doesn't stop mid-year

Penalties accumulate when the monthly work gets forgotten during the annual push. The <Term name="withholding-tax">withholding tax</Term> return is due monthly by the 25th. From 1 July 2026, the ordinary VAT return covers three tax periods and is due within 15 days; specified entities and nil returns have 20 days. A taxpayer may instead file for each monthly period during the following period. Keep monthly records under either option.[^vat-year][^ita-year] Both have their own workflows in [VAT return periods and filing](/en/tax/monthly-vat-return) and [TDS and VDS on vendor payments](/en/tax/tds-vds).

## What could not be verified for this page

- **The exact trade licence renewal deadline.** It varies by city corporation and municipality, and no single current source covers them all. Check your own licence's expiry and note the date separately.
- **An annual submission of audited accounts to the VAT office.** Some professional guidance refers to this obligation, but it does not appear in the text of the VAT Act 2012 or the 2016 Rules reviewed for this page. Confirm the current position with your accountant.

## Checklist

- [ ] Closed the books and reconciled the bank within a month of the year-end
- [ ] Engaged the auditor in July or August, not December
- [ ] Had the audit report approved at a board meeting
- [ ] Held the AGM within the calendar year
- [ ] Appointed next year's auditor at the AGM
- [ ] Completed both RJSC filings within 21 and 30 days of the meeting
- [ ] Filed the income tax return with audited statements by Tax Day
- [ ] Paid all four advance tax instalments on time where applicable
- [ ] Renewed the trade licence and any sector licences
- [ ] Filed every required VAT return and monthly withholding return through the year

Rule of thumb: **The audit is the first link. Schedule audit work in July and track each deadline separately; start in October and everything through March's return becomes a scramble.**
