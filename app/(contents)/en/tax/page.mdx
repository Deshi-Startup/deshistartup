# Tax, VAT and Bookkeeping

> **In short:** Separate three jobs: identify the obligations that apply, keep the records needed to meet them, and track filing and payment dates. A certificate alone does not complete the work.

## Keep identity, filing and money movement separate

A **TIN** identifies a taxpayer; a **BIN** concerns the VAT system. A registration certificate is not the same thing as evidence that a return was filed for a particular period. NBR's service directory separates income-tax return verification, e-Return, VAT/BIN and payment services.[^tax-service-map]

Consider a fictional small company whose accountant asks for records for an upcoming filing. The founder has a tax certificate and says “tax is done.” The correct question is which period and obligation the accountant means, what must be submitted, and what evidence confirms completion.

A useful tracking row looks like this:

| Field | Fictional preparation record |
|---|---|
| Person/entity and task | The company; specified return to be confirmed by its accountant |
| Reporting period | Write the exact period, not “this year” |
| Due date | Unconfirmed until checked against the applicable current rule |
| Records | Sales invoices, costs, bank/provider statements and deductions |
| Owner and review | Founder assembles records; accountant confirms scope and deadline before filing |
| Completion evidence | Relevant submission acknowledgement and payment evidence where applicable |

Do not put today's date in the deadline field just to fill it. “Unconfirmed” is a useful warning that prompts action. Once the requirement is confirmed, replace it with the actual deadline and keep the source/date beside it.

For bookkeeping, if a fictional invoice is BDT 10,000 but the bank receives BDT 9,500, record both. Ask whether the BDT 500 is a documented deduction, fee, partial payment or discrepancy. Do not invent a tax classification from the difference. Gather supporting evidence before posting the adjustment.

## Prepare a brief before asking for advice

Record the person or entity involved, actual business activity, start date, sales records, payment routes, existing registrations and notices received. Mark gaps rather than estimating missing records as facts.

For each possible obligation, keep **applicability / official source and date / reporting period / deadline / responsible person / proof of completion**. Confirm current rules for your situation with NBR or an appropriately qualified adviser. Do not infer your obligations solely from another founder's threshold, certificate or filing experience.

## Start the records today

Gather invoices, receipts, bank and payment statements and records of refunds or deductions. Keep personal and business transactions distinguishable. Use the [reconciliation guide](/en/payments/reconciliation) to investigate unmatched amounts.

This section helps you prepare and organise the work. The applicable rules and deadlines need checking for your activity and reporting period.
