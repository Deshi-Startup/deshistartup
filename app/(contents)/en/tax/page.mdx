# Tax, VAT and Bookkeeping

> **In short:** Getting a tax registration is the beginning of the work, not evidence that every return or payment is complete. Identify the person or company involved, keep the supporting records and confirm each applicable filing period and deadline. Give every recurring task an owner.

An accountant asks for the company's records, but the invoices are in email, the courier statements are in a portal and some purchases were paid from your personal account. You may already have the registration certificates. The immediate problem is reconstructing what happened well enough to prepare the next return and know what, if anything, must be paid.

This section helps you organise that work. If you are just starting, it also helps you ask which registrations and obligations apply before copying another founder's arrangement. The answer can depend on the legal structure, activity and reporting period, so a rate or deadline remembered from another business is not a safe starting assumption.

## Know whose records and obligations you are handling

A **TIN** identifies a taxpayer; a **BIN** belongs to the VAT system. They answer different questions, and neither certificate is proof that a return has been filed for a particular period. NBR separates income-tax, VAT/BIN, return-verification and payment services in its service directory.[^tax-service-map]

Start by naming the person or entity involved. Are you organising the proprietor's business records, the company's records, or the founder's personal tax work? Keep those identities clear when giving information to an accountant. The [personal-versus-company TIN guide](/en/tax/personal-vs-company-tin) covers that distinction; [e-TIN, VAT and BIN](/en/e-tin-vat-bin) explains the registrations in more detail.

Next, describe what the business does and which registrations it already holds. To investigate VAT, use [when VAT is required](/en/tax/when-vat-required) and check the current rule for the activity. Registration, filing and payment are related tasks, but completing one does not automatically complete the others.

## Keep enough detail to explain the money

Gather sales invoices, purchase receipts, bank and payment-provider statements, refunds and any documents for deductions. Keep personal transactions distinguishable from business ones. A folder of receipts helps, but the records also need to connect each amount to its date, purpose and counterparty so that someone can follow the transaction later.

For example, suppose an invoice is BDT 10,000 and only BDT 9,500 reaches the bank. Record the invoice and the receipt separately. The BDT 500 difference might be a documented deduction, a provider fee, a partial payment or an error. Without the supporting record, calling it “tax” would hide the question instead of resolving it. Ask for the remittance details or deduction document and account for the difference once you know what it is.

Use [bookkeeping setup](/en/tax/bookkeeping-setup) to establish the folders and records, and [reconciliation](/en/payments/reconciliation) to match them against the money received. Doing this regularly keeps unanswered transactions from accumulating until filing time.

## Agree what must be filed, for which period and by whom

When asking an accountant for help, send the business description, start date, existing registrations, records and any notice you have received. Ask them to identify the applicable returns, reporting periods and payment requirements. “Tax is done” is too broad to check. “This return for this period has been submitted, and here is the acknowledgement” is something you can verify.

Record the confirmed deadline and its current official basis alongside the task. Do not copy a date from last year without checking whether it still applies. The [tax calendar](/en/tax/tax-calendar) and [filing guide](/en/tax/filing-returns) give the relevant starting points; confirm the rule for your situation with NBR or a qualified tax adviser.

Also agree when you must hand over complete records. That date needs to leave time to resolve missing information before the filing deadline. Name the person who prepares the work, the person who reviews it and who will submit or pay. Using an accountant does not remove the need to know whether the task was completed.

## Keep the completion records together

After filing, save the submitted return and acknowledgement for the correct taxpayer and period. Keep payment evidence where payment was required, and retain the supporting documents used to prepare the figures. If a return is corrected or a notice arrives later, you should be able to find the version and records involved.

Start today with the most recent bank and provider statements, then identify the invoices and deductions you cannot yet match. That gives you useful work to do immediately while the applicable obligations and dates are being confirmed.
