# Family Objections: How to Talk About Starting a Business

> **In short:** Bring a small, clear plan to the conversation. Explain what you want to test, how you will cover your responsibilities and when you will review the result. Listen to the concern before answering it. You do not need to settle your whole career in one talk.

## Start with this script

Change the words to sound like you. Fill the blanks before you use it.

> “I want to test whether [type of customer] needs help with [problem]. For [number of weeks], I want to spend [hours each week] on it. I plan to keep [job, classes or other duties] going.
>
> “My total spending limit is [amount], from [source of money]. I have checked how I will cover [bills and responsibilities]. I will pause if [clear condition].
>
> “On [date], I would like us to look at what happened. What worries you most about this plan?”

These are suggested words, not a real founder's story. A script cannot promise agreement. Use only promises you can keep. If you cannot cover the bills or find time for a test, work on that gap first.

## Prepare one small plan

Choose a quiet time to speak with the people whose money, time or daily responsibilities the plan affects. Ask them when they can talk.

Before setting a spending limit, write down reliable income and regular expenses. The US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau's budget worksheet uses this basic method.[^monthly-budget] For this test, also allow for bills due soon, debt payments and money kept for emergencies. Expected startup sales are not money you already have.

Do not use shared savings or assign someone extra household work without their agreement. Check your job or study commitments before choosing the hours.

Copy this card into a note. It is a planning aid; discuss any request for a family loan or investment separately.

| Fill in | Your plan |
|---|---|
| One thing I want to learn | Do [customers] have [problem], and what do they do about it now? |
| Work I will do | [For example, speak with customers about a recent experience.] |
| Start and review dates | [Date] to [date] |
| Time I can use | [Days and hours], up to [total hours each week] |
| Responsibilities I will cover | [Work, classes, bills, care or household tasks] |
| Total spending limit | [Amount] for the whole test, from [source] |
| Help someone has agreed to give | [Person, task and time], or none |
| When I will pause | [Missed duty, money limit or another clear condition] |
| What I will bring to the review | [Notes, actual spending, time used and unresolved questions] |

## Listen before giving your answer

Ask what is behind the objection. Is it lost income, study, safety, extra work at home or something else? Say back what you heard and check that you understood. This follows the listening approach described by Harvard's Program on Negotiation.[^listening]

For example: “You're worried that I will stop paying my share of the bills. Have I understood that right?”

Then answer that concern. You can take it seriously while still disagreeing about the next step.

### “Why risk a steady job?”

> “A steady income matters to me too. The plan I am asking you to look at is this small test. I want to keep my job during it and see whether the problem is worth more work. Leaving the job would need a separate plan.”

Use this reply only if you can keep the job and fit the test around it. For a student, change “job” to “classes” and include exam dates. The [semester plan](/en/founder-life/student-semester-plan) can help you work out the hours.

### “Who will pay the bills or do your share at home?”

> “Here is what I am responsible for: [list]. Here is when I will do it and where the money will come from. Is there a task or cost I have missed?”

If the plan relies on another person doing more, ask them directly. Their silence is not an agreement.

### “When will it make money?”

> “I do not know yet. This test will help me learn [specific question]. I will show you the notes and costs on [date]. I cannot promise a profit or a salary from it.”

A few positive comments or first orders do not prove a steady income. Keep the next decision small enough for the evidence you have.

### “What will relatives think?”

> “I know people may ask questions. For now, I am testing one idea while keeping [current responsibility]. That is what I am comfortable telling them. We can agree on which details stay private.”

Do not share another person's finances or private concerns to defend your plan.

## Example: a four-week customer test

Imagine you have a job and want to test an appointment-reminder service for small teams. These numbers are an example, not a budget or timetable every founder should follow.

You choose two three-hour blocks each week. Over four weeks, that is **6 × 4 = 24 hours**. You set a total spending limit of **৳1,000** for calls and travel, using money left after your commitments and emergency needs. The ৳1,000 limit is for all four weeks, not each week.

Your work is to ask potential customers how they handle missed appointments now. You plan to pause if the spending limit is reached or your work or home duties slip. At the review, you bring notes, receipts and a record of time spent. See the [customer interview questions](/en/validation/customer-interviews) to prepare.

If ৳1,000 is not available, use a lower limit or a test that needs no extra spending. Fewer hours are also fine. You have not promised to build the service or leave your job at the end of four weeks.

## Review the plan on the date you chose

Look at what happened together: what you learned, what you spent and whether you kept your commitments. Choose one next step:

- **Stop or pause:** the test caused problems, used its budget or did not answer the question.
- **Change the test:** you learned that the customer or problem was different from what you expected.
- **Try another small step:** the evidence gives you a clear next question. Set new time and spending limits first.

If you still disagree, name the unresolved concern and leave time for another conversation. Do not describe the plan as agreed when it is not. If the talk becomes threatening or you feel unsafe, end it and seek support from someone you trust.

Before you talk, check that the card has a real review date, a total spending limit and a clear pause condition. Then read the opening aloud. Replace any words you would not normally say.
