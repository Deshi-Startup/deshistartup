# Import and Export: Plan Beyond the Supplier's Price

> **In short:** Before paying for an import or promising an export delivery, establish what is being traded, how it will move and how payment will reach the right account. For goods, include transport, clearance and other applicable charges in the budget. A supplier's price alone does not tell you whether the transaction is affordable.

A foreign supplier has sent a quote, or an overseas buyer wants to place an order. The commercial conversation may be going well, but there are still practical questions between the offer and a completed sale: who arranges transport, which documents the bank needs, what happens at customs and when the money is due.

Work through those questions before committing funds or a delivery date. This section brings together the registration, shipment-document and export-payment guides. Goods and services share the need for clear payment terms, but a digital service should not be forced into a physical-shipment process.

## Describe the transaction in terms the bank and carrier can use

Write down the buyer and seller, what is being supplied, the quantity or service scope, origin and destination, value, currency and proposed payment timing. For goods, include the material, intended use and product specification. These details help a bank, authority or logistics provider understand the actual transaction rather than guess from a broad product name.

Goods are classified for customs using an **HS code**. Classification affects which product-specific requirements you need to investigate, so do not choose a code simply because a similar-looking item used it. The Bangladesh Trade Portal provides a commodity lookup, and the Customs Import Export Hub lists general documents, including import and export registration certificates, known as **IRC** and **ERC**.[^trade-records] Those lists help frame the questions; they do not settle every exemption, classification or current charge.

Start with [import-export registration](/en/trade/import-export-registration), then follow the [IRC/ERC application steps](/en/trade/irc-erc-steps) where applicable. If classification or a product restriction remains unclear, resolve it with the relevant authority or qualified specialist before the order leaves you committed to goods you may not be able to clear.

## Find out what the quoted price leaves out

For an import, **landed cost** is the cost of getting the goods to a specified destination. State the destination when using the term: reaching a port is different from reaching your workshop. Ask the supplier and logistics provider which costs their quotes include, and who is responsible for the rest.

Suppose you are considering 100 components for a prototype. In this illustrative budget, the supplier's price is BDT 40,000 equivalent, transport and insurance are estimated at BDT 8,000, and local delivery at BDT 2,000. That adds up to **40,000 + 8,000 + 2,000 = BDT 50,000**, or BDT 500 per component. Customs-related charges and clearance costs have not yet been confirmed.

The order does not therefore fit a BDT 50,000 budget yet. The figure is a partial estimate, not a complete cost. Obtain the remaining charges, check storage or delay costs and note what exchange-rate changes could do to a foreign-currency payment. Keep an unknown charge visible until you have an answer instead of treating it as zero.

## Agree the responsibilities before the shipment

Confirm who books transport, provides insurance where needed, prepares the documents and deals with clearance. Put the delivery location and payment stages in writing. A low quote can reflect fewer services rather than a cheaper way to complete the same job.

For a goods export, prepare the contract or order details with your bank and logistics partners before producing the final documents. The [trade-documents guide](/en/trade/trade-documents) explains how the commercial invoice, packing list and other records fit together. Keep shared facts consistent and record agreed changes, so the bank and carrier are working from the same transaction.

For incoming export money, ask the receiving bank about the route, required records and applicable time limit before choosing a collection service. The [export-proceeds guide](/en/trade/export-proceeds) separates goods shipments from services delivered electronically. If you sell software abroad, [SaaS exports](/en/ideas/export-saas) is the more relevant business starting point; physical-goods paperwork is not a substitute for checking service-export payment requirements.

Before accepting the quote, read the transaction back as a sequence: what you pay or supply first, who acts next, what the full expected cost includes and when you receive the goods or money. Resolve any gap that could stop the transaction. Keep the current source or written response with the quote so that the next person helping you can see what has already been confirmed.
