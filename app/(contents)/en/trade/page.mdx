# Import and Export: Map the Transaction First

> **In short:** Start with what crosses the border, who buys and sells, and how payment and delivery work. Goods and digital services need different investigations.

## Choose your starting point

- **Importing or exporting goods:** start with [import-export registration](/en/trade/import-export-registration), then the [IRC/ERC steps](/en/trade/irc-erc-steps) where applicable.
- **Preparing a goods shipment:** use [trade documents](/en/trade/trade-documents) to organise the paperwork and questions for your bank and logistics partners.
- **Receiving export payments:** use [export proceeds](/en/trade/export-proceeds) to prepare the receiving-bank conversation.
- **Selling software abroad:** start with [SaaS exports](/en/ideas/export-saas), rather than assuming a physical-goods workflow applies.

## Example: estimate the commitment before paying a supplier

For goods, **landed cost** means the costs included in getting the goods to the chosen destination. State that destination and the inclusions. **IRC/ERC** refers to import/export registration certificates; an **HS code** is a customs classification used to identify goods. Which registration, classification and charges apply must be established for the transaction, not guessed from a similar product.

Suppose a fictional founder considers importing a batch of 100 components for a prototype. The supplier quotes BDT 40,000 equivalent, transport and insurance are estimated at BDT 8,000, and local delivery at BDT 2,000. Customs-related charges and clearance costs remain unconfirmed.

The known estimate is **40,000 + 8,000 + 2,000 = BDT 50,000**, or BDT 500 per component **before the unresolved charges**. It is not the final landed cost. If the founder's available budget is exactly BDT 50,000, they do not yet know that the order is affordable.

Their transaction brief records the product specification, origin, destination, intended use, quantity, supplier, proposed payment route, currency and delivery responsibilities. The next work is to confirm classification and requirements with the relevant authority/service provider, obtain the remaining cost information and ask the receiving or paying bank about the applicable documents and route.

A service export has different delivery evidence from a shipment of goods. Do not copy this goods-cost worksheet into a software sale and assume it settles export-payment requirements. Your output is a costed transaction with explicit remaining questions; do not commit money on an estimate that hides them.

The official Customs Import Export Hub lists general documents including IRC and ERC, while the Bangladesh Trade Portal supports commodity searches by HS code. Use these to frame the transaction-specific checks; general lists do not settle exemptions, classification or current charges.[^trade-records]

## Make a transaction brief

Record the product or service, origin, destination, buyer, seller, quantity, value, currency, payment timing and delivery responsibility. Identify any product-specific restrictions or approvals still to be checked.

Before committing money, ask the relevant bank, authority and service provider to confirm the current requirements for that transaction. Keep their source, date and unresolved questions with the brief.

For goods, compare the full proposed cost: supplier payment, transport, insurance where applicable, duties or taxes to verify, clearance, storage and local delivery. Mark unknown amounts as unknown. A supplier's unit price is not the complete landed cost.
