# Bringing Export Earnings Home: The Four-Month Rule

> **In short:** For an ordinary paid export, the money must reach Bangladesh through your bank within the prescribed period, generally **four months**. A foreign balance you never withdraw, whether in Stripe, Payoneer, a foreign bank account or crypto, breaks the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947. Most physical-goods shipments use an <Term name="exp-form">EXP form</Term> before customs. One narrow route is different: a qualifying prepaid home-delivery B2C e-commerce shipment of USD 1,000 or less uses a separate declaration and no EXP at Customs.[^bb-export-consolidated] Services delivered over the internet also do not use EXP. Your next step: pick your path below, then put the applicable payment or delivery deadline on a calendar.

Two founders, two completely different sets of rules. Confusion starts when the two paths are mixed together.

Say **Nusrat** runs a software studio in Chattogram with six staff. A US client pays about USD 11,000 a month for a product they are building together. Nothing is ever shipped. The work goes out over the internet. Her bank has started asking why a growing dollar balance is sitting in her payment platform.

And say **Rezaul** exports dried fruit from Jashore. He shipped USD 22,000 worth to a Malaysian importer on 3 March. His C&F agent has the paperwork, the container is on the water, and he has not thought about a deadline at all.

Nusrat has no EXP form and no customs checkpoint. Rezaul has both. They share one thing: the same four-month clock, from the same law.

---

## Which path applies to you

- **You ship ordinary physical goods out of Bangladesh.** Path A. Declare the shipment on an EXP form before customs formalities, then send the documents through your bank within 14 days.
- **You make a prepaid home-delivery B2C e-commerce shipment worth USD 1,000 or less.** This may qualify for the limited DDP route in Part-J. It uses its own declaration, courier, Bill of Export and bank-reporting steps, with no EXP declaration at Customs. Get your bank's written confirmation that every condition is met before shipping.[^bb-export-consolidated]
- **You deliver software, data work, design, research or advice over the internet.** Path B. The EXP procedure does **not** apply. Your money is handled as an inward remittance instead. The four-month deadline still applies to you, though the circular does not say what day it starts counting from. Settle that with your bank. See [the clock](#the-clock-and-where-it-comes-from).[^bb-export-consolidated]
- **You export software on a disk, flash drive or tape.** Path A, not B. The medium decides, not the content.[^bb-export-consolidated]
- **You are a freelancer, not a company.** Path B. The rules on inward remittances for outsourced services cover individuals as well as firms.[^bb-export-consolidated]
- **You are sending free samples or a small gift parcel.** Neither path, within limits. See [the exemptions](#what-is-exempt-from-declaring) below.
- **You have already been paid in advance.** Read [advance payments](#if-you-are-paid-before-you-ship) before you spend it. That money comes with a one-year duty to actually export.

By the end you will know your current order's deadline, the right channel for your kind of export, how much lands in taka and how much can stay in dollars, and which certificate proves the money came home.

---

## The clock, and where it comes from

> **Rule:** You must bring export earnings home within **four months**. This comes from Bangladesh Bank Notification No. FE 1/77-BB, dated 16 April 1977. If the full payment for a shipment does not arrive inside that period, and Bangladesh Bank has not authorised the delay, you are liable to action under the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947.[^bb-export-consolidated]

Three things about that rule surprise people.

**It is old, and it is live.** A 1977 notification still sets the deadline. Bangladesh Bank restated it in the export circular it reissued in July 2026.

**It covers services too.** The four-month rule applies to payment for services in the same way it applies to goods, using the definition of services in section 2(bbb) of the FER Act, 1947.[^bb-export-consolidated] Nusrat's monthly invoices sit on the same clock as Rezaul's container.

**There is basically one exception.** Raw jute and jute goods can run up to 360 days against an irrevocable confirmed LC or under open account.[^bb-export-consolidated] Nothing else in the circular gives a startup longer.

The duty is personal, not just procedural. Your own signature on the declaration promises to bring the full payment home inside the period Bangladesh Bank sets.[^bb-export-consolidated]

> **Warning:** If you sell services, note what the circular does **not** say. For goods the four months clearly run from the shipment date, because there is a shipment. For services delivered over the internet there is no shipment, and the circular applies the same four-month limit without naming the day it starts. Invoice date, delivery date and contract date are all plausible, and we have not found an official answer. Ask your bank's foreign-exchange desk which date it will count from, get the reply in writing, and use that date. Until you have it, count from the earliest of the three and you cannot be late.

## The rule founders break first

> **Warning:** Keeping export earnings abroad breaks the law. That covers any form: equity or portfolio investment, physical or virtual assets, and accounts in any currency, including cryptocurrency. The only exception is the specific merchant or notional accounts your bank arranges. Doing it breaks section 5(1)(e)(i) of the FER Act, 1947, and can be acted on under section 23(1) of the same Act, under S.R.O. No. 59-Law/2021 dated 8 March 2021.[^bb-export-consolidated]

This is Nusrat's real problem. A payment-platform balance is somewhere money passes through, not somewhere it lives. The circular says plainly that funds must not sit in notional accounts as if they were ordinary bank accounts, and that all receipts must be swept automatically into the bank's collection account.[^bb-export-consolidated]

One sensible-sounding reason for keeping money abroad is that paying for cloud hosting, contractors and software subscriptions is easier from a foreign balance. That need is real, and there is a legal way to meet it. It is the retention account explained further down. Your bank can send software registration, domain, hosting, server maintenance and account verification fees from it without asking Bangladesh Bank first.[^bb-export-consolidated] Use the legal pocket instead of the convenient one.

## Path A: ordinary physical goods

This section is the ordinary goods route. A qualifying prepaid home-delivery B2C e-commerce shipment of USD 1,000 or less follows the separate DDP process above and does not use EXP at Customs.[^bb-export-consolidated]

### The declaration

You have not been allowed to export goods without declaring them to Customs since Government Notifications No. 1(6)/ECS/48 and 1(7)/ECS/48 of 1 July 1948, issued under section 12 of the FER Act. The declaration confirms that the full export value has been, or will be, handled the way Bangladesh Bank directs.[^bb-export-consolidated]

For the ordinary route, that declaration is the **EXP form**. Your bank can give you a user ID for Bangladesh Bank's **Online Export Monitoring System (OEMS)** so you can create electronic EXP forms. Each form has an 11-digit number: four digits for your bank branch code, five for the branch's export register serial, and two for the year.[^bb-export-consolidated]

You submit the electronic EXP form **before** customs formalities. Customs then issues the Bill of Export using the EXP information already in its system, so nobody needs to certify the EXP form separately. Paper EXP forms are only used at customs houses that cannot issue the Bill of Export electronically.[^bb-export-consolidated]

### The 14-day fuse

Once the goods ship, three things have to happen fast:

1. **Report the shipment in OEMS as soon as it ships.** If you do not, your bank must do it from your documents within **14 days of the shipping date**.[^bb-export-consolidated]
2. **Give your bank the signed hard copy** of the electronic EXP form, the Bill of Export certified by Customs, and the shipping documents, **within 14 days of shipment**.[^bb-export-consolidated]
3. **Every shipping document** covering the goods on that EXP form must pass through your bank in the same 14 days, so the bank can report to Bangladesh Bank in time.[^bb-export-consolidated]

Rezaul shipped on 3 March. His documents are due at his bank by **17 March**. His payment is due by **3 July**. Neither date was on a calendar until he read this.

Your paperwork deadline is two weeks after shipping. Your money deadline is four months. Put both on the calendar before the container leaves.

### How the payment can reach you

Payment for exported goods and services can come to you through your bank in freely convertible foreign currency, including Chinese Yuan. It can also come in taka from a non-resident taka account, from escrow accounts under counter-trade arrangements, under "acquiring service" for payments made over the internet, and from online <Term name="payment-gateway">payment gateway</Term> service providers and other legitimate operators.[^bb-export-consolidated]

Notice what is not on that list. A personal foreign account, a friend's account abroad, and cash.

## Path B: services delivered over the internet

This is the sentence that separates the service route from the ordinary-goods route.

> **Rule:** When computer software or data entry and data processing services are exported in **non-physical form**, meaning over the internet or other electronic media, **the EXP procedure does not apply**. The payment is collected under the service-export rules instead.[^bb-export-consolidated]

The same treatment covers business process outsourcing, by firms and by individuals, and other service exports such as business, professional, research and advisory work.[^bb-export-consolidated] There is no shipment, so there is nothing for Customs to certify.

### What replaces EXP: a digital remittance record

Bangladesh Bank discontinued **Form-C and Form-C (ICT)** in the July 2026 circular. For an inward remittance, your bank now collects the payment purpose and any supporting information or documents through a secure digital interface. Until that interface is ready, the bank may collect the same material through its existing digital channels.[^bb-export-consolidated]

Ask the bank which channel it uses and exactly what supporting record it wants for your kind of service. There is no longer a USD 20,000 Form-C threshold to plan around. Your bank must still identify the purpose, check the supporting record and deduct any applicable tax before crediting the money.[^bb-export-consolidated]

### The routes your money can take

There are six legal channels for service income. Each has its own condition, and the third column is the one to read.

| Route | Where it fits | The limit that bites |
|---|---|---|
| Direct bank transfer to your account | Contract clients paying by wire | None stated. The four-month clock still applies |
| **Acquiring service** through your bank | Selling software or services on your own website by card | Needs a merchant agreement with your bank |
| **OPGSP** notional account | Small service exports, freelancing, BPO | **USD 10,000 per transaction**[^bb-export-consolidated] |
| Marketplace or payment service provider merchant account | App stores, platforms, payment aggregators, digital wallets | You must promise to bring the money home inside the legal period[^bb-export-consolidated] |
| "Freelancer card", a dual-currency international card | App and game developers with recognised training credentials | The bank still captures the remittance purpose and supporting record digitally[^bb-export-consolidated] |
| <Term name="mfs">MFS</Term> and payment service providers licensed by Bangladesh Bank | Small IT and e-commerce payments | Depends on the provider's own arrangement[^bb-export-consolidated] |

Read the OPGSP row twice. That route only works for non-physical service exports "of value not exceeding USD 10,000 per transaction".[^bb-export-consolidated] Nusrat invoices USD 11,000 a month. She is USD 1,000 over a ceiling she has never heard of.

Do not split the invoice into two payments to squeeze under it. Use the row above or the row below instead. The circular describes the marketplace and payment-service-provider route separately and does not repeat that per-transaction ceiling, and an ordinary bank wire has no such limit at all.[^bb-export-consolidated] Email your bank and ask which route it will use for an invoice your size. One email settles a year of guessing.

Whichever route you use, one rule never changes. Service exporters may hold **no accounts abroad for storing money**, other than the merchant or notional accounts described above.[^bb-export-consolidated]

## What actually lands in your account

Money that comes home splits in two. A set percentage can stay in foreign currency in an **Exporters' Retention Quota (<Term name="erq">ERQ</Term>) account** at your bank. The rest is converted to taka. The percentage depends on what you export.[^bb-export-consolidated]

| Exporter | How much you may keep in foreign currency |
|---|---|
| Software, data entry and processing, other ICT services | 50% of net export earnings brought home[^bb-erq-ict] |
| Goods with high local value added | 30% of the FOB value brought home |
| Other service exporters (non-physical) | 30% of receipts brought home |
| Goods with high imported content, such as apparel from imported woven fabric, POL products, electronic goods | 15% of the FOB value brought home |
| Resident agents and trade facilitators | 10% of commission earnings |

You can hold the balance in US Dollar, Pound Sterling, Euro, Japanese Yen or Chinese Yuan. Above USD 2,000 you can put it in an interest-bearing term deposit.[^bb-export-consolidated]

**Nusrat's month:**

```
Inward remittance, software services      USD 11,000
Purpose and supporting documents sent
through the bank's digital channel
ERQ retention at 50% (ICT services)       USD  5,500  <- stays in FC
Converted to taka for payroll, rent, tax  USD  5,500
```

**Rezaul's shipment:**

```
Repatriated FOB value                     USD 22,000
ERQ retention at 30% (high local value)   USD  6,600  <- stays in FC
Converted to taka                         USD 15,400
```

Two businesses of similar size. Nusrat can hold about 67% more foreign currency per dollar earned than Rezaul, purely because of what she sells. If either of them wants to spend abroad on an office, a conference or a foreign hire, that percentage is their budget. What you can and cannot do with an ERQ balance is on [cross-border expansion](/en/growth/cross-border-rules).

## The proof: your PRC

A **proceeds realisation certificate (<Term name="prc">PRC</Term>)** is the paper your bank issues to confirm that your export payment actually arrived. It is step 14 of the government's fourteen-step export process, the very last one.[^export-policy-2024]

Banks issue PRCs on a set form, through OEMS for direct exports and through the online import monitoring system for deemed exports. They must send the signatures of authorised signatories to the Bond Commissionerate and the Duty Exemption and Drawback Office, so PRCs can be checked.[^bb-export-consolidated] Banks are told to be extremely careful when issuing them, because a PRC unlocks bond facilities, duty drawback and cash incentives.

Do not confuse an incentive rate with the ERQ retention rate. The FY 2026–27 schedule lists a **6%** export incentive for software, ITES and hardware and **2.5%** for individual freelancers exporting software or ITES.[^bb-incentive-2026] Eligibility and audit conditions still apply, and the government can change these rates for the next fiscal year.

Ask for the PRC as soon as the money lands, not when somebody demands it. It is also the cleanest proof of a clean export record, whether for a bank, an investor checking your company, or your next registration renewal.

## What is exempt from declaring

The declaration and repatriation rules do not apply to genuine trade samples inside the Export Policy limits, travellers' personal effects, ships' stores and transhipment cargo, goods shipped on government or military order, gift packets sent with a declaration that the contents are under the Export Policy limit and no foreign exchange is involved, and packets covered by a Bangladesh Bank certificate saying no foreign exchange is involved. Customs allows the exemption once it is satisfied the export qualifies.[^bb-export-consolidated]

Two limits matter to a startup, and both are in the Export Policy 2024-2027. Samples other than medicine are capped at **USD 10,000 FOB per exporter per year**. Gift items or parcels are capped at **USD 2,000**, or the taka equivalent.[^export-policy-2024] Eligibility turns on the shipment value and the other exemption conditions, not the number of baskets or parcels. Add sample value across the year and let Customs confirm the exemption; quantity alone does not decide it.

Posting a parcel is not a way around this. In a 2024 Accelerating Bangladesh webinar on export basics, a participant described sending jamdani sarees and bags to buyers in India through the post office, taking 22 to 30 days, and said herself that it was not really export.[^ab-export-essentials] She was right, and not only because of the delay. Past the sample and gift limits, a postal parcel carries the same declaration and repatriation duties as a container, and the payment still has to reach you through your bank.

## If you are paid before you ship

Advance payment is common with new buyers, and it comes with conditions. Your bank takes a declaration on an Advance Receipt Voucher and reports it to Bangladesh Bank's online module. It must be satisfied that you hold an irrevocable LC or contract, that your past export record and capacity are good enough, and that the advance carries no interest. You must then complete the export **within one year** of getting the advance. If you do not, the advance may have to be refunded, taken first from your ERQ account and then from taka.[^bb-export-consolidated]

Treat that as a cash-flow warning. An advance is not money you can count as yours until the goods actually go.

## Selling to your own foreign company

If your buyer is a parent company, subsidiary or branch office you control, the usual declaration that you have no interest in the buyer does not fit. Since **4 August 2026**, Bangladesh Bank allows an intercompany declaration instead. You declare that the deal is at arm's length and reflects a competitive market price, and that you follow transfer-pricing rules and anti-money-laundering standards. This applies to exports as well as imports, and it changes the relevant paragraph of the July 2026 export circular.[^bb-intercompany]

Your bank must still check, on documents, that the relationship is genuine.[^bb-intercompany] If you are thinking about a foreign holding company or subsidiary, read [cross-border expansion](/en/growth/cross-border-rules) and [FDI rules](/en/funding/fdi-rules) before you set the transfer price, not after.

## When the money does not arrive

Buyers go quiet. Ships run late. Disputes happen. The rules expect this and give it a shape.

- **Late or missing payment can lead to punitive action** under the FER Act, 1947. If payment does not arrive, or arrives short, inside the set period, your bank must get a **full explanation** from you and send it to Bangladesh Bank.[^bb-export-consolidated]
- **Overdue cases are reported every month.** Banks upload overdue EXP form details to OEMS **by the 10th of each month**, with the reason, what they have done to collect, and any court case.[^bb-export-consolidated]
- **A short payment is not automatically fatal.** If the gap between the EXP form value and the bill is small and comes from genuine trade charges, the bank can accept the documents and record the adjustment on the form with its stamp and signature.[^bb-export-consolidated]
- **A lost shipment has its own steps.** If goods are lost in transit before you are paid, an insurance claim must be made as soon as the loss is known, and the insurance details written on the EXP form.[^bb-export-consolidated]

The moment a payment looks late, call your bank yourself with the dates and the correspondence. Somebody is going to write that explanation. Better it is yours than a reconstruction.

## Common mistakes

These mistakes either create an overdue record or leave you without the evidence your bank needs.

| Mistake | What happens | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|
| Leaving earnings in a payment platform or foreign account | Breaks section 5(1)(e)(i) of the FER Act, actionable under section 23(1)[^bb-export-consolidated] | Treat foreign balances as money in transit. Bring it home through your bank |
| Assuming service exports need an EXP form or Form-C | Weeks chasing declarations Bangladesh Bank does not use for internet-delivered services[^bb-export-consolidated] | Use the bank's digital inward-remittance channel and send the purpose and supporting record it requests |
| Assuming services escape the four-month clock | An overdue file with no explanation ready | The same four months apply to services[^bb-export-consolidated] |
| Splitting an invoice to fit under the USD 10,000 OPGSP ceiling | You are structuring a transaction to get around a stated limit | Ask your bank which route fits an invoice your size |
| Waiting for the C&F agent's documents | The 14-day window closes on your bank, not on the agent[^bb-export-consolidated] | Write the handover deadline into the agent's contract |
| Only asking for a PRC when you need it for an incentive | Chasing a certificate months after the fact | Ask for the PRC when the money lands |
| Spending an export advance as if it were revenue | You may have to refund it if the export does not happen within a year[^bb-export-consolidated] | Hold the advance against the order until it ships |

The first three turn a healthy export business into a compliance problem. All three come from not knowing which path you are on, which is why this page starts there.

## Checklist

- [ ] Path identified: ordinary physical goods, qualifying prepaid B2C DDP goods, or non-physical services
- [ ] Applicable deadline on a calendar: four months from shipping for an unpaid ordinary-goods export; the start date your bank confirmed for services; or one year to export against an advance
- [ ] For ordinary goods: OEMS user ID requested from your bank
- [ ] For ordinary goods: EXP declared before customs formalities, shipment reported in OEMS on the day it ships
- [ ] For ordinary goods: signed EXP, Bill of Export and shipping documents at your bank within 14 days
- [ ] For the small prepaid B2C DDP route: bank confirmed the USD 1,000 ceiling and every Part-J condition in writing; the separate declaration, courier, Bill of Export and reporting steps are assigned; no EXP was filed at Customs
- [ ] For services: the channel your bank will use confirmed in writing, with any per-transaction ceiling
- [ ] For services: remittance purpose and supporting documents sent through the bank's confirmed digital channel
- [ ] No foreign balance used as savings. Only merchant or notional accounts, in transit
- [ ] ERQ account open, and the right retention percentage being applied
- [ ] PRC requested as soon as the money arrives
- [ ] Any advance matched to an order that will ship within a year
- [ ] For sales to a company you control, the intercompany declaration discussed with your bank

**You are done when:** for every order you can name the route, the declaration that applies, the payment or delivery deadline, the route the money took, the amount credited to your retention account, and the PRC number, without asking your accountant.
