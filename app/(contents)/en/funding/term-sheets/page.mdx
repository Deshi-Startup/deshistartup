# Term Sheet Basics in Plain English

*This guide is adapted from Shoumik Shahriar's ["Term Sheet 101"](https://shoumik.substack.com/p/term-sheet-101) on SS Notes.*[^shoumik-term-sheets]

> **In short:** Will the investor wire the money immediately after a good first meeting? Absolutely not. First, they will send a 4-to-5 page Term Sheet. While it is not the final legal agreement, it establishes the company's valuation, investment amount, and what control the investor gets.

## Two Core Categories of Term Sheet Clauses

Every term sheet clause falls into one of two functional categories:

### 1. Economics — Who gets what?
- **Valuation:** The agreed Pre-money and Post-money equity valuation of the company.
- **Investment Amount:** Total capital being injected by the investor.
- **ESOP Pool:** Unallocated shares (typically 10%–15%) set aside for employee incentives, usually carved out of the pre-money valuation.
- **Liquidation Preference:** Determines payout order during an exit or winding up (who gets their money back first).

### 2. Control & Governance — Who makes key decisions?
- **Board Seats:** Number of directorship seats assigned to the investor.
- **Protective Provisions:** Reserved matters requiring explicit investor approval (e.g., selling the company, incurring debt, or changing core business scope).
- **Information Rights:** Contractual obligations to deliver monthly or quarterly financial statements and operational dashboards.

## The Impact of ESOP Pool on Pre-money Valuation

Investors usually require the ESOP pool to be created from the pre-money valuation. The larger the pool, the more the founders' shares get diluted. Negotiate a realistic pool size that matches your actual hiring needs.

**Example:** Suppose the Pre-money valuation is 10 Crore BDT. The investor puts in 2 Crore BDT. The Post-money valuation becomes 12 Crore BDT, and the investor gets 16.67% (2/12).

Here is how a 10% versus a 20% ESOP pool impacts founder ownership:

| Metric | 10% ESOP Pool | 20% ESOP Pool |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-money Valuation | 10 Crore | 10 Crore |
| Investment | 2 Crore | 2 Crore |
| Investor's Share | 16.67% | 16.67% |
| ESOP Pool | 10% | 20% |
| **Founder's Share** | **73.33%** | **63.33%** |

As you can see, a 20% pool drops founder ownership significantly to 63.33%.

## Liquidation Preference: What happens when the company is sold?

Liquidation preference dictates who gets paid first during a liquidity event. Always negotiate for a **1x Non-participating** preference.

**Example:** Suppose your company is sold for 15 Crore BDT. An early investor had put in 2 Crore BDT for 20% equity.

- **1x Non-participating:** The investor chooses between their 20% share of the exit (3 Crore BDT) OR their initial capital (2 Crore BDT). Since 3 Crore is higher, they take 3 Crore. The remaining 12 Crore goes to the founders.
- **1x Participating (Double dipping):** The investor first takes back their initial 2 Crore BDT. Then, they take 20% of the remaining 13 Crore (which is 2.6 Crore). They get a total of 4.6 Crore BDT. The founders are left with only 10.4 Crore BDT.

## Binding vs. Non-Binding Provisions

Most clauses in a term sheet are non-binding. However, two clauses become legally enforceable upon signing:

| Clause Name | Legal Status | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Valuation & Investment Amount | Non-binding | Subject to modification during final legal drafting. |
| Board Seats & Voting Rights | Non-binding | Negotiable until definitive agreements are signed. |
| **Exclusivity / No-Shop** | **Binding** | Prevents founders from negotiating with other investors for 30–60 days. |
| **Confidentiality** | **Binding** | Prohibits disclosing deal terms or valuation to third parties. |

## Bangladesh Context: Term Sheets and Next Steps

Once the term sheet is signed, several Bangladesh-specific nuances apply:
- **SHA and RJSC:** The term sheet is followed by the Shareholders' Agreement (SHA). Once the SHA is signed and funds are wired, you must file a Return of Allotment with the RJSC (Registrar of Joint Stock Companies and Firms).
- **Stamp Duty:** In Bangladesh, signing an SHA incurs stamp duty, which varies depending on the transaction value.
- **Enforceability:** The non-binding clauses of a term sheet are not enforceable in Bangladeshi courts. However, breaching binding clauses like exclusivity can lead to claims for damages.
- **Protective Provisions:** Local and international investors might include strict provisions regarding foreign loans or setting up subsidiaries. Review these carefully before they make it into the final SHA.
