# Investor CRM: A Fundraising Tracker You Can Actually Use

> **In short:** Keep a list of suitable investors, each conversation and the next step. Check what each investor funds before you contact them. Label amounts with their currency and explain your numbers. Record which files you shared. Count money as received only when it reaches the company. The aim is clear next actions, not a long list of names.

## Copy the tracker

CRM stands for customer relationship management. It is a way to track people and your contact with them. Here, you use it for investors. A private spreadsheet is enough to start. A <Term name="funding-rounds">funding round</Term> is one attempt to raise money for a stage of your business. Use two tabs: one for investors and one for conversations.

```text
INVESTORS — one row per fund or investor
Name | Official website | Relevant contact and role
Stages | Sectors | Geography | Investment size, if published
Lead or follow, if known | Relevant past investment
Source URL | Last checked | Fit: yes / unclear / no
Why we fit | Open question | Conflict or sharing restriction

CONVERSATIONS — one row per interaction or next action
Investor | Date | Founder owner | Channel
Round and amount sought | Currency | Metrics period
Stage | What actually happened | Evidence/reference
Deck version and link | Data shared and access scope
Next action | Owner | Agreed date
Objection or pass reason | Follow-up permission
Amount discussed | Amount agreed in documents | Cash received
```

Use business contact details that are public or shared with you for this purpose. Keep private notes and document links restricted to the fundraising team. Do not turn the tracker into a list of scraped personal contacts.

## Begin with a round brief

Use this once you can explain what the investment would fund and why your business could fit the investor. If those are unclear, polishing the spreadsheet will not help.

Write the next result you want to reach and what it will cost. Add the amount and currency you want to raise, cash you already have and how you will spend it. Set the date by which you need a decision. State whether you seek investment for shares, a loan or a grant. Each comes with different terms. Do not treat them as the same kind of money.

Keep one dated set of numbers. **Revenue** is sales income recorded for your business. It may be recorded before payment reaches the bank. <Term name="gmv">GMV</Term> means gross merchandise value: the full value of sales through a platform. Your revenue may be only part of that amount. If you run a marketplace, ask an accountant whether your revenue is the whole sale or just your fee.

## Verify fit from current sources

Read the investor's own website or form. Check which business stages, countries and sectors it funds. Look for how to apply and how much it invests. If a fact is missing, mark it unknown. One famous firm it funded does not tell you all its rules.

Use startup news and deal lists to find names. Then check what each investor funds now. ExitStack says its dashboard covers funding raised by founders locally in Bangladesh.[^exitstack-dashboard] It can help you find past deals. A past deal does not prove that the investor can fund your company today.

You can sort the first shortlist without inventing precision.

| Fit label | Evidence | Next action |
|---|---|---|
| Yes | Published mandate matches the company and round | Prepare a specific approach |
| Unclear | A relevant past deal, but current mandate is missing | Ask the unresolved fit question |
| No | Explicit stage, geography or sector mismatch | Remove from this round's active list |

Do not choose by fame. Start with investors who fund your kind of firm and explain how to apply. A famous fund that cannot invest at your stage is not a useful target for this round.

## Follow each recipient's actual requirements

Accelerating Asia's guide asks for money figures in USD. It warns against using GMV as revenue and asks founders to check that their files can be opened.[^aa-application] Startup Bangladesh's form asks for revenue, GMV and earlier investment in BDT.[^sbl-application]

So label every money figure with its currency. Keep a version of the figures for each form. If you convert BDT to USD, keep the BDT amount too. Record the exchange rate, its source and the date. Use a checked rate for a real form; this guide does not give a live rate.

For illustration only, BDT 1,200,000 divided by an **assumed** BDT 120 per USD equals USD 10,000. The assumption is not a live rate. Omitting the unit and entering 1,200,000 into a USD field would describe a very different business.

Check the application route as carefully as the numbers. Startup Bangladesh's “Let Us Know About Your Startup” page explicitly says its communication form is not an investment application.[^sbl-contact] Record a submission as an application only when it went through the correct route and you have the confirmation.

## Use stages that require evidence

The words in a pipeline should describe events you can point to.

| Stage | Evidence needed |
|---|---|
| Researched | A source-backed reason for fit |
| Contacted or applied | Dated message or submission confirmation |
| Meeting scheduled/completed | Calendar agreement or meeting notes |
| Follow-up or diligence | A specific request and responsible person |
| Terms under discussion | Actual proposed terms, with status recorded |
| Documents completed | Relevant agreements and remaining conditions checked |
| Funded | Company account receipt matched to the transaction |

An investor can pass or pause at any stage, even after a good meeting. Signed papers may still have steps to complete before payment. During <Term name="due-diligence">due diligence</Term>, the investor checks your records and claims. A request for those records is not a promise to invest. Use the tracker to record events. Get advice on what any signed terms mean.

## Write a short, relevant first message

Use an introduction when the mutual contact agrees, or use the investor's published application channel. Do not imply a relationship you do not have.

```text
Subject: [Company] — [clear business description], raising [amount/currency]

We help [customer] do [job]. In [period], we achieved [one measured result].
We are raising [amount and currency] to reach [specific milestone].
Your published focus on [specific mandate] appears relevant because [reason].
Here is our short deck: [approved link/version].
Is this within your current investment scope? If so, we would welcome
a short conversation or guidance on your preferred application process.
```

For a first message, send a short <Term name="pitch-deck">pitch deck</Term>: slides that explain the business and funding request. Leave out private customer, contract and staff details. Test whether the intended reader can open it. Keep your full <Term name="data-room">data room</Term>, the folder of detailed company records, private.

## Worked example: fewer active rows, better follow-through

Imagine a Bangladeshi software startup assembling 18 investor leads. This is an invented workflow example.

Five exclude its stage, three exclude its geography, six fit published criteria and four remain unclear. The active work is six tailored approaches and four fit checks. It is not 18 copies of the same email.

Suppose the six approaches lead to three meetings. One investor asks to check more records. The founder records the request for a table showing which customers stayed. A teammate will prepare it before the next meeting. Of the other two investors, one passes because the firm is too early. The other asks to hear back after more customers renew.

The money received remains **zero**. Applying a made-up “50% chance of closing” to an investor's possible cheque does not make that cash available for payroll. Keep scenario planning separate from confirmed receipts.

After each interaction, update the stage, key objection, exact next action and date. If the investor says “come back after three months of renewals,” record that evidence requirement rather than scheduling a generic reminder every week.

## Run a weekly review

Budget a few focused hours for the first verified shortlist, then reserve a suggested 30 minutes each week to review actions. Actual research and diligence time vary widely; no CRM makes a funding timeline predictable.

Check what you promised to send and what is late. Give each live conversation a next step. Look at why investors pass. If several ask for the same missing proof, do that work before sending more messages.

- [ ] Every active investor has a current source-backed fit reason.
- [ ] Currency, metric definition and reporting period are explicit.
- [ ] The correct application route is recorded.
- [ ] Shared documents have a version and intended access scope.
- [ ] Every active conversation has one owner and next action.
- [ ] Passes and follow-up preferences are respected.
- [ ] Cash received is separate from interest, proposed terms and signed documents.

Your first useful task is to verify ten plausible investors and remove the obvious mismatches. Use the [data room checklist](/en/funding/data-room) when a real diligence request arrives, and the [investor update template](/en/funding/investor-updates) for people who have agreed to receive updates.
