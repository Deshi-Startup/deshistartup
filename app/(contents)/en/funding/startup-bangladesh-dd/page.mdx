# Applying to Startup Bangladesh: Terms, Process and Checklist

> **In short:** Startup Bangladesh Limited is the ICT Division's venture capital fund. Its published terms are specific: BDT 25 lakh to 5 crore per round, a maximum 49% stake, and an expected exit in five to eight years.[^sbl-faq] By the fund's own account, getting to a term sheet takes **one to four months or longer**.[^sbl-faq2] So start this early. It is not a bridge for a company two months from running out of cash. The application also asks things founders do not expect: your other business involvements, your loan history, and whether you have guaranteed someone else's loan.[^sbl-application] Get those written down before you open the form. Your next step is the readiness list below.

Say Tasnia runs a healthtech company in Dhaka with four staff. She has a working product, about 900 paying users, and eleven months of runway. Someone tells her to "apply to Startup Bangladesh". She opens the form expecting to upload a pitch deck, and finds nine sections. One of them asks whether she has ever guaranteed anyone else's bank loan.

She has. Her question is whether that disqualifies her. That is the wrong question. The right one is whether she can explain it in one paragraph before anybody asks.

---

## Who should apply, and who should not

The fund publishes its own stage definitions. Find yours first.[^sbl-application]

- **Seed:** "Have a <Term name="mvp">MVP</Term>, tested in the market, have a customer base." Tasnia is here.
- **Growth:** "Have business in commercial operation with solid traction, existing customer and recurring revenue."

Then check the rest.

- **You are registered in Bangladesh with high growth potential or real social impact.** That is the published eligibility frame, along with having an MVP and a track record of growing customers and revenue.[^sbl-application]
- **You have an idea and no product.** Not yet. The published criteria start at a tested MVP with users.
- **You are a fund manager, not a founder.** There is a separate fund-of-funds route with its own expression-of-interest process. Different door, different form.[^sbl-what-we-do]
- **You need money inside eight weeks.** Unlikely, by the fund's own timeline. See the steps below and raise a bridge somewhere else.
- **Your company is not a technology or innovation business.** The published sector list is Fintech, Edtech, Agritech, SaaS, AI/ML, Logistic & Travel, Foodtech, Mobility/Logistics, Health, Entertainment and Lifestyle, and Others.[^sbl-application] "Others" is real, but the fund's mandate is technology-based innovation.[^sbl-who-we-are]

By the end you will know whether to apply now or in two quarters, and what to gather first.

## What this fund is, and why it matters

Startup Bangladesh Limited started in March 2020. It calls itself "the flagship venture capital fund of ICT Division" and "the first and only venture capital fund sponsored by the government". It launched with **BDT 500 crore**. It invests "in the form of <Term name="equity">equity</Term>, convertible debt and/or grants in pre-seed, seed and growth stage startups", and also works through co-investments, as a fund-of-funds, and as an asset manager.[^sbl-who-we-are] [^sbl-what-we-do]

The word *government* changes two practical things.

**The mandate is wider than returns.** Applicants are asked to show how they fit the SDGs, the National ICT Policy, the 8th Five-Year Plan and Vision 2041. The pitch deck is expected to cover impact and alignment with government vision alongside the business model.[^sbl-faq] [^sbl-faq2] A deck that only argues revenue growth answers half the question.

**The paperwork bar is the public-money bar.** Expect your compliance file to be checked properly, not waved through on founder chemistry. Everything in [investor due diligence and your data room](/en/funding/data-room) applies here with less slack, not more.

## The published terms

| Term | What the fund states |
|---|---|
| Transaction value | BDT 25 lakh to BDT 5 crore per round[^sbl-faq] |
| Ownership taken | No minimum. **Maximum 49%** stake in each company[^sbl-faq] |
| Instruments | Equity, convertible debt and/or grants[^sbl-who-we-are] |
| Expected exit | Within **5 to 8 years**, subject to market conditions[^sbl-faq] |
| Time to <Term name="term-sheet">term sheet</Term> | 1 month to 4 months or longer[^sbl-faq2] |
| Terms per deal | "Specific to the nature of the business model, <Term name="valuation">company valuation</Term>, the industry and market"[^sbl-faq2] |

Two rows deserve a second look. The **49% ceiling** means the fund will not hold a majority of your shares. That is worth knowing before you negotiate, but do not read it as a promise that you keep control. A minority shareholder can still get veto rights, board seats and consent rights through the shareholders' agreement, and none of that shows up in a percentage. Read the actual documents, and see [term sheets](/en/funding/term-sheets). The **5 to 8 year exit** is the number to hold against your own plans. It tells you what the fund needs to happen, and it frames the whole [valuation](/en/funding/valuation) conversation.

> **Warning:** The fund's two FAQ pages disagree about the lower limit. One says "BDT 25 lac to BDT 5 crore (per round)". The other, on the same site, says "BDT 50 lac to BDT 5 crore (per round)".[^sbl-faq] [^sbl-faq2] Both were live on 21 August 2026. Do not rule yourself in or out on either number. Ask the investment team what the current floor is, and get the answer in writing.

## The five steps, and where each one stalls

The fund describes its process like this.[^sbl-faq2]

1. **Initial screening**, looking at "a) Problem, b) Solution, c) Product-Market Fit, d) Market Fundamentals, e) Business Model, f) Team."
2. **Business, financial and legal due diligence**, plus structuring, for deals that pass screening.
3. **Deal structuring.**
4. **Investment Committee review and Board approval.**
5. **Share Subscription Agreement and other documents signed**, then the money is paid out.

A promising application gets an **Indication of Interest** and an invitation to a stakeholder meeting before diligence properly begins.[^sbl-faq2]

Each step has a different audience. An investment analyst reads step 1 and spends minutes, not hours, so your problem and your traction have to be clear fast. Finance and legal reviewers read step 2, and they do not care how good the story is. A committee handles step 4, and they see a written recommendation, not you. The most common mistake is polishing everything for step 1 and arriving at step 2 with nothing filed.

**The timeline is the real constraint.** One to four months *or longer* just to a term sheet. Then diligence, structuring, committee, board and documents still follow. If your runway maths assumes money inside a quarter, do the maths again.

## What the application actually asks

The form has nine sections. Most of it is what you would expect: company name, a product description capped at 400 characters, address, team headcount, tech team size, revenue and GMV in BDT, B2C and B2B client counts, formation type, incorporation date, number of existing investors, capital raised so far, pitch deck link, website, social profiles and a startup video link.[^sbl-application]

Three items surprise founders, all in the founders section.[^sbl-application]

- **CV links for every founder.** Not a bio in the deck. An actual link. Write these before you need them.
- **Other business involvements.** Every other company you are part of.
- **Loan history and guarantor status.** Bank name, amounts, whether anything is overdue, and whether you have guaranteed someone else's loan.

That last one is Tasnia's question. The published materials do not say what the fund does with the answer, so nobody can promise you it is harmless. What they do show is that the fund asks. The thing you control is whether they hear it from you or find it later, and being found out is the worse of the two. Private funds check founder credit too, so the habit is worth building beyond this one fund. The details are in [investor due diligence and your data room](/en/funding/data-room).

**The 400-character product description is a real constraint.** That is about two sentences. Write it separately. Cut it down to the problem, who has it, and what your product does about it. Then paste it in.

## The documents

Have these ready before you apply, not after.[^sbl-faq] [^sbl-faq2]

- [ ] **Investment pitch deck**, covering financial projections, business model, product-market fit, impact, fit with government vision and the SDGs, and company details including founder and team information
- [ ] **Certificate of Incorporation** and Memorandum & Articles of Association
- [ ] **Trade licence**, current
- [ ] **e-TIN** certificate
- [ ] **VAT registration (BIN)** certificate
- [ ] **Tax holiday permissions**, if you have any
- [ ] **Founder CV links**, one per founder
- [ ] **Loan and guarantor disclosure**, written out with amounts and status
- [ ] **Startup video link**, if you have one

Applications and materials go to the investment team at the fund's published address, `investment@startupbangladeshvc.gov.bd`.[^sbl-faq]

This list is shorter than a private fund's diligence pack, because this is the *application*, not the diligence. Everything in the six-folder data room matters at step 2. Putting it together before you apply is what turns a four-month process into a shorter one.

## Writing the impact section without SDG-washing

The fund asks about impact and fit with government vision. Founders often stick three SDG logos on a slide and move on. Reviewers notice, and it is the wrong instinct.

A claim you can defend has three parts.

1. **The specific outcome your product changes.** Not "improves healthcare", but "cuts the number of days between a rural patient's first symptom and a qualified consultation".
2. **How you know.** The measurement you already collect, with the current number.
3. **Why it holds as you grow.** Say whether the outcome comes out of your business model naturally, or is a cost you carry. Only the first one survives scale.

If you cannot fill in part 2 from data you already have, say so, and describe the measurement you are setting up. An honest gap reads better than a claim nobody can check, and the reviewer has seen a hundred of those.

## Checklist

- [ ] Your stage identified against the fund's published seed and growth definitions
- [ ] Current transaction floor confirmed with the investment team, in writing
- [ ] Runway rechecked against "1 to 4 months or longer" to term sheet, plus diligence and documents
- [ ] Product description written and cut to 400 characters
- [ ] Founder CV links live and shareable
- [ ] Other business involvements listed
- [ ] Loan history and any guarantees written out, with bank, amount and status
- [ ] Pitch deck covers business model, financial projections, product-market fit, impact and government-vision fit
- [ ] Impact claim has an outcome, a current measurement, and a reason it survives scale
- [ ] Incorporation certificate, MoA/AoA, trade licence, e-TIN and BIN certificates scanned
- [ ] Six-folder data room started, so step 2 does not begin from zero

**You are done when:** you can fill in the form in one sitting without stopping to hunt for a document, and every disclosure question is answered by a paragraph you wrote before anyone asked.
