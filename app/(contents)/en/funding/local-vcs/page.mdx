# Local VC in Bangladesh: Check the Fit Before You Pitch

> **In short:** A local venture capital (VC) investor may back a startup that can grow fast, usually in return for ownership or other agreed investment rights. A name on the Bangladesh Securities and Exchange Commission (BSEC) list does not tell you whether that investor is taking founder pitches now. First check what the investor backs, what stage your startup is at, and where founders should apply. Use the [investor directory](/en/directory/investors) for names and source dates, then the [investor tracker](/en/funding/investor-crm) for outreach.[^bsec-managers] [^bsec-funds]

This page is a first check for a founder building a scalable business in Bangladesh. It does not rank funds or promise that anyone will invest. If you are still deciding between your own revenue, a grant, a loan and selling shares, start with the [funding roadmap](/en/funding).

## Firm, fund or manager: which name are you seeing?

**VC** means venture capital: money invested in young businesses that investors believe can grow enough to return more than they put in. The deal may give an investor company shares, or use another agreed instrument. Ask what rights and future payments the actual deal creates. VC is different from a grant or an ordinary loan. A [term sheet](/en/funding/term-sheets) is a draft of the main deal terms. Read it before agreeing to a real offer.

An **investment firm** is the organisation you meet. A **fund** is a pool of money set up to invest. A **fund manager** runs a fund and decides how it invests within its rules. In Bangladesh, BSEC publishes separate lists of registered managers and funds under its alternative-investment rules. These rules cover private pools of investment money. For example, its fund list names IDLC Venture Capital Fund I and its manager, IDLC Asset Management Limited. Those are related names, but they are not the same entity.[^bsec-managers] [^bsec-funds]

The BSEC lists help you check what is registered under its alternative-investment rules. They do **not** say that every listed fund is investing today, fits your sector, or has a founder application open. Nor do they describe every possible startup investor in Bangladesh. For current fit and contact steps, check the investor's own site and ask its team if anything is unclear.[^bsec-managers] [^bsec-funds]

## Run these five checks before you pitch

1. **Is VC the right kind of money?** Write what the money would help you prove or deliver, and how the business could grow beyond its first customers. If repayments or giving up ownership would put the business under strain, compare other funding paths first.
2. **Does the investor back Bangladesh?** Look for a current statement about where it invests. A fund can have a Bangladesh address, a past Bangladesh deal or a global website without taking new Bangladesh pitches today. Ask if its current fund can invest in a Bangladesh-registered company.
3. **Do your stage and work fit?** Read the investor's own stage and sector notes, not a generic “top VC” list. For example, Anchorless says it mainly invests at seed stage (an early funding round), while IDLC Venture Capital says it supports young, scalable companies built around technology. These are examples of different stated focuses, **not** an open-application promise.[^anchorless] [^idlc]
4. **Who would invest, and on what terms?** Note the firm or fund name and whether the proposal is shares, a loan that may turn into shares, or something else. The exact company, rights and documents depend on the offer. Do not treat a fund's published size or a past deal as the amount you can get. Ask a Bangladesh investment lawyer to review a real offer.
5. **Is there a current founder route?** Follow the investor's official application page or current instructions. Check the page date and whether it is for founders. Startup Bangladesh's general “Let Us Know About Your Startup” form says it is **not** an investment application; its separate form for investing in other funds is for fund managers. Its [startup investment guide](/en/funding/startup-bangladesh-dd) explains the founder path.[^sbl-forms]

**Fictional example:** A Dhaka software team has a working product and a few paying customers. It finds a fund in a regulatory list, but the fund's own site says it only backs much later companies. The team marks it “not now” and checks another investor's current stage, Bangladesh focus and founder route before writing a pitch. A registered name alone did not answer the useful question.

Keep one short note per candidate:

```text
Investor and official site: __________
Why our stage, work and location fit: __________
What is still unconfirmed: __________
Current founder application or contact route: __________
Decision: approach now / check later / not a fit
```

## Where to go next

Use the [investor directory](/en/directory/investors) to find source-dated records. Open each investor's official site again before contacting it. If the fit looks real, use the [investor tracker](/en/funding/investor-crm) to record your evidence, conversation and next step. The [fundraising journey](/en/journeys/raise-angel-vc-funding) covers the pitch, the documents an investor may review and the proposed deal terms. A warm reply is not a funding commitment, and a signed term sheet is not cash in your account.
