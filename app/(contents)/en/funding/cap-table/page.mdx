# Cap Table Basics in Plain English

> **In short:** A <Term name="cap-table">Cap Table</Term> (Capitalization Table) is a master spreadsheet that breaks down exactly who owns what in your startup. It tracks how many shares each person holds, what they paid, and how ownership gets diluted over time as you raise more funds.

## Copy a starter cap table into a spreadsheet

Use this worksheet for a simple company with one share class, meaning its shares carry the same rights. Issued shares are shares the company has already assigned to holders. The worksheet separates them from shares covered by options and the unused option reserve. An option is a right to buy a share later; this simple model assumes one share per option. The last column includes all of these in the fully diluted total.[^carta-cap-table]

Paste the tab-separated block into cell A1 of an empty spreadsheet. Format column E as a percentage. Replace the zeros with your own share counts. Keep the sheet private because it contains ownership details.

```text
Holder	Issued shares	Options / reserve	Fully diluted shares	Fully diluted share
Founder 1	0	0	=B2+C2	=IF($D$5=0,"",D2/$D$5)
Founder 2	0	0	=B3+C3	=IF($D$5=0,"",D3/$D$5)
Unused option pool	0	0	=B4+C4	=IF($D$5=0,"",D4/$D$5)
Total	=SUM(B2:B4)	=SUM(C2:C4)	=SUM(D2:D4)	=IF(D5=0,"",SUM(E2:E4))
```

Say each founder has 50,000 issued shares, and you reserve 10,000 options. Enter 50,000 in B2 and B3, and 10,000 in C4. The fully diluted total is 1,10,000. Each founder's share of that total is 50,000 ÷ 1,10,000 = **45.45%**, and the unused pool is **9.09%**. The pool is not a shareholder, and those options are not issued shares.

For another holder, insert a row above Total and copy a holder row's D and E formulas into it. Check the formulas use that holder's row and the updated Total row, and that every total includes all holders. When you grant an option from the unused pool, move that count to the holder's row rather than counting it twice. Keep issued shares in column B and unexercised options or the unused reserve in column C. Once an option is exercised and the share is issued, move that count from C to B.

Before using the result, check the total is 100% and the issued counts match your company records. Keep share classes, grant dates, prices, vesting dates (when someone earns shares or options) and supporting documents in separate records. This starter does not calculate shares from a SAFE or convertible note. These are funding agreements that may turn into shares later. It also does not split voting rights or sale proceeds. Ask your company lawyer to check the full model before a funding round.

## Why Is a Cap Table Essential?

At inception, equity ownership appears simple—such as two co-founders splitting ownership 50-50. However, as investors enter, Employee Stock Option Pools (ESOP) are carved out, or [SAFE notes](/en/funding/safe-convertible-notes) convert, the equity equation rapidly becomes complex.[^shoumik-cap-table]

A clean cap table helps you accomplish three vital functions:
1. **Ownership Clarity:** Gives an immediate snapshot of fully diluted ownership across founders, employees, and investors.
2. **Dilution Modeling:** Lets you simulate future fundraising rounds to see exactly how your personal stake will shrink.
3. **Governance & Control:** Helps track board control and voting rights before you sign a [Term Sheet](/en/funding/term-sheets).

## Share Dilution: A Step-by-Step Scenario

The most common failure mode for founders is not understanding dilution. Let’s walk through a practical scenario.

Imagine you and your co-founder start with 1,00,000 shares total (50,000 each). This is a fully diluted model: reserved options are included alongside issued shares. Whole-share counts and percentages below are rounded for illustration; reconcile the exact allotment and rounding in the legal documents.

**Step 1: Angel Round**
You raise 10 Lakh BDT at a 1 Crore BDT post-money valuation. The angel gets 10%.
- New shares issued: 11,111
- Total shares become: 1,11,111
- Your ownership: 45%, Co-founder: 45%, Angel: 10%.

**Step 2: ESOP Pool Created**
You carve out a 10% options pool for future hires.
- Shares reserved for options, not yet issued: approximately 12,345
- Fully diluted total: approximately 1,23,456
- Your ownership dilutes down to 40.5%.

**Step 3: Seed Round**
You raise 50 Lakh BDT at a 5 Crore BDT pre-money valuation.
- Share price: 5 Crore / 1,23,456 = 405 BDT per share.
- New shares issued: 50 Lakh / 405 = 12,345 shares.
- Total shares become: 1,35,801.

**Final Cap Table:**

| Shareholder | Total Shares | Ownership % |
|---|---|---|
| Founder 1 | 50,000 | 36.8% |
| Founder 2 | 50,000 | 36.8% |
| Angel Investor | 11,111 | 8.2% |
| ESOP | 12,345 | 9.1% |
| Seed Investor | 12,345 | 9.1% |

## Vesting: Protecting the Equity

A four-year <Term name="vesting">vesting</Term> schedule with a one-year cliff is a common founder arrangement, not an automatic rule. The agreement must define what happens to unvested rights and the lawful transfer process. Issued ownership and vested entitlement are different records.

Imagine your co-founder Arif joins in January 2024 with 40,000 shares.
- **1-Year Cliff:** If Arif leaves before 12 months, none of the 40,000 shares have vested.
- **After 1 Year:** In January 2025, 10,000 shares vest. They are no longer subject to the agreed unvested-share conditions.
- **Monthly Thereafter:** The remaining 30,000 shares vest over the next 36 months (roughly 833 shares per month).
- **Early Departure:** After 18 months, 40,000 × 18 ÷ 48 = 15,000 shares have vested; 25,000 remain unvested. Apply the documented leaver provisions with counsel. Shares do not automatically return to the company. Avoid rounding each month's fraction before calculating the cumulative entitlement.

## Cap Tables and RJSC Compliance in Bangladesh

A spreadsheet cap table is useful for modelling ownership, but it is not the company's statutory register. Under the Companies Act, the company must keep a register of members showing each member and the shares held; entries in that register and the company's share certificates are prima facie evidence.[^companies-act-cap-table]

- **Form XV (Return of Allotment):** When a company with share capital allots shares, it must file the return and any required supporting documents with the Registrar within 60 days of the allotment.[^companies-act-cap-table]
- **Director appointments:** Form IX records a director's consent to act; the signed consent must be filed within 30 days of appointment. Form XII records directors' particulars and any appointment or change, and must be filed within 14 days. Form X is the list of consenting initial directors filed with the incorporation application; it is not a return of allotment.[^rjsc-director-filings]

Keep the cap table, register of members, share certificates, corporate approvals, and RJSC filings aligned. Discrepancies can raise questions or delay due diligence. Ask a Bangladeshi company lawyer or qualified practitioner what corrective filing, if any, is needed.

## Three Dangerous Cap Table Mistakes

1. **Excessive Early Dilution:** Giving away 40%–50% equity in an angel round leaves founders under-incentivized. Series A VCs usually run away from cap tables where founders own too little.
2. **Unvested Founder Equity:** If a co-founder leaves without a vesting schedule in place, they take their massive equity chunk with them, making the startup uninvestable.
3. **Unwritten Equity Promises:** Casual verbal or email promises like "I'll give you 2% for your help" lead to disastrous legal disputes. Document everything.
