# International Investors: Pitching, Due Diligence, and Etiquette

> **In short:** Treat a foreign raise as a relationship, not one pitch. Deshi Startup recommends sending 3–6 months of honest, metric-driven updates before asking for a check, learning your seven numbers, putting product and traction before country macros, and keeping an audit-ready data room. Defensiveness in Q&A and a messy <Term name="cap-table">cap table</Term>, the record of who owns what share of the company, can stop a deal.

Imagine Sami, founder of an enterprise healthtech startup in Dhaka. Through a mutual contact he secures a 25-minute video call with an investment principal at a Singapore venture fund.

Twelve minutes in, Sami is still on macro slides — Bangladesh's 170 million people, smartphone penetration, GDP growth. The investor nods politely and asks a single question: *"What is your monthly net burn and gross margin?"*

Sami hesitates. *"We are very frugal and spend carefully as needed."*

Forty minutes later a templated rejection lands: *"Thank you for sharing your journey. While the Bangladesh market is exciting, this is too early for our mandate."*

Sami reads that email as a verdict on Bangladesh. It is not. The investor passed because a founder who cannot state his own gross margin from memory has told them something about how the company is run — and no amount of market size fixes that. Everything below is about not being Sami on that call.

## Who needs this, and when

Use the table to decide whether foreign venture equity fits the kind of company you are building, not whether the company is good or bad.

| Startup Profile | Relevance | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Venture-Scale Tech (SaaS, FinTech, B2B, Logistics)** | **High** | LightCastle reported only USD 6 million of startup funding in H1 2026, all from global investors. Its USD 124 million total for 2025 was dominated by one USD 110 million merger.[^lightcastle-investors] That concentration is why an institutional raise often means speaking to foreign funds. |
| **Global Export Software & AI** | **High** | Foreign investors bring USD capital plus commercial distribution networks across Southeast Asia, Europe, and North America. |
| **Traditional SME / Lifestyle Business** | **Low** | Venture funds are built for outlier returns, so a sound traditional business may still lack a plausible fund-returning outcome. Look instead at [bank loans](/en/funding/bb-startup-fund) or [revenue-share financing](/en/funding/islamic-finance). |

The dividing line in that table is not company quality. It is whether a fund can plausibly return its whole fund from your one deal. A profitable BDT 4-crore-a-year business is a better company than most startups and still a bad fit for a venture fund — those two facts are not in conflict, and hearing "no" is not a judgement on the business.

You can skip this guide if your business does not need institutional venture equity, or you are bootstrapping on customer revenue.

## The minimum mental model: the three frontier filters

Use these three questions to prepare for the risks a foreign venture investor is likely to test. They are Deshi Startup's organising frame, not a universal investor scorecard.

| Filter | What the Investor Worries About | How a Prepared Founder Answers |
|---|---|---|
| **1. The Currency & Exit Hurdle** | BDT depreciation against USD, and the legal path for returning capital at exit. | Explains the currency plan and takes legal and tax advice on any [holding-company structure](/en/funding/fdi-rules) before creating one. |
| **2. Capital Efficiency** | Will this startup spend heavily before reaching product-market fit? | Uses its own budget to show how much runway the raise buys and which product or customer milestone it reaches. |
| **3. Execution Velocity ("Lines, Not Dots")** | Can these founders hit quarterly targets and navigate hard local logistics? | Builds a track record with transparent, metric-driven monthly updates *before* asking for a check. |

Notice what none of the three filters ask about: the size of the Bangladeshi market. That question was settled before the call was booked. Sami spent twelve minutes answering a question nobody had.

### 1. The currency and exit hurdle

The taka has historically depreciated against the US dollar. A fund investing dollars will therefore model what local-currency growth becomes in its own reporting currency. Ask which exchange-rate and return assumptions it is using. It will also examine how exit proceeds can move under the applicable foreign-exchange and corporate rules. Do not create an [offshore holding company](/en/funding/fdi-rules) only because a deck template says so; take Bangladesh and destination-country legal and tax advice first.

### 2. Make the capital-efficiency case from your own budget

A lower operating cost is an advantage only when your model proves what it buys. Show the roles you will hire, their monthly cost, the product or customer milestone you expect to reach, and the runway funded by the raise. Compare milestone per dollar from your own budget, not from an invented Silicon Valley benchmark. Low burn is not a moat by itself; it matters only if it lets the team learn, ship, and win customers efficiently.

### 3. Build a track record before the ask

An investor meeting you once sees a single data point — a "dot". They cannot tell from it whether you are accelerating or flattening out. Deshi Startup's preparation rule is to send brief, honest monthly updates for 3–6 months before asking for money, so those dots join into a visible trajectory — a "line". This is editorial guidance, not a universal investor screening requirement. The email structure is on the [Monthly Investor Update Template](/en/funding/investor-updates) page.

This is the cheapest advantage in this entire guide and the one founders skip most often, because it requires starting six months before you need the money.

## A dilution stress test, not a market benchmark

There is no universally correct early-round dilution percentage. The three cases below are editorial planning scenarios: use them to see how today's round compounds with later rounds, not as evidence of what the market will offer.

The table turns those three cases into questions you can take into a cap-table review.

| Planning case | Dilution | Question to answer |
|---|---|---|
| **Lower-dilution case** | 15% | Does the cash buy a meaningful milestone without leaving the company underfunded? |
| **Higher-dilution case** | 20% | What do later option pools and rounds do to founder ownership? |
| **Stress case** | 35% | Is the check and milestone valuable enough to justify the cumulative ownership cost? |

The stress case makes the cumulative effect visible. If the founding team gives up 35% at pre-seed, then 20% at seed and 20% at Series A, it retains `0.65 × 0.80 × 0.80 = 41.6%` before any option-pool top-up. That does not make 35% a universal deal-breaker. It gives you a number to place beside the cash and milestone bought.

Run the exact proposed round through your [cap table](/en/funding/cap-table), including every signed instrument and planned option pool. Negotiate from that result, not from a borrowed benchmark.

## The seven numbers you must know cold

Deshi Startup's preparation card uses seven numbers. Learn them well enough to answer without searching through your model during a call. The prompts in the last column are editorial preparation notes, not reported Bangladesh market benchmarks or universal screening criteria; your actual history and model come first.

| Metric | Plain Meaning | Planning prompt, not a rule |
|---|---|---|
| **Monthly gross revenue / GMV** | Total transaction value processed on your platform per month. | Show the recent month-over-month sequence, including flat or down months. |
| **Monthly net revenue** | Gross revenue minus refunds, discounts, and platform or gateway fees. | Shows real monetisation, not just volume. |
| **Gross margin (%)** | (Net revenue − direct delivery and server costs) ÷ net revenue. | Show your actual margin and name the direct costs; do not borrow another sector's target. |
| **Customer acquisition cost (CAC)** | Total sales and marketing spend divided by new paying customers won. | Count all ad spend and sales commissions. Details in [Unit Economics](/en/metrics/unit-economics). |
| **LTV to CAC ratio** | Lifetime gross profit per customer divided by CAC. | State the ratio from your own cohorts and every assumption behind lifetime value. |
| **Monthly net cash burn** | Total cash out minus total cash in, per month. | State a clear figure, e.g. "$4,500 a month". |
| **Cash runway** | Total bank balance divided by monthly net burn. | State the exact months left and the month in which fundraising must start. |

Investors are not testing recall for its own sake. Each number is a proxy for a habit: knowing your burn means you watch the bank account, knowing your CAC means you measure marketing rather than trust it, and knowing your gross margin means you understand what you actually sell. That last one is where Sami came apart, so it is worth taking apart properly.

### Gross margin, the number Sami fumbled

**1. Name.** Gross margin.

**2. Plain meaning.** Out of every 100 taka a customer pays you, how many are left after the costs of serving that specific customer.

**3. Sami's own numbers.** His healthtech platform bills a clinic BDT 20,000 a month. Serving that clinic costs him BDT 3,000 in cloud hosting, BDT 1,500 in SMS and payment gateway fees, and BDT 2,500 for the part-time support agent who handles their tickets.

**4. The arithmetic.**

```
Direct cost to serve = 3,000 + 1,500 + 2,500 = BDT 7,000
Gross profit         = 20,000 - 7,000        = BDT 13,000
Gross margin         = 13,000 / 20,000       = 65%
```

Note what is *not* in that list: Sami's own salary, office rent, and the two engineers building next quarter's features. Those costs exist whether or not this clinic signs, so they sit below the gross margin line. Founders routinely include them, report a much worse margin than they have, and confuse everyone including themselves.

**5. What Sami says next time.** *"Gross margin is 65%, mostly cloud and gateway fees. Net burn is $4,200 a month against $29,000 in the bank, so about seven months of runway — which is why I'm raising now rather than in December."*

That is fourteen seconds. It answers the question asked, volunteers the follow-up question, and demonstrates the habit the investor was actually testing for.

## The 25-minute remote pitch

If an invitation gives you 25 minutes, use the rehearsal structure below. It is a Deshi Startup agenda, not a universal investor format.

| Time Window | Presentation Segment | Core Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| **00:00–02:00** | Professional check-in | One-sentence value proposition and meeting alignment. |
| **02:00–05:00** | Customer pain | The specific, painful problem in Bangladesh or the global market. |
| **05:00–12:00** | Product & traction | Live product demo, 6-month revenue growth, and cohort retention. |
| **12:00–17:00** | Market & moat | Target market size (TAM — the total annual spend if you won every possible customer), business model, and founder unfair advantage. |
| **17:00–25:00** | Q&A & next steps | Direct answers to investor risks, and agreeing data room access. |

**Cohort retention**, which appears in the 05:00–12:00 slot, means grouping customers by the month they joined and tracking how many of each group are still paying three, six, and twelve months later. It can be a useful early-stage chart because it separates a business that keeps customers from one that merely keeps buying new ones.

The structure puts product and traction before market size on purpose. That ordering is the exact inverse of what Sami did, and it is the most common fixable error in this guide.

### Critical remote pitching etiquette

- **Hardware and audio quality:** test your microphone, lighting, and connection before the call. Echoey audio or a dark camera feed reads as low professional polish, fairly or not.[^accelerating-asia-etiquette]
- **Skip the country overview:** foreign investors already know Bangladesh has 170 million people and high mobile penetration. Spend at most 60 seconds on macro context and the rest on your product, retention cohorts, and distribution economics.[^accelerating-asia-etiquette]
- **Address failures honestly:** when asked about problems, never claim everything is fine. Say something like *"our courier return rate spiked to 19% in Q2 because of regional floods, so we added mandatory phone verification and brought it back to 11%"*. That answer proves operational maturity in a way no growth chart can.[^accelerating-asia-etiquette]

The third point is worth more than it looks. Investors already assume something is broken — every startup has something broken. The information they are actually gathering is whether you know which thing, and what you did about it.

## The due diligence data room

Once an investor decides to write you up in an investment memo, they will ask for a virtual data room. That is a secure Google Drive, Notion or DocSend folder. Build it before anyone asks so ownership gaps can be found, corrected or disclosed before a reviewer opens the folder.

The full folder list, what each reviewer checks your documents against, and the Bangladesh compliance gaps that stall diligence are in [investor due diligence and your data room](/en/funding/data-room). Four things are specific to raising from abroad, and they belong in the folder from day one.

- **The foreign company's papers.** <Term name="incorp-certificate">Certificate of Incorporation</Term> and <Term name="moa-aoa">Articles of Association</Term> for the [Singapore Pte. Ltd. or Delaware C-Corp](/en/funding/fdi-rules), not just the Bangladeshi operating company.
- **The inward-equity trail.** Keep AD bank-issued encashment certificates for capital remittances already received and evidence that each share issue was intimated or reported through Bangladesh Bank's FEID route. Include BIDA or the relevant zone-authority registration only where it applies to the industrial project; it is not a universal foreign-investment document.[^bb-inward-equity] [^bida-registration-scope]
- **A fully diluted [cap table](/en/funding/cap-table).** Show ordinary shares, preferred shares and the **ESOP pool**, the shares set aside for employee options. Check it against every signed [SAFE, convertible note or earlier term sheet](/en/funding/safe-convertible-notes).
- **IP assignment agreements** signed by all founders, employees and contractors, assigning the work to the entity the investor is buying into.

That last line is an ownership risk. If the contractor who wrote your first version in 2023 never signed an assignment, your company may be unable to show clearly that it owns the product. In a holding-company structure, the assignment must also name the entity the investor is buying into. Check and correct this before sharing the data room.

## Common mistakes

Use the table to rehearse the disclosure or correction you will make before a reviewer raises each issue.

| Mistake | Why It Breaks the Deal | Safe Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Defensiveness during technical Q&A** | Investors probe weaknesses to see how founders handle pressure; defensiveness signals low coachability.[^accelerating-asia-etiquette] | Acknowledge the risk calmly: *"That's a real risk. Here's our current mitigation..."* |
| **Messy cap tables with dead equity** | Inactive holders or unsigned equity arrangements create questions about control, incentives and what a later investor is buying. | Reconcile every grant with the signed vesting or buyback terms, then take counsel on any change. Do not impose a supposedly standard schedule retroactively. See [Cap Table Basics](/en/funding/cap-table). |
| **Pitching US multiples to frontier investors** | Copying a Silicon Valley SaaS multiple ignores the company's market, currency, growth and exit path. | Compare only with disclosed regional rounds you can document, then show how the proposed valuation follows from your own round size, dilution and milestone. |
| **Disappearing between rounds** | Reaching out only when cash runs out gives the investor no track record to assess. | Send monthly updates whether the numbers are up or down. See [Investor Updates](/en/funding/investor-updates). |

Rows one and four are the same mistake at different speeds: both are a founder managing the investor's impression instead of giving them information. The relationship that produces a check is built out of unflattering facts delivered on time.

## Your seven numbers, on one card

Fill this in and keep it open on a second screen during every investor call, until you no longer need it.

```text
  Month:  ____________

  1. Monthly gross revenue / GMV:        BDT/USD ____________
     MoM growth vs last month:           ____________ %
  2. Monthly net revenue:                BDT/USD ____________
  3. Gross margin:                       ____________ %
     (what the direct costs are:  ____________________________)
  4. CAC:                                BDT/USD ____________
  5. LTV : CAC ratio:                    ____________ : 1
  6. Monthly net burn:                   BDT/USD ____________
  7. Cash in bank:                       BDT/USD ____________
     Runway (bank / burn):               ____________ months

  The one thing that went badly this month, and what I did:
  ____________________________________________________________

  My raise: ____________ at a ____________ post-money cap,
  which gives me ____________ months of runway.
```

The last two lines are not decoration. A founder who cannot say what the money buys, in months, has not finished deciding how much to raise.

## Checklist

- [ ] Prepared a concise pitch deck in PDF; this guide uses 10–12 slides as an editorial planning range, not a universal requirement.
- [ ] Memorised the seven core numbers and can say gross margin and runway in one breath.[^accelerating-asia-etiquette]

- [ ] Reconciled every signed [SAFE or convertible instrument](/en/funding/safe-convertible-notes) with the cap table and the [holding-company structure](/en/funding/fdi-rules).
- [ ] Built an investor nurture list and started sending monthly email updates.
- [ ] Assembled an audit-ready data room, including the four cross-border items above.
- [ ] Confirmed every founder, employee, and past contractor has signed an IP assignment.
