# Bangladesh Bank Startup Fund: 4% Startup Loans and Equity Finance

> **In short:** Commercial banks lend to registered, innovative, scalable startups at a maximum 4% a year, refinanced by Bangladesh Bank's BDT 500-crore revolving fund. If your company is under 12 years old and every founder is 21 or older, you can borrow up to BDT 8 crore — term up to 8 years, grace up to 2 years — but the 4% is only a price cap: each bank still decides security and approval under its own credit policy. Work out your repayment coverage first, then apply at a bank's SME/startup desk.

Imagine Nabil and Farhana, co-founders of an agritech platform in Bogura. After twelve months of operations, their software connects 200 vegetable farmers with Dhaka grocery retailers. They need BDT 25 Lakh to lease a collection warehouse, buy cold-storage crates, and expand their delivery team.

An early angel offers exactly that — BDT 25 Lakh, for a permanent 20% stake. When Nabil tries the other route and walks into a commercial bank branch, the manager asks for a land title deed or an apartment mortgage worth BDT 50 Lakh. Nabil owns neither.

So Nabil and Farhana are stuck where thousands of Bangladeshi founders get stuck: **give away a fifth of the company for one warehouse, or stop growing because nobody in the family owns spare land.**

Bangladesh Bank built its startup finance framework for exactly this problem. This guide covers how the loan route works today, what changed in the July 2025 master circular, how the 4% maths compares with equity, and — the part the circular does not answer — what you offer as security when you have no land.

## Who needs this, and when

| Startup Situation | Applicability | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **Registered, tech-based business with traction** | **High** | Banks can lend up to BDT 8 crore at a maximum 4% a year, refinanced by Bangladesh Bank.[^bb-master-circular] |
| **Founders seeking to preserve early equity** | **High** | A capped 4% loan is a fixed, known cost — unlike 15%–25% equity surrendered forever.[^bb-master-circular] |
| **Pre-revenue idea / unvalidated concept** | **Low** | Banks approve loans under their own credit policy, so expect to show registration documents, 6–12 months of operating history, or a working MVP. |
| **New venture of a large industrial group** | **Ineligible** | The master circular excludes new ventures of large industries or groups, and restructures or divisions of existing businesses.[^bb-master-circular] |
| **Traditional commodity trading or retail shop** | **Ineligible** | The business must be innovative, technology-based and scalable under the circular's definition.[^bb-master-circular] |

Two of those five rows are hard exclusions rather than discouragement. If you are a division being spun out of a bigger group, or a trading business without a technology angle, no amount of paperwork gets you in — check that first, before spending weeks on projections.

You can skip this guide if your startup is a venture-scale software platform that will burn cash for years without operating cash flow. Equity is the right tool there — see [SAFE, Convertible Notes, and Priced Rounds](/funding/safe-convertible-notes).

## The minimum mental model: two rails

The framework Bangladesh Bank runs today has two separate rails: one for **loans**, one for **equity**.[^bb-master-circular]

| Rail | How the money moves | What it means for you |
|---|---|---|
| **Loan rail** | A bank lends you its own money at a maximum 4% a year. Bangladesh Bank then *refinances* the bank — reimburses it at 0.5% — from a BDT 500-crore revolving fund. | Your interest cost is capped; the bank's risk is cut, which is why banks take part at all. |
| **Equity rail** | Since 2021 every scheduled bank has set aside **1% of its annual net profit** into its own startup fund. Since the July 2025 master circular, that money no longer finances new loans — it will go as equity into a **venture capital company** Bangladesh Bank is forming to invest in startups. | The bank route now includes equity investment of BDT 2–8 crore by stage, but detailed guidelines are still to come in a separate circular. |

Two words in that table do the heavy lifting, so take them slowly.

"Revolving" means the fund recycles. When you repay, the money returns to the BDT 500-crore pool and refinances the next startup's loan. The pool is not a queue that empties.

"Refinance" does **not** mean the bank hands you Bangladesh Bank's money. The bank lends its own money first, then gets reimbursed at 0.5%. That is a very cheap funding source for the bank, and it is the only reason a bank will accept a 4% ceiling on a loan it would otherwise price in the mid-teens.

That second point has a consequence worth holding on to: the bank is not doing you a favour, and it is not taking a policy instruction. It is taking a commercial deal that only works if you repay. Everything in the credit process below follows from that.

## Loan terms and parameters

Four numbers decide whether this scheme fits your plan, so read them in prose before the reference table.

**How much you can borrow depends on how old the company is, not how big the plan is.** Under 2 years old, the ceiling is BDT 2 crore. Between 2 and 6 years, BDT 5 crore. Between 6 and 12 years, BDT 8 crore. Term loan and working capital share that one ceiling — it is not two separate budgets.

**The price is capped at 4% a year, charged quarterly on the outstanding balance.** Quarterly matters. A founder who budgets a monthly EMI out of habit will be short three times a year.

**The term runs up to 8 years, and that 8 years includes the grace period.** A 2-year grace does not buy you a 10-year loan; it buys you 6 years of principal repayment inside the same 8.

**The grace period is 6 months to 2 years**, set by the type of venture rather than by negotiation.

Everything else the circular fixes is here.[^bb-master-circular] All figures govern the scheme from 9 July 2025.

| Parameter | What the circular says |
|---|---|
| **Loan size (term + working capital combined)** | Set by the company's operational age: **under 2 years** → up to BDT 2 crore; **2–6 years** → up to BDT 5 crore; **6–12 years** → up to BDT 8 crore. |
| **Interest rate** | Maximum **4% a year**, charged **quarterly** on the outstanding amount (compounding). Bangladesh Bank refinances the bank at 0.5%.[^tbs-startup-fund] |
| **Term loan** | Up to **8 years including the grace period**. |
| **Grace period** | **6 months to 2 years**, depending on the type of venture. |
| **Working capital** | Renewable, up to 8 years from first disbursement; beyond that, at market rates. |
| **Founder age** | **21 or older**, with no upper age limit. |
| **Company age** | Registered in Bangladesh no more than **12 years** ago (trade licence or RJSC date). |
| **Security** | Set by each bank's own credit policy — the circular does not waive collateral. |
| **Women entrepreneurs** | At least **10%** of each bank's scheme lending must go to women entrepreneurs. |
| **Priority** | Startups selected through government, university or international "startup hunting" programmes get priority.[^bb-master-circular] |

> **Keep in mind:** The 4% is a price cap, not a promise. The bank still runs you through its normal credit appraisal, and can refuse outright or lend less than your stage ceiling.

## The collateral question the circular does not answer

This is Nabil's actual problem, and the circular does not solve it. The security row above is the shortest row in the table and the most consequential: Bangladesh Bank caps what the loan can cost, and leaves what you must pledge entirely to each bank's own credit policy.[^bb-master-circular] There is no statutory collateral waiver in this scheme. Anyone who tells you a 4% startup loan is automatically unsecured is wrong.

What that leaves is a negotiation, and land is only one of the things a bank will consider. In Bangladeshi SME lending the usual alternatives, in roughly the order banks accept them, are:[^accelerating-bd-banking]

- **A charge over the assets the loan buys.** Nabil's cold-storage crates, racking, and delivery vehicles get hypothecated to the bank. This is the most natural fit for the scheme, because the loan creates the security.
- **Personal guarantees from the directors.** Cheap to give, and genuinely risky — a personal guarantee follows you personally if the company fails. Understand it before signing, not after.
- **A third-party guarantee.** A financially established guarantor who is not a founder. This is where a supportive family member helps without a land deed changing hands.
- **A charge over receivables.** If Nabil's grocery retailers pay on 30-day terms, that invoice book is an asset a bank can lend against.
- **Partial cash security or a lien on a fixed deposit.** Common as a top-up when the other pieces do not quite cover the exposure.

These are practitioner-reported patterns from bank startup desks, not entitlements written into the circular — a specific bank may want all of them, or something else entirely.[^accelerating-bd-banking]

> **Rule:** Ask what security the bank wants in the first meeting, in writing, before you build the full application. A bank that will only take land is a bank you should find out about in week one, not week nine. Ask two banks, and treat security terms as comparable — because they are.

## Debt vs equity: the worked comparison

Back to Nabil and Farhana's choice: borrow BDT 25,00,000 at 4%, or sell 20% to the angel.

**The loan side is known in advance.** With a 5-year term and a 1-year grace period, interest is billed quarterly on the outstanding balance.

- Year 1 (grace): interest only — 1% of the outstanding balance per quarter. 4 quarters × BDT 25,000 = **BDT 1,00,000**.
- Years 2–5: the BDT 25,00,000 is repaid in 16 quarterly instalments of about **BDT 1,69,858** each.
- Total interest over 5 years: **about BDT 3,17,700**. Total repaid: about BDT 28,17,700.

**The equity side is unknown, and potentially much larger.** The cost of equity is the stake you gave away, valued at whatever the company turns out to be worth. Say the company sells for BDT 2 crore in year 6: 20% × BDT 2,00,00,000 = **BDT 40,00,000**. That is over twelve times the loan's interest cost. At a BDT 10-crore exit, the same stake is worth BDT 2 crore.

| Metric | 4% Loan (BDT 25 lakh, 5-year term) | Angel Equity (20% dilution) |
|---|---|---|
| **Cash in** | + BDT 25,00,000 | + BDT 25,00,000 |
| **Total cost** | ≈ BDT 3,17,700 interest, fixed and known in advance | 20% of future company value — BDT 40 lakh at a 2-crore exit, unbounded above that |
| **Payments while building** | Quarterly, from day one of the grace period | None |
| **Ownership after 5 years** | 100% retained | 80% |

The trade is not "cheap versus expensive". It is **fixed cost versus shared upside**. Debt costs a known amount and must be paid in a bad month too. Equity costs nothing while you are small and a fortune if you succeed. Nabil and Farhana are buying warehouse space and crates — assets with a predictable payback — which is exactly the case where fixed cost wins. A founder buying three years of engineering salaries for an unproven product should read that same table and choose the other column.

## The concept behind credit approval: Debt Service Coverage Ratio

Whatever your stage, a credit officer tests one thing above all: can your cash flow carry the instalments? The metric is the **Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR)**.

### 1. Name

Debt Service Coverage Ratio (DSCR).

### 2. Plain meaning

How many times over your monthly operating cash covers your monthly loan instalment.

### 3. The formula

```
DSCR = Monthly operating cash flow ÷ Monthly loan instalment
```

### 4. Worked Bogura example

Nabil and Farhana bring in BDT 3,00,000 of monthly revenue and spend BDT 2,20,000 on operating costs — procurement, salaries, fuel.

- Operating cash flow: BDT 3,00,000 − BDT 2,20,000 = **BDT 80,000 a month**.
- Their quarterly instalment of BDT 1,69,858 works out to about **BDT 56,600 a month**.
- DSCR: 80,000 ÷ 56,600 = **1.41x**.

At 1.41x, every taka of instalment is covered 1.41 times over. The 0.41 is the buffer — the room for a slow month before the instalment starts eating into the business.

### 5. Action

Build the same ratio for your own loan before you apply. Banks commonly look for at least **1.25x**, and want more comfort in seasonal businesses. The circular sets no fixed ratio; each bank applies its own credit policy. If your ratio comes in under 1.25, you have three levers: borrow less, stretch the term so the instalment falls, or take a longer grace period.

## Eligibility: the mandatory requirements

Every one of these must hold. There is no partial qualification:[^bb-master-circular]

1. **Startup definition:** an innovative, technology-based, scalable venture, or a domestic–foreign joint venture. A restructure or division of an existing business does **not** qualify.
2. **Registered in Bangladesh**, no more than 12 years old at application (trade licence issue or RJSC registration date).
3. **Founder age:** every entrepreneur must be 21 or older. There is no upper limit.
4. **Clean credit record:** no founder may be a loan defaulter in Bangladesh Bank's Credit Information Bureau (CIB) — the central database of everyone's loans and defaults.
5. **The bank's own credit test:** accounts and projections that show repayment capacity, which is the DSCR test above.

Requirement 4 is the one that ends applications late and painfully, because it turns on personal history rather than the business. An old defaulted credit card belonging to one co-founder disqualifies the whole company. Pull every founder's CIB report at the start, not after three months of preparation.

Startups selected through recognised "startup hunting" programmes run by government, semi-government bodies, universities, or national and international organisations get priority processing.[^bb-master-circular]

## Step-by-step application roadmap

Each stage below finishes with something you can hold, which is how you know it is actually done:

| Stage | Action Required | Deliverable |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Check stage and limit** | Work out your company's operational age and which ceiling applies. | Borrowing target inside the stage ceiling.[^bb-master-circular] |
| **2. Ask about security** | First meeting: what will this bank accept, and does it require land? | Written answer from two banks. |
| **3. Assemble the dossier** | [Trade licence](/trade-license), company [e-TIN](/tax/e-tin), [RJSC documents](/registration/private-limited), plus 6–12 months of accounts. | Registration pack + reconciled accounts. |
| **4. Build the repayment case** | Three-year projections showing quarterly instalments covered. | DSCR calculation, itemised fund use with equipment quotes and supplier invoices. |
| **5. Apply at a startup desk** | Go to the SME/startup desk, not a retail branch. | Complete loan proposal package. |
| **6. Survive appraisal** | Site visit, credit committee, security negotiation. | Sanction letter with rate, term, grace. |
| **7. Drawdown** | Sign documents; funds hit the corporate account. | Quarterly repayment schedule begins. |

Stage 2 is out of order compared with how most founders do this, and that is deliberate. Asking about security early costs one meeting; discovering it at stage 6 costs two months.

### Where to apply: startup and SME desks

Do not walk into a general retail counter. The staff there are measured on consumer loans and will not have handled this scheme. Approach the SME and emerging-business or startup desks instead. Banks that publicly run such desks include the following — practitioner-reported, so confirm the current desk at the branch:[^accelerating-bd-banking]

- **BRAC Bank PLC** — Emerging Business & SME Banking Division.
- **The City Bank PLC** — SME & Medium Business Unit, and City Manarah for Islamic customers.
- **Eastern Bank Ltd (EBL)** — SME Banking & Innovation Unit.
- **Prime Bank PLC** — SME & Startup Lending Desk.
- **IDLC Finance PLC** — SME & Venture Credit Division.

## Cost and time

- **Price:** the circular caps interest at 4%, but it does not cap processing or documentation fees. Banks charge those under their own schedules. Ask for the written fee list before submitting, and compare two banks — the 4% is identical everywhere, so fees and security terms are the only things left to compare.[^bb-master-circular]
- **Time:** there is no statutory deadline for sanction. In practice, expect weeks to a few months from submission to disbursement, depending on the bank and how clean your accounts are. That range is practitioner experience, not a rule.

Plan around the slow case. If Nabil needs the warehouse before the December harvest, an application started in November is already late.

## Common mistakes

| Mistake | Why It Hurts | Safe Action |
|---|---|---|
| **Assuming 4% means collateral-free** | The circular caps the price, not the security; banks still apply their own credit policy.[^bb-master-circular] | Ask in week one what security the bank wants, and offer a charge over the purchased assets before you offer land. |
| **Ignoring quarterly billing** | Interest is charged every quarter on the outstanding balance, so a founder budgeting monthly gets caught short. | Model quarterly instalments, not monthly EMI fiction. |
| **A founder with a bad CIB record** | One defaulter among the entrepreneurs disqualifies the whole application.[^bb-master-circular] | Pull every founder's CIB report and clear personal defaults first. |
| **A restructure or large-group venture** | Restructured businesses and new ventures of large industrial groups are outside the scheme.[^bb-master-circular] | Check the definition before investing months in an application. |
| **Exceeding the stage ceiling** | Term and working capital share one combined limit per stage. | Keep total borrowing inside your stage's cap. |
| **Applying at a retail branch** | General branches rarely process startup loans. | Go to the SME/startup desk with a complete dossier. |

The first row is the one that costs the most time, because it is not discovered until late. The third is the one that costs the most emotionally, because it is nobody's fault at the company.

## Checklist

- [ ] Confirmed the business meets the innovation test and is not a restructure or large-group venture.[^bb-master-circular]
- [ ] Checked the company's operational age and the matching loan ceiling (2/5/8 crore).[^bb-master-circular]
- [ ] Verified every founder is 21+ and clean in CIB.[^bb-master-circular]
- [ ] Asked two banks, in writing, what security they require.
- [ ] Assembled trade licence, e-TIN, and RJSC documents.
- [ ] Built three-year projections with quarterly instalments and a DSCR of 1.25x or better.
- [ ] Itemised what the money buys, with supplier quotes or proforma invoices.
- [ ] Asked for each bank's written fee schedule and compared the two.
- [ ] Submitted the application to the SME/startup desk.
