# SAFE, Convertible Notes, and Priced Rounds in Plain English

> **In short:** Figuring out what your startup is worth on day one is tough. A SAFE lets investors write you a check today while kicking the valuation can down the road to your next major funding round. This saves you time and legal fees, but if you don't model how multiple SAFEs stack up, you might give away too much of your company by surprise.

## Why Use a SAFE Instead of a Priced Round?

When you’re just starting out with an idea or a rough MVP, haggling over your company's valuation with investors can kill momentum.

In a traditional **Priced Round**, you set a price per share and issue equity right away. This means drafting a massive Shareholders' Agreement, spending weeks negotiating terms, and paying hefty legal fees.

Y Combinator created the **SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)** to bypass this headache. It’s not debt, so you don't have to worry about interest rates or maturity dates. It’s simply a promise: conversion follows the equity-financing trigger in the contract; the standard YC form has no minimum round-size threshold. Check out our [Funding Hub](/en/funding) for more on how fundraising works.[^shoumik-safe-convertible-notes]

## The Four Levers of a SAFE

A SAFE contract uses a few simple mechanics to figure out how many shares the investor eventually gets:

1. **Valuation Cap:** Think of this as a safety net for early investors. If they invest with a cap of BDT 10 Crore, and your next round values the company at BDT 50 Crore, the early investor still gets their shares priced as if the company was only worth 10 Crore.
2. **Discount Rate:** This gives the SAFE holder a percentage discount (usually 15% to 20%) on the share price paid by new investors in the upcoming priced round.
3. **Cap and Discount:** A negotiated instrument may include both; YC’s published standard forms separate cap-only and discount-only versions. When it converts, the math is run for both options, and the investor gets whichever gives them more shares.
4. **Post-money vs. Pre-money:** In a valuation-cap post-money SAFE, investment divided by the cap estimates ownership if the cap determines conversion. That is before the priced round’s new money and any new option-pool increase dilute it; it is not a permanently fixed stake.[^yc-safe]

## How the Math Works: A Quick Example

For a simplified example, Tk 50 lakh invested on a Tk 10 crore **post-money cap** implies 5% before new-round dilution, if the cap applies. Assume there are no other instruments or pool changes affecting this calculation.

If a later priced round issues 20% of the post-round ownership to new investors and there is no pool increase, that 5% becomes 5% × 80% = 4%. A lower priced-round valuation or different contract can change the conversion result.

Run the actual share-price definitions and all instruments in your [cap table](/en/funding/cap-table). A ratio of two headline valuations alone does not establish the price per share.

### The Danger of SAFE Stacking

If you raise money on 3 or 4 different SAFEs before ever doing a priced round, you're "stacking" SAFEs. When that priced round finally happens, all those SAFEs convert into shares at the same time. Many founders are shocked by how much of their ownership evaporates in that single moment. Build a model and project the dilution before taking that next check.

## SAFE vs. Convertible Note

| Feature | SAFE | Convertible Note |
|---|---|---|
| **What is it?** | A straightforward agreement for future equity | A debt instrument (essentially a loan) |
| **Interest Rate** | None | Interest as agreed in the debt terms |
| **Maturity Date** | No deadline | Must convert or be repaid by a specific date |
| **Overhead** | Potentially simpler documentation; local implementation still needs review | Involves debt terms and heavier legal paperwork |

## How SAFEs Work in Bangladesh

Instead of raising funds directly into a local Bangladeshi entity, many local startups use a "flip structure." They set up a holding company in US Delaware or Singapore and raise money from foreign investors using SAFEs at the holding company level.

- **Holding-company structure:** A foreign entity may issue an instrument governed by its own jurisdiction. That does not waive Bangladesh rules on creating or funding it, transferring IP, receiving operating-company investment or reporting. Review the full structure before acting.
- **Local conversion:** First confirm that the proposed instrument and transaction are lawful for the Bangladeshi issuer. Share issuance then needs the applicable corporate approvals, registers and RJSC filings; filing a form alone does not validate the instrument.
- **Foreign funds and Invest Bangladesh:** When foreign investment reaches a Bangladeshi bank account, preserve the Bangladesh Bank reporting trail and check whether the operating project also falls within Invest Bangladesh's registration or approval scope.
- **Confirm the lawful route before receiving money:** We have not established a general Bangladesh approval for a SAFE from the absence of an express prohibition. Ask Bangladesh counsel and the authorised-dealer bank to confirm instrument classification, approvals, remittance, conversion and reporting for the exact transaction.
