# SAFE, Convertible Notes, and Priced Rounds in Plain English

> **In short:** Figuring out what your startup is worth on day one is tough. A SAFE lets investors write you a check today while kicking the valuation can down the road to your next major funding round. This saves you time and legal fees, but if you don't model how multiple SAFEs stack up, you might give away too much of your company by surprise.

## Why Use a SAFE Instead of a Priced Round?

When you’re just starting out with an idea or a rough MVP, haggling over your company's valuation with investors can kill momentum.

In a traditional **Priced Round**, you set a price per share and issue equity right away. This means drafting a massive Shareholders' Agreement, spending weeks negotiating terms, and paying hefty legal fees.

Y Combinator created the **SAFE (Simple Agreement for Future Equity)** to bypass this headache. It’s not debt, so you don't have to worry about interest rates or maturity dates. It’s simply a promise: when your company raises a larger, formal priced round later on, this investment will convert into shares. Check out our [Funding Hub](/funding) for more on how fundraising works.[^shoumik-safe-convertible-notes]

## The Four Levers of a SAFE

A SAFE contract uses a few simple mechanics to figure out how many shares the investor eventually gets:

1. **Valuation Cap:** Think of this as a safety net for early investors. If they invest with a cap of BDT 10 Crore, and your next round values the company at BDT 50 Crore, the early investor still gets their shares priced as if the company was only worth 10 Crore.
2. **Discount Rate:** This gives the SAFE holder a percentage discount (usually 15% to 20%) on the share price paid by new investors in the upcoming priced round.
3. **Cap and Discount:** Many SAFEs include both. When it converts, the math is run for both options, and the investor gets whichever gives them more shares.
4. **Post-money vs. Pre-money:** Most founders today use the Post-money SAFE. It makes the math crystal clear—both you and the investor know exactly what percentage of the company they are locking in the moment the paper is signed.

## How the Math Works: A Quick Example

Let's walk through the numbers. Imagine a SaaS startup in Dhaka raises BDT 50 Lakh on a SAFE with a valuation cap of BDT 10 Crore.

A year and a half later, the startup raises a priced round at a BDT 30 Crore pre-money valuation. 
The new investors will buy shares based on the 30 Crore valuation. But because your early investor took a bet on you on day one, their SAFE converts at the 10 Crore cap. 

This means the early investor gets their shares at a third of the price the new investors are paying. Always run these numbers in your [cap table](/funding/cap-table) spreadsheet before signing anything so you know exactly where you stand.

### The Danger of SAFE Stacking

If you raise money on 3 or 4 different SAFEs before ever doing a priced round, you're "stacking" SAFEs. When that priced round finally happens, all those SAFEs convert into shares at the same time. Many founders are shocked by how much of their ownership evaporates in that single moment. Build a model and project the dilution before taking that next check.

## SAFE vs. Convertible Note

| Feature | SAFE Note | Convertible Note |
|---|---|---|
| **What is it?** | A straightforward agreement for future equity | A debt instrument (essentially a loan) |
| **Interest Rate** | None | Accrues annual interest (usually 5%–8%) |
| **Maturity Date** | No deadline | Must convert or be repaid by a specific date |
| **Overhead** | Cheap, fast, and simple | Involves debt terms and heavier legal paperwork |

## How SAFEs Work in Bangladesh

Instead of raising funds directly into a local Bangladeshi entity, many local startups use a "flip structure." They set up a holding company in US Delaware or Singapore and raise money from foreign investors using SAFEs at the holding company level.

- **The Flip Structure Advantage:** Issuing a SAFE under Delaware or Singapore law is incredibly fast. You don't have to wait on the Registrar of Joint Stock Companies and Firms (RJSC) to process share allotments or capital increases every time a check comes in.
- **Converting at RJSC:** If you do issue a SAFE directly from your Bangladeshi entity to local investors, converting it requires filing the proper share allotment paperwork and board resolutions with the RJSC. 
- **BIDA and Foreign Funds:** When foreign investment eventually hits your Bangladeshi bank account, you must declare it properly following Bangladesh Bank and Bangladesh Investment Development Authority (BIDA) regulations.
- **Bangladesh Bank's Stance:** The central bank doesn't have an explicit ban on convertible instruments like SAFEs. However, when those instruments convert into equity, your bank will scrutinize the valuation reports and inward remittance documents to ensure everything is legitimate.
