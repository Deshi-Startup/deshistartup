# Fundraising Data Room Checklist

*This guide is adapted from Shoumik Shahriar's ["Data Room Development for Startup"](https://shoumik.substack.com/p/data-room-development-for-startup) on SS Notes.*[^shoumik-data-room]

> **In short:** Signing a [Term Sheet](/funding/term-sheets) is just the beginning. Next, investors dive into due diligence to verify your legal and financial claims. A well-organized virtual Data Room accelerates this process by weeks, while missing paperwork can cause the entire deal to collapse at the last minute.

---

## Why Is a Data Room Critical?

Imagine a Dhaka-based fintech startup lands a term sheet for a 10 Crore BDT (approx. $1M) Series A round. But their data room isn't ready. When the investors ask for VAT receipts and board resolutions, the founders start scrambling. A due diligence process that should have taken three weeks drags on for three months. Meanwhile, market conditions worsen, and the lead investor backs out. 

Investors want to make sure they aren't buying into hidden legal liabilities or tax bombs. Setting up a secure, structured cloud folder (like Google Drive or Dropbox) is the best way to prove your startup is a safe bet.

---

## The 6 Essential Data Room Folders

Your data room should contain six primary folders. Here is exactly what goes in them and why investors care.

### 1. Corporate & Registry Documents
**Why it matters:** Investors need to verify the company legally exists and who actually has the authority to make decisions.
- **Certificate of Incorporation:** Certified copy from the RJSC.
- **Constitutional Documents:** <Term name="moa-aoa">Memorandum of Association</Term> (MOA) and Articles of Association (AOA).
- **Form XII:** The official list of current company directors.
- **Minutes Books:** Documented board resolutions, AGM, and EGM minutes.
- **BIDA Registration:** Essential if you are raising foreign investment.

### 2. Financial Statements & Tax Compliance
**Why it matters:** Investors are checking if you're sitting on unpaid tax liabilities that could turn into massive penalties later.
- **Audit Reports:** Financial statements audited by a CA firm for the past 1–3 years.
- **Tax & VAT Registrations:** Up-to-date <Term name="e-tin">e-TIN</Term> and Business Identification Number (<Term name="vat-bin">BIN</Term>/VAT) certificates from the NBR.
- **Filing Receipts:** Annual corporate income tax return acknowledgments and monthly VAT returns (Mushak 9.1).
- **Banking Records:** Bank statements for all active accounts and details of any outstanding loans.

### 3. Legal & Commercial Contracts
**Why it matters:** They want to see if your major customer contracts are real and legally enforceable.
- **Trade License:** Active commercial <Term name="trade-license">trade license</Term> from the City Corporation.
- **Office Lease:** Registered lease agreement for your office or warehouse.
- **Material Contracts:** Major agreements with suppliers, B2B customers, or payment gateways.

### 4. Technology & Intellectual Property (IP)
**Why it matters:** The company needs to legally own the code and brand it is profiting from.
- **Trademarks:** Registration certificates or filing receipts for your brand name and logo.
- **Domain & Software Ownership:** Proof that the company (not the founder personally) owns the website domain and code repositories.

### 5. Human Resources & Employment
**Why it matters:** Informal employment terms can lead to disgruntled former employees claiming they own a piece of your IP.
- **Organogram:** Current organizational hierarchy and executive leadership profiles.
- **Employment Contracts:** Standard offer letters and NDAs signed by core team members.
- **Contractor IP Assignment:** Written agreements transferring IP rights from external developers or agencies to the company.

### 6. Cap Table & Prior Investment
**Why it matters:** Investors need absolute clarity on who owns what percentage of the company before they buy in.
- **Cap Table:** An up-to-date breakdown of shareholder equity, including [cap table](/funding/cap-table) calculations.
- **Prior Convertible Instruments:** Legal documents for any existing [SAFE notes or convertible debt](/funding/safe-convertible-notes).

## 3 Common Red Flags in Bangladesh

- **Missing VAT Returns:** Even if your annual audit looks clean, failing to file monthly Mushak 9.1 returns is a massive red flag that will halt due diligence.
- **Missing TIN Certificates:** Not having up-to-date personal e-TINs for directors or failing to match NID spellings perfectly with RJSC filings.
- **Informal Agreements:** Operating on verbal agreements with co-founders or early developers. Without written IP assignment, you could face legal disputes over software ownership.

## Action Plan

- [ ] Create the 6 standard sub-folders in Google Drive, Notion, or Dropbox.
- [ ] Scan clear PDF copies of all RJSC, NBR, and City Corporation documents and sort them into the right folders.
- [ ] Review your trade license and VAT returns. If anything is expired, start the renewal process today.
