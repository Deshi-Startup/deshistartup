# Start Here: Your First Steps as a Founder in Bangladesh

> **In short:** Start with a person and a problem, learn how they handle it today, then choose a small next step that fits your time and money. You do not need a finished product or a company merely to think clearly about the idea. Check the requirements for an activity before you actually carry it out.

This page is for someone who wants to build a business but does not know the first move. By the end, you can write a useful starting note, ask a few concrete research questions and choose what evidence to collect next. You can do that while studying, employed, caring for others or exploring a career change. Reading and learning tools are free and need no account.

## Understand what you are trying to build

A startup is searching for a business model that can work repeatedly and grow beyond work performed personally by its founders. An app is not the model. The model explains **who gets value, who pays, how the value is delivered and whether the money covers the work**.

A service business can develop a scalable product; an online shop can remain a small business. “Software,” “agency” and “e-commerce” describe aspects of a business, not mutually exclusive levels of ambition. Start by understanding the job and your intended scale rather than forcing a label.

A **hypothesis** is an explanation you want to test. **Validation** is learning whether that explanation holds up. An **MVP**, or minimum viable product, is an initial usable version delivering the core benefit. You may need conversations or a manual test before it makes sense to build one.

## Follow one beginner from idea to first action

**Fictional example:** A student in Rajshahi has four hours this week and no travel budget. She notices a training centre repeatedly answering calls about timetable changes. She wonders whether a scheduling service would help.

Her first note separates what she saw from what she imagines:

```text
People: managers of nearby training centres who handle timetable changes.
Observed: one centre received several calls after a class time changed.
Possible problem: informing everyone may require repeated manual work.
Current alternative: phone calls and a shared messaging group.
Unknown: whether this recurs, matters enough to change, or is handled well already.
First task: ask relevant managers about their most recent class change.
Limits: four hours this week; phone conversations or an existing nearby visit.
```

She has not established that managers need an app. That is exactly why the first task is useful.

### Ask about the last event

She explains that she is researching the task, agrees a convenient time and asks permission to take notes. She asks:

1. “What happened the last time a class changed?”
2. “How did you tell people, and what did you do afterward?”
3. “Did anything go wrong? When does the current method work well?”
4. “Do you remember the time or cost involved? It is fine if you do not.”
5. “Who would decide to change how this is done?”

She avoids “Would you buy my app?” It would invite a prediction before she understands the work. She records no student contact details. If managers decline, she records that too; no response does not prove there is no problem.

### Choose the next step from the evidence

Suppose two managers describe repeat confusion, while a third says a shared calendar works well. She writes both findings. She could next show a simple timetable-confirmation process to a willing manager using made-up data. She should observe whether the manager can use it and what remains difficult.

A demonstration tests understanding and usability. A later offer with a real scope and price tests buying behaviour. Neither should be described as a sale until the relevant purchase actually happens. “Contact me when it launches” is an expression of interest, not a commitment to pay.

If no recurring difficulty appears, she can change the customer group or stop pursuing this problem. The week was useful because it avoided an unsupported commitment to build.

## See how the rest of the business connects

Once you can deliver something useful, you need a way to find customers, agree the offer, deliver it, receive money and handle problems. These pieces interact:

| Job | Question to settle |
|---|---|
| Customer and offer | Who buys what, at which price and under which terms? |
| Delivery | Who performs the work, by when, and what counts as complete? |
| Payment and records | When does money arrive, and which order or invoice does it belong to? |
| Problems | Who handles cancellation, failure, refund or complaint? |
| Legal and safety preparation | What applies to the actual activity, premises, people and data? |

For example, a service sold for BDT 1,000 with BDT 700 of stated costs leaves BDT 300 before any omitted costs. If the customer pays next month but the BDT 700 is due tomorrow, the sale does not supply tomorrow's cash. **Profit and available cash are different questions.** The amounts are illustrative, not recommended prices.

Incorporation, tax identity, local permissions and sector approvals are also different questions. Check the relevant requirements before taking payment, hiring, renting premises or testing a regulated or sensitive activity. There is no universal “do paperwork later” exemption for a small test.

## Make a plan you can actually carry out

Use a review cycle rather than a compulsory 30-day deadline. For the fictional student's four-hour week, 30 minutes go to the initial note, 30 to invitations, up to two hours to conversations and one hour to comparing notes. If only one person replies, she reviews the evidence and recruitment problem instead of claiming a finished market study.

Your schedule can differ. Protect essential household spending, employment commitments and responsibilities to others. Set an affordable limit before the task, not after the budget is exhausted.

```text
The people and task I want to understand:
What I observed / what I am assuming:
What people do now:
One question that could change my decision:
Who I can reach and how:
The action I will take:
Time and money I can commit:
What I must check or agree before acting:
Review date:
What happened / what remains unknown / next step:
```

You are ready for your first action when you can name a specific person or group, ask a question about a real task and explain what you will record. You are not required to quit a job, hire a team or raise funding to complete that learning task.
