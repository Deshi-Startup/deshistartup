# Private Limited, OPC, or Sole Proprietorship: A Decision Guide

> **In short:** Three questions settle it for most founders: is there one owner or several, is ownership likely to be shared within the next year, and how much does limited liability matter. A foreign shareholder overrides whatever those three answers are and sends you down a separate path. Walk through the questions below and most readers land on their structure in two minutes. The full cost and timeline comparison lives on [a separate page](/registration/structure-comparison) — this one is just the decision path.

---

## Question 1: One Owner, or Several?

**One owner** – go to Question 2.

**Several owners** (co-founders, partners, or family starting together) – skip to Question 4. A sole proprietorship or OPC isn't legally available to multiple owners at all.

---

## Question 2 (Solo): Is Ownership Likely to Be Shared Within 12 Months?

Answer "yes" if a co-founder, investor, or family member getting shares is even on your mind, whether or not it's decided.

**Yes, it's likely.** Consider [private limited](/registration/private-limited) from the start. Moving from a sole proprietorship or OPC to private limited later is an extra step — the trade license, bank account, and everything else has to be redone while the business is already running.

**No, I want to run this alone.** Go to Question 3.

---

## Question 3 (Solo, No Sharing Planned): How Much Does Limited Liability Matter, and Can You Raise BDT 2.5 Million?

This is really two conditions at once — both need to be true for the second option to make sense.

**Liability isn't a major worry, or capital is tight, or you'll likely need a bank loan.** A [sole proprietorship](/registration/sole-proprietorship) fits. Less paperwork, but business debt can reach your personal assets.

**Limited liability matters, and BDT 2.5 million is on hand or raisable.** Look at an [OPC](/registration/opc). You get a limited-liability company without a co-founder, though bank loans can be harder since there's no separate guarantor to offer — the OPC guide has a real example of that risk.

---

## Question 4 (Multiple Owners): Will Ownership Be Split into Shares, or Is a Profit-Sharing Agreement Enough?

**Just profit-sharing, small-scale service or trading, no rush to scale.** Consider a partnership. A deed gets filed with RJSC, but there's no concept of share capital or directors. [Founder agreement](/cofounders/founder-agreement) covers what a written agreement needs to cover.

**Ownership split into shares, an investor might join, or you want larger corporate contracts.** Look at [private limited](/registration/private-limited). Settle the equity split and vesting in writing before registering — see [equity splits and vesting](/cofounders/equity-and-vesting).

---

## Any Branch: A Foreign Shareholder Changes the Path

Whatever the four questions above answer, if any owner or shareholder is not a Bangladeshi citizen, or money is coming in from abroad, go straight to [basics of starting a foreign-owned company](/registration/foreign-owned-company). It walks through the separate BIDA registration and bank encashment steps that sit alongside RJSC.

---

## Quick Results Table

| Your answers | Structure |
|---|---|
| Solo, no sharing planned, liability isn't a big worry | Sole proprietorship |
| Solo, no sharing planned, liability matters, capital available | OPC |
| Solo, but sharing is likely | Private limited (from the start) |
| Multiple owners, profit-sharing only | Partnership |
| Multiple owners, shares | Private limited |
| Foreign shareholder, any answer above | Foreign-owned company path |

Rule of thumb: **When unsure, err toward private limited, not sole proprietorship or OPC. Growing into more structure later is easy; unwinding out of one is not.**
