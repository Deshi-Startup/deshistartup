# The RJSC Filing Calendar: Which Return Is Due When

> **In short:** RJSC's whole year runs off the date of your AGM. Within 21 days of that meeting you file the annual list of shareholders and summary of share capital (Schedule X),[^companies-act-annual-return] and within 30 days the audited balance sheet and profit and loss account.[^companies-act-balance-sheet] Separately, a director change gives you 14 days, a registered office change 28, and a new share allotment 60 — those clocks start on the day the event happens, not at the AGM.[^companies-act-change-returns] The cost of being late is not just RJSC's late fee. The Companies Act carries its own per-day fine with no ceiling on it, and it can land on a director personally.[^companies-act-annual-return] So the moment the AGM date is fixed, put both the 21-day and the 30-day deadline on your calendar.

When the AGM must be held, what the quorum is, how to keep minutes — that is a separate guide: [Board meetings, AGMs, and minute book basics](/registration/board-meetings-agm). This page has one job: once the meeting is over, which document goes to RJSC within how many days, and what happens if it doesn't.

---

## What "returns" means at RJSC

RJSC's own FAQ splits returns into two kinds: the annual return, and a return for any change in the entity.[^rjsc-faq-returns] The split is worth remembering, because the two clocks start in different places. The annual return's clock starts on the day of the AGM. A change return's clock starts the day the change happened — there is no waiting for the AGM.

Both fall under the same fee schedule on the portal, and both carry their own late penalty.

---

## How the yearly cycle runs

Four steps, each resting on the one before it.

**1. Close the books and get them audited.** Section 183 of the Companies Act 1994 requires the board to lay a balance sheet and profit and loss account before the members at every AGM, and those accounts must be audited.[^companies-act-balance-sheet] Section 212 restricts who can audit them: only a chartered accountant within the meaning of the Bangladesh Chartered Accountants Order 1973.[^companies-act-auditor] However small the company, keeping your own books in order is not enough.

**2. Hold the AGM.** The audited accounts are laid before the meeting, and under section 210 next year's auditor is appointed at the same meeting.[^companies-act-auditor] The company must inform the auditor within 7 days of appointment, and the auditor must tell RJSC in writing within 30 days whether they accept it.[^companies-act-auditor]

**3. File Schedule X within 21 days.** Section 36 requires every company having share capital to prepare a list of members and a summary of share capital. It must be completed within 21 days of the meeting, and filed with RJSC inside that same window.[^companies-act-annual-return]

**4. File the balance sheet within 30 days.** Under section 190, three copies of the balance sheet and profit and loss account go to RJSC within 30 days of the date they were laid before the meeting, along with three copies of everything the Act requires to be annexed to them.[^companies-act-balance-sheet]

The first year works slightly differently. Section 36 says the first list must be made within 18 months of incorporation,[^companies-act-annual-return] and section 81 gives the first AGM the same 18-month window.[^companies-act-agm] The two run together.

### The same thing with dates on it

Say your financial year ends 30 June and the AGM is held on 20 December. Two deadlines follow:

| Filing | Clock starts | Last day |
|---|---|---|
| Schedule X (list of members and summary of share capital) | 20 December | 10 January |
| Audited balance sheet and profit and loss account | 20 December | 19 January |

Nine days apart. In practice most late filings happen inside those nine days, because founders assume both documents can go in together. The Act sets two separate deadlines, so keep them separate on the calendar too.

Pushing the meeting back does not help either. Under section 183 the accounts laid before the AGM must cover a period ending within the nine months before the meeting — twelve months for a company with business or interests outside Bangladesh.[^companies-act-balance-sheet] If you apply before that window expires, the Registrar can extend it by up to three months.[^companies-act-balance-sheet]

## No AGM does not stop the balance sheet clock

This is the most commonly misread part. Many founders assume that because the AGM never happened, there is nothing to file.

The Act says the opposite. Section 190 provides that where the AGM for a year has not been held, the balance sheet must be filed within 30 days of the last day on which that meeting should have been held.[^companies-act-balance-sheet] Skipping the meeting does not remove the filing duty — it only moves where the clock starts.

On top of that, if the meeting does not adopt the balance sheet, or if no meeting was held at all, a statement of that fact and the reasons for it must be annexed to the balance sheet.[^companies-act-balance-sheet] In other words, "we held no AGM this year" ends up in writing in RJSC's own file.

## The returns that have nothing to do with the AGM

Beyond the once-a-year work, certain changes inside the company must be reported to RJSC on their own. These windows are short, and the counting starts on the day of the event.

| What happened | Deadline | Section |
|---|---|---|
| Filing particulars of the first directors | 14 days | 115(2)(a)[^companies-act-change-returns] |
| Any change of directors or managers, or in their particulars | 14 days | 115(2)(b)[^companies-act-change-returns] |
| Notifying or changing the registered office address | 28 days | 77(2)[^companies-act-change-returns] |
| A new share allotment | 60 days | 151(1)[^companies-act-change-returns] |

The directors' return is the document RJSC calls "Form XII" — the same form used at registration; see [Private limited company registration](/registration/private-limited). For the others the Act says only "the prescribed form" and gives no number. Form names and numbers change from time to time, so check the portal's current list before filing.

The 60-day allotment window is the one most often missed, because when investment lands or a new co-founder joins, everyone is busy with money and contracts. The Registrar can extend the period if you apply before it expires, but that is at their discretion.[^companies-act-change-returns] For keeping the share numbers and the paperwork consistent, see [Share capital, directors, and shareholders](/registration/shares-and-directors).

## Being late costs you twice

Founders usually know one number — RJSC's late fee. In reality two separate bills run side by side.

**One: RJSC's fee schedule.** Filing any return or document on time costs 200 taka per document. Filing late adds 500 taka per day, capped at 1,000 taka per document.[^rjsc-fees-schedule] The exposure on this side is bounded — never more than a thousand taka per document.

**Two: the Companies Act fines.** Here there is no cap. The Act sets a separate daily maximum depending on what went unfiled:

| What wasn't filed | Maximum fine in the Act |
|---|---|
| Schedule X and the annual list (s. 36) | up to 200 taka per day[^companies-act-annual-return] |
| Balance sheet and profit and loss account (s. 190) | up to 100 taka per day[^companies-act-balance-sheet] |
| Return of directors' particulars (s. 115) | 500 taka[^companies-act-change-returns] |
| Return of share allotment (s. 151) | up to 1,000 taka per day[^companies-act-change-returns] |
| Failing to hold the AGM (s. 81) | up to 10,000 taka, plus up to 250 taka per day while the default continues[^companies-act-agm] |

Run the arithmetic once. Say Schedule X goes in a year late. RJSC's late fee stops at 1,000 taka for that document. But section 36's daily maximum of 200 taka across 365 days comes to 73,000 taka — seventy-three times the late fee.

Two things to keep in mind here. The Act says "not exceeding," so this is a maximum rather than an automatic invoice, and enforcement is a separate process. Still, with no ceiling, the exposure grows with every day that passes. And both section 36 and section 190 say that alongside the company, any officer who knowingly and wilfully permits the default is liable to the same penalty.[^companies-act-annual-return][^companies-act-balance-sheet] The liability does not stop at the company's name — it can reach a director personally.

Missing the deadline does not close the door, though. Section 350 provides that a document or return required to be filed or registered under the Act may still be filed after the specified time on payment of the prescribed late fee — without prejudice to any other liability.[^companies-act-late-filing] So clear a backlog as early as you can, because the exposure grows every day it sits.

The full fee and late-fee tables are in [RJSC fees and timelines](/registration/rjsc-fees).

## What to check before you file

- **The signatures.** Section 36 requires the Schedule X copy to be signed by two directors including the managing director — or, where there is no managing director, by a director together with the managing agent, manager, or secretary. A certificate from those signatories, confirming the list states the facts as they stood on the day, goes with it.[^companies-act-annual-return]
- **The extra certificate for a private company.** The annual return must carry a certificate signed by a director or officer confirming the company has issued no invitation to the public to subscribe for shares or debentures. If membership exceeds fifty, a further certificate about who those extra members are is also required.[^companies-act-annual-return]
- **Three copies of the balance sheet.** Section 190 asks for three copies, and three copies of the annexures as well.[^companies-act-balance-sheet]
- **The challan.** Pay against the challan the portal generates after submission, and keep the challan and the submission proof in the same folder.

## Common mistakes

- **Assuming that no AGM means nothing to file.** Section 190 gives no such relief; the clock simply runs from the last day the meeting should have been held.[^companies-act-balance-sheet]
- **Treating the two deadlines as one.** Schedule X has 21 days, the balance sheet 30. Filing them together as a habit means the 21-day date is the one you miss.
- **Calling the meeting without an audit.** Without audited accounts section 183 is not satisfied, and the auditor must be a chartered accountant.[^companies-act-balance-sheet][^companies-act-auditor]
- **Staying quiet about a director change.** When someone leaves or joins, the 14-day clock starts that day. Waiting for the next AGM means the window has long closed.[^companies-act-change-returns]
- **Budgeting off an old fee table.** Before paying, treat the amount on the portal's challan as final.

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Had the accounts audited by a chartered accountant after the financial year closed
- [ ] Laid the audited accounts before the AGM and appointed the next auditor
- [ ] Put both the 21-day and 30-day deadlines on the calendar separately
- [ ] Collected the required signatures and certificate for Schedule X
- [ ] Attached the additional private-company certificate
- [ ] Filed three copies of the balance sheet and its annexures
- [ ] Reported any mid-year change of director, office, or shares within 14, 28, or 60 days
- [ ] Kept every challan and submission receipt in one folder

Rule of thumb: **The day the AGM ends, put two dates in your phone — 21 days and 30 days. The real cost of being late isn't RJSC's thousand-taka late fee; it's the Act's daily fine, which has no ceiling to stop at.**
