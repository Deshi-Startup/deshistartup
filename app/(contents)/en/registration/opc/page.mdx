# One Person Company (OPC) under the Companies Act 1994

> **In short:** Most people assume a private limited company always needs at least two shareholders. Since 2020, a single person can incorporate one alone — this is called a One Person Company, or OPC.[^opc-law] You get a limited-liability company without a co-founder, but it requires at least BDT 2.5 million (25 lakh) in <Term name="paid-up-capital">paid-up capital</Term>, a named nominee, and — since the Finance Act 2026 — the same tax rate as an ordinary private limited company.[^finance-act-2026] Very few founders actually take this path, and the reasons why matter as much as the mechanics.

---

## What an OPC Actually Is

On 26 November 2020, the Companies Act 1994 was amended to add OPC provisions. RJSC began accepting OPC registrations from 23 May 2021.[^opc-law] Under the statutory definition, an OPC is a company whose sole shareholder is a natural person, not another company.[^opc-law] The founder is both the sole shareholder and the director. One person can hold only one OPC at a time — you cannot own more than one simultaneously.[^opc-law]

---

## Don't Confuse It with a Sole Proprietorship

A [sole proprietorship](/en/registration/sole-proprietorship) and an OPC both have a single owner, but legally they are completely different things. In a sole proprietorship, the business and the owner are the same legal person, so debt liability reaches the owner's personal assets. An OPC is a separate incorporated body, like a private limited company, so debt liability is generally limited to the company's own assets. That one difference is the OPC's real draw, even with the extra paperwork.

---

## Why a Nominee Is Required

The MoA must name a nominee with that person’s written consent. Under section 392B, the nominee becomes the shareholder if the sole shareholder dies or becomes unable to manage the company or of unsound mind. The law also provides for withdrawing consent and replacing a nominee; changes must be recorded and reported through the prescribed process.[^opc-tnp]

---

## The Capital Band: BDT 2.5 Million to 5 Crore

Section 392C sets two separate bands: paid-up share capital of BDT 25 lakh to 5 crore, and turnover in the immediately preceding financial year of BDT 1 crore to 50 crore.[^opc-capital] These are different measures. Before applying, ask RJSC how the preceding-year turnover condition applies to your business, especially a new venture with no trading history. Ordinary private limited companies have no comparable universal minimum capital.

Section 392C(2) provides for conversion to a private or public limited company, subject to the necessary conditions, where both the capital and turnover exceed their respective limits. It does not describe an automatic conversion. Get advice before crossing a limit or admitting another shareholder.[^opc-legalseba]

---

## The Tax Advantage Is Gone

Through FY 2022-23, an OPC paid tax at a lower rate than an ordinary company (22.5% at the time) — that tax break was the OPC's biggest draw. The Finance Act 2026 removed that separate rate. From FY 2026-27, OPCs, trusts, and partnership firms all pay the same 27.5% as an ordinary non-publicly-traded company.[^finance-act-2026] With full bank-transfer compliance, an OPC likely qualifies for the same reduced 25% rate a private limited company does — confirm the exact conditions with your own accountant. After this change, the reason to choose an OPC is limited liability and a corporate identity, not tax savings.

---

## How Many Founders Actually Choose This

According to The Business Standard, only 172 OPCs had been registered as of September 2022, compared to 12,500 newly registered private and public limited companies over the same period.[^tbs-opc] The report names three big reasons for the low number: the BDT 2.5 million minimum capital is too high for small entrepreneurs, bank loans are hard to get, and general awareness of OPCs is low.[^tbs-opc]

The bank-loan problem shows up clearly in one real case. Al-Amin Dewan, who owns a shoe factory in Dhaka's Hazaribagh, registered an OPC in 2021 with BDT 2.8 million in capital. When he applied to multiple banks for a BDT 10 million loan to grow the business, he was rejected every time — because in an OPC, the same person is chairman, director, and shareholder, so there was no separate guarantor to offer.[^tbs-opc] Within a year and a half, he applied to convert the company to a private limited one. This is one person's experience, not every bank's policy, but it's worth asking a bank directly about its OPC lending stance before you need the loan.

## How Registration Works

Most of it follows the [private limited registration steps](/en/registration/private-limited). The differences are:

- The MoA and AoA need to include the nominee's name and details
- Form IX records the sole shareholder’s consent to act as director
- A foreign shareholder needs a bank account opened and an <Term name="encashment-certificate">Encashment Certificate</Term> for money remitted from abroad, submitted to RJSC before incorporation

After approval, a digitally signed <Term name="incorp-certificate">Certificate of Incorporation</Term>, MoA/AoA, and <Term name="form-xii">Form XII</Term> are sent to the email linked to your RJSC account. You then need the applicable business registrations, while retaining the OPC-specific meeting and filing rules below: <Term name="e-tin">TIN</Term>, <Term name="trade-license">trade license</Term>, <Term name="vat-bin">VAT registration</Term>, and any sector-specific license.

---

## When to Consider an OPC, and When Not To

- **Consider it if:** you want to run the company alone, have no plans to bring in a co-founder, meet both the capital and preceding-year turnover conditions, and limited liability matters more to you than the paperwork saved by a [sole proprietorship's](/en/registration/sole-proprietorship) unlimited liability.
- **Skip it if:** capital is tight, you'll likely need a bank loan, or a co-founder or investor could join within a few months. An ordinary [private limited company](/en/company-types) may suit shared ownership, but it needs at least two genuine members. Resolve ownership and eligibility before applying.

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Prepared your own details as the sole shareholder
- [ ] Checked the paid-up-capital band and preceding-year turnover condition with RJSC
- [ ] Chosen a nominee and obtained their consent and documents
- [ ] Asked a bank directly about its lending policy for OPCs, if a loan is likely
- [ ] Factored in that the tax rate now matches a private limited company
- [ ] Confirmed there's no likely need for a co-founder in the near term

Rule of thumb: **If you want limited liability without a co-founder, look at an OPC. But if capital is tight or a bank loan is likely, go in knowing that constraint upfront rather than discovering it later.**

## OPC has its own filing calendar

Section 392F requires at least one director meeting in each half calendar year. Section 392I requires financial statements and supporting documents to be filed within 180 days of financial year-end. Use these OPC provisions rather than assuming the ordinary company AGM timetable applies unchanged.[^opc-calendar]
