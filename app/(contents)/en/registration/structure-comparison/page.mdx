# Business Structure Comparison Table with Costs and Timelines

> **In short:** Sole proprietorship avoids company incorporation, but you still need the applicable <Term name="trade-license">trade license</Term>, <Term name="e-tin">e-TIN</Term>, VAT/BIN and sector permissions. OPC has a statutory <Term name="paid-up-capital">paid-up capital</Term> band and a separate preceding-year turnover condition. Company capital is distinct from a government fee. Foreign investment adds banking and reporting steps. Compare ownership, eligibility and the full compliance budget alongside the estimated costs below.

Cost and time are only half of this decision — the other half is liability and ownership, covered at [sole proprietorship vs. private limited](/en/company-types). This page just puts the money and the days in one place.

---

## Five Paths at a Glance

| Structure | Government cost (approximate) | Time | Minimum capital |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | Trade license fee, varies by area[^trade-license-fee] | A few days to 2-3 weeks | None |
| Partnership | RJSC registration fee BDT 0, deed stamp duty scales with capital, court fee BDT 20 per application[^rjsc-partnership-fee] | 2-4 weeks | None |
| OPC | Name clearance BDT 500/name, MoA/AoA stamps, filing fee close to private limited[^rjsc-company-fees] | 3-6 weeks | BDT 2.5 million (max 50 million)[^opc-capital] |
| Private limited | Name clearance BDT 500/name, MoA stamp BDT 1,000, AoA stamp BDT 2,000, filing BDT 1,200, capital fee BDT 0 up to 1 million[^rjsc-company-fees] | 3-6 weeks | None (though banks, foreign investment, or licensing may set their own conditions) |
| Foreign-owned company | Private limited costs above, plus bank/remittance charges; Invest Bangladesh fee only if project registration applies | 6-10 weeks, depending on bank encashment | No universal company-law minimum; separate conditions may apply to branch offices, visas or licences[^foreign-company-process] |

These figures are government fees and stamp duty as observed on 4 August 2026 — they exclude accountant, lawyer, or agent fees. Timelines are estimates; an RJSC query or correction request can extend them.

---

## Same Steps, Different Depth

All five paths rest on the same three steps: settle a name, prepare and file paperwork, get official recognition. What changes by structure is depth.

- **Sole proprietorship** does not incorporate at RJSC; arrange its applicable trade licence, tax registrations and sector permissions.
- **Partnership** registration at RJSC is available under section 58. An unregistered firm faces important restrictions on enforcing contractual rights in court under section 69, so assess registration before signing contracts. A firm has no company share capital or directors.[^partnership-registration]
- **OPC and private limited** both need the [full name-clearance and registration process](/en/registration/private-limited), from <Term name="moa-aoa">MoA</Term>/AoA through the forms.
- **Foreign ownership** adds a bank <Term name="encashment-certificate">Encashment Certificate</Term>, share-issue reporting and, for an eligible industrial or service project, Invest Bangladesh registration.

## A Worked Comparison: Private Limited vs. OPC

The RJSC paperwork for both is nearly identical. The real difference is capital. Proposing 3 names and keeping <Term name="authorized-capital">authorized capital</Term> within BDT 1 million, the government cost for a private limited company is:

```text
Name clearance (3 × 500) + MoA stamp 1,000 + AoA stamp 2,000 + filing 1,200 + capital fee 0
= 1,500 + 1,000 + 2,000 + 1,200 + 0 = BDT 5,700
```

An OPC must meet the BDT 25 lakh minimum paid-up-capital requirement and the separate preceding-year turnover condition. This is company capital, not a fee paid away to the government or money necessarily frozen in a bank account. It can fund legitimate company operations. Compare its eligibility and funding requirements with the ordinary private-company route before applying.

---

## Where Time Actually Slips

The 30-day validity of <Term name="rjsc-name-clearance">name clearance</Term> runs on the same rule across every company-type structure.[^rjsc-name-fees] Beyond that, three places tend to add the most delay:

- **Mismatched shareholder or director details** – differing spellings across NID, passport, and forms trigger an RJSC query
- **Raising OPC's capital** – if BDT 2.5 million isn't ready, the whole process stalls
- **Bank encashment for foreign investment** – the time it takes for money to arrive from abroad and the bank to issue its certificate is usually the biggest bottleneck on this path

---

## Checklist Before Deciding

- [ ] Budgeted for accountant or lawyer fees on top of government fees
- [ ] Checked whether I actually have the structure's minimum capital on hand
- [ ] Planned around the 30-day name-clearance window
- [ ] Kept name spellings consistent across every shareholder/director document
- [ ] Accounted separately for bank-encashment time if a foreign shareholder is involved

Rule of thumb: **Separate fees, company capital and operating costs. Check eligibility first, then budget for incorporation and the ongoing compliance the structure needs.**
