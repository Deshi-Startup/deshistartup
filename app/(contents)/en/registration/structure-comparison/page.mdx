# Business Structure Comparison Table with Costs and Timelines

> **In short:** The cheapest and fastest path is a sole proprietorship — just a trade license and an e-TIN. The most capital gets tied up in an OPC, which requires at least BDT 2.5 million in paid-up capital. The most steps belong to a foreign-owned company, since it needs BIDA registration alongside RJSC. Private limited and partnership sit in the middle — both need RJSC filing but neither has a fixed minimum capital. The table below puts the real cost and time for all five paths side by side.

Cost and time are only half of this decision — the other half is liability and ownership, covered at [sole proprietorship vs. private limited](/company-types). This page just puts the money and the days in one place.

---

## Five Paths at a Glance

| Structure | Government cost (approximate) | Time | Minimum capital |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sole proprietorship | Trade license fee, varies by area[^trade-license-fee] | A few days to 2-3 weeks | None |
| Partnership | RJSC registration fee BDT 0, deed stamp duty scales with capital, court fee BDT 20 per application[^rjsc-partnership-fee] | 2-4 weeks | None |
| OPC | Name clearance BDT 500/name, MoA/AoA stamps, filing fee close to private limited[^rjsc-company-fees] | 3-6 weeks | BDT 2.5 million (max 50 million)[^opc-capital] |
| Private limited | Name clearance BDT 500/name, MoA stamp BDT 1,000, AoA stamp BDT 2,000, filing BDT 1,200, capital fee BDT 0 up to 1 million[^rjsc-company-fees] | 3-6 weeks | None (though banks, foreign investment, or licensing may set their own conditions) |
| Foreign-owned company | Private limited costs above, plus BIDA registration | 6-10 weeks, depending on bank encashment | No fixed legal minimum, though a substantial amount is expected in practice[^foreign-company-process] |

These figures are government fees and stamp duty as observed on 4 August 2026 — they exclude accountant, lawyer, or agent fees. Timelines are estimates; an RJSC query or correction request can extend them.

---

## Same Steps, Different Depth

All five paths rest on the same three steps: settle a name, prepare and file paperwork, get official recognition. What changes by structure is depth.

- **Sole proprietorship** needs just a name and a trade-license application — nothing goes to RJSC at all.
- **Partnership** files a deed with RJSC, but there's no concept of share capital or directors.
- **OPC and private limited** both need the [full name-clearance and registration process](/registration/private-limited), from MoA/AoA through the forms.
- **Foreign ownership** adds BIDA's steps and a bank Encashment Certificate for money remitted from abroad, on top of the same process.

---

## A Worked Comparison: Private Limited vs. OPC

The RJSC paperwork for both is nearly identical. The real difference is capital. Proposing 3 names and keeping authorized capital within BDT 1 million, the government cost for a private limited company is:

```text
Name clearance (3 × 500) + MoA stamp 1,000 + AoA stamp 2,000 + filing 1,200 + capital fee 0
= 1,500 + 1,000 + 2,000 + 1,200 + 0 = BDT 5,700
```

Government fees look similar for an OPC, but you also need at least BDT 2.5 million sitting in the bank to open one — money a private limited company doesn't require. The real cost difference isn't in the fees, then. It's in the capital that gets tied up.

---

## Where Time Actually Slips

The 30-day name clearance window runs on the same rule across every company-type structure.[^rjsc-name-fees] Beyond that, three places tend to add the most delay:

- **Mismatched shareholder or director details** – differing spellings across NID, passport, and forms trigger an RJSC query
- **Raising OPC's capital** – if BDT 2.5 million isn't ready, the whole process stalls
- **Bank encashment for foreign investment** – the time it takes for money to arrive from abroad and the bank to issue its certificate is usually the biggest bottleneck on this path

---

## Checklist Before Deciding

- [ ] Budgeted for accountant or lawyer fees on top of government fees
- [ ] Checked whether I actually have the structure's minimum capital on hand
- [ ] Planned around the 30-day name-clearance window
- [ ] Kept name spellings consistent across every shareholder/director document
- [ ] Accounted separately for bank-encashment time if a foreign shareholder is involved

Rule of thumb: **Don't choose a structure by the fee amount — look at where the money actually gets tied up. Fees sit in the thousands everywhere, but OPC and foreign ownership tie up capital in the lakhs.**
