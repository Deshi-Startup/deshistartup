# Partnership Deed: Agree the Terms Before You Sign

> **In short:** If you and another person will run a partnership firm, agree in writing who puts in money, how you share profit and loss, who can make decisions, and what happens when someone leaves. Ask a Bangladesh lawyer to turn those decisions into a deed before you sign. A deed does not register the firm at RJSC or remove a partner's personal liability.[^partnership-act]

This guide is for a team choosing a **partnership firm** in Bangladesh. A deed is the written agreement between its partners. It is not the share document of a private limited company. If your plan depends on company shares or equity investment, compare [business structures](/en/registration/structure-comparison) first. Company founders need a different [founder agreement](/en/cofounders/founder-agreement).

You can leave this page with a set of decisions for a lawyer, not a contract ready to sign. Exact wording, stamp duty, signing steps and the right structure depend on your situation.

## Why the words matter

Under the Partnership Act, partners can set many of their rights and duties by agreement. If they do not agree otherwise, the Act's starting rule is equal shares of profit and loss. It also gives partners a role in running the business and access to its books. A partner may be able to bind the firm in its usual business. Partners are jointly **and individually** liable for acts of the firm while they are partners. That means a partner may have to answer personally for a firm obligation, not just for their agreed share.[^partnership-act]

That last point matters even if your deed says “no one may sign a contract above BDT 50,000 alone.” A limit agreed only between partners may not protect the firm against an outside party who did not know about it. A lawyer can help you set the rule and work out how to make it known to banks, suppliers and customers who deal with the firm.[^partnership-act]

## Copy this meeting sheet

Sit down together before asking someone to draft the deed. Write one clear answer for each line. A blank or a vague “we will decide later” is a decision still waiting to happen.

```text
Firm name and main address: __________
What the firm will do: __________
Partners' full names and addresses: __________
Start date and planned duration: __________
Each person's cash, assets or work: __________
When each contribution is due: __________
Profit shares (total 100%): __________
Loss shares and extra cash calls: __________
Who does which work; any pay: __________
Who can use the bank account: __________
Who can sign and at what limit: __________
Which decisions need everyone: __________
Who keeps books; when all can see them: __________
Who owns code, brand and equipment: __________
New partner, exit and death plan: __________
How to value and pay an exiting partner: __________
How disagreements will be handled: __________
Who checks filing, fees and records: __________
```

**Money:** Write each contribution separately from the profit split. Say whether an extra payment is more capital or a loan to the firm. Agree how the partners handle a loss, take money out, and approve a large expense. Do not assume that putting in more cash automatically gives someone a larger profit share; the deed needs to state the agreed split.[^partnership-act]

**Work and authority:** Name who handles sales, product and accounts. Decide who may use the bank account, sign customer work or borrow money. State which decisions need every partner. Check with your lawyer how a limit on one partner's authority will be communicated to outsiders; a private limit alone may not stop a firm obligation.[^partnership-act]

**Assets and exits:** List any code, designs, brand, domain, equipment and customer contracts already owned by a person or another business. Decide what, if anything, will actually be transferred or licensed to the firm. Write how a new partner joins, how someone leaves, how the remaining partners will settle the accounts, and what happens if the firm closes. Do not assume a sentence in the deed transfers an asset the signer does not own. Ask the lawyer which separate documents and notices are needed.[^partnership-act]

## Try the sheet on one example

**Fictional example:** Mina and Rafi are testing a software service in Dhaka. Mina will put in BDT 150,000 and build the product. Rafi will put in BDT 50,000 and sell it. “We will split things fairly” does not answer whether their cash, monthly work, profit and losses have the same split.

Suppose they discuss **60% of profit and loss for Mina and 40% for Rafi**. On a BDT 100,000 profit, Mina's share would be 100,000 × 60% = **BDT 60,000** and Rafi's would be 100,000 × 40% = **BDT 40,000**, before any separate tax or withdrawal question. They also need to decide what happens if the firm needs another BDT 100,000, who can sign a BDT 200,000 customer contract, and how to value the product if one leaves. These are questions to agree and take to a lawyer, **not recommended percentages or deed clauses**.

If Mina and Rafi expect to issue company shares or raise VC money soon, they should compare a private limited company before setting up a partnership. A partnership profit share is not the same thing as company shares.

## Check the deed against registration details

Signing a deed and **registering a firm** are separate steps. Section 58 of the Partnership Act describes a registration statement with the firm name, business places, when each partner joined, their full names and permanent addresses, and the firm's duration. All partners or specially authorised agents sign and verify it. RJSC's partnership **Form-I** also asks for details such as contributions and profit shares, which should match the deed if one is used.[^registration] [^rjsc-form]

The Act allows a firm to register, but section 69 places serious limits on certain court claims based on contracts by an unregistered firm or partner. It has exceptions, including claims connected with dissolving a firm and settling its accounts. Ask a lawyer what registration means for your contracts; do not treat “we signed a deed” as proof that RJSC registered the firm.[^registration]

Before filing, use the [RJSC partnership forms page](https://roc.gov.bd/pages/forms/6922d9fd933eb65569e01a23) and the [RJSC portal](https://app1.roc.gov.bd/psp/rjschome) to check the live form, document and payment steps. We opened the official forms list and Form-I on 25 September 2026, but the forms list says it was last updated in 2016. These pages do not confirm today's portal flow, fee, stamp amount or processing time. Ask RJSC or your lawyer for the current requirements and a written quote covering legal work, government charges and any stamp duty. If the registered firm's partners or details later change, check the Act's notice rules and the matching RJSC form.[^registration]

## Before anyone signs

- [ ] We chose a partnership firm deliberately and understand the personal-liability risk.
- [ ] Each contribution, profit share and loss share is written clearly; the percentages add up.
- [ ] We agreed who works, uses the bank account and signs for the firm.
- [ ] We listed existing assets and checked who can legally transfer or license them.
- [ ] We agreed how someone joins, leaves, is paid out or resolves a dispute.
- [ ] Names, addresses and dates in the draft match the proposed RJSC Form-I details.
- [ ] A Bangladesh lawyer has checked the deed, signing method and current filing costs before we sign.
- [ ] If we register the firm, we will keep the accepted RJSC record and update it when required.
