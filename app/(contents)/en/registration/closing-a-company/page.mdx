# The Legal Routes for Closing a Company

> **In short:** Stopping the business does not stop the company. Stop filing returns and the obligations keep running while daily fines accumulate — and those fines can land on an officer who knowingly permits the default, not just on the company.[^companies-act-default-penalties] Suspending business for a whole year is itself a ground for the Court to wind the company up.[^companies-act-court-winding-up] Nor does being struck off end it. The Act states plainly that after dissolution the liability of every director and member continues, and can be enforced as if the company had never been dissolved.[^companies-act-strike-off] If you can pay your debts, the cleanest route is a members' voluntary winding up, which begins with the directors making a declaration of solvency.[^companies-act-voluntary-winding-up] Closing properly looks expensive, but it is the cheaper option — the bill for walking away never stops.

What to weigh before deciding to close is a separate question; see [Before you close the company](/registration/before-closing). This page assumes the decision is made, and sets out the legal routes.

---

## Four routes, and one that isn't yours to take

Section 234 of the Companies Act 1994 gives three modes of winding up: by the Court, voluntary, and subject to the supervision of the Court.[^companies-act-court-winding-up] Alongside those sits the Registrar's own power to strike a company off.

| Route | When it applies | Who starts it |
|---|---|---|
| Members' voluntary winding up | Debts can be paid in full within three years | The shareholders |
| Creditors' voluntary winding up | No declaration of solvency can be made | Shareholders first, then creditors |
| Winding up by the Court | Debts can't be paid, or a statutory ground exists | The company, a creditor, or another petitioner |
| Striking off by the Registrar | The Registrar believes the company is no longer operating | The Registrar |

That last route is not in the company's hands. The whole process under section 346 begins on the Registrar's own initiative, not on an application from the company.[^companies-act-strike-off] Waiting it out — "if we do nothing, one day it'll just get struck off" — is not a plan.

## What actually happens if you just walk away

Many founders assume that since the business has stopped, RJSC will forget about it too. The Act does not work that way, and the liabilities stack up one on top of another.

**The filing obligations keep running.** For as long as the company is on the register, the duty to file the annual list and the balance sheet continues. Default carries a fine of up to 200 taka per day under section 36 and up to 100 taka per day under section 190, and failing to hold the AGM carries up to 10,000 taka under section 82.[^companies-act-default-penalties] The full calendar is in [The RJSC filing calendar](/registration/annual-returns).

**A year of dormancy is itself a ground.** Under section 241, if a company does not commence business within a year of incorporation, or suspends its business for a whole year, the Court may order it wound up.[^companies-act-court-winding-up] The same applies if a private company's membership falls below two.[^companies-act-court-winding-up]

**A small creditor can open the door.** Section 242 provides that where a creditor owed more than 500 taka serves a written demand at the registered office and the company neglects for three weeks to pay or otherwise satisfy it, the company is deemed unable to pay its debts.[^companies-act-court-winding-up] One old vendor bill can become the basis for a petition.

**Moving doesn't stop the notices.** Section 346 provides that where no office has been registered, a letter or notice may be sent care of a director, manager, or other officer — and if none of them is known to the Registrar, to each of the subscribers to the memorandum at the address given there.[^companies-act-strike-off] The address you wrote five years ago is where the letter goes.

**And dissolution does not end the liability.** Once the Registrar strikes the name off, the company is dissolved — but the proviso to section 346 is explicit: the liability of every director and member, if any, continues and may be enforced as if the company had not been dissolved.[^companies-act-strike-off] Being struck off is not a discharge.

## If you can pay: members' voluntary winding up

For a startup that can pay its creditors in full, this is the cleanest route. The steps are set by the Act.

**1. Make the declaration of solvency.** Under section 290 the directors — or, where there are more than two, a majority of them — make a declaration at a board meeting, verified by affidavit, that they have made a full inquiry into the company's affairs and have formed the opinion that its debts can be paid in full within three years of the commencement of the winding up.[^companies-act-voluntary-winding-up] The declaration must be supported by the company's auditors' report, and delivered to the Registrar before the notices for the meeting go out — otherwise it has no effect at all.[^companies-act-voluntary-winding-up] If you can't make this declaration, the process becomes a creditors' voluntary winding up instead.[^companies-act-creditors-winding-up]

**2. Pass the resolution.** Section 286 allows a company to be wound up voluntarily by special resolution.[^companies-act-voluntary-winding-up] The winding up is deemed to commence on the day the resolution passes, and from that day the company must cease to carry on business except so far as required for the beneficial winding up.[^companies-act-voluntary-winding-up]

**3. Advertise within 10 days.** Section 289 requires notice of the resolution to be advertised in the official Gazette within 10 days of it passing, and also in a newspaper circulating in the district of the registered office.[^companies-act-voluntary-winding-up] Failing to do so carries a fine of up to 100 taka per day.[^companies-act-voluntary-winding-up]

**4. Appoint a liquidator.** The company in general meeting appoints one or more liquidators and fixes their remuneration. On that appointment all the powers of the directors cease, except so far as the meeting or the liquidator sanctions their continuance.[^companies-act-members-winding-up]

**5. Hold an annual meeting if it runs past a year.** If the winding up continues for more than one year, the liquidator must call a general meeting at the end of each year — or within 90 days of the year's close — and lay an account of the liquidation before it.[^companies-act-members-winding-up]

**6. Final meeting, then dissolution.** Once the affairs are fully wound up, the liquidator prepares an account and calls a final general meeting, advertised at least one month beforehand. Within one week of the meeting, a copy of the account and a return go to the Registrar, and three months after registration the company is deemed dissolved.[^companies-act-members-winding-up]

Note the timing at the end. From the day the resolution passes to dissolution takes several months at minimum, because the one-month advertisement and the three months after registration are both fixed by the Act.

## If you can't make the solvency declaration

A winding up begun without a declaration of solvency is a creditors' voluntary winding up.[^companies-act-creditors-winding-up] Here the creditors carry far more weight.

The company must summon a meeting of creditors, and the creditors and the company may each nominate a liquidator at their respective meetings.[^companies-act-creditors-winding-up] At the end, a final meeting of creditors is called alongside the company's, and once the accounts and returns from both reach the Registrar, the company is dissolved three months later.[^companies-act-creditors-winding-up]

Making a solvency declaration while knowing the debts can't be met is dangerous. Before taking this route, sit down with a corporate lawyer and a chartered accountant.

## Winding up by the Court

Section 241 lists the grounds on which the Court may wind a company up:[^companies-act-court-winding-up]

- a special resolution that the company be wound up by the Court
- default in filing the statutory report or in holding the statutory meeting
- failure to commence business within a year of incorporation, or suspension of business for a whole year
- a private company's membership falling below two
- inability to pay its debts
- the Court's opinion that it is just and equitable to wind the company up

Startups rarely choose this route themselves — a creditor or a shareholder usually drags them into it. So when debts start piling up, it is better to decide on a voluntary winding up before someone else goes to court.

## How striking off actually unfolds

Section 346 sets out the process step by step, and knowing the timings makes it clear why this isn't a route you'd pick.

| Step | What happens | Timing |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The Registrar, having cause to believe the company isn't operating, writes to ask | – |
| 2 | If no reply comes, a second letter by registered post | 30 days after the first, then within 14 days |
| 3 | If still no reply, or a reply saying it isn't operating, a Gazette notice | 30 days after the second letter |
| 4 | Unless cause is shown, the name is struck off and dissolution is notified | 90 days after that notice |

It takes time, but the outcome is not in the company's control. Meanwhile the filing defaults and fines keep accruing throughout.

If the company, a member, or a creditor is aggrieved by the striking off, they can apply to the Court. If satisfied, the Court may restore the company to the register, and it is then treated as if its name had never been struck off.[^companies-act-strike-off]

Where a company is already being wound up, and either no liquidator is acting or the liquidator's returns have not come in for six consecutive months after the Registrar's demand, the Registrar may publish the same kind of notice.[^companies-act-strike-off]

## Everything outside RJSC

Closing a company is not only RJSC paperwork. These have to be finished too, and each has its own office and process:

- **Income tax:** the final return and settling any tax liability. That process belongs to NBR, so talk to a tax adviser before closing the books.
- **VAT or turnover tax:** if you hold a <Term name="vat-bin">BIN</Term>, the registration has to be cancelled. See [BIN application and VAT registration](/tax/vat-bin).
- **Trade licence:** don't assume that simply not renewing is enough — ask the relevant city corporation or municipality.
- **Employee dues:** wages, notice period, and any benefits owed. For the labour law side, see [Labour law basics](/team/labor-law).
- **Banking and payments:** closing the company account, payment gateway, and merchant accounts.
- **Sector licences:** an IRC, ERC, food, pharmaceutical, or other licence must be surrendered under its own rules.

None of these six sit in the Companies Act; each runs on its own authority's rules. Have a chartered accountant or company secretary build you a single list so nothing is left hanging.

## What it costs

The RJSC filing fee for a winding-up application is 20 taka for both private and public companies.[^rjsc-fees-schedule] Don't read too much into that number — it is only RJSC's own filing fee.

The real cost sits elsewhere: the liquidator's remuneration, the auditors' report, legal fees, the Gazette and newspaper advertisements, and settling outstanding debts and taxes. None of those has a published government rate, so this page gives no figure for them. Get quotes from two or three places, and confirm in writing what each quote includes.

## Checklist

- [ ] Worked out whether the debts can genuinely be paid in full within three years
- [ ] If solvent, filed the declaration with the auditors' report at the Registrar before the meeting notices went out
- [ ] Advertised in the Gazette and a newspaper within 10 days of the special resolution
- [ ] Appointed a liquidator and recorded in writing that the directors' powers ceased
- [ ] Advertised the final meeting at least one month in advance
- [ ] Sent the account and return to the Registrar within one week of the meeting
- [ ] Closed income tax, VAT, trade licence, employee dues, banking, and sector licences one by one against a list
- [ ] Took advice from a CA and a corporate lawyer throughout

Rule of thumb: **Abandoning the company is the most expensive decision available. A director's liability survives even the striking off — so however costly closing properly looks, it is the cheaper path.**
