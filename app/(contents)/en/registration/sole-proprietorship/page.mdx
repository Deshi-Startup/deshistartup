# Should You Start as a Sole Proprietorship?

> **In short:** A sole proprietorship needs no separate registration at RJSC or any other central registry. A [trade license](/trade-license) plus your own personal e-TIN is your entire "registration." Business income is counted as your personal income, not taxed at a separate company rate. Once annual turnover crosses BDT 3 million, Turnover Tax applies; past BDT 5 million, full VAT registration applies.[^nbr-vat] Because the business and you are not legally separate, business debt is fully your own liability too. That last point is really the decision that matters most here.

Comparing structures is not this page's job – see [company-types](/company-types) for that. This page assumes you're already leaning toward a sole proprietorship and want to know what actually running one involves.

---

## Registration Here Doesn't Mean RJSC

Many people assume "registering" a business always means going to RJSC. For a sole proprietorship, that's not true. Two documents are enough:

1. **Trade license** – from your city corporation, paurashava, or union parishad, proving this business operates at this address. The full process is in the [trade license guide](/trade-license).
2. **Personal e-TIN** – not a company TIN, your own taxpayer identity. [Personal e-TIN vs. company TIN](/tax/personal-vs-company-tin) explains the difference.

Those two documents are enough to legally run the business. There's no MoA, AoA, or shareholder list to file with RJSC, because no separate "company" is being created in the eyes of the law. The business is simply you, operating under a trading name.

---

## Tax Is Calculated Like Your Own Income

This is where most of the confusion happens. A private limited company pays tax at a fixed corporate rate. A sole proprietorship has no separate corporate rate at all. Business profit gets added to your personal income and taxed at Bangladesh's ordinary progressive individual rates: BDT 400,000 tax-free for a general taxpayer, then successive bands at 10%, 15%, 20%, 25%, and 30%.[^nbr-slab] The Finance Act 2026 locked these rates in for five years, from AY 2026-27 through AY 2030-31, with the tax-free threshold stepping up to BDT 500,000 by the final year.[^finance-act-2026]

In practice, that means your tax rate climbs as the business does well. This is one reason many growing sole proprietorships eventually move to a private limited company – not only for investors, but because the tax picture can then be structured separately from the owner's personal income.

---

## When VAT or Turnover Tax Kicks In

NBR's VAT registration guidance sets out three bands:[^nbr-vat]

| Annual turnover | What applies |
|---|---|
| Up to BDT 3 million | No VAT or Turnover Tax |
| BDT 3,000,001 to 5 million | 3% Turnover Tax |
| Above BDT 5 million | Full VAT registration, at 15% |

Start preparing for BIN registration before you actually cross a threshold. Finding out at the last minute usually means penalties or a messy retroactive filing. [e-TIN, VAT, and BIN](/e-tin-vat-bin) covers the application steps.

---

## Banking and Liability Sit in the Same Place

You can, and should, open a separate bank account under the trade license name. But a separate account doesn't mean separate liability. In a sole proprietorship, the business and the owner are legally the same person, so if a business debt goes unpaid, a creditor can reach the owner's personal assets too. Under a registered private limited company, that liability generally sits with the company itself, not the individual – see the full comparison at [sole proprietorship vs. private limited](/company-types).

---

## If the Owner Steps Away or Wants to Sell

A sole proprietorship has no legal existence of its own, so it doesn't survive without the owner. If the owner dies, the trade license, bank account, and contracts have to be redone in the name of an heir or successor – they don't transfer automatically. Selling the whole business as one package also has no simple legal shortcut. The buyer needs a new trade license, and contracts with customers and suppliers often need to be re-signed. How complicated this gets depends on the size and specifics of the business – talk to a lawyer before any large transfer.

---

## The Path to Converting to a Private Limited Company Later

Moving from a sole proprietorship to a private limited company isn't a single upgrade button – it takes real, separate work:

- [RJSC name clearance and registration](/registration/private-limited) has to be done from scratch; the old trade license doesn't automatically move to the company's name
- A new trade license and bank account are needed under the company's name
- Running contracts, invoices, and supplier relationships need to be re-set under the company
- Equipment, stock, or the brand need to be formally assigned to the company

Knowing these steps ahead of time turns the conversion from a sudden shock into a planned move.

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Trade license obtained, or the process is underway
- [ ] Personal e-TIN exists, and business income is being tied to it
- [ ] A separate bank account has been opened for the business
- [ ] Preparation is underway for Turnover Tax once annual turnover passes BDT 3 million
- [ ] I've accepted that business debt can become my personal liability
- [ ] I have a rough sense of when I'd move to a private limited company as the business grows

Rule of thumb: **If you want minimal paperwork, a sole proprietorship is the easy path. But once business debt could get large, or ownership starts being shared with someone else, treat that as the signal to move toward a private limited company.**
