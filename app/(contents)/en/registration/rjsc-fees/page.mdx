# RJSC Fees and Timeline: The Full Budget Picture

> **In short:** Once authorized capital goes past 10 lakh taka, RJSC's fee math changes — the AoA stamp rises from 2,000 to 4,000 taka, and a share capital fee starts stacking on at 80 or 130 taka per lakh.[^rjsc-fees-schedule] Fees don't stop after registration either: annual return and change filings cost 200 taka per document, and late filing adds a 500-taka-per-day penalty, capped at 1,000 taka per document.[^rjsc-fees-schedule] The Companies Act's 30-day provision isn't a promise — in practice, queries and portal load can push it longer.[^companies-act-registration] So don't close your budget after reading the government fee table, and leave room for notary, professional, and bank charges too.

---

## How many tiers RJSC's fees actually have

500 taka per proposed name for name clearance, and 4,700 taka in government cost to register with authorized capital up to 10 lakh taka — both numbers are already on this site's [name clearance](/registration/name-clearance) and [private limited registration](/registration/private-limited) guides. This page goes a level deeper: how the fee climbs past 10 lakh, which fees keep coming after registration, and how long things actually take beyond the 30-day statutory window.

RJSC's fee schedule has five major categories: name clearance, registration (stamp, filing, and share capital fees combined), return filing, certified copy issuance, and winding up.[^rjsc-fees-schedule] Private companies, public companies, foreign companies, trade organizations, societies, and partnership firms each have their own rates. This page covers only private-company rates, since that's what matters for most Deshi Startup readers. The same rates apply to an OPC too, since an OPC is legally registered under the private-company structure.

## How the fee climbs as authorized capital rises

The MoA stamp is a flat 1,000 taka and the 6-document filing fee is a flat 1,200 taka — both stay the same no matter how much capital you're registering.[^rjsc-fees-schedule] But the AoA stamp and the share capital fee both climb with authorized capital.

RJSC's fee schedule bands the AoA stamp into three tiers:[^rjsc-fees-schedule]

| Authorized capital | AoA stamp |
|---|---:|
| Up to 10 lakh taka | 2,000 taka |
| Above 10 lakh, up to 3 crore taka | 4,000 taka |
| Above 3 crore taka | 10,000 taka |

The share capital fee is fully zero up to 10 lakh taka. Above that, an additional fee stacks on per 1 lakh taka or part thereof:[^rjsc-fees-schedule]

| Authorized capital | Share capital fee |
|---|---:|
| Up to 10 lakh taka | 0 taka |
| Above 10 lakh, up to 50 lakh taka | 80 taka per 1 lakh or part thereof |
| Above 50 lakh taka | 130 taka per 1 lakh or part thereof |

Say you're registering an OPC at the minimum 25 lakh taka in capital.[^rjsc-fees-schedule] The AoA stamp is 4,000 taka, since you've crossed 10 lakh. The share capital fee applies to the 15 lakh taka above the 10-lakh threshold, at 80 taka per lakh — 1,200 taka. Add the 1,000-taka MoA stamp and the 1,200-taka filing fee, and the government cost comes to 7,400 taka; add one name-clearance fee of 500 taka, and the total is 7,900 taka.

Here's how the total jumps as capital keeps rising:

| Authorized capital | Government total (one name clearance) |
|---|---:|
| Up to 10 lakh taka | 4,700 taka |
| 25 lakh taka (OPC minimum) | 7,900 taka |
| 1 crore taka | 16,400 taka |

The formula: MoA stamp 1,000 + AoA stamp (by tier) + filing fee 1,200 + share capital fee (by tier) + name clearance 500.[^rjsc-fees-schedule] Each additional proposed name adds another 500 taka. This is only the government fee and stamp total — it doesn't include payment charges or professional fees, which are covered separately below. Above 3 crore taka in authorized capital, or for public/foreign companies, the rates differ — confirm against the portal's challan or RJSC's own fee schedule before paying.

## Fees don't stop after registration

Company registration isn't a one-time cost. While the company is running, RJSC charges separately for return filing and certified copies.

RJSC returns come in two kinds: annual returns, and returns for any change at the company (directors, registered office, capital).[^rjsc-faq-returns] Both fall under the same fee schedule:[^rjsc-fees-schedule]

| Action | Fee |
|---|---:|
| Filing any return or document on time | 200 taka per document |
| Filing after the deadline | 500 taka per day, capped at 1,000 taka per document |

The late fee is calculated separately per document. If two forms are filed late, the penalty is assessed separately for each, not combined. The full annual return calendar and required paperwork are in a [separate guide](/registration/annual-returns).

Certified copies or record inspection also carry a fee:[^rjsc-fees-schedule]

| Item | Fee |
|---|---:|
| Record inspection | 100 taka |
| Copy of Certificate of Incorporation | 100 taka |
| Copy of Certificate of Commencement | 100 taka |
| Non-judicial stamp on MoA, AoA, or other documents (each) | 20 taka |
| Court fee stamp (per application) | 20 taka |
| Document copying (per 100 words or part thereof) | 5 taka, minimum 100 taka |

When you'll need which copy and how to apply is covered in full in the [certified copies guide](/registration/certified-copies).

When closing a company entirely, RJSC's own winding-up filing fee is just 20 taka for both private and public companies.[^rjsc-fees-schedule] That figure is only RJSC's filing fee, not the full cost of closing a company — accountant fees, tax clearance, and settling liabilities can add up to far more. What to think through before closing is covered in a [separate guide](/registration/before-closing).

## The gap between RJSC's 30-day provision and real-world timing

Section 23 of the Companies Act states that if documents are in order, the Registrar has a 30-day provision.[^companies-act-registration] That figure is a statutory limit, not a promise. How long it actually takes in practice depends on queries, document mismatches, and portal load.

RJSC doesn't publish a specific day count for name clearance — how long it takes varies by application. The 30-day validity of a clearance and the rules for extending it are already covered in detail in the [name clearance guide](/registration/name-clearance). The reminder here: start preparing your registration paperwork the moment you get clearance, since the validity clock starts from the issue date.

There's no official day-count promise for the registration step itself, but a few legal advisory firms have published numbers from their own service experience. One firm claims 3 to 4 working days for a certificate when documents are complete and query-free,[^jural-timeline] another gives a figure of 5 to 10 working days.[^vatax-timeline] Both are service providers' experience-based estimates, not RJSC's own commitment. Queries or document mismatches will push the time further.

Placing these numbers in a wider context makes the picture clearer. In a February 2024 report, The Business Standard quoted BIDA's then-executive chairman, Lokman Hossain Miah, saying that all government services needed to start a business take 35 days in Vietnam, 49 days in Indonesia, and 60 days in India — versus roughly six months in Bangladesh.[^tbs-timeline] The same report described RJSC's plan to bring the entire registration process online, which, if it succeeded, was meant to bring the time down to one week.[^tbs-timeline] As of when this page was written (4 August 2026), how far that full automation has actually gotten couldn't be independently verified. So don't set a deadline for an urgent contract or launch assuming "one week."

The most common reasons registration takes longer:

- Small mismatches in name, address, or ID details across documents
- Share numbers not matching between the MoA, AoA, and subscriber page
- Submitting a foreign shareholder's encashment certificate paperwork late
- Sitting on a query instead of answering it quickly, or wasting time rebuilding the whole application from scratch

A detailed list of these mistakes is in the [RJSC common mistakes guide](/registration/rjsc-mistakes).

## What to budget beyond the government fee

All the tables above are government fees and stamps paid directly to RJSC. In practice, a few more costs apply that RJSC doesn't collect:

- **Notary:** Some documents need notarization. Cost varies by notary and by city; RJSC doesn't publish a fixed rate for this, so ask your notary in advance.
- **CA or lawyer fees:** Many founders hire a CA firm or corporate lawyer to prepare the MoA/AoA and handle the full filing. This fee varies a lot by firm and scope of work, so don't decide based on a single quote. Confirm in writing whether the quote includes the government fee or is separate from it.
- **Bank charges:** Both opening the company's bank account and, for foreign shareholders, obtaining the encashment certificate for incoming funds can carry the bank's own service charges. Ask the branch directly.

None of these three has a single published government rate, so this page doesn't give a specific taka figure for them. Get quotes from at least two or three places and budget extra.

- [ ] Calculated the AoA stamp and share capital fee for your authorized capital
- [ ] Added the name-clearance fee based on how many names you're proposing
- [ ] Budgeted for annual return and change-filing fees
- [ ] Put filing deadlines on the calendar to avoid late fees
- [ ] Didn't set a hard deadline based only on the law's 30-day provision
- [ ] Left extra budget for notary, professional, and bank charges
- [ ] Treated the portal's challan as final before paying

Rule of thumb: **Don't close your budget after reading the fee tables, and don't close your timeline assuming "30 days and done." Fees rise with capital, timelines stretch with queries — leave slack for both.**
