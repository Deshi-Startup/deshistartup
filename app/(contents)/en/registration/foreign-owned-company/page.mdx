# Basics of Starting a Foreign-Owned Company

> **In short:** Company incorporation happens at RJSC whether the shareholders are Bangladeshi or foreign. Foreign capital adds a temporary bank account, a bank <Term name="encashment-certificate">Encashment Certificate</Term>, share allotment and Bangladesh Bank reporting. Invest Bangladesh registration is a separate scope question: its current FAQ says manufacturing and service industries can register, but commercial and trading activities do not require it.[^bida-faq] If share issuance is properly reported, repatriating profit generally does not need prior Bangladesh Bank approval.[^bb-repatriation] This page covers only where the path diverges from [domestic private-limited registration](/en/registration/private-limited).

Suppose a Bangladeshi-origin entrepreneur based in Singapore wants to open a software company in Dhaka, with their own money and no local co-founder. Getting <Term name="rjsc-name-clearance">name clearance</Term> on the RJSC site alone does not finish the job. Proof that money arrived from abroad, the reporting trail and a check on whether Invest Bangladesh registration applies — those extra layers get added to the process.

Because this touches foreign exchange and ownership, mistakes here are expensive to fix. Sit down with a lawyer or accountant who works on FDI before any major decision.

---

## RJSC is required; Invest Bangladesh depends on the activity

Like a domestic company, name clearance and incorporation go through RJSC. Invest Bangladesh's current project page calls its service **industrial project registration**, while its FAQ says both manufacturing and service industries can register and that commercial and trading activities do not require registration.[^bida-faq] Foreign ownership alone therefore does not make Invest Bangladesh registration universal. If the activity falls within its scope, use Invest Bangladesh OSS — still hosted at the legacy `bidaquickserv.org` address — and check the current service instructions before filing.[^bida-faq]

---

## Joint Venture, or Fully Foreign-Owned

A private limited company can have a minimum of two and a maximum of 50 shareholders.[^tnp-foreign] The company can be wholly foreign-owned without a Bangladeshi shareholder, but an ordinary private limited company still needs at least two members. Wholly foreign-owned does not mean a single individual can hold every share. A local partner is an option for a joint venture. How much foreign ownership is allowed varies by sector, so once the sector is settled, check the current policy with Invest Bangladesh and the relevant sector regulator.

Two lighter alternatives to incorporating exist, useful for understanding the market before committing to a full company:

- **Liaison office** – for communication and market research only, cannot make direct sales or profit-generating transactions
- **Branch office** – operates as an extension of the foreign parent company, no new separate legal entity is created

---

## How to Bring Capital Into the Country

Before filing with RJSC, a bank account has to be opened in the company's name at a scheduled Bangladeshi bank. The foreign shareholder's portion of the capital has to be sent to that account through the formal banking channel — not cash, not an informal route. Once the money arrives, the bank issues an Encashment Certificate. Keep it for RJSC and the authorised dealer bank's reporting trail, and for Invest Bangladesh if the project registration applies.[^bida-faq]

There is no universal statutory minimum capital in company law specifically for foreign shareholders.[^jural-capital] Do not confuse a locally incorporated company with a branch, liaison, representative or project office: Invest Bangladesh's FAQ says those office forms must bring at least USD 50,000 within two months of approval.[^bida-faq] Work permits and investor visas have separate document, fee and eligibility rules, so ask the authorised dealer bank and the current Invest Bangladesh service desk before finalising the capital plan.

---

## Repatriating Profit Is Easier Than It Used to Be

Bangladesh Bank’s FDI guidance permits remittance of cash dividends, net of tax, to non-resident shareholders without prior approval, subject to the required records and reporting.[^bb-repatriation] Ask the authorised dealer bank for the applicable route. NITA is relevant to investments made through that account; it is not a universal prerequisite for every direct-investment dividend. Share-sale proceeds have separate valuation, reporting and approval rules under the 8 March 2026 master circular.[^bb-share-sale]

---

## Who Actually Runs the Business Day to Day

Holding shares and actually running the business from an office in Bangladesh are two different questions. Being a remote shareholder doesn't require a special visa. But a foreign national who wants to actively run the company from within Bangladesh runs into work permit or investor visa questions, which are outside this page's scope. Immigration rules change often, so rather than specific advice here, this is a reminder to raise that question early.

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Budgeted for RJSC, bank and reporting steps, plus Invest Bangladesh registration only if it applies
- [ ] Planned to open a bank account in the company's name at a Bangladeshi bank
- [ ] Arranged to send capital through the formal banking channel, not cash
- [ ] Prepared to obtain and retain the Encashment Certificate
- [ ] Checked the sector-specific foreign-ownership limit with Invest Bangladesh and the sector regulator
- [ ] Looked into visa/work permit requirements if running the business day-to-day from Bangladesh
- [ ] Consulted a lawyer or accountant experienced with FDI

Rule of thumb: **Do not assume either extreme: RJSC alone is not the whole foreign-capital process, and Invest Bangladesh registration is not automatic for every foreign-owned company. Check the activity, then preserve the bank and reporting trail.**
