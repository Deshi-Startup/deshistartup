# Basics of Starting a Foreign-Owned Company

> **In short:** A domestic founder only needs to register with RJSC. With a foreign shareholder, BIDA registration comes alongside it, and you need a bank Encashment Certificate as proof that capital arrived from abroad through the banking channel.[^bida-faq] If share issuance is properly reported, repatriating profit generally doesn't need prior Bangladesh Bank approval.[^bb-repatriation] This page covers only where the path diverges from [domestic private-limited registration](/registration/private-limited).

Suppose a Bangladeshi-origin entrepreneur based in Singapore wants to open a software company in Dhaka, with their own money and no local co-founder. Getting name clearance on the RJSC site alone doesn't finish the job. Proof that money arrived from abroad, BIDA registration, and the rules for sending profit back out — three extra layers get added to their process.

Because this touches foreign exchange and ownership, mistakes here are expensive to fix. Sit down with a lawyer or accountant who works on FDI before any major decision.

---

## Two Registrations Are Needed: RJSC and BIDA

Like a domestic company, name clearance and registration go through RJSC. Alongside that, registration with BIDA is also required — this is the body that oversees foreign investment in Bangladesh and runs the One Stop Service for foreign investors.[^bida-faq] The two agencies' steps run close together in time; name clearance and opening a bank account for capital both sit early in both processes.[^bida-faq][^tnp-foreign] Sources differ slightly on the exact sequence of which has to finish "first," so check BIDA's current guidance before applying.

---

## Joint Venture, or Fully Foreign-Owned

A private limited company can have a minimum of two and a maximum of 50 shareholders.[^tnp-foreign] A foreign founder can incorporate fully under their own ownership, or bring in a local partner as a joint venture. How much foreign ownership is allowed varies by sector, so once the sector is settled, check the current policy for it with BIDA.

Two lighter alternatives to incorporating exist, useful for understanding the market before committing to a full company:

- **Liaison office** – for communication and market research only, cannot make direct sales or profit-generating transactions
- **Branch office** – operates as an extension of the foreign parent company, no new separate legal entity is created

---

## How to Bring Capital Into the Country

Before filing with RJSC, a bank account has to be opened in the company's name at a scheduled Bangladeshi bank. The foreign shareholder's portion of the capital has to be sent to that account through the formal banking channel — not cash, not an informal route. Once the money arrives, the bank issues an Encashment Certificate, which is the proof RJSC and BIDA need that the capital genuinely came from abroad.[^bida-faq]

There's no universal statutory minimum capital in company law specifically for foreign shareholders. In practice, though, banks and BIDA aren't comfortable with small amounts, and some legal advisory firms note that a work permit or investor visa for a foreign employee can practically require a larger capital figure.[^jural-capital] The exact number depends on your sector and plans, so talk directly to BIDA or a bank before finalizing a figure.

---

## Repatriating Profit Is Easier Than It Used to Be

If share issuance and transfer are properly reported to Bangladesh Bank, repatriating dividends on those shares generally doesn't need prior approval.[^bb-repatriation] This runs through a Non-resident Investor's Taka Account (NITA). Rules for repatriating share sale proceeds have also loosened recently — media reports describe a specific ceiling below which prior approval isn't needed.[^bb-share-sale] That ceiling can change, so check Bangladesh Bank's own portal or your bank before repatriating.

---

## Who Actually Runs the Business Day to Day

Holding shares and actually running the business from an office in Bangladesh are two different questions. Being a remote shareholder doesn't require a special visa. But a foreign national who wants to actively run the company from within Bangladesh runs into work permit or investor visa questions, which are outside this page's scope. Immigration rules change often, so rather than specific advice here, this is a reminder to raise that question early.

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Budgeted time and cost for both RJSC and BIDA registration
- [ ] Planned to open a bank account in the company's name at a Bangladeshi bank
- [ ] Arranged to send capital through the formal banking channel, not cash
- [ ] Prepared to obtain and retain the Encashment Certificate
- [ ] Checked the sector-specific foreign-ownership limit with BIDA
- [ ] Looked into visa/work permit requirements if running the business day-to-day from Bangladesh
- [ ] Consulted a lawyer or accountant experienced with FDI

Rule of thumb: **Don't assume knowing the RJSC process is enough for a foreign-owned company. Understand BIDA, bank encashment, and profit repatriation as three separate pieces.**
