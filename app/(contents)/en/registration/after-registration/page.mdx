# What to Do After Company Registration

> **In short:** Getting the <Term name="incorp-certificate">Certificate of Incorporation</Term> means the company exists — it doesn't mean it's ready to operate. Seven or eight more tasks remain, and each one depends on the one before it. No <Term name="e-tin">TIN</Term> blocks the <Term name="trade-license">trade license</Term>; no trade license blocks the bank account. Skip the order and you'll end up back at the same office more than once. Each task has its own detailed guide elsewhere on this site; this page's only job is to sequence them. Start with the company's TIN — nearly every step after this one needs it.

Many founders assume registering the company is the hard part, and everything after gets easier automatically. In practice it sometimes goes the other way, because not understanding which document depends on which sends you back to the same office two or three times. This page assumes a domestically-owned private limited company. If there's a foreign shareholder, extra steps apply — see [Basics of starting a foreign-owned company](/registration/foreign-owned-company).

---

## Step 1: Get the company's TIN

Once you have the Certificate of Incorporation, get the company's own TIN first. The trade license, bank account, and VAT registration all need this number for nearly every step that follows. A director's personal TIN and the company's TIN are two different things — mixing them up causes accounting and return-filing problems later. The application happens online through NBR's portal.

Details: [e-TIN: whose name, how to apply](/tax/e-tin), and the rule for keeping personal and company numbers separate is in [Personal e-TIN vs. company TIN](/tax/personal-vs-company-tin).

---

## Step 2: Get the trade license

Once the company's TIN is in hand, move to the trade license. Nearly every bank includes it in the required-documents list for opening an account, so skipping it means an empty-handed trip to the bank. You apply at the city corporation or municipality that covers your registered office's address. Write the business category correctly — this one mistake alone causes trouble later when matching VAT or bank paperwork.

Details: [Trade License – permission to operate locally](/trade-license).

---

## Step 3: Open a bank account in the company's name

With TIN and trade license in hand, the account-opening application gets a lot easier. Customer money, payment gateway settlements, and shareholders' <Term name="paid-up-capital">paid-up capital</Term> all flow through this account. Mixing business money into a personal account makes it hard to keep the paid-up capital accounting straight later. Minimum deposit amounts vary a lot by bank, so don't give up after checking just one.

Details: [Business bank account: which bank to open with](/payments/bank-account). If you sell online, gateway or merchant account applications may also require a DBID — requirements vary by provider; see [What you need to open a merchant account](/payments/merchant-account) and [Digital Business Identity (DBID)](/registration/dbid).

---

## Step 4: Check whether you need VAT or turnover tax registration

Not every company needs <Term name="vat-bin">VAT registration</Term>, but "we'll figure it out later" isn't a safe assumption either. Turnover and business type together determine whether you need full VAT registration, turnover tax listing, or neither yet. Even with low turnover, certain goods or services fall under NBR's special list and require registration — and a major buyer may ask for a specific kind of invoice simply because you have a BIN.

Details: [When you need full VAT registration vs. turnover tax](/tax/when-vat-required), for the math see [30 lakh or 50 lakh](/tax/vat-thresholds), and for the registration steps see [BIN application: what to match before filing Mushak-2.1](/tax/vat-bin).

---

## Step 5: Check for sector-specific licenses

A trade license doesn't mean every permission is covered. Regulated sectors — food, pharmaceuticals, factories, import/export, transport, fintech — need additional licenses. Some approvals, like a fire license or environmental clearance, take a while to get, so if your business falls into any of these sectors, start that application now rather than leaving it for the last minute.

Details: the [Licenses & approvals](/licenses) section has sector-specific guides.

---

## Step 6: Hold the first board meeting, issue share certificates, and fix the registers

Once the paperwork rush eases a bit, put the company's internal records in order too. Call a board meeting to finalize bank account signatories, issue share certificates to shareholders, and keep the member register current. Verbally agreeing "we're at 60-40" doesn't count as proof — a bank or a future investor will want paperwork. Write meeting decisions into the minute book starting from day one.

Details: [Share capital, directors, and shareholders: make the numbers match](/registration/shares-and-directors), and [Board meetings, AGMs, and minute book basics](/registration/board-meetings-agm).

---

## Step 7: Set up basic bookkeeping

Start recording sales, expenses, and bank transactions in a ledger or simple bookkeeping software from month one. Because the business is small, many founders let bookkeeping slide, thinking they'll sort it out later. When it's time to file a VAT return or do a year-end audit, that means sitting down to reconcile old receipts and bank statements at once — which takes far longer. Keep the receipt or invoice for every transaction, and reconcile against the bank statement at least once a month.

---

## Step 8: Set up the annual compliance calendar

Finishing all the steps above doesn't end the work. After registration, obligations continue — the AGM, regular board meetings, and RJSC's annual return filing all have deadlines. Missing a date means growing penalties, so put these dates on your phone's calendar from day one.

Details: [RJSC annual returns and filing calendar](/registration/annual-returns).

---

## Checklist

- [ ] Got the company's TIN
- [ ] Applied for the trade license, or already have it
- [ ] Opened a bank account in the company's name
- [ ] Checked whether VAT or turnover tax registration applies
- [ ] Started any sector-specific license application that applies
- [ ] Held the first board meeting and updated share certificates and the member register
- [ ] Set up a regular bookkeeping routine
- [ ] Put AGM, board meeting, and annual return dates on the calendar

Rule of thumb: **Follow the order: TIN, trade license, bank account. Each depends on the one before it — get the sequence wrong and you'll end up back at the same office.**
