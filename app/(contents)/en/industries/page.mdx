# Industry Guides: Adapt the Plan to the Sector

> **In short:** A sector changes who buys, how delivery works and which risks must be checked. Use this page to identify those differences before copying a business model.

## Start with an available sector journey

- [Edtech](/en/journeys/build-an-edtech-startup): connect a learning problem with the buyer and delivery model.
- [Agritech](/en/journeys/build-an-agritech-startup): investigate the people and operating constraints in the value chain.
- [Fintech](/en/journeys/build-a-fintech-startup): investigate the activity and regulatory boundaries before testing a financial service.
- [Local services](/en/journeys/build-a-local-services-startup): connect demand with reliable service delivery.
- [Travel technology](/en/journeys/build-a-travel-tech-startup): work through the customer, partner and fulfilment questions.

These journeys are starting points, not evidence that a sector is attractive or that a particular activity is permitted.

## Example: similar software, different business questions

A **user** receives or uses the service; a **buyer** pays for it. They may differ. A **value chain** is the sequence of people and organisations needed to produce and deliver the result.

Compare two fictional products that both send reminders:

| Question | Training-centre timetable tool | Fresh-produce collection tool |
|---|---|---|
| User and buyer | Staff use it; an owner may approve payment | Collection staff use it; the paying party must be established |
| Failure to investigate | People arrive at the wrong class time | Collection is missed and produce may deteriorate |
| Delivery dependency | Accurate timetable and contact process | Timing, transport, handling and accurate pickup information |
| First learning task | Observe a recent timetable change with dummy records | Map a recent collection from grower to receiving point |

The software feature does not determine the business. In the second case, a reminder cannot repair missing transport capacity. In either case, permissions, safety and data practices must be checked for the actual activity; the table does not establish legal eligibility.

A useful sector brief would state “transport availability is unknown” instead of assuming an app controls collection. Write the actor responsible for every hand-off, the consequence if it fails and how you will investigate it. You are done when the brief exposes a sector-specific constraint that changes your first test.

## Make a sector brief

On one page, name the user, buyer, existing alternative, delivery partners, payment timing and the most serious failure a customer could experience. Add the permissions or specialist advice you must investigate before a test.

Then choose one unknown to research with [customer interviews](/en/validation/customer-interviews). Use [licenses and approvals](/en/licenses) for an applicability check and [market research](/en/ideas) for evidence about demand.
