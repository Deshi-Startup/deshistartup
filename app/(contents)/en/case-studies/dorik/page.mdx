# Dorik

<CaseCompany slug="dorik" locale="en" />

> **In short:** Operating from Sylhet, Dorik grew from a bootstrapped side project into a global website builder used by more than 100,000 creators and agencies around the world. Its big breakthrough came from keeping things simple: letting non-technical people build fast, clean websites without writing code. But as marketing agencies began using the platform to build client sites, the founders realized their cheap pricing was actually hurting them - it made corporate buyers suspicious and flooded customer support. Dorik’s journey is a fantastic playbook for SaaS founders on finding <Term name="pmf">product-market fit</Term> and matching prices to the customers it serves.[^founder][^profile][^pricing-decision]

A small business owner who needs a website rarely wants to learn complicated software code. They just want a clean online storefront: their services clearly listed, photos of past work, and a simple contact form for new customers.

Dorik was built to make that easy. Founded in Sylhet, the platform lets creators and marketing agencies worldwide design and launch custom websites without writing a single line of code. Instead of spending years building a huge, complex tool, the founders started with a simple single-page website builder, charging real users during private beta tests to make sure people would actually pay for it.[^early-review][^first-sales]

## The idea came from work the founders already understood

Mizanur Rahman spent years working as a freelance web designer on international freelancing sites. In 2014, he began selling website design templates, but noticed a constant frustration: buyers who did not know how to code struggled to customize HTML, CSS, and WordPress files. Between 2014 and 2018, he tried several times to build an easy drag-and-drop website builder. In a 2021 interview, he shared that those early attempts failed because the software was too complicated and his team was too small.[^founder]

By 2019, modern web technologies like React had made it much easier to build fast website tools. With better engineering skills and steady customer requests for a simpler tool, Mizanur and Anwar Hussain decided to try once more. That project became Dorik.

Their template buyers gave them an exact target user: small business owners and freelancers who wanted a modern website without learning to code or paying expensive design agency retainers.

A 2022 feature by The Daily Star reported that most of Dorik's 45-person team worked from Sylhet, alongside colleagues in Dhaka and Chattogram.[^profile] Building a global software business did not require moving to Silicon Valley or London; it required building an intuitive product that customers across the world could discover, test, and buy online.

## The first product left a lot out

When Dorik launched its private beta in 2020, it did not try to do everything: users could build only single-page websites, connect a custom domain name, and collect customer emails through contact forms. A review from July 2020 noted that multi-page menus and blog publishing were still in progress.[^early-review]

A single-page website builder might sound limited compared to giants like WordPress. But keeping the first version small let the founders finish and launch quickly. A single-page site solved real, urgent problems: a freelancer needed an online portfolio, a startup wanted a quick landing page, or a local service business wanted a contact form.

Importantly, the early review noted that Mizanur actively asked for feedback from strangers on the internet, not just friendly past customers.[^early-review]

Testing software with strangers is essential. Friends and loyal fans often forgive confusing buttons and bugs; strangers will tell you immediately when something is hard to use or broken.

## The first buyers paid for access, not a promise of future revenue

In a July 2020 Indie Hackers post, Mizanur shared how he added a payment checkout to the private beta, landing eight paying customers in the first seven days. When early users asked for a lifetime deal, the team offered a $49 lifetime pass. After a customer posted an excited tweet about the editor, Mizanur sent an email to registered beta users, making $1,054 in direct cash sales within 24 hours.[^first-sales]

Charging money early proved that the product had real value. Paying users tested features thoroughly, reported bugs, and recommended the tool to friends while the software was still young.

The team followed this initial success with a public launch on Product Hunt on 10 August 2020, winning the #2 Product of the Day award.[^launch] Notice the smart sequence: Dorik proved customers would pay real money before seeking launch day publicity.

For a <Term name="bootstrapping">bootstrapped</Term> startup, lifetime deals bring in welcome cash to pay for servers and developers without selling company shares. But founders must remember: lifetime payments bring zero monthly subscription revenue (<Term name="mrr-arr">MRR</Term>), while users expect free hosting and customer support forever.

Upfront cash gave Dorik early breathing room, but building a lasting software company meant moving to monthly and annual subscriptions.

## Agencies changed what the product needed to do

In October 2022, Dorik launched a white-label agency portal alongside an announcement of pre-seed funding from US venture investors.[^funding] White-labeling lets design studios and marketing agencies put their own brand and logo on the Dorik website editor for their clients.

Serving marketing agencies completely changed Dorik's business:
- A solo creator pays for just one personal website.
- A design agency builds, hosts, and manages dozens of websites for paying clients, bringing in far higher customer value (<Term name="ltv">LTV</Term>).

To help agencies, Dorik built agency-specific tools: custom client dashboards, user permissions, client billing integrations, and smooth site handoff tools.[^agency]

Winning agency accounts took demo calls and fast customer support. The attraction was more sites per customer, with ongoing client work giving agencies a reason to keep using Dorik.

Customer stories, like Melissa Buening from design studio Unicorns, show how creative agencies made Dorik part of their daily client workflow.[^customers]

Adding agency features proved a great lesson: features that mean nothing to solo bloggers - like client billing and custom logos - are the exact features that let you charge higher prices to businesses.

## When being cheap began to hurt

By late 2023, Dorik hit an unexpected wall: being too cheap was hurting sales. In a March 2024 retrospective, Mizanur explained that corporate buyers assumed a $4-a-month website builder had to be low-quality or unreliable. At the same time, charging so little meant the team had to sell hundreds of plans just to replace lost users.[^pricing-decision]

In December 2023, the founders raised prices significantly for all new users:

The results surprised the founders. They worried that monthly paid-customer acquisition would drop by half, but monthly paid-customer growth only fell by 15%. Because their prices were almost four times higher, recurring revenue expanded significantly. Earning an extra $100 a month in subscription revenue (<Term name="mrr-arr">MRR</Term>) now took just four new customers instead of 25.[^pricing-decision]

By letting existing users keep their old low rates, Dorik rewarded early fans while earning much higher revenue from new buyers.

This shows that pricing in business software is a trust signal. If you price your software too cheap, serious companies assume it is unreliable and walk away.

## Look at what must remain true after a price rise

Charging higher prices gives you the money to build a great service, but it also means customers expect more.

When a customer pays $39 to $79 every month, they have zero patience for software bugs or server downtime. Contact forms must never lose an email lead, website security certificates (SSL) must renew automatically, and support questions need answers in hours, not days.

Dorik was now helping agencies deliver client websites, not just helping individuals publish a page. Its pricing needed to cover the support that came with that larger role.

Software pricing should not be based on local living costs in Bangladesh. It should reflect the business value you create for the customer in their home country.

## AI tackles the blank page - but the website still has to work

In February 2024, the company launched Dorik AI on Product Hunt, winning community recognition.[^ai-launch] The tool generates a complete website layout, draft copy, and relevant images from a simple written prompt.[^ai]

This tackled a common beginner problem: staring at an empty white screen. Even with nice design templates, people who are not designers struggle to build a page from scratch. Generating an editable first draft in 30 seconds helps them get started immediately.

Yet an AI draft is only the beginning. The business owner still needs to tweak the text, verify prices, and set up payment links before sharing the site.

Cool AI features bring in curious visitors, but keeping paying subscribers depends on how reliable, fast, and easy the website builder is every day.

## How the business gets paid now

As of September 2026, Dorik sells monthly, yearly, and agency plans, with agency subscriptions costing $79 a month on annual billing ($948 per year) or $99 on monthly billing.[^plans]

Selling software abroad also means making checkout and payouts work across borders. A customer needs to be able to pay, and the company needs to receive the money.

For a founder planning a similar business, checkout, payout timing, and refunds belong in the launch plan alongside the product.

Payment schedules also affect company survival (<Term name="runway">runway</Term>). Annual subscriptions bring in a full year of cash upfront to pay developer salaries, but the company must support those customers all year and earn their renewal to avoid <Term name="churn">churn</Term>.

From its engineering base in Sylhet, Dorik balances low software development costs with the high demands of serving paying customers across multiple global time zones.

## What is worth copying - and what is not

- **Build solutions for problems you see every day.** Dorik grew out of real frustrations customers had with web templates, giving the founders a clear roadmap.
- **Launch with a small, working version.** Starting with a simple one-page builder let the team ship quickly and get real customer feedback without wasting months on complicated extra features.
- **Listen when your customer base changes.** When marketing agencies started using the builder, Dorik built custom agency dashboards and client billing instead of treating them like solo bloggers.
- **Set up international payment systems properly.** Test the checkout, payout, and refund process before inviting customers to subscribe.
- **Price based on the value you provide, not your local costs.** Low prices can scare away professional business clients; charging fair commercial rates builds customer trust and increases revenue.

Avoid selling lifetime deals without budgeting for future server and support costs. Do not mistake a one-day spike on Product Hunt for long-term customer growth. And never think that cheap office rent makes up for slow customer support in global software markets.

Dorik proved that an engineering team based in Sylhet could build a world-class software product competing directly with Silicon Valley tools. Succeeding in global SaaS means constantly matching your product, target audience, and pricing.
