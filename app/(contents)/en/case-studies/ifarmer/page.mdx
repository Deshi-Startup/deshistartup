# iFarmer

<CaseCompany slug="ifarmer" locale="en" />

> **In short:** iFarmer did not begin with the business it runs today. Fahad Ifaz and Jamil Akbar started with an urban-rooftop farming marketplace, discovered within about six months that the model was too difficult to scale, and pivoted in August 2018 toward a much bigger rural problem: farmers' limited access to finance, quality inputs, information and dependable markets.[^pivot] The company first connected farm projects with outside funders, then used farmer and transaction data to bring banks and other financial institutions into the model.[^banks-2025] By 2026 it had become a broader agriculture platform spanning finance, input distribution, advisory, produce market linkage and several software products.[^current-site] The founder lesson is not "build everything." It is to start with one painful bottleneck, use each transaction to learn more about the customer, and add adjacent services only when they strengthen the original system.

## Problem

Small farmers do not face one isolated problem. Financing is difficult, but a loan alone does not guarantee good inputs, useful advice, a healthy crop or a buyer at harvest. iFarmer's post-pivot model treated these problems as connected rather than separate.[^agfunder-2022]

That matters because credit risk in agriculture is partly operational. A lender wants to know who the farmer is, what is being grown, when the crop should be harvested and whether there is a realistic route to repayment. iFarmer's model tries to create that visibility through farmer data, monitoring and market linkage.[^banks-2025]

## Why the problem was Bangladesh-specific

Bangladesh has a very large smallholder agriculture economy, but conventional bank distribution does not reach every farmer efficiently. Many farmers also lack the documentation, collateral or formal credit history that standard lending processes prefer.[^banks-2025]

At the same time, the rural economy is built around physical retailers, field relationships and crop cycles. A purely digital app would not solve all of those constraints. iFarmer therefore combined software with field teams, retailers, collection points and institutional partners.[^sofol-2021]

## Founding insight

The important insight came after the first idea failed. Fahad had spent years working around agriculture, market systems and financial inclusion. When the rooftop model stalled, the team moved toward a problem where that domain experience mattered.[^founder-2020]

<CaseDetail title="The pivot changed the customer, not just the feature set">

The 2018 change was a real pivot. The original user was an urban property owner with spare rooftop space. The new model focused on farmers who needed productive capital and support, plus funders, banks, retailers and buyers who needed better information and coordination.[^pivot]

That is a useful distinction for founders. A pivot is not always "add a new feature." Sometimes the right move is to admit that the original market is too small or operationally awkward and rebuild around a different customer problem.

</CaseDetail>

## First wedge

The first scalable wedge was financing. iFarmer connected outside capital with farm projects and used those transactions to learn more about farmers, farms and repayment cycles.[^founder-2020]

Finance also created a reason to build the rest of the stack. If iFarmer wanted financed farms to perform, it had an incentive to care about input quality, agronomy, monitoring, harvest timing and market access. That is how a financing product could become a broader agriculture operating system rather than remain a simple marketplace.[^agfunder-2022]

## Early customers

The early model required trust on both sides. Farmers had to accept a new intermediary between themselves and capital, while retail funders had to believe that a young startup could monitor real agricultural activity and return money after a farm cycle.[^founder-2023]

iFarmer did not begin by forcing every farmer into a smartphone app. Sofol was designed for field facilitators and structured data collection, reflecting the reality that assisted digital service was more practical for many rural users at the time.[^sofol-2021]

That sequencing changed later. Folon, launched in November 2024, moved more services directly into farmers' hands through a smartphone app with advisory, learning, weather and financing functions.[^folon-2024]

## Trust building

Trust came from visibility. iFarmer collected farmer information, monitored projects and gave banks more structured information about activities that had historically been hard to observe remotely.[^banks-2025]

The Mutual Trust Bank pilot became especially important because iFarmer later reported nearly 100% timely repayment in that cohort. That is a company-reported pilot result, not an audited portfolio-wide default rate, but it helped demonstrate that the operating model could support formal-bank lending.[^banks-2025]

Partnerships also became a trust signal. A farmer card with Visa and UCB, debt from an international impact-finance provider and recognition from the OPEC Fund all tell banks, suppliers and farmers that established institutions have conducted their own engagement with the company.[^farmer-card-2024][^opec-2026]

## Local lessons

**Do not fall in love with the first interface.** The rooftop idea sounded modern, but the available market and operating model were weak. The founders moved to a less glamorous but much larger structural problem.[^pivot]

**Use domain knowledge to choose the pivot.** Fahad's earlier work in agriculture and market systems meant the second idea came from a problem he already understood rather than from a trend list.[^founder-2020]

**Digitise around the customer, not ahead of the customer.** iFarmer initially relied on facilitators and retailer relationships instead of assuming every farmer would immediately use a smartphone app.[^sofol-2021]

**A bank partnership can be distribution, not just financing.** When a bank funds a farmer cohort, iFarmer can become the execution and monitoring layer. That creates customer acquisition, revenue and institutional validation in the same relationship.[^banks-2025]

## Constraints, mistakes and complexity

**The first business model failed.** The rooftop marketplace was abandoned after about six months because supply was hard to secure and the opportunity was not large enough to scale as intended.[^pivot]

**Agricultural insurance did not become an easy adjacency.** Fahad later described iFarmer's smallholder insurance experiment as one of the company's failures. The lesson is that adjacent products can look logical on a strategy slide and still fail in the field.[^founder-2023]

**The model is operationally heavy.** Input distribution and produce procurement require warehouses, retailer relationships, field operations and working capital. The Pioneer and Symbiotics facilities are useful evidence that the company now needs dedicated capital for those cash cycles.[^pioneer-2025][^symbiotics-2026]

**Public KPIs are not always easy to reconcile.** The founder reported close to BDT 6 billion of finance facilitated in late 2023, while a 2024 Future Startup article later cited BDT 2.45 billion. The current website reports BDT 11.5 billion+.[^founder-2023][^pivot][^current-site] Those figures may use different definitions or data sets, but the public sources do not provide a complete reconciliation.

That does not prove misconduct. It does mean analysts should ask for a clear KPI dictionary: registered farmer versus active farmer, finance facilitated versus loan disbursed, and cumulative versus period-specific figures.

**Full-stack can become a complexity trap.** Finance, input distribution, market linkage, advisory, software, payments and exports can reinforce each other, but each also adds teams, systems and working-capital needs. Fahad has written that the company at times became too focused on revenue and valuation instead of farmer value.[^founder-2023]

## How the model fits together

By 2026, describing iFarmer only as a farm-investment app would miss most of the business.

**Finance** helps farmers and agricultural businesses access capital and gives iFarmer a reason to build credit and project data.[^current-site]

**KriShop and input distribution** connect retailers and farmers to agricultural inputs. **Market linkage** connects produce with buyers. **Advisory and monitoring** help manage crop and repayment risk. **Folon** moves more of that relationship directly to the farmer's phone.[^current-site][^folon-2024]

The strategic loop is straightforward:

`finance → farmer data → inputs/advisory → harvest visibility → market linkage → repayment/transaction data → better future underwriting`

That loop is an interpretation of the company's integrated product model, not a claim that iFarmer has publicly proven superior credit performance across its entire portfolio.

## Example: the UCB maize programme

A 2022 programme with UCB shows the model in practice. The partners targeted 1,089 maize farmers in Bogura, Sirajganj and Gaibandha, with a target of about 1,000 tonnes of maize. UCB handled formal banking access while iFarmer supported inputs, advice, crop monitoring and planned procurement after harvest.[^ucb-maize-2022]

The value to a bank was therefore broader than lead generation. iFarmer was trying to manage the conditions around the loan, not merely introduce a borrower.

## Business model

AgFunder reported in 2022 that iFarmer primarily earned through commissions on loans and marketplace fees around input and output transactions.[^agfunder-2022] The company's product surface has expanded since then, so that older description should not be treated as a complete 2026 revenue breakdown.

A useful way to think about the model is that iFarmer sits between several economic participants: farmers, banks and NBFIs, agricultural retailers, input manufacturers and produce buyers. It can earn when it successfully coordinates capital or commerce between them.

The company does not publicly disclose enough current segment data to show take rates, gross margins, contribution margins or which business line produces most of its profit.

## Capital

The best-documented equity round is the $2.1 million pre-Series A announced in May 2022. IDLC Venture Capital Fund led the round, with Millville Opportunities and Startup Bangladesh participating.[^agfunder-2022]

The capital mix later changed. In 2025, Pioneer Facility provided a $500,000 three-year working-capital loan. In April 2026, Symbiotics provided $1.5 million of working-capital financing to support input distribution and market linkage.[^pioneer-2025][^symbiotics-2026]

In June 2026, the OPEC Fund awarded iFarmer $250,000 through its Innovation for Development Award. That should be described as an award or non-dilutive development resource, not as a conventional venture-equity round.[^opec-2026]

This sequence matters. Equity is useful for building teams and technology, but inventory and produce-procurement cycles may be better matched with working-capital debt once a company reaches sufficient scale.

## Where iFarmer stands now

iFarmer's current website says it has more than 310,000 registered farmers, more than 30,000 registered retailers, BDT 11.5 billion+ in finance facilitated, 256,000+ tonnes of farm produce sold and 220,000+ advised farmers. It says its network reaches 42 districts and that the company has entered export markets and introduced private-label products.[^current-site]

Those are company-reported operating metrics, not audited public financial statements. The website does not publish enough information to calculate current active-farmer rate, loan default rate, annual revenue, profitability, export revenue or service-level margins.

The recent external signals point to an operating company that continues to attract institutional relationships: the $1.5 million Symbiotics facility was announced in April 2026, and the OPEC Fund award followed in June.[^symbiotics-2026][^opec-2026]

## What to copy

- Be willing to kill a fashionable first idea when the market is too small or the operation is too awkward.
- Choose the next problem from real domain knowledge, not from whatever category investors currently like.
- Start with one painful wedge. For iFarmer that wedge was finance, not a giant all-in-one agriculture app.
- Use transactions to create better data. A farmer profile is more valuable when it is connected to inputs, crop cycles, sales and repayment.
- Use existing networks as distribution. Banks, agricultural retailers and field teams can reach customers that paid digital ads may not.
- Move from assisted service to self-service only when customer behaviour and device access make it practical.
- Match the financing instrument to the job. Working-capital debt can make more sense than equity for repeat procurement cycles.

## What not to copy

- Do not keep a failed first model alive because you already built the product.
- Do not assume every adjacent service belongs inside the company just because it serves the same farmer.
- Do not present pilot repayment as portfolio-wide credit quality.
- Do not treat registered farmers as the same thing as active, financed or retained farmers.
- Do not publish cumulative-looking KPIs without definitions that let readers compare one year with another.
- Do not let the "full-stack" story hide the cash and operational burden of inventory, logistics and procurement.
- Do not confuse awards, partnerships or financing events with proof of profitability.

## Still unknown

iFarmer does not publicly disclose a complete current cap table, audited annual revenue and profit history, current valuation, customer acquisition cost, farmer retention, service-level margins, portfolio arrears or write-offs, or detailed default rates by lender and cohort.

Public sources also do not provide enough information to measure Folon's monthly active users, retention or conversion into finance and commerce. The farmer-card programme has not published nationwide active-card or transaction-volume data in the sources reviewed.[^folon-2024][^farmer-card-2024]

The company says it has entered export markets, but its current public pages do not specify export destinations, tonnage, customers or revenue contribution.[^current-site]

These gaps do not cancel the evidence of scale. They define what a serious lender, investor or analyst should ask for before turning company-reported reach into a financial conclusion.
