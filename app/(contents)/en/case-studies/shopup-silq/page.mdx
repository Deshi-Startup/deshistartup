# ShopUp / SILQ

<CaseCompany slug="shopup-silq" locale="en" />

> **In short:** ShopUp found that a seller could have customers and still be unable to fund the next order. Money was stuck with couriers. That helped lead it into finance and delivery.[^founder-discussion] Its later wholesale business took the same question into neighbourhood shops: how do you keep goods moving when cash moves slowly? The answer created a much larger business - and much larger costs.

ShopUp began by helping Facebook sellers manage their businesses. Over time, it became a supplier to physical shops, built delivery services, worked with lenders and brought in an engineering team from India. In 2025, it joined Saudi Arabia's Sary to form SILQ.[^history]

## At a glance

ShopUp has not simply changed its name to SILQ. The distinction matters when reading a group funding announcement or comparing financial results.

## Start with the seller who already has a business

ShopUp's own history starts with Afeef Zaman visiting his grandparents in Bauphal, Patuakhali. He saw pottery businesses struggle against larger companies with better distribution. The problem was not simply making something people wanted. Small producers also needed supplies, delivery and finance.[^about]

ShopUp first offered its tools to Facebook merchants who were already taking orders. These sellers had businesses to run and needed help managing them.[^history]

Working with active sellers gave ShopUp concrete problems to investigate: late parcels, missing payments and stock a merchant could not yet afford to buy.

ShopUp also met sellers away from the screen. In February 2018, it held a SuperSellers workshop with The Daily Star. Online business owners attended sessions on marketing and customer behaviour, including a talk by a Facebook representative.[^seller-workshop]

The workshop gave ShopUp a way to meet merchants and help with their existing businesses. How many went on to become paying customers is not reported.

## The next product was hiding inside a delayed payment

Some sellers struggled to fund the next order while their money was still with the courier. In a 2022 Bangladesh Angels discussion, ShopUp's team described how those held-up payments helped prompt a loan product. Delivery delays and slow cash collection also led it to build REDX.[^founder-discussion]

These are related problems, but the solutions do different jobs. A loan supplies cash while a seller waits. A better courier can shorten the wait itself.

A loan could let the seller restock, but the next sale would leave them waiting again. Improving delivery and payment collection could shorten that wait and reduce the need to borrow.

The delivery status only tells part of the story. For the merchant, the sale is not finished until the money is available to use again.

[Truck Lagbe's enterprise service also faced a cash gap](/en/case-studies/truck-lagbe#enterprise-customers-brought-a-second-business), paying truck owners promptly while some customers paid later. Releasing money a courier has already collected calls for faster settlement; offering customers an agreed period to pay requires cash to fund that wait.

## The first lending model used a partner's strengths

In the BRAC pilot, ShopUp assessed a seller's business and proposed loan terms. A local BRAC branch processed an approved loan and paid it into a bank or bKash account. ShopUp then collected instalments from sales receipts and passed them to BRAC.[^brac-pilot]

BRAC staff reported that the pilot had served 380 clients by January 2019. Their account described borrowers interested in taking a second loan after repaying the first. That is partner-reported feedback, not a published measure of default rates or long-term borrower outcomes.[^brac-pilot]

ShopUp brought knowledge of the seller's trade; BRAC brought lending operations and local branches. That gave the startup a way to test merchant loans using an established lender's services.

It also reveals a local design choice: a digital application can still depend on a physical institution that the customer knows. Moving a service online does not make those relationships disappear.

## Why supply neighbourhood shops rather than replace them?

Mokam moved ShopUp into supplying goods to existing retailers. Its model connects mills and brands to shops; REDX provides logistics alongside that trade.[^about]

A neighbourhood shop already has a location, a customer base and daily demand. A supplier can help it buy stock without taking over the final sale to the household. ShopUp's team explicitly said in 2021 that it intended to remain a business-to-business platform rather than sell directly to consumers.[^service-problems]

This is a different route from building a new consumer shopping habit. The shopkeeper keeps the customer relationship. The supplier competes on price, availability and the effort needed to restock.

There is a real advantage here, but also a limit. The app can group orders and record demand. Someone still has to source the right goods, move them and fix mistakes. A wrong delivery is not made cheaper by a well-designed order screen.

For ShopUp, growing beyond software meant taking responsibility for those physical details. That explains both the opportunity and the investment required.

## Buying a team solved a problem that hiring had not

Expansion created a talent problem too. In the Bangladesh Angels account, the team said ShopUp struggled to attract the engineers it needed while the company was little known. Voonik offered experienced people and a working technology platform.[^founder-discussion]

The February 2020 deal was specific. Voonik's business-to-business operation and technology team joined ShopUp, along with founders Sujayath Ali and Navaneetha Krishnan. Its consumer retail business went to Schoolay, a separate company.[^voonik]

ShopUp was bringing in the team and technology it needed for business customers, while Voonik's consumer operation went elsewhere.

An acquisition can shorten the time needed to build a team. It also creates work that a hiring plan does not: joining systems, agreeing roles and making two groups work together. The public accounts explain the hiring constraint. They do not isolate how much time or money the deal saved.

## Credit records became a customer problem of their own

<CaseDetail title="The test: can a merchant see that a payment has cleared?">

The Business Standard's August 2021 reporting found that Baki had launched before every step was automated. Dues and payments were not always recorded on time, creating confusion. A local sales representative said the service had stopped; Afeef said it was still running for selected shops, based on their transaction history.[^service-problems]

For a merchant who has paid, an outdated balance is more than a bookkeeping error. It leaves them unsure what they owe and whether they can place another order.

**Test repayment and error correction as carefully as loan approval.** A merchant needs to see that a payment has cleared and know whom to contact when the figures differ.

</CaseDetail>

The same 2021 report described delivery complaints. Afeef acknowledged that support needed to improve and said the company was working on them.[^service-problems] These are dated findings, not evidence that the same faults persist today.

## Large sales came with large costs

In May 2024, The Business Standard reported on ShopUp Pte Ltd's audited accounts for the year ended June 2023. Group revenue was about US$129 million, while the annual net loss exceeded US$49 million. Wholesale supplied most of the revenue.[^group-accounts]

ShopUp responded that more than US$14 million of the year's expense came from a non-cash employee share plan. Excluding that charge, it said the annual loss had fallen. That distinction matters: an accounting loss is not the same as cash spent in that year.[^group-accounts]

By July 2024, ShopUp was reporting a profit in one part of the business: Mokam CPG, its packaged-goods distribution division. It shared these results at a press meeting.[^division-results]

<CaseDetail title="What remains from BDT 100 of sales?">

| Mokam CPG, financial year 2023-24 | Company-reported figure |
|---|---:|
| Revenue | More than BDT 2,000 crore |
| Operating profit | BDT 26 crore |
| Operating profit per BDT 100 of revenue | About BDT 1.30 or less |

The last row is our calculation: 26 ÷ 2,000 × 100 = 1.30. Since revenue was **more than** BDT 2,000 crore, the actual ratio would be lower. The source describes unaudited management figures.[^division-results]

Operating profit is what remains after operating costs, before items such as interest and tax. It is not the group's final net profit.

</CaseDetail>

A small amount left from each sale makes ordinary operating choices important. Damaged stock, a costly delivery or a late payment can consume money the business expected to keep. Fast stock movement and careful collections can matter as much as winning another order.

The two reports are not contradictory. They cover different periods and different parts of the business. A profitable division can sit inside a loss-making group. A later improvement does not erase earlier losses.

This is why founders should examine [unit economics](/en/metrics/unit-economics) alongside total sales, and [cash flow](/en/metrics/cashflow-vs-profit) alongside reported profit.

## Capital changed form as the business changed

ShopUp's early growth was funded by investors buying ownership in the business.[^history] But stock and merchant finance also need money that can cycle through repeated purchases and repayments.

In August 2024, ShopUp announced US$6.5 million in debt capital from Saison Investment Management Private Ltd, part of Japan's Credit Saison group.[^saison] Debt is money to repay; it is different from selling shares to an investor.

That distinction becomes even more important after the Sary merger. The April 2025 package totalled US$110 million, including both equity and a financing facility for SILQ Financial. Equity buys ownership; a financing facility makes funds available under agreed terms. The announcement did not split those amounts. Afeef became group chief executive, while Sary's Mohammed Aldossary led the finance arm.[^merger]

In July 2026, lender Fasanara announced a US$75 million facility for SILQ's Fina business in Saudi Arabia.[^fasanara] The following month, SILQ reported US$100 million in facilities from Fasanara, Gemcorp and Amwal for that Saudi business.[^fina]

These are dated financing announcements, not a reason to add US$175 million to a ShopUp Bangladesh funding total. Nor does a facility prove that all available money has been used or lent successfully.

The merger offers a possible advantage: knowledge about sourcing, trade and finance can travel across markets. Yet local buying habits, delivery costs and credit risks still need local answers. The announcement establishes the plan, not the size of the benefit achieved.

## What founders can take from this

- **Study where a transaction gets stuck.** A merchant may need faster settlement more urgently than another marketing tool.
- **Work with the customer's existing business.** ShopUp moved into supplying neighbourhood shops that already served households.
- **Use partners where they bring a real capability.** BRAC's branches and lending operations were part of the early loan model.
- **Make records and recovery part of the product.** A payment that is not shown correctly can undo trust built by an easy sign-up.
- **Check which business a profit figure describes.** Division, group, operating profit and net profit answer different questions.

Do not copy ShopUp's final structure as a launch plan. Running wholesale, delivery and finance adds several kinds of risk at once. A smaller founder can learn from the sequence without building the whole group.

## What we still cannot tell

The reviewed sources do not establish current audited group profit, long-term merchant retention, the full losses on financed orders or measured gains from the merger. The early workshop and loan pilot show how ShopUp reached some merchants; they do not reconstruct its first ten paying customers.

A seller waiting for payment helped lead ShopUp beyond software. Following that problem meant carrying stock, running delivery and accounting for other people's money. The company could do more for a shopkeeper, but it had taken on a much harder business to run.
