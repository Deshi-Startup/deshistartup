# Truck Lagbe

<CaseCompany slug="truck-lagbe" locale="en" />

> **In short:** Truck Lagbe spotted a strange pricing puzzle in Bangladesh trucking: the price of hiring a truck had less to do with the weight of the cargo and much more to do with where the truck was headed next.[^early] When a truck delivers goods to another district, it often has to drive home empty. By matching those empty return trucks with businesses that needed goods shipped, Truck Lagbe lowered transport costs for companies while helping drivers earn extra money. But expanding from an everyday booking app into shipping for big corporations revealed a brutal cash-flow trap: corporate clients paid later, while truck owners needed payment straight away.[^idlc] Truck Lagbe's journey shows how a simple matching app in an emerging market quickly turns into a heavy financing operation.

When a commercial truck finishes a delivery in another district, the driver faces a tough choice: drive all the way back empty, paying for diesel and toll bridges with zero earnings, or park at a crowded truck stand for days waiting for a local broker to find a load. If an app can match that empty truck with cargo heading back to the driver's home town, the return trip can earn money toward its costs.

Truck Lagbe built Bangladesh's biggest online trucking network to solve this mismatch. It connects shippers with truck owners for one-off trips, while also managing contract logistics for large corporations.[^ifc-review] But booking a truck on a phone is only the first step; making sure cargo arrives safely, navigating highway checkpoints, and paying truck owners promptly are what actually keep freight moving.

## A price puzzle led to the company

Anayet Rashid first ran into trucking headaches while managing his family's commodity trading business. In an interview with IDLC, he explained how wild swings in truck rental prices could wipe out trade profits overnight.[^idlc]

The price puzzle became obvious during two shipments sent to Jessore just one week apart. One truck charged 35,000 taka because the driver expected to drive all the way back to Dhaka empty. A second truck charged only 8,000 taka because its home base was already in Jessore; the driver just wanted a load to cover his fuel costs on the way home.[^early]

Matching these empty return trips consistently could unlock huge savings. Businesses would pay lower freight rates, while truck owners could earn money on trips they used to drive at a total loss.

However, matching an empty truck takes exact timing. The truck must have the right size, be in the right town, and show up before factory goods spoil or assembly lines stall. If waiting for an app booking delays a trucker by two full days, the lost time costs more than the discounted fare.

## Early interest was easier to find than a working service

While the founders were still doing research, they won a national startup contest run by the government's ICT Division. News coverage brought instant publicity. Phone calls flooded in from business owners and truckers looking for loads before the software or driver network was ready.[^early]

This early attention forced the team to build quickly. Businesses with factory cargo need a real truck at their gate, not a software prototype.

By March 2021, farming startup iFarmer was using Truck Lagbe to haul fresh vegetables from rural fields to wholesale markets in Dhaka.[^ifarmer] Relying on Truck Lagbe’s on-demand network allowed young startups to move goods across the country without spending money to buy their own truck fleets.

Outsourcing trucking frees up management time, turning unpredictable fleet maintenance into a simple per-trip fee.

## The trust problem existed on both sides

At first, the founders assumed their biggest problem would be reassuring cargo owners that drivers would not steal their goods. But when they talked to truck owners at the Tejgaon truck terminal, Anayet found an even bigger worry: truck owners and drivers were terrified of losing their vehicles.[^idlc]

A September 2020 operational update highlighted the dangers drivers face: criminals calling in fake cargo requests to lure trucks onto lonely highways for hijackings, or booking shipments packed with illegal drugs like yaba pills. To protect drivers, Truck Lagbe introduced strict phone and national ID verification for all cargo senders before letting them post a trip.[^verification]

A two-sided marketplace only works when both sides feel safe. A cargo owner risks the value of their goods, but a truck owner risks a 30 to 50 lakh taka vehicle and the driver's life. If either side feels unsafe, nobody uses the app.

For marketplace founders, this proves that removing all friction during customer sign-up can be dangerous. Letting unverified people request trucks might boost app numbers, but if criminals target drivers, your truck supply vanishes overnight.

## Useful details beat a generic booking form

Truck Lagbe's booking screen asks for exact details: precise pickup and drop-off points, truck weight capacity, loading time, and what kind of goods are inside. Registered truckers then bid on the trip, letting the customer choose the best combination of price and driver rating.[^booking]

Every single detail prevents an expensive mistake. Pinpointing an exact location stops a giant 15-ton truck from getting stuck in a narrow village alley. Knowing the cargo type ensures fragile goods or dry cement bags are not loaded onto an open flatbed truck in the rain.

In a 2021 interview, Anayet shared two practical design choices made for Bangladesh: using familiar local landmarks instead of formal street addresses, and adding a button to directly rehire a favorite driver.[^evolution]

Letting customers rehire the same driver solves a common marketplace dilemma. In traditional brokerage, once a shipper and driver meet, they swap phone numbers to bypass the app and avoid platform fees. Truck Lagbe welcomed rehires, knowing that corporate clients stay on the platform for payment security, trip insurance, digital dispute help, and guaranteed truck supply, rather than forced app lock-in.

## Different trips need different pricing

In May 2021, Truck Lagbe outlined its two pricing methods: an auction-style bidding marketplace for long-distance district trips, and instant fixed rates for 1-ton and 2-ton pickup trucks inside Dhaka.[^rates]

This hybrid model matches how logistics works in real life:
- For short city trips with predictable traffic, fixed prices save everyone from exhausting price haggling.
- For long trips across river ferries and highway bridges, dynamic bidding reflects fuel prices, weather delays, and the chances of finding a return load.

| A trip needs an answer to… | Why it matters |
|---|---|
| Can this vehicle carry this load? | Size alone may not establish safe carrying capacity. |
| Can it reach the pickup at the agreed time? | A delayed truck or delayed cargo ties up the other side. |
| What work does the price include? | Waiting, extra stops and loading arrangements can change the job. |
| What can the vehicle do afterward? | The next useful trip affects the owner's overall earnings. |

This table shows the key factors that decide whether a truck match succeeds.[^booking][^rates]

For a factory manager, having 20,000 trucks on an app means nothing if no verified vehicle is parked at their loading dock at 6:00 PM.

## Enterprise customers brought a second business

In 2021, Anayet split Truck Lagbe into two separate operations: an open marketplace for everyday one-time trips, and an enterprise division that handles contract shipping for big corporations.[^evolution]

Large multinational and national companies work very differently: they need dedicated account managers, guaranteed on-time delivery contracts, monthly invoices, and connections to their inventory software.

While corporate contracts bring huge, steady shipment volumes, they completely change the company's financial risk. Corporate clients demand long payment credit, while truck drivers need cash on the spot.[^idlc]

<CaseDetail title="Worked example: paying truck owners before clients pay">

The IDLC interview describes the gap: truck owners get paid straight away, but corporate clients get time to pay. Here is a worked example of the cash this can tie up.

Suppose a platform pays truck owners 1 crore taka a month, spread evenly through the month, while its clients pay 60 days later.

**Cash needed to bridge the gap: 1 crore × 2 months = about 2 crore taka.** A 90-day wait would raise that to about 3 crore, before overhead or disputed bills.

These are example payment terms. The lesson is to base the cash plan on what you must pay out and how long you wait to collect, not just the total value of bookings.

</CaseDetail>

This working capital requirement changes the business model. A marketplace can own no trucks and still need substantial financing to cover the gap between paying owners and collecting from clients.

This is why spot trips and corporate contracts need different metrics. The spot marketplace relies on matching software and platform commission fees (<Term name="take-rate">take-rates</Term>). Corporate freight acts like a financing business that requires careful invoice collection (<Term name="accounts-receivable">accounts receivable</Term>) and treasury management. See our guide on [cash flow versus profit](/en/metrics/cashflow-vs-profit) for more details.

[ShopUp ran into a similar cash delay](/en/case-studies/shopup-silq#the-next-product-was-hiding-inside-a-delayed-payment), where online sellers waited for delivery couriers to return cash on delivery. Fast software can speed up trip booking, but it cannot change corporate payment terms.

## The role of brokers became more complicated

Tech startups often talk about eliminating traditional middlemen. But by 2021, Anayet realized that traditional truck brokers at transport hubs like Tejgaon had deep, trusted relationships with fleet owners who did not use smartphones or trust apps.[^evolution]

Instead of fighting traditional brokers, Truck Lagbe partnered with them, bringing their driver fleets onto the platform.

Tech platforms must know the difference between middlemen who just add cost and middlemen who do real work. Local truck brokers know the drivers, manage crowded truck yards, and help resolve roadside disputes. Trying to push them out without doing their daily work only turns truckers against the app.

While Truck Lagbe's ads highlight booking directly without middlemen,[^owners] working with traditional brokers helped the platform sign up truck fleets in distant district hubs.

## Some digital progress still needed paper

In October 2021, Anayet pointed out that despite building a modern mobile app, the company still had to print paper delivery receipts (*challans*). Warehouse gatekeepers, factory receiving docks, and highway police checkposts simply refused to accept digital receipts on a phone screen.[^idlc]

In emerging markets, software has to fit existing habits. Demanding an all-digital, paperless system when factory clerks and tax officers demand ink-stamped paper creates instant roadblocks.

On the other hand, truckers adopted digital payments very quickly: Anayet reported that truck owners adopted in-app digital fee collections within four weeks of launch.[^evolution]

Founders should not assume that traditional workers are "afraid of technology." People adopt new tech when it makes their lives easier (like getting paid immediately via mobile money). But where legal rules or safety require paper, paper stays.

## Growth has to work for drivers too

A venture capital press release rarely talks about driver working conditions. In a 2021 review, the International Finance Corporation (IFC) studied Truck Lagbe's operations. It pointed out gaps in tracking driver working hours, accident records, and truck maintenance rules, requiring formal improvement plans.[^ifc-review]

In response to IFC's requirements, Truck Lagbe rolled out a formal driver code of conduct and updated its operating guidelines by January 2022.[^ifc-review]

An independent 2023 study by Fairwork Bangladesh evaluated Truck Lagbe on worker fairness. While the report raised questions about accident liability and formal contracts in Bangla, surveyed truckers confirmed that Truck Lagbe consistently paid agreed fares in full and on time without unfair deductions.[^fairwork]

To give truckers a financial safety net, Truck Lagbe partnered with Guardian Life Insurance to offer low-cost life and disability insurance designed for transport workers.[^insurance]

A trucking marketplace cannot survive by squeezing driver earnings. Rising diesel prices, toll fees, vehicle loan installments, and rough highway conditions determine whether a trip makes economic sense for a driver. Cutting driver pay to give unrealistic discounts to cargo owners will eventually destroy your supply of trucks.

## What the investors added

In September 2021, the IFC completed a $2.5 million equity investment in Truck Lagbe as part of a $4 million funding round, bringing institutional governance and corporate connections.[^ifc][^later-funding]

In July 2023, Singapore-based PSA International - one of the world's largest container port operators - led another strategic investment round.[^psa][^later-funding]

For a global port operator like PSA, investing in Truck Lagbe provides visibility into Bangladesh's inland freight routes, linking sea container ports in Chittagong and Mongla directly to manufacturing factories inland.

## What founders can take from Truck Lagbe

- **Target empty return trips and idle capacity.** The biggest cost savings in logistics come from matching trucks that would otherwise drive home empty, not from buying new vehicles.
- **Solve safety for suppliers before chasing more customers.** Truck owners risk losing their vehicles to criminals; verifying cargo senders protects your supply base.
- **Plan for corporate payment delays.** Map when you pay truck owners against when clients pay you. Arrange enough working capital to cover that gap.
- **Respect real-world paper requirements.** Do not force paperless workflows if factory security and tax inspectors require physical stamped delivery receipts.
- **Partner with traditional brokers where they add real value.** Local brokers have deep community trust; working with them can expand your truck fleet faster than trying to replace them completely.
