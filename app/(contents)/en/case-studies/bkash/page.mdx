# bKash

<CaseCompany slug="bkash" locale="en" />

> **In short:** bKash did not win by starting with a fancy smartphone app. It started by making one everyday task simple: helping people send money home when they did not have a nearby bank branch. The company used basic phone menus (USSD) that worked on cheap feature phones without internet. It built a huge network of neighborhood shopkeepers so people could deposit and withdraw cash anywhere. And it teamed up with BRAC Bank to follow central bank rules from day one. Once millions of people trusted bKash with their money, the company added shopping payments, remittances from abroad, savings, and small bank loans. The big lesson for founders: solve the basic infrastructure first, earn customer trust, and only then add complex financial products.[^world-bank-2017][^bkash-about]

## Problem

The problem was simple to see, but very difficult to fix. Millions of people in Bangladesh owned mobile phones, but most had no bank account. Sending money home to a village, receiving wages, or saving a little cash meant carrying paper notes on long bus journeys or relying on informal couriers who could lose the money.[^world-bank-2017]

Traditional bank branches cost too much money to set up in small towns and villages. They were not built to handle small transactions like sending 500 taka. Kamal Quadir later explained that bringing ordinary people into the financial system was the core reason for starting bKash: traditional banks simply could not afford to serve low-income customers.[^mit-sloan-2023]

## Why the problem was Bangladesh-specific

Bangladesh had millions of mobile phone users, a cash-based daily economy, and families spread across the country who needed to send money back and forth. The demand was clearly there. But bKash could not simply copy Kenya’s famous M-PESA service, because the central bank decided that mobile money in Bangladesh had to be led by licensed commercial banks.[^world-bank-2017]

That rule shaped how the company was set up. BRAC Bank became the licensed bank partner, while Money in Motion brought the mobile tech experience. This partnership was not just legal paperwork; it was the foundation of the whole business from day one.[^world-bank-2014]

## Founding insight

The key insight was that a phone alone could not solve the problem. Digital money is only useful if people can easily turn paper cash into phone balance, and turn that phone balance back into cash whenever they need it.

So bKash built two things at the exact same time: simple phone software and a nationwide network of human agents. The early service ran on USSD technology, so people did not need a smartphone or an internet connection. At the same time, local tea stalls, mobile recharge shops, and pharmacies became mini-banks where people could deposit and withdraw money.[^world-bank-2014]

<CaseDetail title="The agent network was part of the product">

In its first year, BRAC helped bKash sign up and manage roughly 5,000 local shop agents. After that initial test, bKash switched to working with regional distribution companies that could manage tens of thousands of local shops across the country.[^world-bank-2014]

Software founders often miss this point: bKash's real protective wall (<Term name="moat">moat</Term>) was not just computer code. It was code plus real cash on the counter, trusted local shopkeepers, strict rules, and a smooth way to keep agents supplied with cash every day.

</CaseDetail>

## First wedge

The first wedge was not loans, stock trading, or fancy shopping features. It was the simplest possible transaction: deposit cash, send it to another phone, and withdraw it at the other end.

Starting with this narrow focus worked because it solved an urgent, everyday headache. A factory worker in Dhaka could send money home to their parents in a distant village in seconds. The parents did not need a bank branch; they just walked over to the corner shop, showed a code, and took home cash.

Once people were comfortable with that habit, bKash added more everyday features. Users could recharge mobile airtime, pay utility bills, shop at stores, connect their bank accounts, and receive foreign remittances. The smartphone app arrived in 2018, long after bKash had already become a household name.[^bkash-about]

## Early customers

Getting early customers meant having agents everywhere people walked. bKash needed agents in the very shops where people were already buying airtime, medicine, or tea. The World Bank reported that starting with BRAC’s local network gave bKash a strong foundation before it hired regional distribution firms.[^world-bank-2014]

This sparked a powerful loop:
- More agents made bKash convenient for everyday people.
- More paying customers gave shopkeepers a reason to become agents.
- More transactions made the system more valuable for everyone.

For founders, the takeaway is clear: customer growth is not just about running ads. If users cannot access your service in the real world when they need it, marketing alone will not help.

## Trust building

People are naturally careful with their hard-earned money. bKash earned trust by partnering with BRAC Bank and following strict central bank rules. Just as importantly, it placed a familiar face between the user and the software: the neighborhood shopkeeper.[^world-bank-2017]

Over time, trust shifted directly into the software. The mobile app, digital ID checks (<Term name="kyc">e-KYC</Term>), instant bank transfers, and QR codes at checkout counters let people do business without ever visiting an agent. Today, the word "bKash" has become everyday slang in Bangladesh for sending money digitally.[^bkash-about]

That brand power is huge, but it is not unbreakable. In financial services, a single major security failure or network outage can destroy customer confidence fast.

## Local lessons

**Build for the phone your customer already carries.** In 2011, requiring a smartphone would have locked out most people in Bangladesh. Using USSD menus let bKash reach millions of people using cheap basic phones.[^world-bank-2014]

**Cash is not the enemy of digital money.** In an economy that runs on cash, a digital wallet needs a reliable physical cash network to be useful. Agents acted like living, breathing neighborhood ATMs.

**Strict rules can become your biggest advantage.** Working under central bank regulations was challenging, but it gave customers and big institutions total confidence that the platform was safe. bKash embraced those rules instead of trying to skirt around them.[^world-bank-2017]

**Build new products on top of existing habits.** Simple money transfers came first. Digital loans and savings accounts were added years later, only after millions of people already used the app every week.

## Constraints, tradeoffs and competitive pressure

**Managing cash at agent shops is hard work.** Having 300,000 agents sounds great, but it falls apart if an agent runs out of cash or digital balance when a customer walks in. Running this network takes active logistics, daily field support, and fair commissions - not just software servers.[^world-bank-2014]

**Cash-out fees cause customer friction.** bKash made much of its revenue from cash-out fees, but customers frequently complained about the cost. In addition, the central bank periodically adjusts transaction limits and rules, meaning bKash must constantly adapt its pricing inside regulatory boundaries.[^daily-star-market-2025][^limits-2025]

**The Nagad price war.** When postal-backed Nagad entered the market, it showed that even a giant market leader can face real pressure. Nagad introduced instant digital sign-ups (<Term name="kyc">e-KYC</Term>) directly through mobile telecom menus and cut cash-out fees to just 9.99 taka per thousand on its app, well below bKash's 1.85% fee. While bKash kept its lead in active users and total money moved,[^daily-star-market-2025] the fight proved that expensive cash-out fees remain a weak spot.

**Growth is not always profitable.** To defend its lead, bKash spent heavily on marketing, signing up merchants, and upgrading its smartphone app with help from Ant Group. TBS reported that bKash took net losses from 2019 through 2021 before returning to solid profits in mid-2022.[^tbs-q1-2026] Defending market share against cheap, subsidized rivals takes deep pockets and patience.

**Rules limit lending and deposits directly.** Under central bank rules, mobile wallet operators cannot act like full commercial banks. They cannot hold customer deposits for lending or issue loans directly from their own balance sheet. That is why bKash partners with licensed banks to offer loans and savings.[^bkash-loan][^bkash-savings]

## From wallet to financial platform

The biggest shift in bKash's strategy is that it no longer just moves cash between phones.

Together with City Bank, eligible users can get small, instant personal loans right inside the bKash app without collateral. The bank provides the regulated lending capital, while bKash uses customer transaction history to help check creditworthiness.[^bkash-loan] News reports noted that the loan limit was later increased to 50,000 taka with regulatory approval.[^prothom-loan]

Savings accounts work through similar partnerships. Customers can set up small recurring savings plans inside the app, but the actual money is held by licensed banks and financial institutions.[^bkash-savings]

Remittance is another big pillar. bKash connects with global money transfer services, allowing workers abroad to send money straight into family bKash accounts from their foreign phones.[^bkash-remittance][^bkash-abroad]

The lesson here is not to pile on random features. It is to connect new services to the same trusted user account, phone number, and daily habits people already have.

## Business model

bKash earns fees across its network from cash-outs, person-to-person transfers, utility bill payments, merchant checkouts, and commissions on financial services.

To understand bKash’s <Term name="unit-economics">unit economics</Term>, start with the people behind a cash withdrawal. Agents keep cash available, distributors help them manage their balances, and mobile networks connect the service. The World Bank’s case study describes commissions and network charges as costs of running this system. The fee a customer pays is not all income that bKash gets to keep.[^world-bank-2017]

When someone receives money through bKash and uses it to pay bills or save, they can do more without withdrawing cash.[^bkash-remittance][^bkash-savings] That reduces the need for cash handling. The business still has to earn enough from each service to cover its costs.

## Capital and strategic investors

bKash started as a joint venture between BRAC Bank and Money in Motion. The International Finance Corporation (IFC) invested in 2013, followed by the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation in 2014. Later, Ant Group joined, followed by SoftBank.[^world-bank-2014][^bkash-about]

Each investor brought something unique:
- BRAC Bank provided the banking license and local trust.
- IFC and the Gates Foundation brought world-class governance and credibility.
- Ant Group brought advanced payments technology and app design.
- SoftBank brought massive growth capital.

The most famous funding round came in 2021. TBS reported that SoftBank Vision Fund II invested $250 million for a stake of over 10%, valuing bKash at roughly $2 billion.[^softbank-2021]

bKash has stated that it has raised a total of $381 million from its investors over the years.[^bkash-investment] That is a company-reported figure, and public records do not show every individual check.

## Financial performance

bKash's recent financial results look very different from the loss-making years between 2019 and 2021.

Its audited 2025 financial statement showed 6,565.56 crore taka in revenue and 676.33 crore taka in net profit. At year-end, the company held 16,221 crore taka in total assets, including 11,004.59 crore taka of electronic money in circulation.[^bkash-financials-2025]

The statement also notes a total comprehensive income of 661.16 crore taka, which accounts for employee retirement obligations and should not be confused with basic net profit.[^bkash-financials-2025]

Growth stayed strong into 2026. In the first quarter of 2026, bKash reported 1,802.43 crore taka in revenue after VAT and 184.09 crore taka in profit after tax.[^bkash-financials-q1-2026]

These numbers prove that bKash has become an enormously profitable business. However, public records do not show the exact profit margins for each individual service or how much risk future loan expansion might bring.

## The next question: digital banking

bKash has applied for a digital banking license under the name bKash Digital Bank. As of August 2026, Bangladesh Bank was still reviewing the final round of digital bank applications.[^digital-bank-2026]

This distinction is important. bKash is not a licensed digital bank today. It operates as a mobile financial service provider. A separate banking license would let it offer new financial products under different rules if approved.

For founders, the lesson is clear: even after dominating a market for over a decade, entering a newly regulated industry takes fresh capital, new technology, and patience with regulators.

## Where bKash stands now

bKash reported passing 83 million registered customers by April 2026, backed by over 350,000 agents and around 900,000 merchants.[^bkash-about] Independent news reports from year-end 2025 noted about 82 million total accounts, with roughly 47 million active in the previous 90 days.[^bonikbarta-2026]

The gap between registered users and active users is an important metric. Founders should never look only at total sign-ups to judge a company's health.

The key takeaway is that bKash built a massive network of distribution and trust, then turned that scale into consistent profits. Its next challenge is not just signing up more phones, but encouraging people to do more inside the app while protecting against fraud and competition.

## What to copy

- Focus on one frequent, painful problem before building a long list of features.
- Design for the phones your customers actually own right now.
- Treat real-world distribution as part of your core product. The agent network was a protective wall (<Term name="moat">moat</Term>) software alone could not build.
- Work closely with regulators early if your business touches money, identity, or savings.
- Add new products only when your existing customer base gives you an unfair advantage.
- Pick investors who bring technical skills and credibility, not just high valuations.
- Track active monthly users and transaction quality, not just total sign-ups.
- Remember that growing fast and making money are not the same thing; big scale can still lose money without careful cost controls.

## What not to copy

- Do not assume an app can ignore physical real-world operations in a cash-heavy country.
- Do not treat legal licenses as an afterthought to deal with later.
- Do not assume having the biggest market share means you can ignore cheaper rivals.
- Do not describe partner-bank loans or savings as if your startup were a licensed bank.
- Do not use total registered users to mask slow daily usage.
- Do not assume past profits guarantee that launching loans or credit products will be safe.
- Do not try to become a "super app" without giving users a clear, simple reason to use each new feature.

## Still unknown

Public filings do not reveal the exact profit margins for each individual service. We also do not have official public data on customer acquisition costs (<Term name="cac">CAC</Term>), multi-year user <Term name="retention">retention</Term>, fraud losses, or default rates on loans given through partner banks.

The complete funding history also has gaps. The company lists its main investors, but public records do not disclose the exact price and ownership terms for every investment round.[^bkash-investment]

Finally, the digital bank license outcome remains pending. Until Bangladesh Bank announces its decisions, exactly what bKash Digital Bank can launch is still to be decided.[^digital-bank-2026]
