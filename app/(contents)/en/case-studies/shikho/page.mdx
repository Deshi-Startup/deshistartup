# Shikho

<CaseCompany slug="shikho" locale="en" />

> **In short:** Shikho began with a single online maths course, then expanded into live video tutoring, a career skills platform, and a physical exam center in Dhaka.[^fs-launch][^fs-acquisition][^founder-centre] Its journey shows how listening to student and parent needs can shape an online business. But as you study its growth, keep three basic questions separate: Do people pay for it? Do students actually learn better? And can the company cover its heavy operating costs?

## At a glance

Shikho sells learning support for Bangladesh's school and admission exams.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Founded | 2019, by Shahir Chowdhury and Zeeshan Zakaria.[^fs-seed] |
| First product | A mobile course for general maths in classes 9-10, leading to the Secondary School Certificate (SSC) exam.[^fs-launch] |
| Core offer | Classes, practice tests, notes and progress reports.[^shikho-site] |
| Early revenue model | Paid access to courses, with free content or trials to let learners try the teaching.[^fs-seed] |
| Funding milestone | More than US$8 million in total funding announced by May 2025. The latest round's size was not disclosed.[^sbl-investment] |

The first offer was specific: help with one subject before an important exam.

## The problem: access to good teaching

After working in banking in London, Shahir Chowdhury wanted to build a technology company in Bangladesh. He first looked into mobile money, but felt established services like bKash already did a great job. Then he read about fast-growing education startups in India and China, which caught his attention. Teaching was also in his blood - both of his parents were teachers.[^trt-founder]

The problem was clear: quality education was unevenly spread. If you lived in a village or small town, getting great tutors or coaching was nearly impossible. Even in Dhaka, private tutoring was expensive. Shikho set out to put high-quality lessons into an app on any smartphone.[^fs-seed]

Teaching the right curriculum in the right language was critical. In a 2022 interview, Shahir stressed that Shikho focused on teaching the Bangladeshi national syllabus in Bangla. An educational app only matters if it helps students pass the exact exams they take at school.[^row-founder]

## The first course was narrow, but complete

Shikho started with one subject: Class 9 - 10 General Mathematics for the SSC exam. The course included recorded video lessons, chapter notes, worked-out solutions, and quizzes to highlight weak spots. Students paid a fee to get access for a few months.[^fs-launch]

Starting narrow was a smart move. Parents and students could judge a clear promise: "This will help you pass SSC maths." It also let the team learn how to make great lessons before trying to cover every grade and subject.

Even for one subject, creating good content takes hard work. You have to write scripts, record clean videos, build practice quizzes, and explain every solution. Any founder building an online course needs to budget time and money for all four.

While 10 Minute School taught for free for years before [testing paid courses with a 75-taka PDF](/en/case-studies/10-minute-school#business-model), Shikho charged for its complete exam package from day one.

Shikho's app includes two helpful feedback tools: a practice-test score sheet and a chapter progress report.[^shikho-site] They show an important truth: sitting through a video does not mean a student actually understands the lesson.

Once a report points out where a student is struggling, the next question is: **how do they fix it?** Do they need another video, a clearer worked example, or help from a live teacher? A good test report is only useful if it helps the student take the next step.

## What brought in paying customers

In August 2022, Shahir told Rest of World that most sales happened right before major national board exams (SSC and HSC) or university entrance tests, with about 170,000 monthly active users.[^row-founder]

Urgent exam deadlines make parents and students willing to pay. But test preparation faces an inherent cohort transition: **high annual course <Term name="churn">churn</Term>**. Once students complete their SSC or HSC exams, their need for that specific curriculum ends. You cannot sell them the same study package next year. Unless Shikho successfully transitions a Class 10 student into Class 11 courses, it must continuously acquire new cohorts of paying students every academic year.

Unpredictable school calendars also make forecasting revenue tricky. In his early TRT World interview, Shahir explained how pandemic school closures and delayed board exams made it difficult to predict when families would buy courses.[^trt-founder]

Selling education involves two different people: the student who watches the lessons, and the parent who holds the wallet. In June 2025, Shahir described how this changes by grade:[^ro-founder]

| Learner's stage | How he described the buying decision |
|---|---|
| Classes 6-8 | Parents choose the course and enforce daily study discipline. |
| Classes 9-11 | Parents and students evaluate the product together. |
| Class 12 and admission preparation | The student's own academic urgency drives product selection. |

This means marketing must speak to both audiences: it must reassure parents that the course matches the official syllabus and will improve grades, while keeping the app fun and easy enough for teenagers to use daily.

## Earning a family's trust

In April 2024, Shikho opened its first Exam and Experience Center in Farmgate, Dhaka - the historic heart of Bangladesh's coaching industry.[^founder-centre] The center serves two purposes: students take supervised practice exams on paper, while parents can sit down with staff, test the app on tablets, and ask questions.[^fs-centre]

<CaseDetail title="The offline bridge: overcoming digital skepticism">

In Bangladesh, families are used to physical coaching centers like UCC or Focus. Parents trust physical classrooms, timed practice tests with paper answer sheets, and meeting teachers face-to-face. When a child's future is on the line - like getting a GPA-5 or getting into medical school - parents hesitate to trust just a smartphone app.

The Farmgate center acts as a real-world bridge. It helps hesitant parents feel comfortable buying an online course. But opening physical centers is expensive: it adds high commercial rent, electricity bills, branch staff salaries, and tablets. To pay for those brick-and-mortar costs, the center has to sign up lots of students who would not have joined through online ads alone.

</CaseDetail>

Sitting down with an advisor in person helps overcome parental doubt, trading pure software profits for real-world trust.

## Why the two brands stayed separate

In November 2021, Shikho acquired Bohubrihi, a leading Bangladeshi platform for professional and career courses. As part of the deal, Bohubrihi co-founder Yanur Islam Piash joined to lead the career learning division.[^fs-acquisition]

The acquisition gave Shikho a way to serve people beyond school exams. Courses in coding, data analysis, and digital marketing could reach university students and working adults. Keeping a learner through more stages of their education could increase <Term name="ltv">lifetime value</Term>.

The group brought its professional skills courses together under the Bohubrihi brand. Shikho would focus on classes 6 through 12 and university admission preparation.[^fs-bohubrihi] Two brands let the team shape courses, prices, and messages for different needs: preparing for an exam or building a career.

## What scales, and what still costs money

Shikho grew from single maths courses and 7-day free trials into year-long study subscriptions that include animated video lessons, live evening classes, homework review, and printed "FutureBook" physical guides delivered to homes.[^fs-seed][^shikho-school]

The cost of running each part of an education business varies widely:

| Part of the service | How easily it scales | What drives the cost |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded videos and quizzes | Very high: adding another student costs almost nothing. | High upfront cost to film videos, write questions, and pay for cloud servers. |
| Live video classes | Moderate: classes can hold many students, but teachers get paid per hour. | Teacher salaries, class moderators, and question helpers. |
| Physical centers and printed books | Low: costs go up with every new building and student. | Commercial building rent, center staff, paper printing, and courier shipping. |

Recorded videos offer high software-style profit margins (<Term name="gross-margin">gross margins</Term>). But to keep students motivated and parents happy, you need real human help - live teachers, doubt solving, and practice exams. As an education company adds live tutoring and offline centers, it begins to look more like a traditional tutoring school.

Partnerships offer another way to reach learners. In April 2026, Grameenphone announced discounted Shikho maths courses for classes 6 to 10 inside MyGP.[^gp-partnership] That puts the courses in an app people already use, giving Shikho another place to be discovered.

## Capital and funding

Shikho raised venture capital early. These announcements show the scale of its funding. Keep in mind that fundraising numbers are not company profits or revenues.

| Announcement | What was reported |
|---|---|
| July 2021 | US$1.3 million in new early-stage investment, following US$275,000 in earlier funding.[^fs-seed] |
| March 2022 | A further US$4 million, led by Wavemaker Partners. Shikho planned to expand its courses and team.[^fs-funding] |
| May 2025 | Startup Bangladesh announced investment alongside local investors. It put total funding above US$8 million, without stating the new round's size.[^sbl-investment] |

The last figure is a total sum raised to date, not fresh cash added on top of earlier rounds. Nor does it tell us how much cash is left in the bank today.

Fundraising does not always go smoothly. In August 2024, Shahir stated that a planned 5 crore taka investment from government-backed Startup Bangladesh had been cancelled due to political issues. The Daily Star reported his statement, though it was not an independent audit of the fund's internal decisions.[^ds-funding] Startup Bangladesh later announced its investment in May 2025.[^sbl-investment]

Until money actually lands in your bank account, an investment deal is never guaranteed. Startups must always have a plan to pay salaries if an investor check is delayed.

## AI help when a student gets stuck

In February 2025, Shikho launched a test version of an AI study helper for its paying users to answer homework questions in Bangla. Shahir told The Daily Star that human teachers reviewed its answers and that students could rate the responses.[^ds-ai]

Using AI to help a student who is stuck on a homework problem makes great sense. But automated tools can make mistakes, and simply giving a student the final answer can stop them from learning how to solve problems themselves.

The true test of any learning tool is simple: can the student solve a similar math problem on their own the next day? That is the gold standard for education, even if public reports have not published those test scores yet.

## What founders can copy

- **Start with one clear promise.** Focusing on one subject and one exam gives customers an obvious reason to buy.
- **Understand who pays versus who uses the product.** Parents pay the fees, but students use the app. You must satisfy both.
- **Guide the student's next step.** When a student fails a practice quiz, immediately show them which lesson to re-watch.
- **Keep different audiences separate.** Career courses and school test prep attract different people with different goals. Giving them separate brands keeps marketing clear.

## What not to copy blindly

- **Big video production budgets.** Shikho had venture capital funding early on. Bootstrapped founders should start with simpler screen recordings before spending heavily on animated studios.
- **Opening physical centers too soon.** Brick-and-mortar branches add heavy rent and payroll costs that must be covered by real student fees.
- **Confusing app downloads with paying users.** App installs, free trials, and paying subscribers are completely different numbers.
- **Adding AI just for marketing buzz.** If you introduce an automated tool, make sure it actually helps students learn and does not feed them wrong answers.

## What remains unknown

As of September 2026, Shikho’s website mentions over 3 million students and 4.5 million app downloads.[^shikho-site] These are marketing figures. They do not disclose how many students are actively paying every month.

Public records do not share audited revenues or profits. We also do not have verified data on customer acquisition costs (<Term name="cac">CAC</Term>), how many students return for the next grade, the exact costs of the Farmgate center, or independent studies on whether app users get higher board exam scores.

Every education business must answer three separate questions: **Will customers pay? Do students actually learn? And can the business cover its costs?** Having a good answer to one does not mean you have solved the other two.
