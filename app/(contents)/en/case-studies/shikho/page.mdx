# Shikho

<CaseCompany slug="shikho" locale="en" />

> **In short:** Shikho began with one maths course, then added live teaching, a skills business and a physical centre.[^fs-launch][^fs-acquisition][^founder-centre] Its story shows how different customer needs can shape a business as it grows. Study those choices, but keep three questions separate: do people pay, do they learn, and can the business cover its costs?

This case is for founders building an education business or a service that helps people learn a skill. It follows Shikho's choices from its first course to its newer work with artificial intelligence (AI) and partners. The lessons are our analysis; claims from Shikho and its investors are marked as such.

## At a glance

Shikho sells learning support for Bangladesh's school and admission exams. These are the basics behind the story.

| Item | Detail |
|---|---|
| Founded | 2019, by Shahir Chowdhury and Zeeshan Zakaria.[^fs-seed] |
| First product | A mobile course for general maths in classes 9–10, leading to the Secondary School Certificate (SSC) exam.[^fs-launch] |
| Core offer | Classes, practice tests, notes and progress reports.[^shikho-site] |
| Early revenue model | Paid access to courses, with free content or trials to let learners try the teaching.[^fs-seed] |
| Funding milestone | More than US$8 million in total funding announced by May 2025. The latest round's size was not disclosed.[^sbl-investment] |

The starting point was a specific exam need. The ambition was much larger.

## The problem: access to good teaching

Shahir Chowdhury had worked in banking in the UK. When he looked for a business to build in Bangladesh, financial technology was his first thought. But he felt companies such as bKash already served that space well. Education offered a different opening. Both his parents were teachers, and a report on education startups in China and India drew his interest.[^trt-founder]

The problem he chose was access to teaching. In Bangladesh, a family's options depended on where it lived and what it could spend on tutors or coaching. Shikho's proposed answer was to put structured lessons on a phone.[^fs-seed]

Language and exam fit mattered too. In a 2022 interview, Chowdhury described the focus as teaching Bangladesh's national curriculum in Bangla. A useful product had to help students with the syllabus and exams they actually faced.[^row-founder]

This is a useful way to think about a local product: translating the words is only part of the job. You also need to match the task, the deadline and the way people judge success.

## The first course was narrow, but complete

Shikho began with general maths for classes 9–10. Its launch report described videos, notes, worked answers and guidance on weak areas. Students bought access for a set period.[^fs-launch]

That choice kept the scope clear. A family could judge one concrete promise: help with SSC maths. The team could learn from one course before trying to serve every class and subject.

But a narrow product is not always a cheap product. Shikho was building several parts of the learning experience at once. A founder copying this approach should separate **a small audience** from **a small amount of work**.

The current academic offer still combines classes, notes, practice tests and report cards.[^shikho-site] The diagram below explains how those parts can support a learner.

The product question becomes more useful when it is specific: **what helps a learner move past a wrong answer?** Another video may help. So may a clearer worked answer, feedback or a teacher. Adding more content is only one possible response.

## What brought in paying customers

In August 2022, Chowdhury told Rest of World that purchases were coming from students who sought out help before board or university entrance exams. He reported about 170,000 monthly active users. That means people using the product during a month, not 170,000 paying customers.[^row-founder]

This suggests that an approaching exam gave the offer urgency. It does not tell us which channel brought in the first buyers or how much each sale cost. The sources reviewed do not provide that early sales breakdown.

The timing also had a downside. In his early TRT World interview, Chowdhury said pandemic school closures and shifting exam dates had removed a clear study target. Restrictions also made it harder to show the product to families in person.[^trt-founder] A need for online learning did not remove the work of finding buyers.

There were also two people to understand: the learner and the parent. Chowdhury described this shift in a June 2025 interview.[^ro-founder]

| Learner's stage | How he described the buying decision |
|---|---|
| Classes 6–8 | Parents choose the course and encourage use. |
| Classes 9–11 | Parents and students decide together. |
| Class 12 and admission preparation | The student's own motivation plays a larger role. |

These are the founder's observations, not a survey of all families. They point to a practical lesson: **the person who uses your product may need different proof from the person who pays.** A student may want help with a hard chapter. A parent may want to see steady effort and progress.

## Earning a family's trust

In April 2024, Shikho opened its first Exam and Experience Center in Farmgate. In his announcement, Chowdhury described two uses: supervised exams, and a place where students and parents could try paid courses on devices with help from staff.[^founder-centre]

A physical visit can make an unfamiliar service easier to judge. It also creates costs that a video library does not have. Future Startup's analysis raised that trade-off: the centres would need to bring enough business value to justify the extra spending.[^fs-centre]

<CaseDetail title="The decision: what should the centre prove?">

Our reading of this move is that Shikho was giving families a way to inspect the service before trusting it. The centre could support the online product without moving every lesson into a classroom.

To judge whether that works as a business, we would want to know how many visitors buy, how long they remain paying customers and what it costs to serve them. We would also need to ask how many would have bought online anyway.

The sources reviewed do not disclose those results. The centre is a useful decision to study, not proof that every online business should open branches.

</CaseDetail>

The Bangladesh-specific lesson is about the whole buying experience. A service can run online while still needing a familiar place, a person or a trusted partner to help a family buy it.

## Why the two brands stayed separate

In November 2021, Shikho acquired Bohubrihi, a platform for professional courses. The reported deal used cash and shares, meaning part ownership in the company. Its price was not made public. Bohubrihi's co-founder Yanur Islam Piash joined to lead the professional learning business.[^fs-acquisition]

That created a new challenge. Helping a school student pass an exam and helping an adult gain a work skill are both education, but the buying reasons are different.

The team tried another split: basic, lower-cost courses under “Shikho Skills” and deeper career courses under Bohubrihi. In a May 2023 interview, Piash said the same learners often needed both. Sending them to two places made learning and marketing harder. The team brought the courses back under Bohubrihi.[^fs-bohubrihi]

It kept the wider split by learner goal: Shikho for school studies, Bohubrihi for work skills. Chowdhury said each brand had its own marketing, product and engineering teams.[^fs-bohubrihi]

The useful lesson is **to organise around the customer's goal**. A shared owner does not require a shared message or course catalogue. Keeping two brands also means more work, so the split needs a reason beyond making the company look bigger.

The interview explains why the team changed course. It does not establish how much the change improved sales or profit.

## What scales, and what still costs money

Shikho's early paid model sold time-limited access to learning material. By July 2021, reporting described a seven-day free trial.[^fs-seed] That is a historical offer, not today's price list. Its 2026 Back to School campaign promotes full-year programmes with classes, tests and a printed FutureBook.[^shikho-school]

The key cost question is how much extra work comes with each new learner. Here is our analysis of the delivery choices, rather than Shikho's internal accounts.

| Part of the service | What can be reused | What still needs paying for |
|---|---|---|
| Recorded lessons and notes | The same material can serve many students. | Production, updates, hosting and support. |
| Live classes and help | A class can teach a group at once. | Teacher time, schedules and answers to questions. |
| Physical centres | A location can serve several batches. | Rent, staff, equipment and local operations. |

The lesson library can spread its costs across many buyers. Personal help and physical space have tighter limits. **A growing user count alone cannot tell you whether these costs are covered.**

Partnerships offer another route to customers. The April 2026 Grameenphone release describes discounted maths courses through MyGP, bringing discovery and payment into an app people already use.[^gp-partnership] It does not disclose sales, Shikho's share of each payment or how much the partnership costs to run.

## Capital and funding

Shikho raised outside money early. These selected announcements show the scale of that backing. They are funding figures, not revenue, profit or the company's value.

| Announcement | What was reported |
|---|---|
| July 2021 | US$1.3 million in new early-stage investment, following US$275,000 in earlier funding.[^fs-seed] |
| March 2022 | A further US$4 million, led by Wavemaker Partners. Shikho planned to expand its courses and team.[^fs-funding] |
| May 2025 | Startup Bangladesh announced investment alongside local investors. It put total funding above US$8 million, without stating the new round's size.[^sbl-investment] |

The last row is a running total, so do not add it to the earlier rounds. Nor should that total be read as cash still available to spend.

Funding was not a smooth path. In August 2024, Chowdhury said a proposed BDT 5 crore investment from Startup Bangladesh had been withdrawn for political reasons. The Daily Star reported his account; it was not an independently established finding about the fund's motives.[^ds-funding] The fund's own May 2025 announcement confirms a later investment.[^sbl-investment]

For a founder, a promised cheque and money received are different planning inputs. Keep a plan for delays.

## AI help when a student gets stuck

The test version of Shikho's AI study tool launched in February 2025. It focused on answering study questions in Bangla. Chowdhury told The Daily Star that educators reviewed outputs and students could rate answers. The first access was for existing paid users.[^ds-ai]

The choice of one task makes sense: help at the point where a learner cannot move forward. But fast answers can be wrong, and a correct answer can still do the thinking for the student.

A useful test would check whether learners can later solve a similar problem on their own. That is our suggested standard for judging the product, not a test Shikho has published. The interview explains the intended safeguards; it does not provide an independent measure of accuracy or learning gains.

## What founders can copy

- **Choose a clear first promise.** One subject and one exam give customers something concrete to judge. Define what a good result looks like before expanding.
- **Understand the buyer as well as the user.** A parent, school or employer may pay for someone else's use. Find out what each person needs to see.
- **Build the next useful step.** When someone finishes a lesson, form or tool, help them act on it. Track where they get stuck before adding features.
- **Keep different customer goals clear.** A new audience may need its own offer and team, even when the underlying technology is shared.

## What not to copy blindly

- **A large production budget.** Shikho had outside backing early. Its spending path is not a starting budget for a founder using personal savings.
- **Branches before evidence.** A physical location needs to earn enough from additional or longer-lasting customers to cover its costs.
- **Growth figures as proof of results.** Downloads, active users, paying users and students who learn more are different groups.
- **An AI feature because it is fashionable.** Start with a problem you can check and a way to catch harmful errors.

## What remains unknown

As checked on 13 September 2026, Shikho's site displays more than 3 million students and 4.5 million app downloads.[^shikho-site] These are company claims. They do not tell us how many people are currently paying, or whether a student is counted once across products.

The reviewed sources do not provide independently checked figures for current revenue or profit. We also lack the cost of winning a paying customer, the share who pay again, costs for each centre, and independent evidence of learning gains. We cannot compare the results of each major change on equal terms.

That leaves three separate questions for any business like this: **Do people pay? Do they benefit? Can the business afford to keep serving them?** Evidence for one does not answer the other two.
