# 10 Minute School

<CaseCompany slug="10-minute-school" locale="en" />

> **In short:** For its first five years, 10 Minute School offered every single lesson for free, funded entirely by corporate sponsorships. But when the pandemic struck and companies froze their marketing budgets overnight, the startup was left with almost no money in the bank. Instead of giving up, the founders wrote a simple spoken English PDF in two weeks and sold it online. Thousands of students bought it immediately, proving that Bangladeshi learners were ready to pay for good digital courses. From that turning point, 10MS evolved into Bangladesh's biggest online learning company - moving from free YouTube clips into paid live evening classes, offline study centers, and major venture capital backing.

## Problem

In Bangladesh, preparing for major university exams has long forced students to move to Dhaka. Every winter, thousands of high school graduates leave their home villages and travel to the capital. They squeeze into cramped student hostels in Farmgate and Nilkhet, paying high room rent and steep coaching fees just to compete for a few thousand public university seats. Families that cannot afford both the coaching fees and living costs in Dhaka are pushed out before the race even begins.

Ayman Sadiq saw this unfair gap with his own eyes while teaching at a Dhaka coaching center in 2012. He watched bright, talented students drop out of test prep simply because their parents could not scrape together the tuition money.[^fs-part-one]

With an estimated 42 million school students across Bangladesh, schools outside the big cities suffer from a severe shortage of great teachers, especially in maths, English, and science.[^techcrunch] Where a student was born determined their shot at higher education.

## Why the problem was Bangladesh-specific

Coaching centers in Bangladesh are more than just extra tutoring; they are a huge social tradition. Parents judge a student's chances of success by the famous coaching brands and star teachers they attend. Because these star tutors taught only in physical classrooms in Dhaka or Chittagong, top-tier teaching stayed locked in those cities.

Language and the official curriculum created a second barrier. Global platforms like Khan Academy or Coursera had great free lessons in English, but they did not help a student studying the Bangladeshi national curriculum. Bangladeshi board exams require following the exact national textbook (NCTB), learning science terms in Bangla, and answering specific question styles for university admission tests.

Money and travel created a third barrier. Affluent families could easily pay for a student to live in Dhaka, but middle-class and village families had to make painful sacrifices. The barrier was even higher for young women: family worries about safety and hostel living in Dhaka meant female students were often left behind. By 2023, 10 Minute School reported that 52 percent of its students lived in rural and semi-urban towns outside Dhaka, and 34 percent were women - groups that were previously locked out of top coaching classrooms.[^techcrunch]

## Founding insight

Ayman Sadiq discovered the power of online video as a university student at IBA, University of Dhaka. Coming from a Bangla-medium school, he had a tough time reading thick college accounting textbooks written in English. Searching online for help, he found YouTube tutorials that explained an entire semester's lessons in just 17 short videos.

The lightbulb went off: a talented teacher standing in front of a camera and whiteboard could explain a tough concept once and share it with millions of students across all 64 districts at the exact same time.[^fs-part-one]

It was a simple idea, but in 2015, nobody had built a complete library of video lessons in Bangla that matched the school curriculum. The real competition was not another software startup - it was the crowded chalkboard inside an expensive coaching center.

## First wedge

Instead of trying to teach every grade at once, 10 Minute School started with the most stressful, high-stakes moment in a student's life: public university admission tests.

High school seniors studying for university admission exams are highly motivated, have strict deadlines, and desperately want exam tips. Once 10MS earned the trust of these students, it worked its way backward: first into Class 11 and 12 (HSC), then Class 9 and 10 (SSC), and eventually younger grades and career skills.

Ayman explained in an interview that they only expanded to a new subject or grade after their current lessons had reached full momentum.[^fs-part-one] The first version of the product was very simple: a basic website embedding free YouTube videos, accompanied by summary notes and short multiple-choice quizzes.

## Early customers

10 Minute School gained its first few million learners without spending a single taka on paid ads. Ayman was already popular among students through his classroom teaching and early Facebook videos. When the website launched on 17 May 2015, viral traffic from Facebook was so huge that the website crashed within 30 minutes. The team worked all night to upgrade the servers and bring it back online.[^fs-part-one]

The initial team was made up entirely of Ayman's former students. Except for one full-time developer, the early team worked without pay, driven by the dream of making quality education free for everyone.[^fs-part-one]

For new founders, there is a big caveat here: building a team of talented unpaid volunteers is not a repeatable business strategy. It worked because Ayman was an extraordinarily popular and charismatic teacher, not because volunteer labor is a normal way to run a company.

## Trust building

The secret to 10MS's early trust was simple: every video, note, and quiz was 100% free. Students and parents could check the quality of the teaching without spending any money.

To keep the lights on without charging students, the team looked for corporate sponsors. In mid-2015, Ayman began pitching mobile telecom operator Robi Axiata. After eight months of meetings, Robi signed a title sponsorship deal in February 2016 for 70 lakh taka in first-year funding. This allowed Ayman to graduate and work on the project full-time. Soon, other brands like IPDC Finance, Unilever, Walton, and IDLC joined as sponsors.[^fs-part-one]

This corporate deal forced the student team to grow up quickly. To sign a formal contract with a big telecom company, the startup had to officially incorporate, get a tax certificate (<Term name="e-tin">e-TIN</Term>), open a commercial bank account, and keep proper accounting books. Ayman later noted that without Robi's corporate rules, 10MS might have stayed an informal YouTube channel for years.[^fs-part-one]

## Local lessons

Silicon Valley investors often tell founders to charge customers from day one. In Bangladesh, 10 Minute School showed that when people have low incomes and rarely use digital payments, corporate sponsorships can buy you the time (<Term name="runway">runway</Term>) to build a household brand.

Second, the company treated social media as its primary top-of-funnel engine. By distributing video lessons directly across Facebook and YouTube where students already spent hours daily, 10MS generated strong organic reach and kept direct customer acquisition costs (<Term name="cac">CAC</Term>) low, even though content production and creator overhead remained real operational expenses.

Third, corporate rules can help you build discipline early. Learning to meet the requirements of a multinational partner forces a young team to set up proper legal and accounting systems before raising venture capital.

## Constraints and limits

**The sponsorship trap.** Living on corporate sponsorships is risky. When the COVID-19 pandemic hit in 2020, companies instantly froze their marketing and charity budgets. Sponsorship checks dried up overnight. Ayman recalled having only a few thousand taka left in the bank and needing personal financial support from his father. The company was just four weeks away from shutting down completely.[^fs-part-two]

**Higher expectations when charging money.** Giving away free lessons is very different from selling paid courses. As Ayman noted, free users do not mind if a website goes down or a note has a typo. But once a student pays, they expect fast customer service, bug-free apps, and personal attention when they get stuck. Fixing these issues required hiring full-time support teams and upgrading servers, which cost real money.[^fs-part-two]

**Hiring tech talent.** Finding experienced software engineers and product managers in Dhaka remains a constant struggle for growing startups.[^fs-part-two]

**Public fame brings political risks.** Being a famous founder can backfire. In July 2024, during the student quota protests, Ayman posted messages on social media supporting student protesters. Days later, government-backed Startup Bangladesh Limited abruptly cancelled a planned 5 crore taka investment in 10MS.[^ds-cancel] After the change in government, the investment offer was restored.[^tbs-restore] Earlier, in July 2020, Ayman faced serious militant threats over controversial social media posts by a former colleague, requiring police protection.[^dt-threats]

## Business model

10 Minute School runs on a freemium model. Its massive library of recorded school lessons remains completely free to the public, drawing in millions of students. The company makes money from paid masterclasses, live online study batches, career skill courses, and offline coaching centers. Ayman pointed out that while 100 percent of content was originally free, moving to a model where 95 percent of lessons remain free still reaches far more students than in their early years.[^fs-part-two]

<CaseDetail title="The first paid product">

The move to paid courses started with a fast experiment in mid-2020: a downloadable spoken English PDF booklet priced at just 75 taka. Written in just two weeks, the digital booklet sold over 100,000 copies, bringing in as much money as a full year of corporate sponsorship.[^fs-part-two] This simple test proved that ordinary Bangladeshi students and job-seekers were ready to pay through bKash and Nagad for clear educational materials.

</CaseDetail>

While [Shikho started with an end-to-end SSC curriculum](/en/case-studies/shikho#the-first-course-was-narrow-but-complete), 10 Minute School used its 75-taka PDF as a quick test of customer demand. It proved people would pay, though a simple ebook could not test live teaching or ongoing student support.

Today, 10 Minute School earns revenue in three main ways:

- **Recorded self-paced courses:** Practical skills such as spoken English and MS Office. The cost of making a course can be spread across many buyers, helping its <Term name="gross-margin">gross margin</Term>. Sales, support, and keeping the material up to date still need a budget.
- **Live online interactive batches:** In his 2022 interview, Ayman described school and admission-prep classes priced at 750 taka per subject per month.[^fs-part-two] Fixed class times, quizzes, and live help give students a routine and reasons to return. Renewals bring in <Term name="mrr-arr">monthly recurring revenue</Term> for as long as students keep paying.
- **Physical study centers:** Opening physical learning centers in busy student hubs like Farmgate and Uttara helps win over hesitant parents. In Bangladesh, parents paying for important admission coaching want in-person exams and face-to-face counseling.

On the spending side, Ayman tracked six main costs: filming lessons, online ads, team salaries, cloud servers, office rent, and camera equipment. Before launching a course for a new grade, the team ran the numbers carefully. They required the expected customer revenue (<Term name="ltv">LTV</Term>) to be at least three times the cost of acquiring the student (<Term name="cac">CAC</Term>).[^fs-part-two]

## Capital and funding

For its first seven years, 10 Minute School ran entirely on corporate sponsorships, cash from sales, and word-of-mouth growth. In early 2022, the company raised its first venture capital: a $2M seed round from Sequoia Capital India's Surge program. Closing that deal took eight months of careful audits and legal checks.[^fs-part-two]

In October 2023, 10MS raised a $5.5M pre-Series A round led by Conjunction Capital, with Peak XV Partners (formerly Sequoia India), Startup Bangladesh Limited, CRED founder Kunal Shah, and Sajid Rahman from MyAsiaVC, bringing total funding to $7.5M.[^techcrunch]

At the time of that round, the company reported 3.2 million monthly active students, over 650,000 paying users, and 6.7 million total app downloads.[^fs-funding]

This gives founders an important lesson: 10 Minute School did not use venture capital to invent a business from scratch. It used venture capital to pour fuel on a business that was already popular, trusted, and making money.

## What to copy

- Build an audience with free, helpful content before charging money. High-quality educational media creates powerful organic reach and keeps direct acquisition costs (<Term name="cac">CAC</Term>) low, provided you account for production overhead.
- Use cheap, simple digital products (like a 75-taka PDF) to test if people will pay before spending months building software.
- Use live classes and helpful mentors to keep students engaged where plain recorded videos fail.
- Raise non-dilutive money early through brand sponsorships, corporate budgets, or vendor partnerships.
- Expand into new subjects only after your existing lessons are running smoothly and sustainably.
- Let corporate partner rules help you set up clean company paperwork, bank accounts, and taxes early.

## What not to copy

- Do not build a company entirely around the founder's personal celebrity. If the founder gets sick or enters a controversy, the whole company suffers.
- Do not treat corporate sponsorship checks as guaranteed yearly income. Marketing budgets are always the first thing companies cut in hard times.
- Do not rely on unpaid volunteers once you move past the initial prototype stage. A serious business requires paid, accountable professionals.
- Do not count on government investment promises until the money is actually deposited in your bank account.
- Do not rely only on pre-recorded videos to make money. Most students need live classes, schedules, and live help to finish what they start.

## Still unknown

10MS does not publish audited financial accounts, official profit numbers, student dropout rates in live batches, or profit margins for its offline centers.

Its promotional materials mention over 17 million learners nationwide across online classes, English courses, and offline coaching centers.[^tenms-site] Founders should view these as self-reported company milestones rather than independently audited user numbers.
