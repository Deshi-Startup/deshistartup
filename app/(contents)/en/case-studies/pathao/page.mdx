# Pathao

> **In short:** Pathao did not begin as a super app. The team tested demand with three delivery riders, a spreadsheet, and hands-on customer acquisition. Ride-hailing grew rapidly from that narrow start. Pathao later spread across ten services and neglected the core rides product, co-founder Hussain M Elius wrote in 2026. There are two big lessons for a new founder: test the behavior before building the app, and do not add a service until the same customer habit and the same company strength genuinely carry over.

## Problem

The first problem was delivering e-commerce products across Dhaka. Pathao began in 2015 by moving goods for small online sellers on motorbikes. In a founder interview, Elius said the team first created a secret Facebook group and asked friends whether they needed anything delivered.[^early-interview]

The team soon saw an adjacent problem. Bikes and riders sat idle between deliveries while passengers lost time in Dhaka traffic. Carrying people on the same bikes could turn an idle asset into a second source of revenue. Pathao therefore noticed not only the passenger's problem but also unused time inside its own operation.[^super-app-retrospective]

## Why the problem was Bangladesh-specific

According to Elius, Bangladesh did not then have a familiar motorbike-taxi habit like Vietnam or Indonesia. The company had to persuade passengers to ride behind a stranger and convince bike owners to join the platform. That is a founder's description of the market, not an independent market survey.[^early-interview]

Regulation also followed the product. Pathao launched rides in 2016. The cabinet approved the Ride-sharing Service Guideline 2017 in January 2018.[^brta-policy] The team was therefore operating a new service while its dedicated regulatory framework was still taking shape.

Bangladesh's opportunity and risk sat in the same place. The absence of a familiar service left room for a new company, but that company also had to lead on trust, driver documents, safety, and regulatory work.

## Founding insight

Elius described the early scene more clearly in a 2026 retrospective. The original Pathao was three delivery riders and a spreadsheet. An early customer named Poter Bibi negotiated the rate down to Tk 60 per delivery. The team tested passenger rides because its delivery riders had idle hours between jobs.[^super-app-retrospective]

The rides MVP was five bikes, Elius's personal phone number, and an Excel sheet. He took bookings by phone and scheduled the rides himself. Before building a full app, the team could answer three questions: would people get on a bike, would it save meaningful time, and would they call again after the first ride?[^early-interview]

The three delivery riders and five ride-hailing bikes describe different stages, not contradictory accounts: one was the original courier operation and the other the later passenger pilot.

## First wedge

The first wedge was e-commerce courier delivery. A narrow customer group, one defined job, and a few controlled bikes let the team learn delivery operations. It then used those assets' idle hours for rides. The December 2016 app turned the phone-and-spreadsheet model into a larger platform.[^early-interview]

Expansion accelerated. By October 2017, Pathao had launched rides in Chittagong and cars in Dhaka. Food delivery followed in January 2018.[^early-interview] Each new service had a plausible argument: it could reuse bikes, drivers, mapping, payments, and operations. Pathao would later learn that two businesses can look adjacent without actually being the same business.

## Early customers

The first ride customers did not come from app-store advertising. The team stood outside universities in Banani, asked students where they were going, and offered to take them there for Tk 100. Many were afraid or confused. After doing this for three days and handing out booking cards, the team began receiving calls.[^early-interview]

Elius's 2026 account describes BRAC University students crossing in five minutes a route that otherwise took thirty. He says word spread through campus Facebook groups without advertising spend.[^super-app-retrospective] This is a founder's recollection, but the lesson is useful: when behavior is new, the founder can stand beside the first user and explain the service.

## Trust building

Trust began as a human process. The team stood in public, answered the phone, matched bookings, and helped customers overcome their hesitation about a first ride. After the app launched, driver names and photos, trip tracking, ratings, and trip sharing turned parts of that human reassurance into product rules.[^nepal-launch]

Safety work did not end at launch. In 2021, Pathao announced accident coverage for passengers and riders on active trips through Green Delta Insurance. The same announcement described GPS tracking, identity information, and an in-app emergency button.[^insurance] These were company-announced measures. There is no public, continuous, independent dataset showing whether accident rates subsequently fell.

## Local lessons

**Look for idle assets.** Pathao did not buy an entirely new infrastructure to find its adjacent market. It used bikes and riders that were idle between delivery jobs. A small founder can look for unused time in a kitchen, vehicle, sales team, or software system.

**Go into the field when the behavior is unfamiliar.** Riding behind a stranger on a motorbike was new. The first sales therefore happened outside universities, not through digital ads. Standing beside the customer reveals hesitation faster.

**Separate shared infrastructure from shared behavior.** Rides and courier shared bikes, maps, and operations. Health and video content required different intent, expertise, and regulation. Elius later wrote that Pathao mistook being inside one app for evidence that the services were one business.[^super-app-retrospective]

**Use a small country as a laboratory.** Pathao launched rides in Nepal in 2018. It did not take health or video there; it took a bike-rides model learned in Dhaka. Elius credits focus on the core service and a local team with helping the company face resistance in that market.[^super-app-retrospective] Pathao's Nepal site still presents ride and food services.[^nepal-site]

## Constraints, mistakes, and the turnaround

**Safety and documents.** A July 2019 Daily Star investigation used internal Pathao documents to report that a large share of active drivers had incomplete documents. It also raised questions about accidents and the company's ability to verify records. Those figures describe a specific period in 2019, not the current fleet.[^daily-star-2019] Pathao received its BRTA enlistment certificate that December.[^brta-license] The founder lesson is that retrofitting compliance becomes costlier and riskier after rapid scale.

**Too many services.** Under a “Pathao Super App” strategy around 2018, the company eventually operated ten verticals, Elius wrote in 2026. The list included rides, courier, cars, and food alongside health and Pathao TV. He says almost nobody used Pathao TV, yet it continued to consume engineering time while bugs and useful features in the core rides product waited.[^super-app-retrospective]

**Spending and headcount after funding.** The same 2019 Daily Star investigation reported 353 job cuts, former employees' allegations of forced resignation letters, heavy marketing spending, and rapid hiring. The article did not contain company answers to every allegation, so these should not be read as adjudicated findings. The episode nevertheless shows how quickly fixed costs can rise after a large round.[^daily-star-2019]

**Gig-worker conditions.** Pathao and Pathao Food provided some safety evidence and earned one point in Fairwork's 2021 Bangladesh ratings, but Pathao's total score was 1 out of 10. The study, based on interviews with 103 gig workers across platforms, found gaps in fair contracts, management, and representation.[^fairwork] The sample does not represent Pathao's entire network, but it carries an important warning: marketplace “supply” is made of people, and their income and safety are part of the product risk.

**Leadership transition.** Fahim Ahmed joined as CFO in 2018, took responsibility for day-to-day operations from mid-2020, and replaced Elius as CEO in November 2021. Elius remained on the board as a senior adviser.[^ceo-transition] In its 2024 funding announcement, Pathao claimed it had become profitable and financially robust over the preceding two years.[^funding-2024] Public evidence does not establish that the leadership change caused the turnaround; it establishes only the sequence.

## Business model

Pathao does not earn from one source. The model combines fees for matching passengers and drivers, restaurant ordering and delivery, merchant parcel logistics, and newer payment and credit-like financial services. On 2 August 2026, the company's site listed Bike, Car, Food, Shop, Parcel, Courier, Rentals, Pathao Pay, and Maps.[^pathao-site]

The real business metric is not total transaction value or downloads. A founder needs to know what remains from each ride or order after driver and delivery payouts, promotions, refunds, support, fraud, insurance, and regulatory costs. Pathao does not publicly report current commissions, margins, or profit by service.

In 2018, Elius said cars had higher margins than bikes and food had higher margins than either.[^early-interview] That was a founder statement about the business at the time, not an audit of full cost or current economics. The 2024 round was explicitly intended to scale Pathao Pay and Pay Later. The company said Pay Later had reached gross profitability.[^funding-2024]

## Capital and funding

The Daily Star reported an $825,000 round from Battery Road Digital and Skycatcher in 2016.[^daily-star-2019] Go-Jek led a pre-Series B in April 2018. The amount was undisclosed, but TechCrunch reported, citing a source, that it exceeded $10 million. Pathao confirmed a valuation above $100 million at the time.[^techcrunch-2018]

That capital supported rapid expansion. The company reported 500 employees, one million monthly rides, and more than one hundred thousand monthly deliveries in 2018.[^techcrunch-2018] These were company-supplied figures. Within a year, reporting described a stalled funding environment, cost pressure, and layoffs.[^daily-star-2019]

VentureSouq led another $12 million pre-Series B announced in September 2024. Pathao said this brought total capital raised to more than $50 million.[^funding-2024] Both the 2018 and 2024 announcements used “pre-Series B.” A founder should therefore compare the date, amount, and terms instead of inferring a company's stage from the round label.

Pathao's funding story has two sides. Capital helped build the app, network, and international operation. Elius's retrospective also says the investor-friendly super-app narrative and Go-Jek's rising valuation encouraged Pathao to build more services than it should have.[^super-app-retrospective]

## What to copy

- Test demand with phones, spreadsheets, and manual operations before building the full app.
- Stand beside the first customer when the behavior is new, and listen for fear or confusion.
- Find idle assets and calculate which adjacent problem the same bikes, team, or software can genuinely solve.
- Before adding a service, ask whether it deepens the same user habit or starts an entirely new business.
- Take one proven product abroad and let the local team handle the market's language and resistance.
- Assign ownership for driver verification, safety, insurance, complaints, and regulatory paperwork before scaling.
- Track unit economics separately for each service instead of using one service's GMV to hide another's losses.

## What not to copy

- Do not make “super app” the starting strategy. First win the one job that makes a customer open your product.
- Do not assume two services share a customer habit because their buttons sit inside one app.
- Do not treat funding and valuation as proof of a durable business. The large 2018 round was followed by cost and layoff trouble in 2019.
- Do not mistake promotion-funded rides for retained customers. Check whether the same cohort returns after discounts stop.
- Do not reduce gig workers to a supply metric. Their net earnings, safety, appeal process, and contract language are business risks.
- Do not treat company-reported user, job, profit, or market-leadership figures as independent audits.

## Still unknown

Pathao does not publish current audited revenue or profit, margin by service, retention, the distribution of drivers' and delivery workers' net earnings, a continuous accident rate, or Pay Later's default and loss rates. In its 2025 tenth-anniversary announcement, the company claimed more than 10 million users, 300,000 drivers and delivery agents, 200,000 merchants, and 10,000 restaurants.[^pathao-ten-years] Those are company figures, not independently audited data.

Public information also does not isolate which services, cost reductions, or management decisions moved Pathao from the 2019 crisis to its claimed profitability in 2024. This case study therefore teaches strategy and operations. It is not a current financial valuation of Pathao or investment advice.
