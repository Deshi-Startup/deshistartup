# Pathao

<CaseCompany slug="pathao" locale="en" />

> **In short:** Pathao did not start as a giant app. It started with just three delivery riders, a shared spreadsheet, and a Facebook group. After delivering packages for online shops, the team noticed something: their riders were sitting idle in the middle of the day, right while Dhaka commuters were stuck in terrible traffic. So they tested passenger rides manually using a phone number before building the app in late 2016. Fast growth brought millions in venture capital, but expanding into ten different services burned too much cash. After painful layoffs in 2019 and a stalled digital wallet, Pathao refocused on its core businesses: courier deliveries, bike rides, and food, before earning a formal central bank license to launch Pathao Pay. The big lesson for founders: master one habit first, make sure each ride or delivery actually makes money, and do not let investor cash hide weak <Term name="unit-economics">unit economics</Term>.

## Problem

Pathao did not set out to fix traffic first. It started with e-commerce parcels. In 2015, online sellers on Facebook had a hard time delivering items reliably across Dhaka. Pathao stepped in with a small fleet of motorbikes. Co-founder Hussain M Elius explained in an interview that the team started in a private Facebook group, asking friends if they needed packages delivered across town.[^early-interview]

Soon, the team spotted a smart opportunity. Their delivery riders were super busy in the morning and evening, but had almost nothing to do in the middle of the day. Meanwhile, millions of everyday people in Dhaka were stuck in gridlock for hours. The founders realized they could use those same motorbikes and riders to carry passengers during the slow hours. That let them turn empty downtime into extra cash before building a full passenger app.[^super-app-retrospective]

## Why the problem was Bangladesh-specific

In countries like Vietnam or Indonesia, people had hopped on the back of motorbike taxis for decades. But Bangladesh had no culture of sharing a two-wheeler with a stranger. Hussain M Elius noted that Pathao had to teach people to trust a brand-new habit, rather than simply putting an old street habit onto a smartphone.[^early-interview]

There were also no clear rules yet. When Pathao launched rides in late 2016, no government policy existed for ride-hailing apps. The cabinet only approved official guidelines in early 2018,[^brta-policy] and Pathao received its formal transport certificate from the BRTA in late 2019.[^brta-license]

This gave Pathao a wide-open field to grow fast without big rivals. But it also meant the company had to figure out safety, driver checks, and legal rules on its own while rushing to keep up with skyrocketing demand.

## Founding insight

Pathao’s early edge was not fancy software. It was figuring out how things worked by hand before writing code.

In a 2026 retrospective, Hussain M Elius described the company’s beginning as three riders and a spreadsheet. He recalled an early merchant bargaining a delivery fee down to 60 taka. Running the business by hand taught the founders what sellers were willing to pay, where deliveries got stuck, and how many riders were actually ready to work - all long before they spent money on developers or marketing.[^super-app-retrospective]

Before writing any app code, the team checked three basic questions: Did passengers actually save time? Did drivers show up when called? And would passengers call back again?[^early-interview]

## First wedge

The first wedge was delivering packages for online shops. It gave Pathao regular daily orders, paying business clients, and a chance to practice onboarding riders and managing routes.

Ride-hailing was built on top of that existing rider base. Then came cars and food. By October 2017, Pathao had launched rides outside Dhaka and introduced cars. In January 2018, it rolled out food delivery.[^early-interview] On paper, adding services looked easy: they all used bikes, maps, and the same recognizable brand. The trap was thinking that sharing an app meant customers would automatically use every service.

Pathao launched in Nepal in September 2018.[^nepal-launch] Its Kathmandu business still runs rides and food today. This showed that expanding a working model into a similar nearby country worked better than building tons of random apps in Dhaka.[^nepal-site]

## Early customers

The first ride customers came from pure street hustle. Elius and the team stood outside universities in Banani, walked up to students stuck in traffic, and offered rides for 100 taka. After days of talking to students face-to-face and handing out cards, people started calling in.[^early-interview]

BRAC University students began sharing the service in campus Facebook groups because motorbikes zipped through Dhaka traffic in minutes instead of hours.[^super-app-retrospective] The practical lesson for founders is simple: when launching a new idea, go stand right next to your first users. Seeing why they hesitate teaches you more than spending money on online ads.

## Trust building

Trust started with real human contact. The team answered phone calls, matched riders, and explained how the service worked. As the app grew, driver ID checks, user ratings, live GPS tracking, and route sharing replaced phone calls with built-in app safety.[^nepal-launch]

Keeping users safe took steady work and money. In 2021, Pathao introduced insurance for passengers and riders on active trips through Green Delta Insurance, backed by GPS monitoring, verified driver records, and an emergency button in the app.[^insurance] In gig apps, trust is more than nice marketing. It requires real safety rules, driver vetting, and insurance if something goes wrong.

## Local lessons

**Put idle assets to work before buying new ones.** Pathao tested passenger rides with motorbikes and riders it already had. Look around your own business: can you use free staff hours, quiet vehicle time, or existing merchant contacts to solve a new problem?

**Sell the habit by hand before building an app.** A Facebook group, phone calls, and talking to students on the street proved that people wanted bike rides. The software came after people were already paying.

**Shared tech is not the same as shared demand.** Delivering packages and giving rides shared bikes and maps. But Elius later explained that trying to build a "super app" pushed Pathao into about ten different services - even things like Pathao TV - while the main rides business got neglected.[^super-app-retrospective]

**Rules and licenses are part of the product.** Pathao’s first digital wallet showed this clearly. Building a handy feature inside your app does not bypass the need for a central bank license.[^pathao-pay-2018]

## Constraints, mistakes and the turnaround

**Driver documents and rules.** A 2019 Daily Star investigation showed serious gaps in driver background checks and paperwork inside Pathao.[^daily-star-2019] Even though Pathao received its official BRTA certificate later that year,[^brta-license] the mess proved a tough lesson: skipping paperwork to grow faster creates a costly backlog that is painful to fix later.

**Too many apps at once.** Hussain M Elius wrote in 2026 that trying to build a super app spread Pathao across roughly ten services. He noted that chasing shiny new ideas pulled engineering time and attention away from rides and parcel delivery - the two engines that actually paid the bills.[^super-app-retrospective]

**The 2019 cost shock and layoffs.** The Daily Star reported 353 job cuts, while other news reports described over 300 employees leaving as Pathao restructured to stop burning cash.[^daily-star-2019][^downsizing-2019] News stories also pointed out harsh complaints about sudden layoffs and forced resignations during the cutbacks.[^daily-star-2019]

**Big ad budgets without lasting results.** Internal records reported by The Daily Star showed that Pathao spent 13.8 million taka on branding and PR between May and mid-June 2019, on top of 25 million taka in January, 9.3 million in February, and 9.19 million in April.[^daily-star-2019] Big campaigns like #EkShatheMovingBangladesh were seen everywhere, but they did not lead to any clear rise in daily rides during the campaign period.[^daily-star-2019] Burning money on flashy ads before locking in strong customer <Term name="retention">retention</Term> just wastes cash.

**Rivals can break your expansion plans.** In 2022, Rest of World reported that Pathao Food’s market share had fallen to about 20% - half of what it was in 2019 - after Foodpanda poured millions into discounts and promotions.[^rest-of-world-food] Just because you have delivery bikes does not mean you can easily beat a focused rival with deep pockets.

**Fair conditions for gig workers.** A 2021 study by Fairwork gave Pathao a low score of 1 out of 10, highlighting worker complaints about low take-home pay, sudden account bans, and poor support when things went wrong.[^fairwork] Looking only at app metrics can hide driver frustration. If drivers feel mistreated, service quality drops, riders complain, and protests follow.

**The turnaround: cutting costs and focusing on what works.** Fahim Ahmed joined as CFO in 2018, became president, and took over as CEO in November 2021.[^ceo-transition] Under his lead, Pathao made big changes: they shut down side projects like Pathao TV and Tong, stopped paying unsustainable driver cash bonuses, turned Pathao Courier into a profitable nationwide delivery network, and made sure each service actually made money on its own.[^leadership-interview-2021]

## The payment setback that became a fintech strategy

Pathao tried launching payments early. In 2018, Bangladesh Bank said Pathao was not authorized to run its wallet. Elius said it had run for about six weeks as an internal wallet and that the company planned to come back under official rules.[^pathao-pay-2018]

Coming back took years. In April 2023, Pathao announced that its subsidiary, Digital Payments Limited, had earned a Payment Service Provider license from Bangladesh Bank.[^psp-license-2023] Pathao Pay then launched in a quiet beta in March 2024,[^pathao-pay-2024] followed by a full nationwide rollout in July 2025.[^pathao-pay-nationwide-2025]

The company's public branding changed too. In October 2023, Pathao switched its old slogan "Moving Bangladesh" to "HERE WITH YOU," making financial services a central part of its future.[^here-with-you]

This is a powerful lesson for founders: a regulatory block does not have to be the end of the road. But fixing it often takes a separate company structure, the right license, and years of patient work.

## Business model

Pathao makes money across four main services:

1. **Rides (Bikes and Cars):** Pathao takes a share of the ride fare, known as its <Term name="take-rate">take-rate</Term>. Drivers cover fuel and vehicle maintenance. Pathao's share must cover its own costs, including support and promotions. If a driver and rider agree to cancel and pay outside the app, Pathao loses that commission.
2. **Merchant Deliveries (Pathao Courier):** A nationwide parcel delivery network for online shops and social-commerce sellers. Pathao charges for delivery based on weight and destination, and for handling <Term name="cod">cash on delivery (COD)</Term>. Grouping parcels along the same route helps spread the cost of each trip.
3. **Food Delivery:** Pathao earns a commission on restaurant orders alongside customer delivery fees. Food delivery involves higher operational friction than rides because couriers travel to restaurants, wait for food preparation, and transport perishable meals safely. Smaller order sizes compress unit economics unless delivery fees or restaurant commissions adequately cover the dedicated courier trip.
4. **Fintech and Credit (Pathao Pay and Pay Later):** Pathao Pay offers digital payments, while Pay Later provides short-term consumer credit.[^pathao-site] Both can reach people who already use Pathao, giving the company a starting audience for these services.

Back in 2018, high-frequency food delivery and car rides were seen as the big growth engines.[^early-interview] Over time, however, merchant parcel logistics provided a steady, foundational volume base. In 2024, Pathao reported that Pay Later had turned profitable and that the consolidated company had sustained operational profitability over the preceding two years.[^funding-2024]

## Capital and funding

The full investment history is not completely public, but the main steps are well documented.

The Daily Star reported that Pathao raised $825,000 from Battery Road Digital and Skycatcher after registering in Bangladesh in 2016.[^daily-star-2019] Dhaka Tribune reported that Indonesian giant Go-Jek invested about $2 million in 2017.[^gojek-2017]

In April 2018, Go-Jek led another investment. Pathao did not share the exact number, but TechCrunch reported it was over $10 million, and Hussain M Elius confirmed the company was valued at more than $100 million.[^techcrunch-2018]

In September 2024, VentureSouq led a $12 million pre-Series B round with Anchorless Bangladesh, Osiris Group, South Asia Tech, and Openspace Ventures. Pathao stated that its total fundraising passed $50 million and that the new funds would grow its fintech products.[^funding-2024]

Notice an interesting detail: both the 2018 and 2024 rounds were called "pre-Series B." Round names are marketing labels, not strict accounting terms. Founders should pay attention to dates, valuations, and contract terms rather than getting excited by the name of a round.

## Where Pathao stands now

Pathao survived the 2019 crash without giving up on its core businesses. For its tenth anniversary in 2025, the company announced over 10 million users, 300,000 riders and delivery drivers, 200,000 merchants, and 10,000 restaurants.[^pathao-ten-years] Keep in mind these are company-reported numbers, not an independent audit of daily active users.

Forbes Asia named Pathao to its 100 to Watch list in 2025, calling it a leading consumer-tech company in Bangladesh across rides, food, logistics, and fintech.[^forbes-2025]

The real takeaway is not that Pathao won every single category. It is that Pathao held on to enough drivers, merchants, and daily users after the 2019 crisis to rebuild its business on solid, profitable ground.

## What to copy

- Test customer interest with simple spreadsheets, phone calls, and real hustle before building software.
- When introducing an unfamiliar habit, go talk to customers face-to-face and see what worries them.
- Use tools, staff, or vehicles you already have before buying new systems.
- Before launching a new service, make sure customers actually want it from you, not just that you have bikes to deliver it.
- Set up proper licenses, safety rules, and paperwork early so you do not have to clean up a big mess later.
- Set a clear goal before spending on big ad campaigns. Getting your name known and getting actual orders are two different things.
- Track profit and loss separately for each service so high volume in one does not hide heavy losses in another.
- Treat government regulations as a strict design rule, not as an obstacle you can ignore as you grow.

## What not to copy

- Do not try to be a "super app" on day one. Win one clear job that gives customers a reason to open your app every week.
- Do not assume that raising big venture rounds means your underlying business is healthy.
- Do not hire a huge team assuming the next investment check will arrive on schedule.
- Do not keep a losing product alive just because it sounds impressive in investor pitch decks.
- Do not celebrate a marketing campaign without checking whether it brought in real paying users.
- Do not treat delivery riders and drivers as simple numbers. Their pay, safety, and respect determine your service quality.
- Do not present self-reported user numbers or claims of market leadership as audited facts.

## Still unknown

Pathao does not publicly share its complete ownership list (<Term name="cap-table">cap table</Term>), audited profits, profit margins for each separate service, customer acquisition costs (<Term name="cac">CAC</Term>), or loan default rates for Pay Later.

The exact dollar amount of the 2018 Go-Jek round was never officially confirmed, nor were the valuation terms of the 2024 financing. Pathao says total fundraising topped $50 million, but public reports do not detail every single check.[^funding-2024]

While Pathao announced that it reached overall operating profitability, we do not have public numbers showing exactly how much profit comes from rides versus courier deliveries or fintech. For founders, the real lesson is how the team shifted from burning cash on endless expansion to building a focused, profitable business.
